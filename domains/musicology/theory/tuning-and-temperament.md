# The Theory of the Musicology Domain: Tuning and Temperament

*Status: draft (2026-11-13). The results the domain's code relies on, with their hypotheses and proofs or citations. Each section names its module. Sizes in cents quoted here are rounded from what the code computes; every identity among ratios is exact, and is checked exactly.*

## 1. Intervals as a Group

*Module `pitch`.* A tone is taken with one frequency $f > 0$, its pitch.

**Definition 1.1 (intervals).** The interval from a tone of frequency $f_1$ to a tone of frequency $f_2$ is the ratio $f_2 / f_1$. Intervals compose by multiplication, so the group of intervals is $I = (\mathbb{R}_{>0}, \cdot)$: the unison is $1$, and the inverse of an interval is the same interval taken downward. The size of an interval in cents is
$$c(r) = 1200 \log_2 r,$$
an isomorphism $c : I \to (\mathbb{R}, +)$ under which the octave $2$ has size 1200 (Ellis 1885).

**Definition 1.2 (just intervals).** The just intervals are the subgroup $J = \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$. For a prime $p$, the $p$-limit $J_p$ is the subgroup generated by the primes up to $p$.

**Proposition 1.1 (the just intervals are free on the primes).** $J$ is a free abelian group whose basis is the set of primes. In particular $J_5 \cong \mathbb{Z}^3$ by $2^a 3^b 5^c \mapsto (a, b, c)$.

*Proof.* The fundamental theorem of arithmetic: every positive rational is a product of integer powers of primes, in exactly one way. ∎

**Proposition 1.2 (the logarithms of the primes are independent over $\mathbb{Q}$).** Let $p_1, \dots, p_k$ be distinct primes and $e_1, \dots, e_k \in \mathbb{Q}$. If $\prod_i p_i^{e_i} = 1$, then every $e_i = 0$.

*Proof.* Let $N$ be a common denominator of the $e_i$. Raising to the power $N$ gives $\prod_i p_i^{N e_i} = 1$ with integer exponents, and Proposition 1.1 gives $N e_i = 0$ for every $i$. ∎

**Corollary 1.3 (intervals with rational exponents).** The set $P = \{\prod_p p^{e_p} : e_p \in \mathbb{Q}, \text{almost all } 0\}$ is a subgroup of $I$ isomorphic to $\bigoplus_p \mathbb{Q}$, the vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$ with the primes as basis; it is $J \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, the smallest subgroup of $I$ that holds $J$ and every root of its elements. An element of $P$ has one family of exponents, and it lies in $J$ exactly when they are integers.

This is the type `Interval`. Every tuning the domain builds has its degrees in $P$: the fifth of quarter-comma meantone is $5^{1/4}$, the semitone of twelve equal divisions $2^{1/12}$. Equality in $P$ is equality of exponents, so identities among tempered intervals are decided in exact arithmetic on morphe's rationals, without a tolerance. *Checked:* the group laws, $x \mapsto x^q$ a homomorphism with inverse $x \mapsto x^{1/q}$, and a ratio factored and multiplied out again.

## 2. Octave Equivalence

*Module `pitch`.* Tones an octave apart are heard as the same note in another register.

**Definition 2.1 (pitch classes).** Let $O = \langle 2 \rangle \le I$. The group of pitch classes is $I / O$, which $c$ carries to the circle $\mathbb{R} / 1200\mathbb{Z}$. Every class has one representative in $[1, 2)$, the octave reduction
$$\operatorname{red}(r) = r \cdot 2^{-\lfloor \log_2 r \rfloor}.$$
By Proposition 1.1, $J / O$ is free abelian on the odd primes.

**Definition 2.2 (inversion).** The inversion of an interval is $\operatorname{inv}(r) = \operatorname{red}(2 / r)$: the representative of the inverse class. It is an involution on $[1, 2)$, it satisfies $r \cdot \operatorname{inv}(r) = 2$ for $1 < r < 2$, and it exchanges the fifth $3/2$ with the fourth $4/3$, the major third $5/4$ with the minor sixth $8/5$, and the minor third $6/5$ with the major sixth $5/3$. *Checked,* with $\operatorname{inv}(ab) = \operatorname{red}(\operatorname{inv}(a) \operatorname{inv}(b))$.

For a rational $r = n/d$ the code finds $\lfloor \log_2 r \rfloor$ from the bit lengths of $n$ and $d$ and one exact comparison, with no logarithm.

## 3. The Chain of Fifths and the Pythagorean Comma

*Modules `pitch` and `tuning`.* Pythagorean tuning, the twelve lü and al-Urmawī's division of the octave are all built from one interval, the fifth $3/2$, and the octave.

**Proposition 3.1 (no power of the fifth is a power of the octave).** If $(3/2)^m = 2^n$ with $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$, then $m = n = 0$. Equivalently, $\log_2 3$ is irrational.

*Proof.* The hypothesis is $3^m = 2^{m + n}$, and Proposition 1.1 gives $m = 0$ and then $n = 0$. ∎

**Corollary 3.2 (the chain never closes).** The class of the fifth has infinite order in $I / O$. The notes $\operatorname{red}((3/2)^k)$, $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, are pairwise distinct, and no set of notes is closed under the pure fifth: in every finite scale some fifth is not pure. *Checked:* in chains of two to sixty notes no circle of fifths is all pure.

*Remark.* Since $\log_2 (3/2)$ is irrational, the classes of $(3/2)^k$ are dense in the circle (Kronecker's theorem; Hardy and Wright 1938, the chapter on it). A chain of fifths approaches every pitch and returns to none.

**Definition 3.3 (the Pythagorean comma, the limma and the apotome).** Twelve fifths exceed seven octaves by the Pythagorean comma
$$\kappa_P = \frac{(3/2)^{12}}{2^7} = \frac{3^{12}}{2^{19}} = \frac{531441}{524288}, \qquad c(\kappa_P) \approx 23.46.$$
The limma is $\lambda = 2^8 / 3^5 = 256/243$, five fifths down and three octaves up (about 90.22 cents); the apotome is $\alpha = 3^7 / 2^{11} = 2187/2048$, seven fifths up and four octaves down (about 113.69 cents). Then $\lambda \alpha = 9/8$, the major tone, and $\alpha / \lambda = \kappa_P$.

**Proposition 3.4 (the chain of twelve notes).** Let $0 \le b \le 11$. The twelve notes $\operatorname{red}((3/2)^k)$, $-b \le k \le 11 - b$, in ascending order, are separated by seven limmas and five apotomes. Eleven of the fifths of their circle are pure, and the twelfth, from the top of the chain to its bottom, is the wolf
$$\frac{2^7}{(3/2)^{11}} = \frac{3/2}{\kappa_P} = \frac{262144}{177147},$$
narrower than the fifth by the Pythagorean comma (about 678.49 cents).

*Proof.* The wolf is what seven octaves leave after eleven fifths. For the steps: the note at place $k$ of the chain stands at position $7k \bmod 12$ of the scale, because $(3/2)^k$ lies within $|k| \cdot 1.96$ cents of $700k$, less than half a step of 100 cents for $|k| \le 11$. Two notes adjacent in the scale therefore differ in the chain by $+7$ or by $-5$ places, and are separated by $\alpha$ or by $\lambda$. If there are $x$ limmas and $y$ apotomes, then $x + y = 12$, and the places return to their start, $-5x + 7y = 0$; so $x = 7$, $y = 5$. ∎ *Checked* exactly for $b = 0, 3, 5$, with the place of the wolf.

**Proposition 3.5 (三分损益, sanfen sunyi).** Let $\ell_0 = 1$, and let $\ell_{k+1} = \tfrac{2}{3} \ell_k$ if that exceeds $\tfrac12$, and $\ell_{k+1} = \tfrac{4}{3} \ell_k$ otherwise. Then $\tfrac12 < \ell_k \le 1$ for every $k$, and
$$\frac{1}{\ell_k} = \operatorname{red}\big((3/2)^k\big).$$
The lengths generated by subtracting and adding a third are those of a chain of pure fifths, if frequency is inversely proportional to length.

*Proof.* By induction. Both rules multiply $1/\ell_k$ by $3/2$ times a power of 2, so $1/\ell_k$ and $(3/2)^k$ lie in one class of $I/O$. If $\tfrac23 \ell_k > \tfrac12$, then $\tfrac12 < \ell_{k+1} < \ell_k \le 1$. Otherwise $\tfrac23 \ell_k < \tfrac12$ (equality is impossible, the numerator of $\ell_k$ being a power of 2 and its denominator a power of 3), so $\ell_k < \tfrac34$ and $\tfrac23 < \tfrac43 \ell_k < 1$. In both cases $1/\ell_{k+1} \in [1, 2)$, the one representative of its class there. ∎

*Checked:* the twelve lü are the Pythagorean tuning of twelve notes with none below the first, exactly, as are five and sixty; among the first twelve lengths the two rules alternate except once; on a first length of 81 the first five are the whole numbers 81, 54, 72, 48, 64; and a thirteenth length is $\ell_{12} = 1/\kappa_P$, short of the first by the Pythagorean comma: the circle of the lü does not close.

**Proposition 3.6 (al-Urmawī's seventeen steps).** Write $L = \lambda$ and $C = \kappa_P$. The seventeen steps
$$L\,L\,C\;\;L\,L\,C\;\;L \;\Big|\; L\,L\,C\;\;L\,L\,C\;\;L \;\Big|\; L\,L\,C$$
(two tetrachords and a tone, the tone being $L\,L\,C = 9/8$; Wright 1978) compose to the octave, and the seventeen notes they reach are $\operatorname{red}((3/2)^k)$ for $-12 \le k \le 4$.

*Proof.* $L$ moves five places down the chain of fifths and $C$ twelve places up. From place 0 the steps reach in turn the places $-5, -10, 2, -3, -8, 4, -1$; then $-6, -11, 1, -4, -9, 3, -2$; then $-7, -12$, and $0$ again: every place from $-12$ to $4$ once. Since there are twelve $L$ and five $C$, the whole is $\lambda^{12} \kappa_P^5 = 2^{96 - 95} \, 3^{60 - 60} = 2$. ∎ *Checked* exactly.

*Remark (the third of eight fourths).* The sixth note of the division, at place $-8$, is $2^{13} / 3^8 = 8192/6561$, about 384.36 cents. It falls short of the just major third $5/4$ by the schisma $3^8 \cdot 5 / 2^{15}$ of §4, less than two cents: a chain of pure fifths long enough holds a third that is nearly just. *Checked.*

*Remark 3.7 (equal divisions that approach the fifth).* A step of $n$ equal divisions of the octave approaches the fifth as $k/n$ approaches $\log_2(3/2) = 0.58496\ldots = [0; 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 1, 5, \ldots]$. The convergents of this continued fraction are $1/1, 1/2, 3/5, 7/12, 24/41, 31/53, 179/306, \ldots$, each the best approximation among fractions of no greater denominator (Hardy and Wright 1938, the chapter on continued fractions). The divisions up to 60 that come nearer the fifth than every smaller one are 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 12, 29, 41 and 53. *Checked* against brute force; the fifth of 53 divisions is 0.068 cents narrow.

## 4. Just Intonation and the Syntonic Comma

*Modules `pitch` and `tuning`.* With the prime 5 the major third $5/4$ and the minor third $6/5$ are consonances, and $J_5 \cong \mathbb{Z}^3$ is the group in which a just scale lives. Modulo octaves it is the lattice of the notes $(3/2)^i (5/4)^j$.

**Definition 4.1 (the syntonic comma).** Four fifths exceed two octaves and a major third by the syntonic comma
$$\kappa = \frac{(3/2)^4}{2^2 \cdot 5/4} = \frac{81}{80}, \qquad c(\kappa) \approx 21.51.$$
It is the ditone $81/64$ over the major third, and the major tone $9/8$ over the minor tone $10/9$.

**Proposition 4.2 (the commas of the 5-limit).** In the coordinates $(a, b, c)$ of $2^a 3^b 5^c$:

| Interval | Ratio | $(a, b, c)$ | Cents | Relation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pythagorean comma $\kappa_P$ | 531441/524288 | $(-19, 12, 0)$ | 23.46 | twelve fifths less seven octaves |
| syntonic comma $\kappa$ | 81/80 | $(-4, 4, -1)$ | 21.51 | four fifths less two octaves and a major third |
| schisma $\sigma$ | 32805/32768 | $(-15, 8, 1)$ | 1.95 | $\kappa_P / \kappa$ |
| diaschisma $\delta$ | 2048/2025 | $(11, -4, -2)$ | 19.55 | $\kappa / \sigma$ |
| lesser diesis | 128/125 | $(7, 0, -3)$ | 41.06 | an octave less three major thirds; $\kappa \delta$ |
| greater diesis | 648/625 | $(3, 4, -4)$ | 62.57 | four minor thirds less an octave; $\kappa^2 \delta$ |

and $\kappa_P = \kappa^2 / \delta$.

*Proof.* Each relation is an addition of the vectors. ∎ *Checked* exactly, each from the stack of consonances that defines it.

**Proposition 4.3 (a diatonic scale cannot be just throughout).** If the fifths $C$–$G$, $G$–$D$, $D$–$A$ and $A$–$E$ are pure, then the third $C$–$E$ is the ditone $81/64 = \tfrac54 \kappa$, not the just third. Of these four fifths and this third at most four are pure.

*Proof.* Definition 4.1. ∎

In Ptolemy's intense diatonic, $1, 9/8, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 5/3, 15/8$, the triads on the first, fourth and fifth degrees are pure, and the comma falls on the fifth of the second degree, $40/27 = \tfrac32 / \kappa$ (about 680.45 cents). In the just scale of twelve notes $(3/2)^i (5/4)^j$, $-1 \le i \le 2$, $-1 \le j \le 1$, nine fifths are pure, two are narrow by $\kappa$ and one is wide by $\delta$; these make $-2\kappa + \delta = -\kappa_P$ in all, as Proposition 6.1 requires. *Checked* exactly.

## 5. Temperaments as Homomorphisms

*Module `tuning`.* A temperament gives up the purity of some consonances so that a comma vanishes.

**Definition 5.1 (temperaments of the 5-limit).** A temperament of rank $r < 3$ is a surjective homomorphism $T : J_5 \to \mathbb{Z}^r$ together with a tuning, a homomorphism $t : \mathbb{Z}^r \to \mathbb{R}$. The tempered size of a just interval $x$ is $t(T(x))$ cents and its error is $e(x) = t(T(x)) - c(x)$. The elements of $\ker T$ are the commas tempered out. Since $J_5$ is free on 2, 3 and 5, $T$ is an $r \times 3$ matrix of integers, the images of the three primes.

*Remark.* The error $e : J_5 \to \mathbb{R}$ is itself a homomorphism. The errors of the octave, the fifth and the major third determine the error of every interval of the 5-limit.

**Proposition 5.2 (equal divisions).** For $n \ge 1$ let $v_p = \operatorname{round}(n \log_2 p)$ and
$$T_n(2^a 3^b 5^c) = a\,n + b\,v_3 + c\,v_5, \qquad t(k) = 1200\,k / n.$$
Then $T_n$ sends each of 2, 3, 5 to its nearest step, the octave has no error, and the kernel of $T_n$ has rank 2; $T_n$ is surjective when $n$, $v_3$ and $v_5$ have no common divisor, as in the four cases that follow. For $n = 12, 19, 31, 53$ the images of $(2, 3, 5)$ are $(12, 19, 28)$, $(19, 30, 44)$, $(31, 49, 72)$ and $(53, 84, 123)$. *Checked.*

**Proposition 5.3 (what twelve divisions temper out).** $\ker T_{12}$ is generated by the syntonic comma and the lesser diesis. It holds every comma of Proposition 4.2.

*Proof.* Both lie in the kernel: $-48 + 76 - 28 = 0$ and $84 - 84 = 0$. Let $u = (-4, 4, -1)$ and $w = (7, 0, -3)$. The index of $\langle u, w \rangle$ in the largest subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}^3$ of rank 2 that holds it is the greatest common divisor of the $2 \times 2$ minors of the matrix with rows $u$ and $w$ (the product of its invariant factors, by the Smith normal form). The minors are the entries of $u \times w = -(12, 19, 28)$, whose greatest common divisor is 1. So $\langle u, w \rangle$ is that subgroup. It is $\ker T_{12}$, which has rank 2, holds $u$ and $w$, and holds an element whenever it holds a multiple of it. The other commas are products of these two by Proposition 4.2. ∎ *Checked:* the cross product, and the image of each comma.

**Proposition 5.4 (meantone).** Let $M : J_5 \to \mathbb{Z}^2$ send $2 \mapsto (1, 0)$, $3 \mapsto (1, 1)$, $5 \mapsto (0, 4)$, so that
$$M(2^a 3^b 5^c) = (a + b,\; b + 4c),$$
a number of octaves and a number of fifths. Then $M$ is surjective and $\ker M = \langle \kappa \rangle$.

*Proof.* $M(2) = (1, 0)$ and $M(3/2) = (0, 1)$. And $(a + b, b + 4c) = 0$ exactly when $(a, b, c) = -c\,(-4, 4, -1)$. ∎

A meantone temperament is $M$ with a tuning $t_F(m, k) = 1200\,m + F k$: the major third is made of four fifths less two octaves, whatever the size $F$ of the fifth. The meantone of fraction $f$ narrows the fifth by that fraction of the syntonic comma, $F = c(3/2) - f\,c(\kappa)$; its fifth is the element $\tfrac32 \kappa^{-f} = 2^{4f - 1}\, 3^{1 - 4f}\, 5^{f}$ of $P$.

**Proposition 5.5 (the errors of meantone).** In the meantone of fraction $f$, writing $\kappa$ for $c(\kappa)$,
$$e(3/2) = -f \kappa, \qquad e(5/4) = (1 - 4f)\, \kappa, \qquad e(6/5) = (3f - 1)\, \kappa.$$
The fifth is pure only for $f = 0$ (Pythagorean tuning), the major third only for $f = 1/4$, the minor third only for $f = 1/3$. Further, $e(45/32) = (1 - 6f) \kappa$ and $e(25/24) = (2 - 7f) \kappa$, which vanish at $f = 1/6$ and $f = 2/7$.

*Proof.* $e(3/2) = F - c(3/2)$. Four fifths less two octaves are $c(81/64) - 4 f \kappa = c(5/4) + (1 - 4f) \kappa$. The rest follows because $e$ is a homomorphism: $6/5 = \tfrac32 / \tfrac54$, $45/32 = (\tfrac32)^2 \tfrac54 / 2$, $25/24 = (\tfrac54)^2 / \tfrac32$. ∎

*Checked:* in exact arithmetic, the fifth of quarter-comma meantone is $5^{1/4}$, its major third $5/4$, and its tone $\sqrt5 / 2$, the mean of the third, from which the temperament has its name; third-comma meantone has $6/5$ and $5/3$, sixth-comma $45/32$, 2/7-comma $25/24$; and the three errors above for seven fractions.

**Proposition 5.6 (the wolf of twelve meantone notes).** A chain of twelve notes of the meantone of fraction $f$ has eleven fifths of size $F$, and its circle is closed by the interval $8400 - 11F$, which departs from the pure fifth by
$$11 f\, c(\kappa) - c(\kappa_P).$$
For $f = 1/4$ it is $2^7 \cdot 5^{-11/4}$, about 35.68 cents wide. The circle closes on a fifth like the others exactly when $f = c(\kappa_P) / (12\, c(\kappa)) \approx 0.090904$, the equal temperament of twelve notes; $f = 1/11$ misses it by $c(\sigma) - c(\kappa)/11 \approx -0.0014$ cents over the twelve fifths. *Checked.*

**Proposition 5.7 (equal divisions that are meantone temperaments).** $T_n$ factors through $M$ exactly when $\kappa \in \ker T_n$. Then $T_n = \varphi \circ M$ with $\varphi(m, k) = n\,m + s\,k$, where $s = v_3 - n$ is the number of steps of the fifth, and $v_5 = 4s$.

*Proof.* $M$ is surjective with kernel $\langle \kappa \rangle$, so a homomorphism from $J_5$ factors through it exactly when it kills $\kappa$. Then $\varphi(1, 0) = T_n(2) = n$ and $\varphi(0, 1) = T_n(3/2) = v_3 - n$. ∎

Twelve, nineteen and thirty-one divisions are meantone temperaments, with $s = 7, 11, 18$; their fifths are those of the fractions $f \approx 0.0909$, $0.3356$ and $0.2409$, near $1/11$, $1/3$ and $1/4$. Fifty-three divisions are not: $T_{53}(\kappa) = 1$. There the Pythagorean and the syntonic comma are each one step, the ditone and the major third are different notes, and the schisma is tempered out. *Checked.*

## 6. Circles of Twelve Fifths

*Module `tuning`.* Let a scale of twelve notes have the degrees $0 = d_0 < d_1 < \dots < d_{11} < 1200$ in cents, continued by $d_{i + 12} = d_i + 1200$. Its interval of $s$ steps on the degree $i$ is $d_{i + s} - d_i$.

**Proposition 6.1 (what every scale of twelve notes owes).** For $1 \le s \le 11$, the twelve intervals of $s$ steps sum to $1200\,s$ cents. Hence:
- the twelve fifths ($s = 7$) depart from pure by $-c(\kappa_P) \approx -23.46$ cents in all;
- three major thirds that fill an octave, on the degrees $i$, $i + 4$, $i + 8$, are wide by the lesser diesis, about 41.06 cents, in all;
- four minor thirds that fill an octave, on $i$, $i + 3$, $i + 6$, $i + 9$, are narrow by the greater diesis, about 62.57 cents, in all.

*Proof.* In $\sum_i (d_{i + s} - d_i)$ every step $d_{j + 1} - d_j$ is counted $s$ times, and the steps sum to 1200. The thirds on $i$, $i + 4$, $i + 8$ sum to $d_{i + 12} - d_i = 1200$, and likewise the minor thirds. Then $8400 - 12\,c(3/2) = -c(\kappa_P)$, $1200 - 3\,c(5/4) = c(128/125)$ and $1200 - 4\,c(6/5) = -c(648/625)$. ∎ *Checked* on eight scales of twelve notes: Pythagorean, just, meantone, equal and circulating.

A tuning of twelve notes is therefore a choice of where the Pythagorean comma goes. Pythagorean tuning puts it on one fifth; the equal temperament spreads it evenly, and is the one scale of twelve notes whose fifths are all alike, each $2^{7/12}$, narrow by $c(\kappa_P)/12 \approx 1.955$ cents, with every major third wide by a third of the lesser diesis, about 13.69 cents.

**Definition 6.2 (temperaments that circulate).** Let $g_0, \dots, g_{11} \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $\sum_i g_i = 1$. The temperament with these fractions has the fifths $\tfrac32 \kappa_P^{-g_i}$ round its circle, in the order $C$–$G$, $G$–$D$, …, $F$–$C$. Its circle closes, $\prod_i \tfrac32 \kappa_P^{-g_i} = (3/2)^{12} / \kappa_P = 2^7$, and the condition $\sum_i g_i = 1$ is necessary for that by Proposition 1.2. The major third on a degree is made of the four fifths that follow it, and departs from just by
$$c(\kappa) - (g_i + g_{i+1} + g_{i+2} + g_{i+3})\, c(\kappa_P).$$

Werckmeister's temperament III has $g = \tfrac14$ on $C$–$G$, $G$–$D$, $D$–$A$ and $B$–$F\sharp$; the temperament known as Vallotti's has $g = \tfrac16$ on the six fifths from $F$ to $B$. *Checked:* both close exactly; the third $C$–$E$ of the first is wide by $c(\kappa) - \tfrac34 c(\kappa_P) \approx 3.91$ cents; the thirds of the second run from $c(\kappa) - \tfrac23 c(\kappa_P) \approx 5.87$ cents wide to the ditone, a whole syntonic comma wide; $g_i = \tfrac1{12}$ throughout is the equal temperament exactly, and the whole comma on one fifth is Pythagorean tuning.

## 7. Beats of a Mistuned Consonance

*Module `pitch`.* A harmonic tone of pitch $f$ is a sum of sinusoids, its partials, at the frequencies $f, 2f, 3f, \dots$

**Proposition 7.1 (beats).** For frequencies $\nu_1, \nu_2$ and an amplitude $A$,
$$A \sin 2\pi \nu_1 t + A \sin 2\pi \nu_2 t = 2A \cos\big(\pi (\nu_1 - \nu_2) t\big)\, \sin\big(\pi (\nu_1 + \nu_2) t\big).$$
When $\nu_1$ and $\nu_2$ are close, this is a tone at their mean whose loudness rises and falls $|\nu_1 - \nu_2|$ times a second.

*Proof.* The sum-to-product identity; the envelope $|\cos \pi(\nu_1 - \nu_2) t|$ has period $1 / |\nu_1 - \nu_2|$. ∎

**Proposition 7.2 (the partials of a consonance that coincide).** Let two harmonic tones have pitches $f_1$ and $f_2 = \tfrac{p}{q} f_1$, with $p/q$ in lowest terms. The partial $j f_1$ equals the partial $k f_2$ exactly when $(j, k) = m\,(p, q)$ for a whole number $m$; the lowest pair is the $p$-th partial of the lower tone and the $q$-th of the upper. If the upper tone is mistuned by $\delta$ cents, this pair beats at
$$|q f_2 - p f_1| = p f_1 \left| 2^{\delta / 1200} - 1 \right| \approx p f_1\, \frac{\ln 2}{1200}\, |\delta|$$
times a second (Helmholtz 1877, part II).

*Proof.* $j f_1 = k \tfrac{p}{q} f_1$ means $j q = k p$, and $p$, $q$ are coprime. With $f_2 = \tfrac{p}{q} f_1 2^{\delta/1200}$, $q f_2 = p f_1 2^{\delta / 1200}$. ∎

The same mistuning beats faster the higher the tones and the higher the partial $p$. On a lower tone of 220 Hz, the fifth of the equal temperament, 1.955 cents narrow, beats 0.745 times a second, and its major third, 13.69 cents wide, 8.73 times. *Checked:* against sums worked by hand, and the formula above.

*Caveat.* The partials of real strings are sharper than whole multiples, the more so the stiffer the string, and the beats of a real instrument differ from these. The rate also says nothing of how loud the beats are.

## 8. What Comes Next

- **Primes beyond 5.** `Interval` holds any primes; the genera of the Greek theorists and later just scales with 7 and 11 need constructors, each from a source read.
- **Chains and their steps.** A chain of $n$ fifths has at most three sizes of step (the three-distance theorem), and two exactly for the well-formed sizes 5, 7, 12, 17, 29, 41, 53 (Carey and Clampitt 1989). Proposition 3.4 is the case of twelve.
- **Temperaments of rank 2 other than meantone.** The remark after Proposition 3.6 is the schismatic temperament, whose kernel is $\langle \sigma \rangle$.
- **Pipes.** A pipe does not sound as its length alone gives; the lü as pipes belong to a module of instruments.
- **Scales that do not repeat at the octave,** and scales known by measurement, with their provenance, through the data library.
