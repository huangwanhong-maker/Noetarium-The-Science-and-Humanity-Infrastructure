# Records of Historical Objects

*Status: seed.* The history domain's records in the format `noetarium.histos/1`, written by the [codebook of objects](../codebook-objects.md) and read by [`objects/record.hpp`](../objects/include/history/objects/record.hpp). The domain's checks read every file here and require it to stand without a fault and to be a functor on the schema. The records by the older codebook, of occasions, beings and ties, are the [canons](../canons/).

| [`tuning-and-temperament.jsonl`](tuning-and-temperament.jsonl) | How the pitches of a scale were fixed, in Greece, China, India, the Arabic-speaking world and Europe, from about 500 BC to 1722: 102 objects (tunings, treatises, persons, instruments and practices, six problems, nineteen places), 137 relations, three emergences written whole | seed |

## 1. How Far It May Be Trusted

**Every statement of the record above was drafted by a language model from memory (Claude Fable 5.2, 2026-11-13) and is of grade C.** None has been held against a source, and none is established. The record lists the references where a keeper would look (Barbour 1951; Cho 2003; Needham 1962; Wright 1978; Barker 1989; Lindley 1984) and attaches them to nothing: an assertion by a reference is added at Histos Harmonia's desk, by a person, with its passage.

What it is good for, as it stands: showing how the domain and the work behave on a record of some size, across traditions. What it is not: a history to cite.

## 2. Conventions of the Record

- Dates before 15 October 1582 are Julian; years before the era are astronomical (239 BC is `-0238`). Most early dates are approximate (`~`).
- A period's bounds are a convention, and its summary says so.
- Places are present-day points; no border of any time is recorded.
- A problem has no date of its own unless a source gives one: it is posed, for the frontier, once its premises have appeared.
- The problem of the circle that does not close has two emergences, of the scale of fifths and of the rule of thirds, with no tie between them recorded.
