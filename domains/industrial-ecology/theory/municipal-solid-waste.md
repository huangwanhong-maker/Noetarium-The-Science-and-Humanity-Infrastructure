# A City's Municipal Solid Waste and SDG 11.6.1

*Status: draft (ADR 0148). The theory note of the industrial-ecology domain's `waste`.*

SDG indicator 11.6.1 is the share of a city's municipal solid waste that is collected and managed in controlled facilities. UN-Habitat's Waste Wise Cities Tool (WaCT, 2021) estimates it in seven steps:

1. what households generate, from a survey;
2. what other sources generate;
3. what is collected and received at facilities;
4. composition;
5. what recovery facilities recover;
6. each facility's level of control;
7. what is managed in controlled facilities.

The module states the account those steps make and what the survey's error does to the indicator. Every ladder, amount and design is the caller's declaration.

## 1. Generation from a Survey

Households are drawn by stratum h (income levels) and, within a stratum, by area (the primary sampling unit). WaCT asks for 10 households in each of 3 areas per stratum, weighed over 8 days with the first day discarded. A household of stratum h weighs w = H_h / n_h, where H_h is the stratum's households and n_h the number sampled.

The total generated by households per day is the weighted total Σ w y_i of y_i = kg_i / days_i. Its variance is peira's ultimate-cluster estimator, with a Student interval on PSUs − strata degrees of freedom. The generation per person is the ratio Σ w kg / Σ w persons · days, linearised.

*Caveat.* Three areas per stratum give two degrees of freedom per stratum, and six over three strata, so the interval is wide. That width is what such a survey knows, and the module reports it as it is.

## 2. Facilities and Control

**Definition 1 (ladder).** A ladder is an ordered list of levels ℓ₀ < ℓ₁ < … < ℓ_m, each with a set of criteria C_k. A facility meeting the set M of criteria stands at the highest level k such that C_j ⊆ M for every j ≤ k. A facility is controlled when it stands at or above a declared level. For 11.6.1 that level is basic control, and the indicator's metadata of 2025 asks for control in operation, not in design.

**Definition 2 (account).** A recovery facility i receives r_i from collection, recovers q_i ≤ r_i, and sends r_i − q_i to a disposal site it names. A disposal site j weighs g_j at its gate, residues included. Its intake from collection is g_j − ρ_j, where ρ_j is the total of residues sent to it. Then:
- delivered: D = Σ_i r_i + Σ_j (g_j − ρ_j);
- collected: D plus what was collected and not delivered;
- managed in controlled facilities: M = Σ_{i controlled} q_i + Σ_{j controlled} g_j;
- 11.6.1 = M / G, where G is the generation.

**Proposition 1 (residues counted once).** When every residue is sent to a named disposal site and ρ_j ≤ g_j for every j, the waste leaving the facilities (recovered, or disposed at a gate) equals D.

*Proof.* Σ_i q_i + Σ_j g_j = Σ_i q_i + Σ_j ρ_j + Σ_j (g_j − ρ_j). Every residue goes to some site, so Σ_j ρ_j = Σ_i (r_i − q_i). The sum is therefore Σ_i r_i + Σ_j (g_j − ρ_j) = D. ∎

Where ρ_j > g_j, residues were declared beyond what the gate weighed, and the excess Σ_j (ρ_j − g_j)⁺ is reported as the account's imbalance. This is the double counting WaCT warns against. A residue sent to no named site is collected and not managed, and the module says so.

## 3. The Indicator's Interval

The amounts at facilities are taken as weighed. Let N be the declared amount from other sources and H the households' generation, with interval [H₋, H₊]. Since M / (H + N) is decreasing in H, the indicator's interval is [M / (H₊ + N), M / (H₋ + N)], the image of H's under a monotone map. The interval can exceed one when the survey is small. That means the survey cannot rule out that more is managed than it estimates is generated. The module reports this without clipping the interval.

## 4. Composition

Sorted samples carry the same weights. A fraction's share is the ratio of weighted totals Σ w kg_f / Σ w kg, with its linearised interval, and the shares sum to one.
