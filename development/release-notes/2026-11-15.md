# Release Notes: 15 November 2026

## 6. The Law Studio: Evidence, Readings and the Plan of Proof, on International Privacy Law First

A new work for legal practice ([ADR 0121](../decisions/0121-the-law-studio.md)). A project is a matter:
- **Evidence.** Each document is kept with the digest of its bytes and read in place. A passage that says a fact holds, or denies it, is marked in the text by selecting its words. An exhibit has a chain of custody; where the chain breaks, what the exhibit shows is undercut and the exhibit stays in view.
- **A model that proposes.** A language model can read a document against the matter's facts. Each passage it quotes is looked for in the document, and one that is not there is refused. What passes waits as a proposal and counts for nothing until a person accepts it. A model can also propose readings of the open terms; a proposal fixes nothing.
- **The law, clause by clause.** A matter is read under a scheme: rules, exceptions and open terms, each rule cited to the clause it renders, in the words of the official text.
- **Readings.** A court's holding on an open term binds the forum, persuades it or is left out, as the forum's legal orders and the date of the question say. The parties argue beside it, by their canons.
- **Answers.** Each question is clear, arguable or untenable, with the rules and clauses it rests on.
- **The plan of proof.** The ways to establish a question and to resist it, each a short list of facts to make out, facts to show do not hold and readings to argue for.

The first body of law is international privacy law: transfers of personal data out of the European Union under Chapter V of the GDPR, with the standard contractual clauses, the EU–US adequacy decision and the Court of Justice's readings in *Breyer*, *EDPS v SRB* and *Schrems II*. The data library now fetches these official texts from the Publications Office and reads them into provisions. Article 17 of the Covenant, Article 8 of the European Convention and Convention 108 stand around them as texts.

An invented matter shows it: an Irish company sends coded health records each night to a processor in the United States. Whether the transfer is forbidden comes out arguable. It turns on whose side "personal data" is judged from, on which no court has ruled, and there are four ways to establish the prohibition and three to resist it. A second, smaller example is an invented bylaw about vehicles in a park.

A scheme is its author's reading of the law. The studio checks that it is whole and that its quotations stand in the clauses; it does not check that the reading is right, and none of it is legal advice.

Beneath the work:
- the law domain can state a body of law, weigh courts' readings for a forum, and compute a plan of proof;
- the visualization layer draws a matter as its argument, or as an outline when it is large;
- the API holds the providers of language models in one place, with keys kept in the person's own settings folder.

Sixteen pictures are in the gallery's §41. Not built yet: the lawfulness of the processing itself, the review of large collections of documents, the transcription of scans, and the policy studio.

## 5. The Archive Studio, Continued: Images, Standards, Arrangement, Datasets, Analyses, JSON-LD and Several People

Everything the Archive Studio left undone is now built ([ADR 0120](../decisions/0120-the-archive-studio-continued.md)):
- **Images in deep zoom.** Every image is served in tiles, as the IIIF standard asks, and read as deep as its pixels go. The archive can be opened in any IIIF viewer, and a IIIF manifest from another archive is taken in with its images and annotations.
- **Standards as templates.** A holding is described in Dublin Core, in Records in Contexts or in CIDOC CRM. A form is a shape that also checks what was written, so a vocabulary is added by writing a shape. Finding aids are read and written in EAD.
- **Arrangement and custody.** The finding aid is arranged in place, from the fonds to its items, and every fault is marked on its unit. A unit, or the whole archive, is packed as a BagIt bag for transfer. Each holding's chain of custody is followed handover by handover, with a verdict on whether it is still what was taken in.
- **Datasets.** A table has a codebook, drafted from it or read from DDI. The table is held to its codebook cell by cell, with each variable's frequencies drawn. Two versions are compared, and two variables of two studies are harmonised.
- **Analyses as tools:**
  - qualitative comparative analysis, with every minimal model;
  - calibration, concept lattices, Mill's methods, and rival explanations weighed clue by clue;
  - passages coded in the reading rooms, with how far coders agree;
  - reliability, item response theory, factor analysis, latent classes and mixed models;
  - whether a table can be shared without disclosing its people.
- **JSON-LD** is read and written, in symploke.
- **Several people.** Started with `--people`, a server knows each person by a key the keeper gives as a link, with a role: reader, contributor or keeper. Each page shows who is here, and what one does is told to the others at once. Every change is written in a sealed journal that can be verified. When two people change one file, their changes are merged by statement, and nothing is lost.

The example archive shows each part, with a fault planted in each for the checks to find. Twelve pictures are in the gallery's §40.

On the way, two faults of the shared engine builder were mended. A page asking several things at once could leave WSL unable to answer; now at most three engines run there at a time. And a server worked out whether its engines were current at every request, which took tens of seconds for an engine on many foundations; it now keeps the answer while no source changes, even for a work that makes its engines afresh at each request as the Urban Studio does, and finds it about four times faster when it must. A server can also keep its engines running between requests, as the Archive Studio now does, so that a request no longer starts a process of its own.

## 4. The Archive Studio

A new studio works on an archive as a project ([ADR 0119](../decisions/0119-the-archive-studio.md)). It is the studio for social data and linked data the user asked for, and is built on symploke. Its holdings may be of any kind: images, sounds, moving images, texts, tables.
- **The archive.** The holdings stand on a wall, each drawn as itself: an image as the image, a sound as the shape of its waves, a text by its first lines, a table by its first rows. One opens in place into a reading room where it is seen, heard or read. There it is described in Dublin Core terms, field by field, each change with its undo. It is annotated after the W3C Web Annotation model: a region of an image, a span of a sound, a passage of a text, each with a note. Its bytes are digested again in the page and checked against the SHA-256 kept when it came.
- **The graph.** Everything said of the holdings is linked data, drawn as the page; a node opens where it stands, with what is said of it. A desk beneath runs SPARQL queries and marks what they bind on the graph. It also checks SHACL shapes and lists their failures, draws what entailment derives dashed with its rule, and keeps a file's text only when it reads, refusing it otherwise at its line and column.
- **The catalogue.** Studies of the CESSDA Data Catalogue are found by words and taken into a project in DISCO.

The project's tables, tools, charts and boards are those of the other studios, on the same host and page. The page now takes views of a work's own and the host routes of a work's own; Market Studio and the Urban Studio are unchanged. Other works can use the API's composition of symploke (`science.linked`) and the visualization layer's drawings of linked data. The example archive, *Papers of the reading room*, is invented throughout and says so; its shapes find the two faults planted in it. Run it with `python applications/archive-studio/engine.py build`, then `python applications/archive-studio/serve.py --open` (port 9150). Ten pictures are in the gallery's §39.

## 2. Linked Data: symploke

A sixteenth foundation, symploke, holds linked data for every work ([ADR 0118](../decisions/0118-symploke-linked-data.md)). It was founded at the user's request for RDF in the digital humanities:
- Turtle, TriG, N-Triples and N-Quads are read exactly, or refused with the line and column, and written back.
- A store keeps every edit with its undo.
- Queries run in SPARQL's core, with property paths and aggregates.
- RDFS and part of OWL 2 RL are applied with each conclusion's derivation.
- SHACL's shapes are checked, with every failure named.
- Graphs are compared up to the names of their blank nodes.

A studio for social and linked data, built on it, comes next.

## 1. Logic, Evidence, Archives, Law and Policy, and the Layers beneath Social Analysis

This commit brings five new foundations, two new domains and the first layers of a social-science archive. Details are in [ADR 0112](../decisions/0112-apodeixis-and-tekmerion.md) to [ADR 0117](../decisions/0117-zetesis-case-oriented-inquiry.md).

- **Logic and verification: apodeixis.**
  - Formulas and sequents; calculi declared as rule schemata, whose derivations a small checker verifies step by step.
  - Classical and intuitionistic logic decided, with countermodels.
  - Satisfiability with checked refutations.
  - Dung's frameworks and ASPIC+, moved from the philosophy domain.
  - Derivation under an interpretation, Γ ⊢_ι φ: whether a sentence follows under each reading of a norm, and whether it is clear, arguable or untenable.
- **Evidence and its interpretation: tekmerion.**
  - Charts of evidence; the force of a report through its source.
  - Bayesian networks and belief functions, from tyche's new modules.
  - The reconstruction of what happened, and evidence recognised with its passages verified.
  - Interpreters that read a norm by any method, a model's proposal among them, joined in panels.
  - Timelines whose conflicts are named; the warrant of evidence.
  - Systematic reviews, from protocol to living update.
- **Archives: archeion.** Holdings known by their digests, with proofs that an archive only appended; arrangement, provenance, custody, retention and access by declared rules; packages for transfer. Datasets described after DDI, with harmonisation across waves.
- **The law domain.** Facts on five axes in three registers, burdens and standards of proof, sources and custom, norms applied through apodeixis, and exhibits authenticated by the archive. Precedent by Horty's reason model; proceedings as a sealed docket. The taxonomy of facts is stated as categories that kategoria verifies.
- **The policy domain.** Evidence-based policy making: theories of change, evidence pooled and graded, transfer to a place, options, and whether to adopt, reject or study first.
  - tyche gained decisions in one step and the value of information.
  - The API gained a way to ask a language model for readings of a norm, accepted only as a proposal of grade C.
- **The layers beneath social analysis and policy making** ([ADR 0116](../decisions/0116-the-layers-beneath-social-analysis-and-policy.md)):
  - **an archive of data:** archeion's datasets, peira's disclosure control, and a catalogue of social-science studies harvested from the CESSDA Data Catalogue by OAI-PMH, kept in the cache since its licence is unstated;
  - **an archive of evidence:** systematic reviews, sensitivity analysis and pre-registered protocols;
  - **zetesis,** a foundation of case-oriented inquiry: QCA with every equally good model kept, the coding of qualitative data, concepts and typologies, case selection, and process tracing;
  - **the quantitative gaps:** microsimulation and psychometrics in nomos, mixed and latent-variable models in tyche, small areas in peira.
