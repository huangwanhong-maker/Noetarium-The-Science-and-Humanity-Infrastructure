# Release Notes: 20 November 2026

## 4. Allocating Resources of Many Kinds by Evolution, in the Urban Studio (ADR 0138)

Resources of many kinds can now be allocated by evolution from scripts and from the Urban Studio, and compared by the people of a project ([ADR 0138](../decisions/0138-evolutionary-allocation-at-the-edges.md)).

- **In the Urban Studio.** *Allocation and justice* has a new case on Melbourne's areas: a care fund claimed by the areas, outreach crews, duties that may not be thrown away, and three sites with a shade canopy's direction, all declared and illustrative. The page shows the allocations the search kept as a field (what serves the most against what serves the least served), opens any of them on the areas with the order in which they were granted, and shows which grants commute, the laws each resource type was checked against, and the search's sealed record.
- **People compare; a model may too.** Two kept allocations are shown side by side, unnamed, and a person says which serves better; the judgements are read as strengths beside the search and never enter it. A model can be asked to compare them as well, each pair shown both ways and a share asked twice to see whether it agrees with itself; its answers are kept apart, as proposals.
- **Claims met in part.** A claim the remaining pool cannot meet can now be met up to what remains, so that the order decides who is met in full and who in part. On the Melbourne case this takes the fund from five of the thirteen areas to all or nearly all of them.
- **From scripts.** Resource types, problems and evaluators are declared in Python or as JSON, and verified, composed and searched by the same engine.

## 3. Evolutionary Allocation of Heterogeneous Resources: Assessment and the Composition (ADR 0137)

Resources of many kinds can now be allocated by evolution in one search: money that divides, crews in units, duties that may not be thrown away, distinct sites, a direction on the circle, granted in an order that matters ([ADR 0137](../decisions/0137-evolutionary-allocation-of-heterogeneous-resources.md)).

- **Declare, then search.** An allocation problem declares its recipients, each resource with its type, its pool and how it is shared out (exact shares, claims that may exceed the pool, units, distinct items, fixed demands), attributes chosen for each recipient, precedence between them, and what a grant changes (a price that rises after each job). The allocation domain composes the search from that declaration.
- **Lawful by construction and by check.** Every resource type is verified before the search begins, and every allocation found is checked: a duty that may not be thrown away must all be assigned, and an allocation that holds some back is refused as unlawful.
- **Evidence in its order.** Allocations are judged by evaluators of many kinds at once (closed forms, simulations, surrogates, a model's or a person's judgements), the stronger kinds deciding first; the best are compared under several principles of justice, never one score; whether order mattered is tested; and the whole search is kept on a sealed record.
- **What the checks show.** On a small declared city case the search finds the best of all 153,600 allocations; on knapsack benchmarks a specialised encoding stays ahead of the general one, by 3.9 points from the bound on the budget tried, as Raidl and Gottlieb found.

## 2. Evolutionary Allocation of Heterogeneous Resources: Resources, Spaces and Orders

The infrastructure can now state what is being allocated, how a search moves among allocations of it, and when the order of allocation matters: the three foundation layers of an evolutionary framework of allocation, built from a survey in nine parts and a staged design, and waiting for review before they are composed in the allocation domain.

- **What a resource is.** A resource type is declared by its components (units, grains, divisible amounts, balances, qualities), what can be thrown away, what cannot be held twice and what converts into what; its laws are checked, each verdict saying how far it reaches (kategoria).
- **Where a search moves.** Every kind of value an allocation is made of (amounts, shares of a whole, units among recipients, subsets, orders, sequences, orientations) is a space with its own distance and a declared typical distance, and spaces combine into products and choices whose crossover stays between its parents. One set of laws checks any space, a person's included (heuresis).
- **When order matters.** Grants acting on a state are tested for whether they commute; orders that differ only by exchanging grants that commute are evaluated once; decoders say what allocations they can reach; operators keep the feature the objective depends on (heuresis, with morphe's normal forms of traces).
- **What the survey found** is in its new Part X: among other things, that two claimants of the last unit do not commute though neither order satisfies both, and that Starkweather's 1991 contrast between precedence and adjacency reappears.

## 1. Allocation under Scarcity: Who First, What and How Much, and by What Procedure (ADR 0136)

When there is not enough, one can now divide a sum by declared rules and see who is served first, what each receives and why, set the result against every rule and order that could have been chosen under several principles of justice at once, keep its procedure on a sealed record, and follow who finally gains ([ADR 0136](../decisions/0136-allocation-under-scarcity.md)).

- **A survey first:** [Allocative justice under scarcity](../../research/literature/reports/Allocative%20justice%20under%20scarcity.md), in five parts (theories of distributive and procedural justice; formal methods of fair allocation; urban and spatial justice; generative, circular and evolutionary economics; practice, safeguards and language models) with a synthesis and a part on what was built. Its findings shape everything below: allocation is a composition of declared rules, not one optimisation; principles conflict and are never summed; procedure confers legitimacy, not correctness.
- **nomos's rules and orders:** rationing rules, the formulas public bodies use (sufficiency first, Porto Alegre's banded points, fiscal equalisation), reserve systems under every precedence, lotteries, fair division and participatory budgeting; principles of distribution as orders over allocations.
- **A domain `allocation`** at height 1 on economics: a round as stages applied in turn to what remains, each naming its principle and who declared it; the round against its alternatives, drawn as the order of dominance with the pivots where the choice is a person's; its record sealed in archeion's journal, every appeal heard for everyone, lotteries anyone can repeat, the rules of procedural justice decided and relevance and ethicality left to people; and who finally gains, through re-spending, rents and the return to those who generate value.
- **In the Urban Studio:** the plug-in *Allocation and justice*, its page a channel whose sluices are the stages and whose plots stand in the order reached, a plot opening in place with its reasons and an appeal to file. Three templates: Warsaw's participatory budget counted again (Pabulib), England's districts by the Indices of Deprivation 2025, and a care fund for Melbourne's areas. A language model may propose stages and restate an objection as an appeal, and never decides.
- **What the cases show:** Warsaw's own count leaves 7.9% of its 78,904 voters with none of their projects funded, the Method of Equal Shares 4.7%; in England a fund to the most deprived tenth of neighbourhoods reaches the income-deprived with 55% of its money, while 76% of them live elsewhere; in Melbourne the choice between serving the neediest areas first and giving by low income turns on which principle is given precedence.
- **Checks:** 50 in nomos, 84 in the domain, 30 at the API, 10 in the data library, 19 for the drawings and 10 in the Urban Studio's suite. Six pictures are in the gallery's §50.

## 2. Detectors of Critical Transitions (ADR 0135)

One can now ask of a record of almost any system whether it is losing its stability. The answer says what kind of loss it points to and where in the system it shows. It also says how often the test is wrong at the record's length, and what no detector could have seen ([ADR 0135](../decisions/0135-detectors-of-critical-transitions.md)).

- **A survey first:** [Critical transitions in structured systems](../../research/literature/reports/Critical%20transitions%20in%20structured%20systems.md). It has five parts: the theory and its detectors; structured states; the Earth system, ecosystems and epidemics; brains and bodies; cities, economies and societies. It holds 928 DOIs, most verified, and a synthesis. It shows where warnings work (controlled experiments, epidemics, traffic, power grids) and where they have failed (field ecology, financial crashes, contested seizure studies, tipping times of the Atlantic overturning).
- **States of every kind.** kinesis reads a quantity, a field over units, compositions with their totals (a population by age, an economy by sector), histograms and covariance matrices. Units may sit on a neighbourhood and in a hierarchy, such as blocks within districts within a city.
- **Honest verdicts.** Each finding is a signal, no signal or undecidable, with its reasons, its false-alarm and detection rates, and what it is blind to. Two things are never mistaken for slowing: a noise that grows or reddens, and a branch outrun by its forcing. A tipping time is given only when its interval is bounded.
- **The climate domain's first models with folds in nature:**
  - the energy balance with its ice–albedo feedback, in a form whose folds give no warning and one whose folds do;
  - the overturning in two boxes, whose folds bound freshwater forcing between 0.964 and 1.480.

  The checks read both models' records with the new detectors.
- **In the Dynamics Lab:** a tool *Critical transitions* on a run of any declared system, and one on a record brought to the Lab. Their answers are drawn as cards: each indicator through its windows, the eigenvalue's path against the unit circle, every test's error rates. A new example sets a fold beside three systems that must not be called slowing, an oscillator and a ring of units.
- **For scripts and works:** the API's `science/transitions.hpp` and `science.transitions`.
- **Checks:** 77 in kinesis, 31 in the climate domain's C++, 23 at the API, 29 for the drawings and 7 in the Lab. Four pictures are in the gallery's §34.1.
