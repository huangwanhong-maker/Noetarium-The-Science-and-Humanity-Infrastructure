# Release Notes: 13 November 2026

## 1. Materials, Emissions, Water, and the Choices Between Them

In this update the material and ecological conditions of people's lives can be studied in the Urban Studio: what a city's buildings hold, what an economy's final demand emits, how a water network serves its people, and how options are weighed ([ADR 0106](../decisions/0106-industrial-ecology-founded.md), [ADR 0107](../decisions/0107-civil-engineering-founded-water-networks.md), [ADR 0108](../decisions/0108-appraisal-futures-and-the-agreement-of-coders.md), [ADR 0109](../decisions/0109-ecosystem-services.md)). This follows the user's handbook of methods for such studies; most of what it asks is now built, and what is not is listed below.

- **Two domains founded.**
  - **Industrial ecology:** a society's stocks and flows of materials and energy:
    - stocks that age and are replaced, by a lifetime law one declares;
    - flows balanced and reconciled with their uncertainties;
    - life cycle assessment;
    - input–output footprints by product, by final use and by region, with the supply chains that carry the most;
    - physical accounts;
    - the materials in buildings, with bounds that widen over what is not known of each building.
  - **Civil engineering:** water distribution networks:
    - EPANET's files read, and heads, pressures and flows solved as EPANET solves them (on its own example network, to a millimetre of head);
    - demands that fall with pressure;
    - a day with tanks and controls;
    - the age of the water, the network's resilience, and which pipes it cannot lose.
- **The plug-in *Materials and flows*,** seven tools and three examples on open data:
  - **Melbourne's centre:** 1,524 buildings hold about 11.2 million tonnes of eight materials, 61% of it concrete. No building's structure is recorded, so the bounds run from 0.8 to 50 million tonnes, and the page says why. Rebuilding to 2050 under a declared lifetime would move about 3 million tonnes each way.
  - **The EU27 in 2022:** final demand causes 3.60 billion tonnes of greenhouse gases along EU supply chains, households' own included. Households cause 57% of it and exports 23%; electricity and food cause the most. What imports embody abroad is not counted, and the page says so.
  - **EPANET's Net3:** 19 of its 117 pipes cannot be closed without some junction getting less than it needs; losing pipe 233 leaves 59% of the demand delivered.
- **The plug-in *Appraisal and futures*,** four tools:
  - alternatives compared on several criteria, with a screen for limits and how far a weight can move before the first changes;
  - costs and benefits over time, with declining discount rates and weights for who gains;
  - Delphi rounds and morphological fields;
  - how far coders agree.
- **Ecosystem services.** The ecology domain gained carbon storage, habitat quality, water yield and soil loss on a grid of land cover, after InVEST.
- **For research on the board:** Tabula's research plug-in gained templates for a research question, tools weighed against it, a study's protocol, a register of uncertainties, and a register of models.
- **Beneath it:**
  - lifetime laws, Bayesian computation (conjugate models, adaptive Metropolis, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, R̂) and uncertainty and sensitivity (Latin hypercubes, Morris, Sobol') in tyche;
  - the leading supply chains with an exact bound on the rest in calx;
  - networks of conduits in physis;
  - appraisal and the agreement of coders in nomos.
- **Mended on the way.** A network cut in two by a closed pipe, its far side drawing no water, is now solved; on EPANET's Net3 it had stopped the analysis.
- **Not yet:** energy systems and power flow, a building's energy hour by hour, mixed-integer programming, agents that plan, ELECTRE, minimax regret, and scenario discovery.
- **In the gallery:** ten pictures in §36.

## 2. Dynamical Systems: a Language, a Foundation and a Lab

In this update dynamical systems of every kind can be declared in one language, run, analysed, and recovered from records, in a new work: the Dynamics Lab ([ADR 0103](../decisions/0103-kinesis-and-the-dynamics-lab.md)).

- **One script, every kind of system.** A flow, a map, a neuron that fires and is reset, a stochastic differential, a pendulum given by its action, a decaying atom, a grammar, and systems made of systems are declared in one notation, built on the user's four primitives: change, structure, constraint and difference. A script that is wrong is told where.
- **The Dynamics Lab** (port 9110). The script is at the left; nineteen tools are at the right; each tool that is run is kept as a study, with what it was asked and what it rests on. One can run a system, find its Lyapunov exponents with the error of their finite time, its equilibria, its diagram as a parameter moves, its basins and its landscape; watch for slowing down before a transition; and read the braid that trajectories make.
- **Answers that say what they rest on.** A braid read from samples is certified only under the bound on speed one states, with its margin; otherwise the windings the record admits are listed. This follows the user's own draft on trajectories and braids, whose numbers are reproduced.
- **From a record to a law.** A law is discovered with none proposed, a template's numbers are fitted, candidates are ranked, and a record is matched against a catalogue of thirty models in every order of its components. What is found is a declaration, which goes back into the script.
- **Four case studies.**
  - **Turbulence.** A shell model of a turbulent fluid: the power put in, the power dissipated and the flux through the cascade agree, the energy falls from scale to scale more steeply than Kolmogorov's 2/3, and the small scales are intermittent. It is a model of the cascade, not a discretised fluid.
  - **A coupling as a term of an action.** Two pendulums joined so keep their energy and pass it between them; joined by a wire one way, nothing is kept. An interaction action is the form of couplings that are forces. Most couplings between living things are not, and stay wires.
  - **A fluid, its chemistry and a species.** Convection, plankton and fish evolve together. With fish the plankton stands exactly where the fish neither grow nor decline; without them there is 4.4 times as much and the fluid overturns half as strongly again. A conceptual model, and said to be.
  - **Behaviours.** Of two animals that call and listen unequally, the one that listens less sets the pace.
- **Beneath it:** kinesis, an eleventh foundation; braid groups in morphe; open quantum systems of any finite dimension in physis; drawings of dynamical systems in the visualization layer, whose line colours are apart in every colour vision.

Not yet: fields that run, the reversal of a stochastic differential, unknown functions, and systems known only as black boxes. The language reads the first three and says they are not carried out.

Twenty-five pictures are in the [gallery](../../docs/gallery.md).

## 3. Time, Care and Relational Life

In this update people's days, the care they give and need, and the ties among them can be studied in the Urban Studio's second plug-in, *Time, care and provision* ([ADR 0104](../decisions/0104-sociology-founded-time-and-relations.md), [ADR 0105](../decisions/0105-social-provision.md)).

- **A day read as a whole.** A day holds 1440 minutes, so the minutes of one activity cannot be compared alone: they are read as parts of the whole, by their ratios. One can find a group's typical day, contrast two days, see who carries each kind of time and compare places. From any sample of days with an outcome, one can also read what moving half an hour from one activity to another goes with.
- **Ties among people.** How closed a circle is, whether groups keep among themselves (tested by permutation), who bridges between circles, and who has few people to turn to.
- **Households and care.**
  - The value of unpaid work at a wage one declares, and time poverty against a line one declares.
  - The care a population needs now and later, with its change split exactly among the population's size, its ageing and how common limitation is.
  - Years lived in health, by Sullivan's method.
  - A labour market's flows between employment, unemployment and inactivity, and what each flow did to the unemployment they would hold.
- **The example *Time and care in Europe*** runs end to end on seven Eurostat datasets.
  - Household and family care is what separates women's days from men's most, in sixteen of eighteen countries. Women carry 58% (Norway) to 84% (Türkiye) of it.
  - Years lived in health agree with Eurostat's own at 65 to a median of 0.10 years.
  - The EU27's people needing care rise by about a fifth by 2050, from 28.0 to 34.3 million, almost wholly from ageing.
  - In France, the flow out of unemployment into work accounts for more than all of the rise in 2020's lockdown.
- **Beneath it:**
  - sociology, founded with code (a day as a composition, ties among people);
  - social provision, a bridge over economics, markets, demography and sociology (households, care);
  - years in health in demography, a labour market's flows in economics;
  - compositions and relational data in tyche.
- **Mended on the way.**
  - Eurostat's projections no longer count older people more than once.
  - Groups of countries are no longer added to their own members.
  - A prevalence now states its unit instead of having it guessed: 0.7% had been read as 70%.
  - Charts colour points by group and turn crowded labels.
  - The studios' pages always show their plug-ins' latest tools.
- **In the gallery:** eight pictures in §35.

## 4. The Urban Studio, and Ecology

In this update the city's living things can be studied, project by project, in a new work: the Urban Studio. Its first subject is the urban forest.

- **A studio for cities.** The Urban Studio (port 9090) holds a project's data as they are, with their columns bound to roles, its slices of space and time, its quantities, and the tools it ran with what they answered. These are drawn as maps and charts and written about on boards. Subjects arrive as plug-ins that bring tools, templates, an engine and declared schemes; more will follow. A project lists the methods it used, with their parameters, and one's own Python scripts run beside the plug-ins' tools.
- **Seen in three dimensions.** A placed table goes to the Urban Science Workbench with one key. The Workbench shows it over the city and flies to it.
- **Ecology, founded with code.** The ecology domain holds:
  - individuals named at species, genus and family, and how far apart their kinds are;
  - diversity counted, and estimated for a fuller count, with areas compared at one coverage;
  - populations counted by stages, and what a constant planting sustains;
  - a forest of individual trees: its composition against declared limits, its carbon by declared equations, its future under removal and planting with the exact spread, the canopy its residents live under;
  - how habitat holds together, by patches and by circuits.
- **The plug-in *Ecology and life*:** ten tools, and two examples on open data.
  - **Melbourne's urban forest:** 82,064 trees with their arborists' assessments, and the measured canopy of 2021 block by block.
  - **New York's street trees, 2015:** the city's census.
- **What Melbourne shows.**
  - Every rank exceeds Santamour's limits: *Eucalyptus camaldulensis* is 10.3% of the trees, *Eucalyptus* 21.6%, Myrtaceae 36%.
  - 642 species are counted; a fuller count would find about 707.
  - Homes stand under 15.8% canopy where the average block has 20.1%.
  - 3,000 trees planted a year would sustain about 105,000, depending most on the survival of the youngest.
- **What every answer says.** What was counted, what was estimated and what was presumed are kept apart. Diameters no tree could have are set aside as errors of the record, and counted.
- **Shared below the works.** Market Studio's project server and page, and the builder of C++ engines that each work had copied, are now general layers of the API and the visualization layer. The Urban Studio is the first work on them.
- **In the gallery:** eleven pictures in §33.
