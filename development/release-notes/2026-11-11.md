# Release Notes: 11 November 2026

## 1. Market Studio

In this update Agora becomes Market Studio: a platform for market analysis on your own data, for any place ([ADR 0093](../decisions/0093-market-studio.md)).

- **Work in projects.** A project holds your data, the slices of space and time you ask about, your assumptions, the tools you ran with what they answered, your views, and your boards. It is kept as a folder.
- **Bring data as they are.** Add a CSV, a table in JSON or GeoJSON. Nothing is asked of its column names: you say which column is the latitude, the time, the name, the amount, and the page guesses first. A filter keeps a part of a large file.
- **Cut slices.** A slice is a region, a span of time and a period. Run a tool on a slice and it is given only the rows within it. The same tool on two slices answers two tables.
- **Fourteen tools are ready.** The market: who could buy, who sells and how much of the spend buys, whose custom an outlet's is, where to open, how large. Through time: openings and closings, how long outlets last, concentration, change between two times. Tables: counts and sums by a column, places in squares, places in areas, a new column from a formula, a table in brief. Each has a form built from what it declares.
- **Write your own.** A tool is a short script in Python that declares its parameters and answers tables, notes and figures. It lives in the project and gets a form like the others. The same script runs on a board in Tabula.
- **See it.** Tables with a place are layers of the map: points, areas, lines. A layer with a time moves with a clock. Six kinds of chart are ready, with counts, sums and means by a column and a second. A script can draw a figure of its own.
- **Write about it.** Send a table, a chart or a whole result to a board of the project: what was found and what was presumed become notes, each table comes with the card that draws it.
- **Results stay honest.** Every run is recorded as a step. When the data, the slice or an assumption changes, the steps that stood on it are marked stale and can be run again. The market's answers say that their kernel is presumed.
- **An example to begin from:** Melbourne's cafés, 2002–2024, on open data, with a plan of nine steps that runs in about a minute.
- **To try it:** `python data/acquisition/recipe.py applications/market-studio/recipe.json`, then `python applications/market-studio/serve.py --open` (port 9030). The engine needs g++.
- **Not yet.** A market's parameters fitted from real choices; regions drawn by hand; the 3D map as a view. Nine pictures are in the gallery.

## 2. The Markets Domain, and Agora

*Agora's page was replaced the same day by Market Studio (§1). The domain stands, and what Agora computed is now the Studio's predefined tools.*

In this update the infrastructure gains a domain of markets and a work that draws a city's market on its map ([ADR 0092](../decisions/0092-the-markets-domain-and-agora.md)).

- **A market is buyers, offers and what goes from one to the other.** The new domain, `markets`, computes what market analysis asks of that: who sells and how concentrated they are, where an outlet's custom comes from, where its custom would go if it closed, what no one serves, how large the market is, what a new outlet would sell and where to open one. Each result is proved in its theory note and checked.
- **Agora shows it on the map.** Melbourne's centre in 2024: 410 blocks and 17,499 places of business in 205 kinds. Choose what is sold (cafés and restaurants, supermarkets, any kind), and ask four questions: the market, whose custom, where to open, how large.
- **Whose custom.** Press an outlet. Its trade area is drawn on the blocks as a density, with the minutes that hold half and four fifths of its custom, and the rivals its custom would go to. Who competes with whom follows from who shares buyers, not from who is largest.
- **Where to open.** Press a point for an outlet there: what it would sell, how much from rivals, from those who bought nothing, and from the seller's own outlets. Or let three of the census's vacant spaces be chosen.
- **How large.** The census's dwellings and offices times assumptions with ranges. The least and the most are exact, and a tornado shows which assumption matters most.
- **What is assumed is said.** The data library holds no sales. The market is presumed: Huff's model on minutes walked along the streets, with what each buyer brings assumed. Every assumption stands at the left of the page, is yours to change, and goes with a finding to the board.
- **The board and the map are its tools.** Each answer has a key that sends it to a board: what was found, what was assumed, a table and a map or a chart. The 2D GIS gained a map of a town at the scale of streets.
- **To try it:** `python data/acquisition/recipe.py applications/agora/recipe.json`, then `python applications/agora/serve.py --open` (port 9030). The engine needs g++.
- **Not yet.** Parameters fitted from real choices; a size for each outlet; other cities; competition between sellers who respond; markets over time. Six pictures are in the gallery.

## 3. An Agent on the Board

In this update Tabula gains an agent ([ADR 0091](../decisions/0091-an-agent-on-the-board.md)): one says in words what one wants on the board, and a language model sets it out.

- **Ask in words.** The key *Agent* opens a panel at the side. "Size the market for a specialty café in Melbourne, with every assumption on a note" gives notes with ranges, a figure that computes from them, and a map of the city's 1,677 cafés and restaurants from a module it ran. "This" and "these" are what is selected.
- **It works in the open.** Each thing it does is a line in the panel as it is done: items added, links joined, a stroke drawn, a module run. Each is a step of the board's history, so Undo takes it back like anything else.
- **It can use the plug-ins, as far as one allows.** Its settings list the plug-ins with what each brings; those ticked are the agent's to draw on, and running modules is a tick of its own. It is given no way to write or run code of its own.
- **One's own key.** The settings take a key, a model (Claude Opus 5.5 by default, with Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Fable 5.1) and how hard it thinks. The key is kept in one's own settings folder on this computer, outside the repository, and is never sent back to the page. Other providers can be added as a class in one file.
- **What it writes is a draft.** It marks its own numbers as assumptions with ranges and says what to check.
- **To try it:** `pip install anthropic`, start Tabula, press *Agent*, and set a key in *Settings*.
- **For plug-ins.** A plug-in of Tabula may now bring a panel and a server side of its own.
- **Not yet.** Its words appear when a step is whole, not as they are written; it reads a board's words, not its pictures; one key does not undo a whole turn. Three pictures are in the gallery.

## 4. A Board for Thought

In this update the infrastructure gains Tabula, a board on which thought is set out and tied to evidence ([ADR 0090](../decisions/0090-a-board-for-thought.md)). It began as a request for market analysis, and the user's turn shaped it: tools are tools, and the analysis is a person's. So the board is the work, and market analysis comes to it as a plug-in.

- **Think on it.** Double-click for a note. Notes are of kinds: an idea, a fact with its source, an assumption, a question, an insight, a decision, a risk. Join two with a link that says *supports* or *contradicts*. Gather notes in regions, draw with a pen, lay sketches on a layer of their own and hide it.
- **Plans and chronicles are written as lines.** `Interviews : after Desk research, 3w` becomes a Gantt chart that shows which tasks cannot slip; `480 BC : Salamis` an event on a line of time.
- **Assumptions compute.** Write `share = 3% [2%, 5%]` on a note and it is a quantity with its range. A figure such as `TAM = buyers * need * spend` follows the notes, carries the ranges through, and says what it rests on.
- **Data, maps and models stand beside the notes.** Paste rows from a sheet for a table; draw it as a chart or on a map. Modules in Python get data from the data library and run models on the board: they read its tables and the assumptions on its notes, and answer tables that a press lays on the board as a map or a chart.
- **A plug-in says what it needs.** A module or a card declares its parameters (a number, a choice, a table of the board, a column, a file), and the panel asks for them. Nothing about a plug-in is written into the page.
- **Market analysis, the first plug-in.** Twelve templates, among them the size of a market from assumptions, a SWOT, the five forces and a buyer's journey. Its modules work on real data: 204 economies of 2023 and what a market could be worth in each, the economies gathered into segments, and the 1,677 cafés and restaurants of the City of Melbourne on its blocks.
- **Four more plug-ins.** The city in three dimensions, with a table's places standing on its roofs. Agents: how fast something would spread among buyers who look to one another, played with a clock beside Bass's curve. History: the events of one of the history domain's canons on a line of time. Research: hypotheses and what speaks for and against them.
- **For any work.** The board itself is in the visualization layer and the contract of its modules in the API, so another work can embed it. Eight pictures are in the gallery.
- **Not yet.** The markets domain, which the plug-in's scripts stand in for; several people on one board. A stylus has not been tried: the pen was drawn with a mouse.

## 5. What Could Be Built

In this update the Urban Science Workbench designs what could be built on a block anywhere ([ADR 0089](../decisions/0089-what-could-be-built.md)), in its plug-in *Architecture*, planned until now.

- **Choose a block and rules.** Pick a block on the map, then a set of rules: a street wall and towers, mid-rise, or towers apart. Each states a height limit, a plot ratio, setbacks and a street wall.
- **The search.** Each building becomes a lot with a podium and a tower within what the rules allow, and a passage may be opened through the block. Thousands of designs are searched at once. The card keeps those no other design betters in all six measures: floor, winter sun on the streets, winter sun on the neighbours' faces, the heat a winter day needs, how well the streets about the block connect, and how much lies within a five-minute walk.
- **On the map.** The design chosen stands in place of the block's buildings, with the hours of winter sun on the ground around it. Keys pick the design best at each measure, or the balanced one.
- **In the Studio.** Capture the view, and the alternatives arrive in the Studio as charts beside it.
- **What it found on three blocks of Melbourne.**
  - **Less floor.** In the city centre, towers set back from a street wall cannot hold the floor that stands now.
  - **More sun.** The streets can gain up to half again their winter sun, or a tenth while keeping four fifths of the floor.
  - **No warmer.** No design needs less heat per square metre than the buildings standing, which fill their lots and share their walls.
- **Beneath it.** The architecture domain gains the rules that bound a building, its massing and street wall, and sun and shade on streets and faces. physis moves any outline inward edge by edge. The whole composition stands in the API, so a study runs it without the page. The gallery's §25 shows it.
