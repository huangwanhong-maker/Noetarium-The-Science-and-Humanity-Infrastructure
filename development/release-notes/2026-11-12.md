# Release Notes: 12 November 2026

## 1. Land Use and Transport, Year by Year

In this update the infrastructure can follow a city's land use and its transport as they change one another, year by year ([ADR 0094](../decisions/0094-land-use-and-transport-interacting.md)).

- **Place a change and watch the years after it.** In the Urban Science Workbench, under the lens Change, the layer *a change, and the years after it* works anywhere. Pick a place: its people, workplaces and main roads come with it. Then put changes on the map: widen or close a road, allow more dwellings in a cell, add 2,000, 10,000 or 40,000 jobs. Run ten or twenty years. The years run twice, as things stand and with the changes. The cells take the colour of what the changes do to their households, the roads of their load in the morning peak, and the years play on the map. The card gives the households living there, the mean journey to work, and the cells that gain and lose most.
- **What moves in a year.** Dwellings are built where last year's rents ran above what building costs, within what the rules allow. Households choose where to live by a market whose rents clear, and value each place for its access to jobs. The journeys to work settle on the roads, slowing as the roads fill, and where people go and the way they take are found together. Melbourne's centre runs ten years in about twenty seconds.
- **Use it from any work.** The model is urban science's module `luti`, and one call in the API runs it for any place's roads and zones. nomos gained the general methods beneath it: flows between places and how they fall with cost (Wilson's models, calibrated as statsmodels calibrates them), and travellers sharing a congested network (Wardrop's equilibrium, the system optimum, the price of anarchy).
- **Journeys to work by year.** The data library gives New York City's journeys to work between its 2,327 census tracts, from the Census Bureau's LODES, for ten years from 2002 to 2023. Other regions are added as cases.
- **What New York's record shows.** The decay of the journey to work with distance weakened over the twenty years, and a year's decay forecasts a later year's flows as well as that year's own. But places that gained access to jobs gained fewer resident workers, not more. The model, fitted to the city's total growth, places that growth no better than spreading it evenly. Building did not follow access in New York's decade, and the study says so. Seven pictures are in the gallery.
