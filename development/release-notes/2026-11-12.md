# Release Notes: 12 November 2026

## 1. The Urban Science Workbench Opens Quickly, and Its Works Can Be Switched Off

In this update the Urban Science Workbench is lighter, and one can choose what it carries.

- **It opens without waiting.** Each time the page opened, the server checked every plug-in's engine against all the C++ it is built from, which kept the page waiting about twenty seconds. The check is now kept while no source changes, and a header is looked for once: the page is answered in about 20 ms, or under two seconds just after the server starts.
- **Works switched on and off.** The lamp *works* beneath the title, or the line at the foot of the lenses, opens the switches. Each work has one, with a line saying what it holds and what it asks of the computer. A work switched off leaves the page: its layers are not drawn, its analyses leave the lenses, its readings are not asked at a point picked, and the server neither checks nor warms its engine. Switched on again, it comes back as it was.
- **Lighter drawing.** The Sun's shadows can be turned off, the pixels drawn halved each way, and the world's buildings held close about the view, not toward the horizon. The analyses that read the streets (reach, configuration, land use) see only what is held.
- **Kept in the browser.** The choices stay from one visit to the next.

## 2. A Studio for Experiments in Category Theory

In this update the infrastructure has a place to try things in category theory and be told exactly what holds ([ADR 0100](../decisions/0100-kategoria-and-the-category-studio.md)).

- **The Category Studio.** One writes a script at the left; at the right each statement is answered in order. A category is given by generators and equations, or taken from a family (ordinals, lattices, groups, finite sets, the simplex category); functors, natural transformations, data on a schema and monoidal theories are declared; claims are stated with `check` and questions asked with `find`.
- **Answers with evidence.** A claim holds, with what makes it so: a derivation step by step, a limit with its legs, an adjoint with its unit and counit. It fails, with the place where it fails: the composite a functor does not keep, the cone that does not factor, the entry at which the two sides of a law differ. Or it is not decided, and says why. A statement that is not understood is answered with its line, its column and what could come there, and the rest of the script is still carried out.
- **Drawings.** A category is drawn by arrows that generate it; a functor as two diagrams, each object's hue shown again beneath its image; a limit with its diagram and apex marked; data as slips of elements joined by lines; a derivation with the part each step replaces marked; and string diagrams, a symmetry being two wires that cross.
- **Eleven examples,** from a commutative square to bialgebras and Frobenius algebras: S₃ from its presentation with (st)³ = 1 proved in four steps; greatest common divisors as products; an adjoint found and one refused with the object that has no universal arrow; data moved along a functor in three ways; the truth values over an ordinal; the simplex category rebuilt from its faces and degeneracies.
- **A new foundation, kategoria.** The mathematics is a library of its own on morphe: finite categories and those presented by generators and equations, functors and adjunctions, limits, set-valued functors with their Kan extensions and classifier of subobjects, monoidal theories with their diagrams and models, and the language of scripts. It is held to 190 checks. The name is provisional.
- **For any work.** The drawings are in the visualization layer (`categories`), and the API offers the language to any work's engine (`science/categories.hpp`).
- **What it is not.** It decides claims about categories given whole or finitely presented. It is not a proof assistant, and a law that holds in one model says nothing of a theory at large.

Start it with `python applications/category-studio/serve.py --open`; the page offers to build its engine the first time. The gallery's §32 holds thirteen pictures.

## 3. A Record of Movements Read as a Foam

In this update a record of who gave what to whom, and when, can be read as one complex, and asked what the order of events, the terms of exchange and the round of money did in it ([ADR 0099](../decisions/0099-the-market-foam.md)). It implements the generative–relational market formalism of three drafts handed in by the principal investigator.

- **A plug-in of Market Studio.** *A record as a foam* brings eight tools that work on any table of movements (an event, a time, a giver, a taker, a kind, a quantity), and a starting project whose record is generated from rules, so that every tool can be tried at once.
- **The record's spacetime.** Each event is a point, each subject a line through the events that touch it, each holding a band between the events that change it. From it: how many events each subject lives through, how much of the record is in causal order, and whether the books balance at every event. Booking holdings at a ledger instead of leaving them with their holders changes the causal order, and the tool shows by how much.
- **Who has the better of whom.** From the exchanges alone, the record's own valuation of each kind is fitted, and each subject's advantage against it follows. What no valuation explains is left as loops, and loops through time compare a price that moved with what was earned by holding.
- **Money's round.** What stood, what passed and the velocity, period by period; and where new money goes first: who gains and who loses when a sum is injected at one place, with the times at which prices rise.
- **Dividing what is short, and two mechanisms.** An estate short of its claims is divided by every rule, with what the order of payment alone can change. Two candidate mechanisms are compared by how much of their difference a record shows and how much it hides.
- **Market Studio takes plug-ins.** A directory under `plugins/` brings a group of tools, templates and the small files a template's project begins with.
- **Beneath it.** The markets domain gained five modules (`system`, `foam`, `valuation`, `money`, `procedure`); tyche, information through a record; nomos, the Shapley value and the division of a short estate. The domain's theory note says what is proved and reproduces the drafts' numbers that follow from their text.
- **Five amendments to the drafts,** each held by a check: a cycle that shares an edge with a triangle does not keep its own holonomy under arbitrage; "the concurrent procedure" is a family of rules indexed by rates; the presentation of claims is not the one the existence proof constructs; receipts in the injection model are $W^{\mathsf T}m$, after which conservation needs no symmetry; and the causal density reported under a ledger needs its setup stated.
- **Not built:** the categorical layer (schemas as categories, attributed rewriting), observers and the reflexive extension, most of the classical cases, and field data.

The gallery's §31 holds five pictures.

## 4. The Design Studio: a Building Drawn by Hand, and the Street It Stands On

In this update the infrastructure has a studio for designing one building, on any lot of any street ([ADR 0095](../decisions/0095-the-design-studio.md), [ADR 0096](../decisions/0096-what-stands-in-the-rooms.md), [ADR 0097](../decisions/0097-the-shell-of-a-drawn-building.md), [ADR 0098](../decisions/0098-the-street-a-building-stands-on.md)). The person designs; the studio tells what the drawing does, after every change. Start it with `python applications/design-studio/serve.py --open`.

- **Draw a plan.** Walls, rooms, doors, windows and stairs are drawn by hand, storey by storey, and every change can be undone. The rooms are found from the walls: each one's net area and width, its light and air, and how it is reached from the door.
- **Be told, one statement at a time.** A code of habitation, a scheme of siting rules and a brief of one's own are each answered statement by statement, with the reason where one fails. The schemes are files: one that states nothing, and Victoria's standards for a dwelling on a lot as the first case. The quantities, a cost as a range and the fabric's heat loss follow the drawing.
- **Furnish it and walk through it.** Forty-four kinds of furniture, fixtures and garden pieces come with their models, and a model of one's own can stand in a piece's place. Each piece is found within its room, clear of the others, usable, and reached by a person of a stated width. The building is seen in the round, or walked through at eye height under the place's Sun and its own lamps.
- **Give it an outside.** A roof flat, shed, gabled or hipped, with its pitch, eaves and cover; walls in render, brick, weatherboards, battens or stone; a hedge, a fence or a wall about the lot; trees and a bench in the garden. The four elevations are drawn from the plan on one sheet. Each room's view of the sky, its daylight factor and its hours of sun are read among the real neighbours.
- **Stand it on a real street.** Any place's buildings and ways come from Overture Maps with no preparation. A way nearby is taken in hand by its name: its section is drawn at any point, with the width between the buildings, the sky and the Sun's ray, and the whole street is unrolled beneath. Its footpaths, verges, lanes and parking are set out by hand, and limits of one's own are found one by one.
- **Ask how high each building may rise.** For every building on the street, the studio gives the height it can reach before a footpath loses the hours of sun asked for, and the sun it takes as it stands. A neighbour can be given another height, and the footpaths and one's own windows answer.
- **Beneath it.** The architecture domain gained the building as it is drawn (`plan`, `programme`, `interior`, `shell`, and the sun at a pane); urban science gained a street between its faces (`section`); physis the roof over any polygon. The API offers both compositions to any work.
- **What is presumed, and said to be.** The building rates, the clear floor each piece needs, a neighbour's height where the map gives none (6 m), and the Victorian values, which were read from a survey and not checked clause by clause. The daylight factor is an estimate. A building's limit is its own: two raised together may take more sun than either alone.

Twenty-five pictures are in the gallery. The studio's name and colours are provisional.

## 5. Land Use and Transport, Year by Year

In this update the infrastructure can follow a city's land use and its transport as they change one another, year by year ([ADR 0094](../decisions/0094-land-use-and-transport-interacting.md)).

- **Place a change and watch the years after it.** In the Urban Science Workbench, under the lens Change, the layer *a change, and the years after it* works anywhere. Pick a place: its people, workplaces and main roads come with it. Then put changes on the map: widen or close a road, allow more dwellings in a cell, add 2,000, 10,000 or 40,000 jobs. Run ten or twenty years. The years run twice, as things stand and with the changes. The cells take the colour of what the changes do to their households, the roads of their load in the morning peak, and the years play on the map. The card gives the households living there, the mean journey to work, and the cells that gain and lose most.
- **What moves in a year.** Dwellings are built where last year's rents ran above what building costs, within what the rules allow. Households choose where to live by a market whose rents clear, and value each place for its access to jobs. The journeys to work settle on the roads, slowing as the roads fill, and where people go and the way they take are found together. Melbourne's centre runs ten years in about twenty seconds.
- **Use it from any work.** The model is urban science's module `luti`, and one call in the API runs it for any place's roads and zones. nomos gained the general methods beneath it: flows between places and how they fall with cost (Wilson's models, calibrated as statsmodels calibrates them), and travellers sharing a congested network (Wardrop's equilibrium, the system optimum, the price of anarchy).
- **Journeys to work by year.** The data library gives New York City's journeys to work between its 2,327 census tracts, from the Census Bureau's LODES, for ten years from 2002 to 2023. Other regions are added as cases.
- **What New York's record shows.** The decay of the journey to work with distance weakened over the twenty years, and a year's decay forecasts a later year's flows as well as that year's own. But places that gained access to jobs gained fewer resident workers, not more. The model, fitted to the city's total growth, places that growth no better than spreading it evenly. Building did not follow access in New York's decade, and the study says so. Seven pictures are in the gallery.
