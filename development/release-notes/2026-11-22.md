# Release Notes: 22 November 2026

## 11. What Visitors' Spending Becomes, and What a Levy Does (ADR 0157)

The visitor stock is now carried into the economy ([ADR 0157](../decisions/0157-tourism-accounts-and-levies.md)).

- **Spending through the economy.** Visitors' nights times their spending a night, over a basket of products, run through the country's input–output table: the value added of what visitors buy, the value added along the supply chains behind it, the inputs imported and the taxes paid. Every euro spent at home ends in one of these, a check the results are held to, so a multiplier above one cannot arise.
- **A levy on nights.** For a levy a night, the price visitors pay and the price operators keep, under declared responses of demand and supply; the revenue, the share visitors and operators each bear, the nights lost and those that move to stays the levy does not reach, the value added lost with the rest, and the levy that would raise the most.
- **A country's table read openly.** The data library reads Eurostat's symmetric input–output tables for any country that publishes them.

On Prague's 24 million visitor nights of 2024, at declared spending, visitors spend about 3.0 billion euro, which becomes 1.06 billion of value added directly and 1.83 billion along the supply chains; a quarter leaks abroad. A declared levy of 2 euro a night raises 47 million euro a year, 77% of it borne by visitors, under the elasticities declared. The levies of real cities are not claimed. Three pictures are in the gallery's §54.1.

## 10. How Many Visitors Are Present, and When (ADR 0156)

How many visitors a city holds on an average day of each month, and from where, can now be read from the nights they spend, for any European region whose accommodation returns Eurostat publishes ([ADR 0156](../decisions/0156-the-visitor-stock.md)).

- **Nights as people present.** A night spent is a visitor present for a day, so a month's nights over its days is the number of visitors present on an average day, domestic and foreign apart.
- **Every reading of "visitor".** Registered hotels and holiday accommodation only; platform rentals too, which Eurostat publishes month by month; stays with friends and relatives or in second homes, and same-day visitors, at rates a user declares, since no return holds them. Each reading gives its own number.
- **Intensity and seasons.** Visitors per thousand residents, nights per resident and per km²; the peak month against the mean and how unevenly visitors spread over the year; each judged against a limit only where someone declares one.
- **Regions to places.** Where platform rentals are published only for a wider region, they are carried to the place in proportion to its registered nights, and the shares add back to the region.

In the Urban Studio's new plug-in *Visitors, how many and when*, the Metropolitan City of Venice holds 146 visitors per thousand residents on an average day of 2024 and 369 in August, against 47 in Prague and 21 in the Brussels-Capital Region. Venice's seasons are by far the strongest; Vienna peaks in December. In Grande Lisboa, platform rentals are 37% of all nights counted. Three pictures are in the gallery's §54.

## 9. Homes and Who Lacks Them (ADR 0155)

SDG 11.1.1, the share of a city's people living in slums, informal settlements or inadequate housing, can now be computed from a household survey, under every reading of the terms the indicator leaves open ([ADR 0155](../decisions/0155-shelter-and-sdg-11-1-1.md)).

- **Five things a home may lack.** A household lacking any one of an improved water source, improved sanitation, enough room, a durable dwelling or secure tenure is counted, as UN-Habitat's metadata says; a household whose housing costs more than 30% of its income is inadequately housed.
- **Every reading of the open terms.** Tenure left out, as it often was, or secure with a document and no fear of eviction; the JMP's ladder, where a shared toilet is not improved; two people to a room instead of three; walls and roof without the floor; a hazardous site counted. Each gives its own share.
- **Missing answers bounded.** Every survey has them. The share is given between counting them as not deprived and as deprived, so that what the survey cannot tell is shown, not hidden.
- **With the survey's own uncertainty.** Intervals come from the survey's weights, strata and clusters, and each district's share is estimated within the whole survey.

In the Urban Studio's new plug-in *Homes and who lacks them*, an invented city's survey of 1,200 households shows between 44% and 64% of people in slum or informal households depending on the reading, and 56% under the metadata's five components. Its new estates stand at 13%, but missing answers about tenure leave that anywhere between 10% and 31%. Four pictures are in the gallery's §53.

## 8. Who Lives within Reach of Open Space (ADR 0154)

SDG 11.7.1, the share of a city that is open space for public use and the share of its people within 400 m of it along the streets, can now be computed for any place from the open land mapped there, under every reading of the terms the indicator leaves open ([ADR 0154](../decisions/0154-open-space-and-sdg-11-7-1.md)).

- **Open land read anywhere.** The data library reads the land use and land cover that Overture's base theme maps for a place: parks, plazas, sports grounds, gardens, cemeteries, grass and woodland, each polygon with what the map calls it. A declared table reads these as kinds of open space, and a golf course, which charges a fee, counts under no reading.
- **Counted once.** A park drawn over grass is one piece of land: the share of the area is the union of what counts, so overlapping layers are not counted twice.
- **Reach under each reading.** Which land counts (parks and plazas; pedestrian areas and sports grounds as well; every green and open land), how far, and whether along the streets or in a straight line, as WHO Europe's rule of a park of half a hectare to a hectare within 300 m asks. Each reading gives its own share.
- **Faster everywhere a place joins a network.** Joining homes and spaces to the streets now goes through a grid and gives exactly the same answer, so a reading that took 22 seconds takes about one. Every tool that finds the nearest service along the streets gains from it.

In the Urban Studio's plug-in, now *Public transport, open space and who can reach them*, 78% of the 220,000 people in a square of 5 km about Melbourne's Town Hall live within 400 m of a park or a plaza along the streets, and 95% of any green or open land. Under WHO Europe's rule, 63% live within 300 m of a park of half a hectare, and 47% of a park of a hectare. Four pictures are in the gallery's §52.1.

## 7. Can a City Afford Its Project, and What Its Waste Emits (ADR 0152, ADR 0153)

The two links the day's earlier work left open are made.

- **Can a city afford its project, with and without the land value it captures?** ([ADR 0152](../decisions/0152-a-projects-financing-plan.md)) A project's cost is met year by year by what its value capture instruments bring in, a reserve and new loans, on the city's own years ahead. The city's coverage of its debt service is read against its floor, and the largest project it could afford is found with the receipts and without them. Capital receipts pay for capital and never raise the operating surplus. On the invented tram line, Ardel could afford a project 2.17 times as large with the receipts pledged to the new loans, and 1.85 times without them.
- **What a city's waste emits, in its inventory** ([ADR 0153](../decisions/0153-what-waste-emits-carried-into-the-inventory.md)). The waste account's landfills and dumpsites emit methane as their past deposits decay, by the IPCC's first-order decay. Composting and burning in the open have factors of their own. The defaults are the IPCC's, each read from the Guidelines with its page, in their 2006 or 2019 edition. The result enters the city's greenhouse gas inventory by the GPC's codes, ready to add to it. The invented city's waste sector is 63,145 t CO2e by the 2006 defaults and 81,149 t by the 2019 Refinement: the choice of edition alone moves it by 29%.

## 5. Who Lives within Reach of Public Transport (ADR 0151)

SDG 11.2.1, the share of people within walking distance of public transport, can now be computed for a city from its timetable, under every reading of the terms the indicator leaves open ([ADR 0151](../decisions/0151-public-transport-and-sdg-11-2-1.md)).

- **A timetable read.** The data library reads GTFS Schedule feeds: the services that run on a date, the stops and the modes that serve them, and each stop's departures through the day. Melbourne's feed is the first case, read for a Wednesday.
- **The service at each stop.** Departures an hour at the peak, the longest wait, and the mean wait of a passenger arriving at random, computed exactly and taken past the end of the peak where the day goes on.
- **Reach under each reading.** People are counted within 500 m along the streets of a bus or 1 km of a train, as the indicator's metadata says. The terms it leaves open are read every way declared: whether a tram is of low or high capacity, whether a stop counts only where its mean wait at the peak is at most 20 minutes, and along the streets or in a straight line. Each reading gives its own share.

In the Urban Studio's new plug-in *Public transport and who can reach it*, 92% of the 271,000 people within 2.5 km of Melbourne's Town Hall live within reach with trams counted as low capacity, and 98.6% with trams counted as high. In a straight line instead of along the streets, everyone does.

## 4. What a Hazard Costs Buildings, under Every Damage Function, and What Losses Cost Households (ADR 0149, ADR 0150)

Two questions every assessment of disaster risk asks can now be answered with the infrastructure, each honestly about what is not known.

- **What a flood costs a city's buildings, as a range** ([ADR 0149](../decisions/0149-vulnerability-fragility-and-the-library-as-a-credal-set.md)). Which damage function holds for a place is rarely known, and the choice moves losses by factors of two to five. A library of functions, such as the JRC's global flood depth–damage curves now read by the data library, is applied as a set of models, each to every building of a class at once. The loss comes out as the range over the models, with the wider envelope beside it that would let every building take its own worst or best curve. A mean over the models is taken only when weights are declared. Floods more frequent than the first map are counted only when asked, a choice that moved Freetown's annual loss eightfold.
- **A fragility learnt from what an event left.** A survey's counts of buildings in each damage state at each intensity give each state's fragility with its standard errors. A fragility held before the event, from analysis or from elsewhere, is updated by the same counts, with a credible band and a warning when the prior bounds what the data could say. What the earlier fragility predicted is set against what was found.
- **What losses cost households** ([ADR 0150](../decisions/0150-household-well-being-losses.md)). After *Unbreakable*, households lose assets, the income they earned and the transfers that fall with the nation's assets. They rebuild from what they would have consumed, at a common pace or at the pace best for each, and spend savings and support on the deepest part of the loss. The loss is valued by the utility of what they consume, expressed as national consumption and as socioeconomic resilience. Measures are judged twice, by the asset losses and by the well-being losses they avoid.

In the Urban Studio's *Lifelines and evacuation*, Freetown's 153,435 buildings under its river floods cost between $3.5 m and $11.8 m a year over nine models of the JRC's library. On an invented city, the poorest fifth bear 22% of the asset losses and 67% of the well-being losses. An embankment for the waterfront saves the most assets and ranks fifth of six by well-being.

## 3. A Community's Greenhouse Gas Inventory and Its Measures, and a City's Solid Waste (ADR 0147, ADR 0148)

A city's climate inventory can now be read as cities report it, and its measures credited without counting their overlap twice ([ADR 0147](../decisions/0147-community-emissions-and-their-mitigation.md)).

- **The inventory as cities report it.** An inventory is entered line by line, each line an activity times its factor, a stated amount, or a key saying why there is no figure (not occurring, included elsewhere, not estimated, confidential). It is read by sector and scope into the two reporting levels of the Global Protocol for Community-Scale Greenhouse Gas Emission Inventories and the territorial total. Power a plant within the city sends to the grid, and a neighbour's waste burnt in the city, are kept where they belong; biogenic CO2 is kept apart. The reading says whether each level is complete and, if not, which cell holds it back; how uncertain each total is by the IPCC's simplest method, with a caveat on shared factors; and which categories make up 95% of the total.
- **Measures credited honestly.** A list of measures (homes retrofitted, a cleaner grid, bus lanes, landfill gas captured) is ranked as the usual abatement cost curve ranks them. Beside each measure's abatement alone stand what it abates in the curve's order and its Shapley value over every order. Two measures on one source can no longer both be credited with what only one of them can save. A proof that any order credits a measure within a fixed interval comes with them, and is checked.
- **The way to a target.** With activities growing at declared rates and each measure ramping in over its years, the years ahead show the gap to a declared reduction and the cumulative against a budget.

- **A city's solid waste and SDG 11.6.1** ([ADR 0148](../decisions/0148-municipal-solid-waste-and-sdg-11-6-1.md)). A city's municipal solid waste is accounted for after UN-Habitat's Waste Wise Cities Tool. Households weighed in a survey by income stratum and area give what the city generates, with its interval and per person by stratum. Each facility stands on a ladder of control by the criteria it meets. A disposal site weighed at its gate counts recovery's residues once, and residues declared beyond what a gate weighed are found. SDG 11.6.1, the share managed in controlled facilities, comes with the survey's interval carried through.

The Urban Studio's *Materials and flows* brings the three tools with an invented town of 320,000 and an invented city of 100,000 households. There, seven measures abate 0.85 Mt where the usual curve would claim 0.87, and a 40% cut by 2030 is missed by 0.46 Mt once its activities grow.

## 2. Knowledge Programmes, Scorecards, the Finances of Local Governments and Land Value Capture (ADR 0144, ADR 0145, ADR 0146)

Four things that work on cities' development needs every day can now be done with the infrastructure, each after a survey of how it is done in practice ([the survey](../../research/literature/reports/Learning%20finance%20scorecards%20and%20value%20capture.md)).

- **Scorecards scored as their scales allow** ([ADR 0144](../decisions/0144-knowledge-programmes-and-scorecards.md)). A scorecard of a city's resilience or a government's capacity can be declared and scored statement by statement, each score with its evidence. Its parts are aggregated by rules that ordinal levels permit: the weakest part, a declared table, the median, a threshold, a profile. A mean of levels is taken only when the scheme says what its levels are worth, and a comparison of such means is shown not to be meaningful when re-coding the levels would reverse it. The weakest statements come out as needs, with their evidence.
- **A programme of learning, from needs to results.** A cohort of cities can be followed from their needs (their scorecards' weaknesses among them) to the activities that reached them, the actions they planned and how fast those were completed, and the results each reached at a sufficient grade of evidence. A claim that the programme contributed to a result is substantiated only when every step of it is evidenced and every other explanation is addressed. Formats are compared by what they were shown to contribute for what they cost.
- **The finances of a local government** ([ADR 0145](../decisions/0145-the-finances-of-local-governments.md)). A city's accounts can be read into ratios against thresholds it declares, projected years ahead with its loans, and asked how much more it could borrow while keeping the coverage of its debt service above a floor. The local government sectors of 28 European countries can be followed from Eurostat's accounts, with a fan of their debt ahead.
- **What a project could take back from the land it raises** ([ADR 0146](../decisions/0146-land-value-capture-and-readjustment.md)). Given the values of the parcels a project touches with and without it, each instrument (a betterment levy, development rights sold by a formula or at auction, tax increment, a charge on new floor area) shows what it would raise, from whom and when, the share of the project's cost it recovers and the share of the rise in value it takes, kept apart since the literature calls both "the capture rate". Tax increment shows what it takes from the other governments' revenue, and a parcel charged more than its rise is named. A land readjustment shows whether its areas balance, what share of their land the owners give, whether they keep their value, whether the sale of reserve land pays for the works, and which new plot each owner receives, with the money that evens out what each is due. On an invented tram line, four instruments together recover 77% of the cost and take 82% of the rise, and one parcel is charged 900 on a rise of 700 by a levy and a sale of rights together.

The Urban Studio's new plug-in *Programmes and public finance* brings all four, with a template on an invented cohort of three cities.

## 1. The Generative Ecology of Projects Corrected after a Reading of Its Papers (ideas)

Two papers were drafted from the design of the generative ecology of projects, and an independent reader of each, a session given the files with the drafting session's conclusions withheld, reported errors. All 33 distinct errors were confirmed at their sources, and 13 of them lay in the committed design record, which is corrected here.

- **A measure that measured something else.** The first prototype had measured the change of a genome's text by a library's similarity ratio, which is erratic on texts of this length. It now measures the share of words kept in order and counts the numerals changed. Read so, genomes told to rewrite every organ kept most of their words and changed most of their numbers: the copier followed its instruction only in part, and the lineages' own notes said otherwise. `p1_heredity.py remeasure` recomputes this without calling a model.
- **Formal statements made exact.** The fourth part of the first proposition now concerns the joint law of observations, with the hypothesis that earlier observations keep their laws, and a counterexample shows why. The corollary on rates by part, the caveat on coordinated changes, the bound on a population of foci and the model of reseeding say only what they prove.
- **The record says what was kept and when.** Only the counts of the chains of reseeding were kept, so what their genomes stated is an inference. The third unit of the first case study was added after the first two were evaluated. Its figures follow the plan's contract as corrected in one tolerance, with the plan's own figures given beside them.
- **A fourth reading recorded.** The record of the case studies gains a section on the reading of the papers. All 131 bound sentences still agree with the results.
- **Headings as noun phrases.** Headings, table headers and figure labels that opened with a question word are restated, and three figures are relabelled.

The design is in [the ideas](../ideas/2026-11-20-a-generative-ecology-of-projects.md), and the idea of the debt of generativity in [its note](../ideas/2026-11-21-the-debt-of-generativity.md).
