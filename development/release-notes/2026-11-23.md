# Release Notes: 23 November 2026

## 6. What a Region's Economy Is Made Of, and How It Changed (ADR 0166)

The Urban Studio gains a plug-in, *A region's economy* ([ADR 0166](../decisions/0166-the-structure-of-regional-economies.md)), answering the question of what economics remained for urban science with the one most directly about cities and regions: how their economies are built and how they changed.

- **Shift-share.** Each region's change of employment split into what its country's growth, its mix of industries and its own departure from its industries' national growth account for.
- **Variety.** How varied a region's employment is, split exactly into the variety among unrelated sections and within related ones.
- **Where it could go next.** The industries a region does not hold that lie nearest those it does, and a test of whether that nearness told which industries regions actually took up.
- **Complexity, read honestly.** The economic complexity index is shown as what it is, a region's position along the strongest division of the industries; among Europe's regions it places manufacturing regions high and capitals of services low, so it is not called sophistication.

On 188 European regions between 2011 and 2019, nearness to what a region already did told which industries it took up, at an area under the curve of 0.659, and which it kept, at 0.627. Prague's employment grew by 133,600, half of it beyond what its industries did across Czechia. Two faults were found and mended while building: an economy that falls apart into separate groups was given an index that only named the groups, and is now refused one; and cells Eurostat withheld in one year only appeared as change, and are now left out at both dates. The general methods went first into nomos and tyche. Pictures are in the gallery's §55.

## 5. How Populations Revise What They Do

The second piece of the economics of evolution is in nomos: a population can now be followed as its members revise their strategies, by imitating those who do better, by choosing the best or a noisy best, or by comparing with the average or with each alternative ([nomos's decision 0017](https://github.com/huangwanhong-maker/nomos/blob/main/development/decisions/0017-population-games-and-evolutionary-dynamics.md)). Each dynamic is given both by the rule an agent follows and by the motion of shares it implies, checked to agree; whether a dynamic moves toward better payoffs and rests only at equilibria can be asked at any state; and a population of finitely many agents is simulated exactly, staying within half a percent of the motion of shares at twenty thousand agents. The classic cases are reproduced, among them a strategy always worse than its twin surviving under the Smith dynamic in a fifth of the population. One expectation was wrong and the check now says so: under the Brown–von Neumann–Nash dynamic a population reaches a strict equilibrium only slowly, as one over twice the time.

## 4. How Much of Productivity Growth Is Selection, Learning, Entry and Exit (ADR 0165)

The economics domain gains its first module of the economics of evolution ([ADR 0165](../decisions/0165-the-economics-of-evolution-and-selection-among-firms.md)): a panel of firms at two dates, each with its weight and productivity, can now have its aggregate productivity growth decomposed by every method of the literature, Baily, Hulten and Campbell's, Griliches and Regev's, Foster, Haltiwanger and Krizan's, Melitz and Polanec's and Price's, each written on tyche's new record of populations so that their relations are exact.

- **What each method calls entry.** Where every firm grows alike, Melitz and Polanec credit entrants with nothing, Griliches and Regev with half the growth of their share, Foster, Haltiwanger and Krizan with all of it; the checks hold each to that.
- **Units that should not matter.** Adding a constant to every productivity moves Baily, Hulten and Campbell's entry and exit and leaves every other method's terms alone, which is why the literature moved past them.
- **Selection between industries and within them**, by Price's equation over the firms that survive.

The modules that follow are planned in the decision: industry dynamics, relatedness and complexity, and economies of learning agents, each after its general methods are in the foundations.

## 3. Who Was Selected, What Changed, Who Came and Who Left

The first piece of an economics of evolution is in tyche: how a population's average changed between two times can now be accounted for exactly ([tyche's decision 0035](https://github.com/huangwanhong-maker/tyche/blob/main/development/decisions/0035-census-records-and-the-price-decomposition.md)), at the user's choice of where to begin after the survey. A population is its members with their weights and a measured character, and who descends from whom: firms in an industry, candidate solutions in one of the infrastructure's searches, or lineages in any evolving population. Price's equation splits the change into selection, transmission, entry and exit, with selection split between groups and within them.

- **The reference named.** Entrants and exiters can be judged against the survivors, against the average before, or against the average of the two times, as the literature on productivity does; the choice changes what entry is credited with, so it is always stated.
- **Credit where it came from.** Transmission is credited to the operator that made each member, entry to its source (an outside proposal, a model, a person), exit to its reason, so that a search can say how much of its improvement entered with proposals from outside.
- **Exact.** Every form is an identity, checked in exact fractions on two hundred random populations; the accounting says what happened, never why.

## 2. A Survey of Evolutionary Economics, for the Design of Its Models

A survey in five parts now sets out what evolutionary economics offers an economics built on the infrastructure's evolutionary search (`research/literature/reports/Evolutionary economics models and methods.md`, with its notes): the formal cores (Nelson and Winter, Metcalfe's replicator and Fisher's principle, the productivity decompositions), the agent-based models of industries and economies and how they are validated, evolutionary games and the evolution of institutions with evolutionary policy practice, evolutionary economic geography and economic complexity, and evolution used as a method within economics.

- **One exact core.** Price's equation accounts for any population with a genealogy, whether firms in an industry or candidate solutions in a search; the productivity decompositions, Fisher's principle and the replicator are instances of it.
- **What is checkable.** Fifty-five checks with known answers are gathered from the five parts, from the identity itself to the cycles of Rock–Paper–Scissors and the exponents of urn models.
- **What is weak.** The large simulation models are validated mostly by the signs of stylised facts, with no full estimation or independent replication found; measures of relatedness and complexity predict but do not identify causes; several original models could not be read and are marked as resting on memory until they are.

It proposes an order of building, the model side first, and the decisions it needs before anything is built. Errors found in the sources, and conflicts between them, are listed.

## 1. Visitors' Nights No Longer Counted Twice (ADR 0164)

Nights booked through platforms are no longer added whole to nights in registered establishments ([ADR 0164](../decisions/0164-platform-and-registered-nights-overlap.md)). Eurostat advises against adding them, because a holiday flat booked through a platform may also be counted by the establishment survey, and the visitor stock and every economic tool built on it had added them.

- **A lower and an upper bound.** The platforms cover holiday and other short-stay accommodation only, so the overlap is at most the registered nights in that class. Platform nights are now counted less that largest overlap, so that none is counted twice; the stock is also shown with them added whole, as an upper bound.
- **Every economic tool corrected.** What spending becomes, the region's own table, two regions, jobs, a levy, the design of levies and the losses when visitors stay away all take the lower bound by default, and each lets the overlap be set to any share between.
- **Checked against the earlier figures.** Set to no overlap, every tool gives exactly what it gave before; nothing else changed.

For Prague the paid nights are 22.6 million rather than 24.0 million: visitors' spending becomes 1,725 million euro of value added rather than 1,829 million, supports about 43,700 jobs rather than 46,300, and a levy of 2 euro a night raises 44.6 million rather than 47.2 million. In Berlin and Venice every platform night could already be a registered one, so their lower bound is the registered nights alone. The gallery's pictures of §54 are retaken with the corrected figures, and the earlier records keep theirs, each pointing to this one.
