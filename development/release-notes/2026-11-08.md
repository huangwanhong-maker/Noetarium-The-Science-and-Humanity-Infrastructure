# Release Notes: 8 November 2026

## 1. How Each Street Stands in the Whole, and What It Explains of Melbourne's Walkers

In this update the Urban Science Workbench measures how each street stands in its network, and tests that against Melbourne's counted walkers ([ADR 0084](../decisions/0084-the-configuration-of-streets.md)).

- **A new layer, anywhere.** Pick a place: its streets within 1.5 km are coloured by their choice (how often they lie on the least paths between all others) or their integration (how near they lie to all others), Space Syntax's measures, at an angular, metric or topological cost and a radius. In Melbourne, the City's counters stand beside them with how far the measures go with the counts.
- **What it found.** Across Melbourne's 87 counters, the shape of the streets explains almost none of where walkers are counted, and neither does the floor space around them, much. The walkers' model explains the hours of the day well and the differences between places no better. What stands at a counter, a station, a mall or the river, matters most. The study is in `research/experiments/2026-11-08-street-configuration`.
- **Grammars that answer for the whole.** Each rule of a street grammar now says how much the streets it joins carry of the whole's routes, and a sampled network is scored by its configuration. Melbourne's context-sensitive grammar keeps the city's choice but builds a less connected whole.
- **Underneath.** morphe holds closeness and betweenness within a radius, held to networkx and to brute force; urban science reads Space Syntax's measures through it. The gallery's §21 shows it.

## 2. Geometric Syntax: Grammars of Streets and Buildings, and a Ninth Foundation

In this update the infrastructure gains syntaxis, a foundation of grammars whose words are geometric, and the Urban Science Workbench a plug-in, *Geometric syntax*, that reads streets, blocks and buildings by them ([ADR 0083](../decisions/0083-syntaxis-grammars-whose-words-are-geometric.md)). It begins from the user's G³ and carries it to the steps the user named.

- **A grammar of any place's streets.** Pick a place: what repeats in its streets, up to rotation, scale and position, becomes a rule. The derivation plays on the map step by step, a column standing where each rule was applied, and replays every segment exactly. Another place can be parsed by the same grammar, and streets that were never drawn can be sampled from it. Sampled freely, it is a probabilistic grammar; within its context, it is context-sensitive and never crosses what is drawn.
- **Blocks and buildings as words of triangles.** Each block or footprint is cut into triangles that depend on its shape alone, and each triangle is a letter by its angles. The page gives the alphabet, how much a neighbour tells about a letter, and a patch grown from the grammar of their gluing. In Melbourne's centre a neighbour tells 1.5 bits of a block's 3.1, and 0.8 of a building's 3.3.
- **The syntax analyser.** Grammars learned from self-similar structures and played back; a turned, enlarged and moved copy parsed by them, wholly; triangles rewritten by Pachner's, the midpoint's and Sierpinski's moves, their derivation recovered from the final triangles alone. Every grammar, derivation and parse can be saved.
- **Underneath.** syntaxis holds all of it for any science, with a theory note of proofs and 70 checks. physis now cuts any polygon into its constrained Delaunay triangles, and urban science and architecture have `syntax` modules for streets and footprints. The gallery's §20 shows it.

## 3. peira, a Foundation of Designs and the Mechanisms of Observation

In this update the infrastructure gains an eighth foundation, peira, for the methods that experiments, surveys, clinical studies, psychology and the social sciences share ([ADR 0082](../decisions/0082-peira-designs-and-the-mechanisms-of-observation.md)).

- **One structure.** Experiments, samples, cohorts and missing values are all full data hidden in part by a mechanism. peira asks what can still be learnt through it, and its theory note gives the proofs.
- **What one can do now.**
  - Design an experiment and its power.
  - Estimate and test effects from the randomisation alone.
  - Weight samples and treatment arms by the probability of being seen, with the doubly robust estimator.
  - Estimate from stratified and clustered surveys, and rake their weights.
  - Analyse survival with Kaplan–Meier, the log-rank test, Cox's model and competing risks.
  - Pool multiple imputations of missing values.
- **How it is checked.** Wherever a design is small, every assignment or sample is enumerated and the results hold exactly. Survival agrees with statsmodels and lifelines on Rossi's recidivism study to about 10⁻¹⁴. 89 checks.
- **What comes next.** Differences in differences and instrumental variables move from nomos to peira, with regression discontinuity, synthetic control and causal graphs; then cohorts, meta-analysis and sensitivity analysis.

## 4. The Studio

In this update the Urban Science Workbench gains the Studio, a page of its own for making figures with live code ([ADR 0081](../decisions/0081-the-studio.md)). It is an initial implementation for feedback. See the [gallery's §19](../../docs/gallery.md).

- **A figure as a canvas.** A project is one canvas of frames, each placed and sized by hand: the 3D world with its own globe, camera and clock, a 2D map following it, charts, drawings and text. The whole canvas is exported as one image at twice its size, with its charts as vector.
- **Scripts in three languages, while it runs.** Write a script in JavaScript, Python or C++ while the project plays. Save it, and it is loaded (compiled, for C++) and joins the next tick. Each script can be paused, stopped, started again or deleted. A version that does not compile has its line marked, and the version before keeps running.
- **What a script knows and draws.** A script is given the view's range in space and time: where it looks, the ground it sees, its heading, its metres to a pixel, the moment, and every frame's size. It draws on the world, or into any frame as a chart or a drawing of its own. The example north arrow and scale bar is such a script.
- **Shared content in a sound order.** Scripts share named resources. The order they run in follows from what each reads and writes. Rules that would make the result depend on chance are refused with a reason: two writers without a merge, or a loop with no delay.
- **The world's data, and Explore's views.** The data library's records go on the world as layers that scripts can read. A chart can follow any resource with no code at all. Explore gains two keys only: *capture* keeps the view for a project, and *studio* opens the Studio.
