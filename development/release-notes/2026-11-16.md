# Release Notes: 16 November 2026

## 2. Evidence in Space and Time, in the Urban Studio

The Urban Studio has a fifth plug-in, *Evidence in space and time* ([ADR 0123](../decisions/0123-evidence-in-space-and-time.md)): an archive of reports about places and times, kept, weighed and acted on.
- **The place is the page.** The place's streets and buildings are drawn from the world's map data. Each claim (that something holds within a radius of a point, during an interval) is a disc, toned by how far its evidence moves it, lavender against and rust for. Each report is a glyph at its place, by kind and by what it says. A claim or a report opens where it stands: its standing, what bears on it and how much, who says it and what it rests on. A claim of your own is placed by a click.
- **Kept, and checked whenever used.** Every file is taken in with the SHA-256 of its bytes and a chain of custody, and handed over from the page. If a file changes on disk, the next use finds it, and the reports resting on it stop counting, with the reason given.
- **Weighed as the reports allow.** A report known within a radius and a span bears on a claim only as far as it may be about the claim's place and time. Reports that repeat one registration, such as one household complaining five times, count once, and the page says what counting them apart would have given. A claim says whether its reports are of events of their own, whose force adds up, or of one event, which they can only settle.
- **What to do.** You declare the acts with their worths, a way of looking first with what it finds and costs, and a budget. Each claim then gets its act, and the places worth looking at first are chosen together, with nothing better possible within the budget.
- **Three tools** answer for boards and the Urban Science Workbench too: the claims weighed and decided as a table on the map; what a kind of report is worth, counted against a reference with its intervals; and how the conclusions fared against what was looked at afterwards.

The example takes two of New York City's public records for the East Village and the Lower East Side, the health department's rodent inspections and 311's rodent complaints, April to June 2024. A complaint was 6.8 times as likely where inspectors found rats as where they passed the lot. Of 399 claims, 80 are established and 3 refuted. Thirty-seven surveys are worth making first within the declared budget. Against the inspections of the quarter after, the archive predicts better than the prior alone (Brier 0.234 against 0.249). It is well calibrated in the middle and overconfident at the ends. The inspector's accuracy, the worths and the budget are the example's assumptions; none of it is advice.

Beneath the plug-in:
- tekmerion weighs reports that share an origin and evidence located in space and time;
- tyche chooses which tests to run before many decisions within one budget, and gives Wilson's and the exact intervals for a proportion;
- calx solves the knapsack with a certificate;
- the API composes them for any work;
- a plug-in of the studio host can now bring a server side and views of its own;
- the visualization layer draws evidence at its places.

Run `python data/acquisition/recipe.py applications/urban-studio/recipe.json`, then `python applications/urban-studio/serve.py --open`, and begin a project from *Rats in the East Village and the Lower East Side*. The record of the example is [research/experiments/2026-11-16-evidence-in-space-and-time](../../research/experiments/2026-11-16-evidence-in-space-and-time/README.md); the pictures are in the gallery's §43.

## 1. The Policy Studio: A Question of Policy Worked from Its Theory of Change to the Decision

A new work for evidence-based policy making ([ADR 0122](../decisions/0122-the-policy-studio.md)). A project is a question: does an outcome follow from an intervention?
- **The theory of change** is drawn from the intervention to the outcome. Each causal claim shows how far it is shown: a full line with its grade in words and pips, or a dashed one. The strongest path is heavier, its weakest link is named, and so is a gap that every path passes through. A state or a claim opens where it stands, with what it rests on and what it needs in the place.
- **The evidence for a claim** is a forest of its studies, pooled, beside the ladder of its grade under a scheme you declare, step by step. A study opens with what the others pool to without it. Studies can be pasted as a table.
- **Here.** That it worked somewhere is one premise of three. The other two are about the place, and each is shown as stated, against, or not stated. Where the studies name strata, the effect is carried to the place through them.
- **The options** are ledgers: an effect, the worth of a unit, whom it reaches, costs year by year.
- **The decision** is to adopt, reject or study first, each with what it is worth in expectation, the study worth running drawn over every size, and whether the decision holds over bounds on a unit's worth.
- **A model that proposes.** A language model can draft a theory's states and claims from a description. It is asked for no effect and no grade. Its draft carries no evidence, so every claim in it stands as not shown, and it becomes the theory only when a person takes it.

The first example reads the thirteen real trials of the BCG vaccine against tuberculosis (Colditz et al. 1994) for a district that is invented, with invented costs and worths. The trials pool to a risk ratio of 0.489 and are graded low under the example scheme. The question asked of the *programme* is not yet shown, because the trials are of vaccination and nothing shows that the programme reaches the newborn. At the district's latitude the ratio to expect is 0.688. On the example's costs the decision is to adopt, and it turns on the worth of a case averted.

A second example, invented throughout, has a theory of two paths and a decision close enough that a study of some eighteen thousand pupils is worth running first.

The decision is by expected worth and consults no threshold of significance. Every scheme, cost, rate and worth is a declaration; the grading scheme is an example written after GRADE and is not GRADE's own text. None of it is advice on any policy.

Beneath the work:
- the API works a whole question in one request, each part answered or said why it cannot be, and reads a pasted table of studies;
- the visualization layer draws theories of change, forests of studies, and the worth of a study and of a unit.

Run `python applications/policy-studio/engine.py build` once, then `python applications/policy-studio/serve.py --open`.
