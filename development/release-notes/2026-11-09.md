# Release Notes: 9 November 2026

## 1. Who Can Reach Care, Schools and Shops, and Who Is Left Without

In this update the Urban Science Workbench answers, anywhere on Earth, who can reach the services they need and who cannot ([ADR 0086](../decisions/0086-access-to-services-and-unmet-need.md)).

- **Pick a place, choose a service and a way of travelling.** Choose care, schools or shops, on foot or by road, within 10, 15 or 30 minutes. The people about the place come from the global population grid, about 1 km a cell. The services are the buildings the map calls hospitals, clinics, schools or shops, each offering its floor space. The roads and paths are those drawn there.
- **What one sees.** Each cell is coloured by its share of what lies within reach, a service's space divided among everyone who can reach it. The cells lie on the ground and follow the hills, and those with nothing within reach are hatched. A card gives the share of people within reach, the people with nothing, those far below the average, how unequal access is, and the share of rural people within 2 km of a main road.
- **What it found.**
  - In Melbourne's centre, 55% of people reach mapped care within 15 minutes on foot, and everyone does by road.
  - In Freetown, 37% reach it on foot, with a thirty-fifth of Melbourne's mapped floor space of care per person.
  - In the hills about Dhulikhel, 99% reach care within half an hour by road.
  - What these numbers show first is how much of each city's care is on the map. Six pictures are in the gallery.
- **Works better.** A place is no longer stranded on a stub of footpath when travelling by road, and people beyond the area loaded are counted as left out, not as without.

## 2. Quasi-Experiments, Causal Graphs and Cohorts in peira; Demography on Them

In this update peira takes its second stage and begins its third, and demography becomes the first domain built on it ([ADR 0085](../decisions/0085-demography-on-peira.md)).

- **Methods moved where they belong.** Panels went from nomos to tyche, and instruments and differences in differences to peira, so that a clinical trial or an urban study reaches them without the social sciences' library. The economics toolbox in the Economy Atlas and the Urban Science Workbench reads them from their new places.
- **Effects when assignment was not controlled.**
  - Treatments adopted at different times, estimated group by group and period by period, where the usual two-way regression misleads (shown: 1.94 against a true 2.43).
  - The effect on compliers with an instrument.
  - Regression discontinuity, sharp and fuzzy.
  - Synthetic control, with placebos.
- **Causal graphs.** Which independences a graph implies and which sets identify an effect, held to linear models in 4,032 cases.
- **Cohorts.** Person-time on the Lexis diagram, exact rates and standardisation.
- **Demography.** Life tables, reproduction rates, projection by the Leslie matrix and the stable population it tends to, each checked against what theory says exactly. For example, a population all in one age group takes the stable structure and grows by its dominant eigenvalue.
