# Figures for Publication

*Seed (2026-11-18). The figures of [the framework from evidence to action](../../2026-11-18-from-evidence-to-action.md), drawn for papers: no titles or explanatory prose inside a figure, a white ground, and a key where one is needed. Each figure is an SVG (the source), a PDF (vector, the page sized to the figure, Source Serif 4 embedded) and a PNG at twice its size.*

The PDFs and PNGs are made from the SVGs by headless Chrome, with Source Serif 4 loaded from Google Fonts. The SVGs alone fall back to Georgia where the font is not installed.

## 1. Captions

**Figure 1. The loop from evidence to action across the three registers.** The condition of the system and the realised action are referred to and not established (ontological register). Evidence is kept with the mechanism that produced it (manifestational register). Everything concluded, evaluated or decided is a relational fact. Interpretation reads the terms of the evidence and the constitution's declared readings. Estimation yields a belief $b \in TS$. Evaluation applies operators $J_k(b; \iota, \sigma)$ in parallel; features, needs, problems, risks and assets are examples of what they yield, not elements of the loop. Policy making revises the policy $\pi$, which maps beliefs to interventions or inquiries. Proposals and transferred practice enter as declared boundary inputs.

**Figure 2. Three nested loops.** Each loop bounds the loop inside it and judges what that loop produces:

- the operational loop applies the policy, one turn per decision;
- the collective-choice loop varies, selects and retains policies, one turn per policy version (knowledge production);
- the constitutional loop revises the spaces, readings, operators, standards, kinds of fact and rules of change, one turn per framework version (governance).

Records pass between versions of the schema by the adjunction $\Sigma \dashv \Delta$, and practices pass between relational systems by transfer (Figure 6).

**Figure 3. Evaluation.** An evaluation is the value of an operator $J_k$ at the estimated state $b$, given readings $\iota$ of its open terms and, where one applies, a standard $\sigma$ with its holder. The three arguments carry the three uncertainties kept apart: state, meaning and value. Operators act in parallel and are deterministic. Features, needs, problems, risks and assets are examples, declared with their operators, each with its own value space $V_k$ and scale. A statement about an evaluation is meaningful only if it is invariant under the group of its scale. A composite, such as a need computed from a feature, is a single operator. Uptake, a holder's commitment to act on an evaluation, belongs to policy making and is not an evaluation.

**Figure 4. The kinds of fact in the three registers.** Arrows point from a kind to the kind it is a subkind of; the order is thin, with Fact terminal. The kinds of the law domain include into the framework's kinds fully and faithfully and keep their registers; the framework adds the kinds outlined in orchid. Example kinds, declared with their operators, are subkinds of Evaluation and bear no relation to one another. Their inclusion has a left adjoint that reads each as an evaluation. All of this is verified by kategoria (`../schema.cat`).

**Figure 5. One step of the loop as a string diagram in a Markov category, $S \otimes B \to S \otimes B$.** The belief is copied and the policy applied. The action is copied and the state transformed, copied and observed. The belief is then updated. No wire runs from $S$ to $\pi$: the policy depends on the system only through evidence. Wires are coloured by register.

**Figure 6. Transfer between two relational systems as a lens.** A practice $\pi_J$ of system $J$ is carried to system $X$ by two maps: $f$, the interpretation of $X$ in the terms of $J$, and $g$, the realisation of the actions of $J$ in $X$. Then $\pi_X = g \circ \pi_J \circ f$. The transfer is valid when the square commutes within a declared tolerance, a condition that the support factors of the mechanism secure.

**Figure 7. The nine rules of the record as commuting diagrams.** A record is a set-valued functor on the schema, and each rule is an equation of paths; a record that violates a rule is not a functor. kategoria verifies one sample record and refuses seven, each by exactly the rule it violates.

## 3. Conventions Held in Every Figure

- **Formal terms only.** Labels are nouns and notation; no spoken or interrogative phrasing ("realised action", not "what was done").
- **No presupposed space, time or discipline.** Loops are counted in turns, not in units of a clock. Transition is between versions and between relational systems. Examples are declared with their operators, not by disciplines.
- **Registers by colour, kinds by outline.** Moss marks the ontological, sand the manifestational, and orchid, lilac and ink the relational. A dashed outline marks what is referred to and not established, or an example rather than an element.
