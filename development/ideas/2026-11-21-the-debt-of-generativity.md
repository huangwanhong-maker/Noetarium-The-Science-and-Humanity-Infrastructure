## 0. Summary

A person or a project has generativity: it goes on yielding new work. The design of a generative ecology shows one way in which this capacity can be moved. Once the generative materials of a person or a project are initialised, a population of agents that attends to knowledge of the environment can prolong that generativity by evolution, with little or no act of the person. The user names what this may cost: a **debt of generativity**, the growing gap, in history and in relational structure, between the project as it is carried forward and the project of the person who began it.

## 2. A First Reading

### 2.1 Generativity and Its Materials

*Generativity* is taken here as the capacity of a person or a project to go on yielding work that is new, that holds, and that is its own. The *generative materials* of a person or a project are what an evolutionary process can be founded on: in the terms of the design, the genomes of its units, which are the compact texts from which its bodies can be developed again, with its record and its corpus.

### 2.2 Relocation

In the design, a garden is founded on a project's materials, lineages vary and develop them, the environment enters through a declared focus and an evidential chain, and what stands is harvested. Generativity that lay in the continuing act of a person then lies, in part, in a process that runs on that person's materials. The relocation is *strategic* in that it is chosen: what is moved to the process, what is kept as acts reserved to the person, and at what rate.

### 2.3 The Debt of Generativity

**Debt of generativity.** The gap, in history and in relational structure, between a project as an evolutionary process has carried it forward and the project as its person originated it and can still account for. It grows as the process prolongs the project's generativity beyond what the person has taken part in.

The user's words measure the gap from the person's original project, and say that it increases gradually. The clause "and can still account for", which lets the point of reference move forward as the person takes up what the process has done, is the assistant's amendment. Without it a debt can only grow; with it a debt can be serviced. Which is meant is for the user to say.

The definition has two components, after the user's words. The gap *in history* is the part of the project's becoming in which the person took no part: variations made, claims warranted and refuted, selections that happened among lineages. The gap *in relational structure* is the part of the relations in which the project now stands that the person did not form: what its statements rest on, whom it serves, which sources and witnesses it admits, what it has promised.

### 2.4 The Sense of the Word Debt

Something is received at once, a project that keeps yielding, against an obligation that grows with it. The obligation is serviced by the person's taking part: reading what stands, restating what is accepted, asking the next question. Left unserviced it compounds, since later work comes to rest on work that the person never took up. Its default is a project that is the person's in name while its history and its relations belong to the process. The word follows technical debt in software, and the analogy has limits that the paper is to state: a debt of this kind has no creditor, and part of it may be an inheritance that a successor takes up.

### 2.5 In the Registers of the User's Framework

In the user's Generative Relational framework a project is a relational object: it emerges from couplings, persists, and has effects, and its manifestations are what observers index. The debt of generativity is then a divergence in the relational register that the manifestational register can hide. A project carried forward by evolution may show the same name, the same style and the same passing checks, while the couplings from which it now emerges are other than the person's. The framework's own caution applies: equal manifestations do not show equal structures.

## 3. Bearing of the Design and Its Studies

Several parts of the design are provisions against this debt, though it was unnamed when they were made. The acts reserved to the inquirer in the fourth case (asking, reading, accepting, signing) are acts of servicing. The rule that a statement's standing is kept apart from its acceptance makes the record a ledger: what stands unaccepted is what the person has yet to take up. Acceptance by restatement is repayment in the strict sense, since what the person has restated has entered the person's own history.

The studies show, at small scale, ways in which such a debt would accrue. In the first prototype an organ that told the copier to rewrite every organ and to change two or three policies was followed only in part: after six generations the genomes kept 61 to 82 percent of the founder's words while 7 to 9 of its ten numbers had changed, and eight of the eighteen notes of those lineages said that the organs had been reworded where most were word for word the parent's. In the case studies, a unit that follows one variant of a classification among several was developed from its identity alone in other variants, none of them the repository's, and a unit of published formulas departed from the repository's conventions in a minority of its developments: other names for the classes of daylight, an azimuth mirrored east for west, a class decided otherwise at its boundary. And the assistant's own record of the prototypes, written by hand and committed, was wrong in six of fifty-nine sentences, which no person had checked. Two later readings then found errors of another kind in the record of the case studies itself, in what its counts were said to mean; a second reader of the same model found them, and the first writer had not. Readers of the drafted papers then found that the first record of the first prototype had measured the change of its texts by a measure that measured nothing, though every number had been bound to its data.

Two formal results of the design bear on how it accrues. Selection acts only through what payers can tell apart (Proposition 1), so that what the person values and no payer observes is under no selection. And in the model of reseeding without an anchor, a rule that the copying model does not supply unasked is lost in the end (Section 4.4), which is a movement of the project's particular history towards a common one.

## 4. Questions for the User

1. Whether the debt is to be understood as a cost only, or also as credit: a person may found more than a life can carry, and intend successors.
2. Whether it is owed by the person, by the project, or by those who later rely on it.
3. Whether it can be measured from the record of a garden: for instance the share of the present state whose lineage passes through no act of the person, and the share of its standing relations that the person has never read.
4. How it relates to generative justice, that is, to whom the value of prolonged generativity returns.
5. Whether "strategic" names the person's strategy, an institution's, or both.
