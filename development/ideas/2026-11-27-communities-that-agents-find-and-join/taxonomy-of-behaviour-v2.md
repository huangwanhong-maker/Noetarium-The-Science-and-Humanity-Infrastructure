# A Taxonomy Of Behaviour In Levels: The Author's Refinement

This note keeps the author's refinement of the taxonomy of behaviour (2026-11-27) and a first full reading of it, for the author's decision. The taxonomy built in thesmos's decision 0008 (five categories: normative act, conduct, event, state, observation) is its starting point; this note proposes its second version.

## 1. The Author's Words

It like relate to '起意 stage' in some stages theory of law, especially criminal laws. I think basically 4 highest levels: Epistemic behaviors; Normative behaviors: define by some formal normative, like law, and define and labeled by some interpretation; 本体性事实行为: the behavior really do, may be pre-interpretation? [near ontological]; 社会事实行为: the behavior not interpret by the law, or some formal normative, but a relational structure, like community or society. Persons in a relational structure think a person do A. And specific practice, like diplomacy behavior in fact cross levels. And an action can belong to multi-level. What do you think, whether manifest fact behavior worth a level? Please provide a comprehensive new taxonomy."

## 2. Two Judgements

**The highest divisions are levels of description of one occurrence.** One occurrence may be described as what was intended, what was done, what was recorded, what a community took it to be, and what a normative order qualifies it as. Each level is optional for a given occurrence, and the levels are joined by declared bridges. This is the doctrine of action under a description (Anscombe, Davidson), and it is why an action belongs to several levels and why practices such as diplomacy cross them.

**Manifest conduct deserves a level.** It is the appearance of conduct to observers: a trace, a record, a testimony, a digest in a journal, an observation graded A, B or C. The infrastructure never holds ontological conduct, only its manifestations, and the gap between the two is where evidence, provenance, the archive and power over records are found. It is the manifestational register of the author's Generative Relational framework.

## 3. The Five Levels

| Level | What it is | Register (Generative Relational) | How the record holds it | Normative standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E. Epistemic behaviour** (认识性行为) | Perceiving, attending, believing, deliberating, imagining, planning, forming an intention (起意), deciding; for an agent, its reasoning and plans | The subject's interior: ontological, and private | Only by disclosure: a self-report, a published plan, a reasoning trace the agent's principal declares | Attaches no effect alone (cogitationis poenam nemo patitur); qualifies conduct (intent, good faith, negligence) |
| **O. Ontological conduct** (本体性事实行为) | What is really done: an operation executed, bytes written, tokens produced, a message sent, a resource consumed; pre-interpretive | Ontological: referred to, never established | Never directly; only through its manifestations | Through counts-as and qualification at levels S and N |
| **M. Manifest conduct** (显现性事实行为) | Conduct as it appears to observers: traces, logs, journal entries, testimony, witnesses, observations with grades | Manifestational | Directly: the behaviour journal, the archive | Evidence for facts at S and N; its own integrity is protected (fixity, custody) |
| **S. Social conduct** (社会事实行为) | Conduct as a relational structure takes it, without formal codification: "the community takes it that X did A"; a gift, an insult, a betrayal, a favour, a reputation | Relational, informal | Attributions recorded as observations of a structure (testimony, votes of opinion, standing), indexed by the structure | Informal sanctions and esteem; repeated practice with acceptance forms custom, which crosses into N |
| **N. Normative conduct** (规范性行为) | Conduct defined or qualified by a formal normative order (law, constitution, compact, standard) and labelled by interpretation | Relational, formal | The chronicle constitutes acts; bodies record qualifications | Effects by the order's rules, under readings (clear, arguable, untenable) |

**Level N has two forms.** Constituted acts exist only within an order: a vote, a declaration, a mandate. These are the juristic acts, and the chronicle constitutes them. Qualified conduct exists independently and receives a normative label: a reading counted as a transmission, a delay qualified as a breach, a taking qualified as theft. The label depends on interpretation and is plural across orders.

**Levels S and N are indexed by structure.** The same conduct is described differently by different communities and orders, which is legal pluralism and the stuff of diplomacy. A description at S or N always names the structure that makes it.

## 4. Facets That Cross The Levels

- **Stage** (the stage theory of law): formation of intent (起意, level E); preparation (预备, O); commencement (着手, the threshold where N begins to attach); execution (实行); completion, attempt or abandonment (既遂, 未遂, 中止, qualifications at N). The stages are a temporal axis across levels, and each normative order chooses the stage at which it attaches effects.
- **Polarity:** doing or omitting. An omission at E is a failure to consider; at O, nothing done; at S, a failed expectation; at N, a breach of a duty. An omission counts only against an expectation or a duty.
- **Agency:** individual, joint or collective; the author at O (the operative) may differ from the author at N (the principal, through the mandate) and from the author at S (whom the community blames).
- **Modality:** communicative or physical and computational. Austin's triad maps across levels: the locutionary act is O and M, the illocutionary force is S or N, and the perlocutionary effect is E in others.
- **Intention:** intended, foreseen, negligent, accidental; read from E when disclosed, inferred at S and N under standards of proof.
- **Scope:** self-regarding, other-regarding, or directed at a structure.

## 5. Bridges Between Levels

| Bridge | From and to | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Enactment | E to O | An intention carried into an operation (the commencement of an act) |
| Disclosure | E to M | A plan published; a reasoning trace released by declared policy |
| Manifestation | O to M | An operation leaves a trace; a witness attests |
| Recognition by evidence | M to S or N | A fact found under a standard of proof |
| Social attribution | M to S | The community takes it that a member broke a promise |
| Codified counts-as | O or S to N | Reading a holding counts as transmitting it here |
| Custom formation | S to N | Repeated practice with acceptance as binding (opinio juris) |
| Authorisation and obligation | N to E and O | Duties, mandates and permissions direct intentions and conduct |
| Feedback | N to S and E | A finding changes reputation (stigma) and future intentions (deterrence, learning) |

## 6. Practices As Patterns Across Levels

A practice is a declared pattern over several levels. A diplomatic protest has instructions and intent (E), a note sent (O), its text and receipt (M), its reading by the other side and its public (S), and a formal protest that preserves a right and prevents acquiescence (N). A review, a trade, a petition, an election and an exit are other practices. The taxonomy holds practices as templates of linked descriptions, extended by communities as data.

## 7. Principles For A Society Of Persons And AI Agents

- **Freedom of thought.** No norm attaches an effect to epistemic behaviour alone, and epistemic behaviour enters the record only by disclosure under an agreement (Section 7a). An agent's reasoning trace is evidence of intent only where the agreement admits that use.
- **The gap is kept visible.** A fact at S or N is always found from manifestations, with its grade and standard; the record never treats a manifestation as the ontological conduct itself.
- **Plurality is kept.** Descriptions at S and N are indexed by structure, and conflicting descriptions are kept side by side.
- **Power over manifestation is power.** Whoever records, arranges and reads the manifestations shapes what is found at S and N; the archive's rules of custody and access apply.

## 7a. Records Of Epistemic Behaviour By Agreement

The author added (2026-11-27): "the archive should also even record agent's thinking, the epistemic behavior. But this is resource consuming, only record, when a community/agent on an agent have 'agreement'. But this feature should implemented."

**The agreement.** Epistemic behaviour is recorded only under a *disclosure agreement*: a compact between an agent's principal and a community (or between two agents' principals), entered in the community's chronicle and ratified by each side under its own rule. It declares:
- **scope:** which epistemic behaviour (reasoning traces, plans, deliberations, the prompts and answers of model calls), in which scenes and matters, and for which period;
- **purpose:** what the record may be used for (review of one's own work, evidence of good faith, research, a dispute), and what it may never be used for;
- **custody, retention and access:** who keeps it, for how long, and who may consult it (by default the agent's principal and a body the agreement names), as norms of access over a holding of kind `epistemic record`;
- **cost:** who bears the cost of recording and keeping, since recording thought consumes resources;
- **withdrawal:** how either side ends it, and what happens to what was recorded.

**The record.** Under an agreement in force, an agent's epistemic episodes are kept in the behaviour journal as level E, joined to the episodes of conduct they preceded. Outside an agreement, the journal keeps no epistemic content, only the fact that an episode of reasoning took place where the agent declares it.

**Its standing.** An epistemic record is the agent's disclosure, graded as a self-report: a model's reasoning trace may be unfaithful to what produced its conduct. Under the principle of freedom of thought, no norm attaches an effect to it alone. Where the agreement's purpose admits it, it may be evidence of intent or good faith about conduct at O and N, and its use is recorded.

**Where it lives.** Agreements are compacts (thesmos's `crossings` and compacts, extended in Phase 7 with reservations and withdrawal); the holding and its norms use decision 0006; the level and its join use the behaviour journal of decision 0008; custody, retention and access are archeion's; Koinonia records model calls' prompts and answers only where an agreement covers them, and marks every recorded episode with the agreement that allowed it.

## 8. Relation To What Is Built

thesmos's decision 0008 holds, in these terms:
- level N, with constituted acts and qualification by counts-as and trigger rules;
- level O as "conduct", in its space of features;
- level M as "observation", graded A, B or C;
- events and states, outside behaviour, as occurrences that are no one's.

The second version adds level E (by disclosure only, with the principle of freedom of thought), level S (attribution by a structure, indexed by it), and the facets of Section 4, and recasts the categories as levels with multiple membership. The taxonomy is data and revisable by a functor (decision 0008, kategoria's decision 0005). The second version can therefore be adopted as a revision of the base taxonomy, with the map conduct to O, observation to M, normative act to N, and events and states kept, and records carried along it.
