## 1. Context

Koinonia's cases so far (ADR 0169, ADR 0170) had communities check a catalogue and keep accounts of the services between them. They showed the order of a society working and its instruments of circulation, on a task divided into identical items. A real task is divided unequally and in stages, and its quality is judged by someone: who judges and how is itself a political question, and with no centre the question of whether to organise at all comes first.

## 2. Decision

1. **No organisation is given.** The case `book` (`koinonia/bookcase.py`) begins with four communities of different orders (a commons of data, a guild of analysts, a cooperative of writers, a forum of the affected) and no league. The forum convenes a conference (`koinonia/partnership.py`), whose envoys propose and vote on charters for an organisation: its coordination (a standing secretariat or a chair rotating among the members), the rule by which it admits a tool to the shared infrastructure, who reviews at first, and the rule for adopting the book. A charter is adopted by every community's consent counted by community, or after three rounds without one by the declared fallback of two thirds; each community then ratifies it by its own constitutional rule through the voices of members of its deciding body, read from thesmos's count; the organisation is founded among those that ratified, on a constitution built from the charter and admitted by thesmos.
2. **The organisation's work, stage by stage**, each stage held in the community whose work it is, by Koinonia's cycle of request, offer, delivery and acceptance: the commons chooses and checks each section's series; the guild plans, runs and replicates its analyses (two deliveries of different origins); the cooperative drafts and revises; the organisation's reviewers score each version on a declared scorecard and vote, and its adoption is read from thesmos's count under the decision rule in force; an auditor checks a share of the numbers of every adopted version and reopens what is wrong; envoys draft the proposals; the council adopts the book.
3. **Who reviews and how is amendable.** The regime of evaluation is a compact of the organisation (the secretariat's or chair's editor; peers of the guild of other origins; a panel of every member; the forum), and the decision "adopt section" a rule (any one, a majority, two thirds, unanimity of the reviewers' origins); envoys may propose to change either when the record gives them a reason. A reviewer's seat is open and its mandate comes from who lent it, so that its ballot is borne by that party and counting by origin tells apart the reviewers of one community; the secretary assigns who reviews each version. The unit of count stays origin: under thesmos a ballot is counted for its bearer, and under the other units some reviewers' ballots would not be counted at all. A reviewer of the origin that analysed, replicated or wrote a section is recused; where every reviewer a regime names is recused, a panel reviews instead, recorded so. A reviewer recommends adopt, minor (adopted once the writer has made the corrections named, without another review, and then audited) or revise; a number rounded from its fact is no fault.
4. **A decision needs its body.** A proposal is carried when it passes under the rule in force among the votes cast and every member of the deciding body has voted, or after four rounds, after which silence is no veto (a declared rule of the case); so for charters, ratifications, the outline and its amendments, tools, the proposals and the book.
5. **The infrastructure extended through the society's politics.** Any working agent may petition, with its delivery, for a tool the bench lacks (a computation over series the shelf holds) or record data the shelf lacks (a gap, which no tool fills); its community's envoy carries a petition for a tool to the council or declines; the council decides by the charter's rule; the guild builds it in a restricted language (a pure function over the shelf's series, run in its own interpreter, its syntax and attributes held to allowlists) and an analyst of another origin checks it on its declared cases before it is admitted.
6. **The instruments.** The data library's `data/products/sdg_shelf.py` (the World Bank's catalogue of 1,498 indicators, the economies and their groups from its API; every indicator's values from the bulk file `WDI_CSV.zip`, split a series a file with the archive's digest, since the API answers a series in tens of seconds; the goals and targets of the 2030 Agenda from the United Nations' SDG API). The API's `science/progress.hpp` and `science.progress`, an engine of its own: nomos's trend to target (its decision 0019, after the United Nations Statistics Division's method), the spread among countries with its decomposition between regions, convergence, breaks and links. Koinonia's `bench.py` presents them to agents as tools whose results carry facts a writer may cite and the auditor check.
7. **The society changed for it.** Agents decide in parallel within a round, their acts gathered in their order; a fault in one agent's decision is recorded and costs it the round; the API's state gives each proposal whether it passes under the decision rule in force, and the decisions in force; the page shows a book being written (the charter, each section's stage and versions, petitions), with a card for each section's versions, reviews and audits.
8. **The typesetter and the record.** `koinonia/typeset.py` sets the adopted book in two columns from the record (the writers' text as written, figures from the results named, the statistical annex from the series accepted, the account of how the book was made); `record_book.py` keeps the run as an archeion bag with the lineage of every number as W3C PROV.

9. **The agents and their models.** About a hundred agents: ten gatherers and sixteen analysts (Claude Sonnet 5.5), thirty writers (six on Claude Opus 5.5 lent by two members of the cooperative, the rest Sonnet), eighteen reviewers (Sonnet), four envoys and the editors (Opus), eight voices (Haiku 5.5), and keepers, secretaries and auditors by rule; decisions within a round taken eight at a time in the run that made the book (the run's `parallel`), the bench's engine kept alive in six processes. The user's budget for the work's models is eighty dollars, the ledger's stop seventy-eight.
10. **A pilot and a reading for error.** A pilot on six sections showed versions a third of the length asked and reviewers marking rounding as fault (twenty-three versions for six sections, all withdrawn); the writers' and reviewers' instructions were changed. An independent reading of the code found thirteen errors and eight risks while the first full run was in its 25th round; that run was stopped and kept, every finding mended (`research/experiments/2026-11-26-koinonia-book/review/reading-for-error.md`), and the case run again from the start.

## 3. Consequences

- **The society organised itself with no centre.** In the run that made the book, each envoy proposed a charter. The commons' was adopted by every community's consent: a chair rotating among the members, tools admitted by two thirds, review by peers, the book by unanimity. Every community ratified it by its own rule, and the partnership was founded in round 4. The council passed nine of sixteen amendments to the outline (30 chapters, 166 sections), allotted 469 pages by the Method of Equal Shares, funding 20 chapters, and in round 62 changed the review to a panel of one reviewer from each member.
- **Its review was stricter than its budget.** 694 reviews recommended revision 537 times. The faults most often major were of inference (435), and most were real. A section needed about three versions; 32 were adopted, 27 withdrawn after their fifth version, and 102 never written, at the user's decision to finish what was in hand within $100. The auditor found 5 numbers wrong in 1,344.
- **The infrastructure was extended through its politics.** Of 275 petitions, 180 named data the World Bank's catalogue lacks; of the 95 for tools, the council admitted 27, of which three were built and found sound and admitted to the bench (a compound rate over a chosen period, a fixed-panel poverty comparison, the pace still required to reach a target), 23 were built and found unsound and one was not yet built when the run ended; six were declined by envoys and 62 never carried.
- **The run ended unfinished.** From round 216 the model provider refused every call for want of credit, so neither the chapter of proposals nor the book was voted on. The book is set as it stands, 170 two-column pages in the layout of a flagship report, the notice of the experiment in front (`docs/reports/2026-11-26-koinonia-book/`). It cost $84.87 in calls ($49.94 writing, $14.40 reviewing); its record is `research/experiments/2026-11-26-koinonia-book/`.
- **Changes from outside the society**, each recorded where it happened and in the book's last annex:
  - by the case's maker: writing by parts; the reviewers' form of faults; trims; reading notes; a rule of re-review; two faults in token limits; the models of writers, reviewers and editors moved to fit the budget;
  - by the user: half length, then finishing what was in hand;
  - by the provider: the end.
- **Not built:**
  - crossings with receipts between the communities' chronicles;
  - a person's review from the page;
  - a second provider of models;
  - data beyond the World Bank's catalogue, which the agents most often asked for.
- **Provisional:** the case's names, the conference's fallback, the rotation of twelve rounds, the scorecard and its rule, the audit's share, the restricted language, the quiet rule.
