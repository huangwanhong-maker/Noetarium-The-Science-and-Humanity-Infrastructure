# ADR 0128: The Indexes of Cities, and Models of Them Across Scales

- **Status:** Accepted, provisionally
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Decided by:** the user, who gave a keyword atlas of urban economics, urban science, market analysis, political economy and resilient infrastructure and asked to "identify all qualitative and quantitative indexes, and make urban science studio can build multi-scale dynamical system model(s) and visualize them on top on them. Also build estimators for some indexes if can, as not all indexes may be presented in the dataset. But if cannot well estimate, them should take up when apply model. Estimators can utilize various models … We may better also survey some papers"; and Claude.

## 1. Context

The atlas holds 4,591 keywords in 30 sections, from microeconomics to Ginza's street management. Most name concepts, methods, instruments or actors; many name, or are measured by, an index: a vacancy rate, a Gini coefficient, a floor area ratio, a net present value, a perceived safety, a stakeholder's salience. The infrastructure already computed some of them (nomos's inequality, concentration, specialisation, accessibility and appraisal; tyche's spatial autocorrelation and diversity; the domains' own), had no list of the rest, no way to say how each behaves from a block to a city, no model whose quantities live on several levels at once, no change of support between zones, and no estimator that says where it should not be believed.

Three surveys were made of the literature and are kept as one report (`research/literature/reports/Indexes of cities across scales.md`, 772 items, 725 verified): multi-scale dynamical models of cities; the estimation of indexes a dataset does not hold; indicator frameworks, qualitative indexes and their dynamics. Their common principles: carry sufficient statistics up the levels and compute indexes from them; allocate down under control totals so that the levels agree; estimate only where an estimator has shown it can, and withhold elsewhere; declare what a record does not identify.

## 2. Decision

1. **A catalogue of indexes in the urban-science domain** (`domains/urban-science/indicators/`): 1,536 indexes (1,366 quantitative, 170 qualitative), each with its kind, unit, how it is carried up a hierarchy (summed, a weighted mean, a ratio of sums, decomposable, recomputed, max, min, median, at one scale only), its scales and tempo, definition and source, how it is observed and may be estimated, how estimable it is, its role in a dynamical model with the standard law that advances it, and where the infrastructure computes it (73). Every keyword of the atlas is accounted for: the index it names, or why it names none. The catalogue is data, drafted by a language model from the atlas without browsing, and says so: its definitions and sources are unchecked, as a statement of grade C is.
2. **kinesis gains `scales`** (its decision 0007): systems whose quantities live on a hierarchy of units, carried between levels only by named operators (sums and reductions up, a parent's value down, shares, logits and z-scores among siblings, the graph Laplacian, the production-constrained inflow), advanced by laws each at its own pace (flows, maps, fast equilibria); every input with how it is known (observed, estimated, declared, modelled); what a model needs and lacks refused by name; ensembles over what is declared, with the declarations ranked; unknowns fitted at any level, with what the record does not identify said; perfect aggregation of linear laws.
3. **tyche gains change of support and estimates that know their limits** (its decision 0028): areal weighting, dasymetric carriage, the densities of ancillary classes, pycnophylactic interpolation; an estimator judged by blocked cross-validation, with conformal intervals and Meyer and Pebesma's area of applicability, each estimate withheld with its reason where it does not beat the mean, lies outside the area, or is wider than the use allows.
4. **The API composes them** (`api/cpp/science/scales.hpp`, `noetarium.scales/1`, with nomos's measures registered as reducers; `science.scales` in Python): describe, run, ensemble, screen, fit, aggregate, estimate, carry.
5. **The visualization layer draws a system across scales** (`visualization/js/dynamics/scales.js`): a model as its levels with their quantities by role, the operators between levels as arrows, its laws, and what it still needs ringed; units on a plane toned by a value.
6. **The data library writes a city's units across scales** (`data/products/city_scales.py`): Melbourne's 603 CLUE blocks within 13 small areas within the City, floor space by use, establishments and dwellings for every census year 2002–2024, what the census withheld with prior weights for its blank cells, neighbours and positions.
7. **The Urban Studio's plug-in *Cities across scales*** (`applications/urban-studio/plugins/scales/`): the view *Across scales* (the blocks toned by an index read at the block, its area or the city, with a ribbon of years; the models drawn; the catalogue opened in place) and five tools: indexes across scales; what the census withheld; an index where it was not observed; where growth goes, across scales; the vacancy cycle and what must be declared. One template, Melbourne 2002–2024.

## 3. Alternatives Considered

- **The catalogue as code**: most of its indexes are definitions, not methods; code is written when an index is computed, with its source checked and a check of its value, and the catalogue then names where.
- **The catalogue in the API or the studio**: it names the objects of urban science, so it is the domain's, as data a work loads (like the vocabularies of linked data).
- **Systems across scales in the grammar of kinesis's language**: a natural extension, left until the form settles (kinesis decision 0007).
- **Estimating every missing index by the best learner**: the survey finds proxies validated by random folds overstated and transfer across places unreliable; an estimate is given only where blocked validation and the area of applicability allow, and otherwise declared.
- **A new domain of urban indicators**: indexes are the urban-science domain's concern, and its modules already compute some; a domain of its own would hold only data.

## 4. Consequences

- kinesis 562 checks (66 new); tyche 1706 (33 new); the API's `check_scales.py` 20; the visualization layer's scales checks 9; the urban-science suite's catalogue checks 9; the Urban Studio's suite 115 (9 new); the page driven in headless Chrome (8 steps, not in the suite).
- **What Melbourne shows** (fitted on 2002–2016, judged on 2017–2024): 30% of the floor space counted is withheld by the census; the 5% of blocks that gained most took 70% of the homes and 90% of the office floor added in 2016–2024, so a model of shares places the change among blocks no better than chance (0.05) though it places part of it among areas (0.35 and 0.67); the vacancy cycle's four rates are not identified by thirteen areas over fourteen years, and are declared. Neither model is a forecast.
- **Not built:** discrete development events; Harris–Wilson's Bayesian inference; reconciliation across levels and temporal disaggregation; Fay–Herriot's extensions, unit-level empirical best and MRP; ordinal measurement models with invariance, ecometrics and ordinal state-space models for qualitative indexes; the offices' thresholds as declared schemes; segregation indices beyond the dissimilarity; SDG 11's indicators to their metadata; UTCI; checking the catalogue's definitions against their sources.
- **Provisional:** the plug-in's name and colour; the catalogue's ids.
