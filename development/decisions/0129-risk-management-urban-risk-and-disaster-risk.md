# ADR 0129: Risk Management, Urban Risk Management and Disaster Risk

- **Status:** Accepted, provisionally
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Decided by:** the user, who asked, with an urban analyst's post in mind whose remit includes "strengthening disaster risk management policies, institutions and regulations and mainstreaming resilience across development sectors including risk assessment and mapping, risk reduction, disaster preparedness and risk financing", "whether we can implement criticality detection; emergency route optimization, planning, and simulation, etc."; who, to Claude's proposal of one domain of disaster risk, answered that it was too broad, "If really want a new domain, I think it should be 'risk management'->'urban risk management'->'disaster risk'. Also 'risk management'->'climate risk'->…", and said "You can proceed"; and Claude.

## 1. Context

The infrastructure already held pieces of disaster risk: Freetown's flood assessment recomputed in the Urban Science Workbench (urban science's `exposure`, tyche's exceedance curves), the reach of any place over its ways (`access`), walkers as agents (`movement`), kinematic waves on networks (physis), the criticality of a water network's pipes (civil engineering), the greedy choice of sites (the markets domain's `space`). It held no notion of risk as such, no ranking of a city's roads by what their failure costs, no evacuation, and no financing of a loss. The user named the shape: risk management at the root, urban risk management beneath it and disaster risk beneath that, climate risk a second branch.

## 2. Decision

**The order of the domains holds the user's tree.** A specialisation stands above the field it specialises and uses its objects:

- **risk-management** (field, height 0, on calx, morphe, physis and tyche): `criticality`, a system of components under a hazard's scenarios, each component forced to failure and to the intact state, with the importance measures of probabilistic risk assessment read for a loss (criticality, risk, Birnbaum, RAW, RRW, Fussell–Vesely) and the weights that integrate over a curve of return periods; `financing`, instruments as layers of each event's loss with declared costs, unfunded loss costed by tiers, strategies compared, the cheapest tower from candidate boundaries, parametric cover with its basis risk. `register` planned.
- **urban-risk-management** (bridge, height 2, over risk management and urban science): `lifelines`, a city's roads contracted to links, people and services joined to them, the loss of a state in person-minutes to the nearest service; every link's closure at a state answered at once by the forest of shortest paths (only links on it change anything, and only what lies beneath them is recomputed), redundancy by link-disjoint paths and the least cut; `response`, coverage by stations within a time, backup and sole coverage, and where to add stations over a hazard's scenarios. `water` and `services` planned.
- **disaster-risk** (field, height 3, on urban risk management): `disruption`, a hazard map's depth read along each link, depth–disruption curves (cars by Pregnolato et al. 2017; on foot a declaration), the scenarios of maps at return periods; `evacuation`, the walking network, reach within a warning with and without capacities, the bound by flows over time (for everyone at once and with the people's departures), the crowd simulated as kinematic waves routed to the nearest refuge with room, and refuges chosen greedily with a bound. `warning` (Fukuzono's and Voight's failure forecasts, signs of transition on kinesis) and `vehicles` planned.
- **climate-risk** (bridge, height 5, over risk management and climate): planned, with its README and `module.json`.

**Foundations first.** morphe's flows in networks (its decision 0019: the greatest flow and its least cut, the cheapest flow with potentials, flows as paths, flows over time on the time-expanded network); tyche's layers of a loss and basis risk (its decision 0029); calx's greedy maximisation of submodular functions with a bound from the instance (its decision 0019).

**The edges and the work.** The API's `science/risk.hpp` composes the three domains (criticality, response, evacuation, financing) for any work; the data library's `japan_hazards.py` takes a Japanese municipality's tsunami and flood maps from the Hazard Map Portal's tiles and its designated evacuation sites from the GSI; the Urban Science Workbench gains the area Miyako and the plug-in *Lifelines and evacuation* (lens Risk): which roads matter most to reaching care (anywhere), walking out of a hazard's reach (Miyako, Freetown), and how the city might meet its flood losses (Freetown). The user then said the work they meant was the Urban Studio ("I means in urban studio one. It seems utilize workbench one now. But also ok"): the Workbench's plug-in stays, and the Urban Studio gains a plug-in of the same name, its analyses as a project's steps: four tools (which roads matter most, who stays within reach of a station, walking out of the hazard's reach, how the city might meet its flood losses) and two templates (Freetown; Miyako), the API's `science.risk` gathering a place's ways, buildings and people for them.

## 3. Alternatives Considered

- **One domain of disaster risk** (Claude's first proposal): the user judged it too broad; the risk of a system and its financing name no hazard and no city, and belong beneath both.
- **Modules in urban science and civil engineering**: would scatter risk across domains, and leave climate risk nowhere to stand.
- **The tree as nested directories under one domain**: the infrastructure's tools read one level of domains, and a specialisation is a dependence; the order of domains already expresses it.
- **An evacuation by agents** (urban science's `movement`): its walkers are calibrated to the Hoddle Grid and its network to a district's model; kinematic waves on physis carry a crowd on any network, conserve it exactly and compare with the bound on the same edges. Agents remain a later option.
- **Moving urban science's `exposure` into disaster risk**: a restructuring, left for its own proposal; disaster risk reads it through its down-set.

## 4. Consequences

- **Checks:** morphe 13 (flows), tyche 16 (layers; tyche 1722), calx 7 (submodular; calx 983); risk-management 27, urban-risk-management 19, disaster-risk 27; the API's `check_risk.py` 17 (the helpers of `science.risk`, and the Workbench plug-in's engine); the Workbench's suite 99; the Urban Studio's suite gains 3. The page was driven in headless Chrome (not in the suite).
- **What the records show** ([the record](../../research/experiments/2026-11-18-lifelines-and-evacuation/README.md); all costs, speeds and departure times declared):
  - *Freetown's roads.* The 1-in-20-year river flood closes 213 of 10,406 road links and cuts 163,179 people (13% of those within 4.5 km) off from the 35 buildings mapped as care. Protecting the link of greatest risk would remove 15% of the expected loss of access, and each of the next four about as much, as alternative ways out of the same districts. The links of greatest criticality lie where no flood reaches: losing the first would multiply the expected loss by 2.5.
  - *Freetown's river flood.* The 49,725 people within its 1-in-100-year reach can all be out within 12 minutes leaving at once. Leaving as Miyako's people did in 2011, at most 92% can be out by 30 minutes, almost exactly the share who have left by then, and the crowd reaches 89%: where dry ground is near, departure decides.
  - *Miyako's tsunami.* All 18,391 people of the zone could be out within 21 minutes leaving at once, and the simulated crowd matches it. Leaving as people did in 2011, at most 85% can be safe by 30 minutes however they are routed, and the crowd reaches 80%: departure, not the streets, is what limits an evacuation there. At 0.62 m/s, the speed observed in 2011, these become 76% and 65%. No mapped tall building would add anyone. The simulation never ran ahead of the bound.
  - *Freetown's losses.* Insurance above a contingent credit costs $17k a year more on average, and cuts the year of the 1-in-100-year flood from $16.3 m to $11.5 m. On the declared costs, the cheapest tower is a credit alone. A parametric cover is refused for want of an index.
- **Found while building:** a curve's weights by tyche's convention (the loss falling linearly to nothing at p = 1) gave the 20-year map half the year; the default now counts nothing below the first map, a lower bound, with the convention kept as an option. A loss per trip weighted by annual probabilities is an expectation over the year's largest flood, not a total over the year, and is labelled so. The local frame's mean sphere would misplace a map's samples by tens of metres; it now uses WGS 84's radii at the centre.
- **Not built:** the hazard in time (water arriving and roads closing as it rises); people of different speeds and groups; vertical evacuation's climbing; evacuation by car and contraflow; cascades among interdependent networks (power, water, roads); the criticality of pairs and minimal cut sets; aggregate covers, reinstatements and the timing of payouts; parametric covers without an index; early warning; climate risk; GloFAS's maps anywhere as scenarios.
- **Japan as a case.** The user asked once to avoid Japan as an example, for the present state of international relations, and then kept it: the data are public, the analysis ordinary and lawful, and the work is meant for an urban analyst's role at the World Bank's centre in Tokyo. Every page and note says that what it shows is an illustration under its declarations, not an official evacuation plan.
- **Provisional:** the domains' names follow the user's words; the plug-in's name; the declared speeds, widths, departures and costs.
