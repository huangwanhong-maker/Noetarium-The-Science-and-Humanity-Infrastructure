HALDEN CARE LIMITED — TRANSFER IMPACT ASSESSMENT
(an invented document, written for the example)

From: N. Okafor, Data Protection Officer
To: the Board
Date: 20 February 2026

1. The transfer

Nightly usage records of the patient-support programme go to Meridian
Analytics in the United States. They are coded before they leave Dublin.

2. The law of the destination

Meridian provides a remote computing service to its customers and may, in our
reading, be an electronic communication service provider that can be directed
to disclose data under section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance
Act. We have not been able to conclude that the law of the United States, as
it applies to an importer that is not on the Data Privacy Framework List,
keeps access by public authorities within what is necessary and proportionate.

3. Supplementary measures

3.1 The records are pseudonymised in Dublin and the key never leaves our
    systems. Meridian holds no key and no other data set from which a patient
    could be singled out.

3.2 The records are encrypted in transit and at rest. Meridian must, however,
    process them in the clear to run its analysis, and it holds the keys to its
    own storage.

3.3 In our assessment the pseudonymisation is an effective supplementary
    measure: an authority that obtained the records from Meridian would obtain
    study codes, not patients.

4. Conclusion

The transfer may proceed on the standard contractual clauses with the
measures in section 3. This assessment is to be reviewed each year, and at
once if Meridian receives a request from a public authority.
