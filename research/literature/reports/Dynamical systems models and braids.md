# One Generator Semantics Joins What Tools Divide

No existing language, collection or tool covers what the new foundation and its application intend, and the gap is the conjunction, not any single part. The surveyed languages each stop short of one union: none states together a flow or map, a hybrid event that changes the state space, noise with a declared law and interpretation, a Lindblad generator, an action, and a rule with a rate. Yet most of that list shares one semantic target, a linear evolution generated by a sum of operators, one per reaction, rule, jump, noise term or collapse operator; an action is the outlier, since it yields a generator only after a derivation. Canonical models across the nine fields supply exact or well-converged reference values, but only when the convention (form of the equations, parameter set, base of the logarithm, nondimensionalisation, dissipator) is stored with the number; of the curated collections, only Sprott's tables hold curated values, while dysts holds automatic estimates and PredefinedDynamicalSystems.jl states that it has no tests. Induction from data divides cleanly on a CPU: closed-form methods (sparse regression with the weak form, DMD with residuals, Kramers–Moyal coefficients, Markov models, causal-state machines, reservoirs, Lindbladian fits in a canonical gauge) are short enough to own in C++, gradient-trained and mixed-integer methods are borrowed through MIT- or Apache-licensed Python, and every result is identified only up to a stated equivalence. For braids, three independent exact word-problem solvers exist and the braid of an open trajectory is properly a morphism in a groupoid of configurations; the dynamics literature has no theorem guaranteeing the braid read from sampled trajectories, and the one certified treatment (Guillemot and Lairez, January 2026) was written for polynomial roots. The operator lifts sort into theorems (Jones, Hecke and Yang–Baxter representations; K-theory of braid group C\*-algebras), exactly computable constructions (Temperley–Lieb and fusion-space matrices over cyclotomic fields) and speculation (braids as particles in quantum gravity). Nearly all mature braid, rigorous-numerics and several neural and PDE tools are copyleft, so an MIT library uses them to generate fixtures and never as code.

## 1. Existing languages each stop one union short of the list

### 1.1 Equation-based languages are either narrow and portable or symbolic and broad

The equation-based languages fall into three kinds **[I]**. Line-oriented solver inputs are the tersest: one XPPAUT `.ode` file states ODEs, maps (`z(t+1)=z*(4-z)`), delays, Volterra integral terms, DAEs, Wiener increments (`wiener`), finite-state Markov variables with a matrix of rates (`markov`) and signed zero-crossing events (`global sign condition {resets}`), with no units, PDEs, modules or symbolic access ([XPP ODE file reference](https://sites.pitt.edu/~phase/bard/bardware/xpp/help/xppodes.html)). Declarative exchange formats carry units and hierarchy over deliberately narrow mathematics: SBML Level 3 adds distributions (`distrib`), space (`spatial`), composition (`comp`) and rules on multistate species (`multi`) only as optional packages ([SBML specifications](https://sbml.org/documents/specifications/)), and LEMS declares dimensions, regimes and transitions for hierarchical components ([LEMS documentation](https://docs.neuroml.org/Userdocs/LEMS.html)). Embedded symbolic systems keep equations as transformable expressions. **ModelingToolkit with Catalyst is the only surveyed system that covers ODEs, DAEs, SDEs, jump systems, events and declared PDEs in one symbolic representation**, with index reduction, alias elimination and tearing, and import from SBML, CellML and BioNetGen ([ModelingToolkit documentation](https://docs.sciml.ai/ModelingToolkit/stable/)); one Catalyst `ReactionSystem` yields ODE, chemical-Langevin, jump and hybrid simulations, with reactions coupled to `@brownians` and `@poissonians` ([Catalyst documentation](https://docs.sciml.ai/Catalyst/stable/)). Even so it has no action, no quantum state space and no rewriting rule beyond reactions **[I]**.

The PDE languages expose their numerical method in their syntax or leave time out. Dedalus requires the left-hand side to be "first-order in temporal derivatives and linear in the problem variables", with nonlinear terms on the right ([Dedalus tutorial 3](https://dedalus-project.readthedocs.io/en/latest/notebooks/dedalus_tutorial_3.html)); UFL is a language of variational forms that "does not implement time in any particular way", from which Jacobians and adjoints of forms are derived ([UFL form language](https://fenics.readthedocs.io/projects/ufl/en/latest/manual/form_language.html)). Units are handled in four ways **[I]**: absent (XPPAUT, PyDSTool, Dedalus, UFL); annotation only, as in Antimony, where units "do not affect the math" ([Antimony reference](https://tellurium.readthedocs.io/en/latest/antimony.html)); checked without conversion, as in ModelingToolkit, where kJ against J fails ([unit validation](https://docs.sciml.ai/ModelingToolkit/stable/basics/Validation/)), and Brian 2, where every equation carries an SI base unit ([Brian 2 equations](https://brian2.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/equations.html)); and typed with unit-carrying literals, as in NESTML's `1 mV` and `1 ms` inside equations ([izhikevich_neuron.nestml](https://github.com/nest/nestml/blob/master/models/neurons/izhikevich_neuron.nestml)). NESTML and LEMS are the nearest precedents for units carried in the type **[I]**. PyDSTool has had no release since January 2020 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/PyDSTool/)).

### 1.2 No hybrid format types a mode change that alters the state space

Two traditions write events. The equation-based one attaches condition–reset pairs to a single set of equations: Modelica's `when` is active "only at the instant" its condition becomes true, `reinit` and `pre()` give the reset, `elsewhen` gives priority, and the specification says simultaneity is not guaranteed and must be modelled ([Modelica specification, ch. 8](https://specification.modelica.org/master/equations.html)); ModelingToolkit writes root-found equations with affects in `Pre(·)` and a separate `affect_neg`, and forbids one event from changing both variables and parameters ([MTK events](https://docs.sciml.ai/ModelingToolkit/stable/basics/Events/)); Antimony writes `at (trigger): assignments` with delay, priority and persistence ([Antimony reference](https://tellurium.readthedocs.io/en/latest/antimony.html)). The automaton-based tradition makes the mode a location with its own flow, invariant, guards and assignments: SpaceEx's format ([SpaceExParser.jl](https://juliareach.github.io/SpaceExParser.jl/dev/lib/methods)), CIF's synchronising automata ([Eclipse ESCET CIF](https://eclipse.dev/escet/cif/)), Ptolemy II's modal models whose refinements may have their own directors ([Lee, EECS-2009-151](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2009/EECS-2009-151.pdf)), and JANI, a JSON format for networks of quantitative automata that is "not designed to be human-writable" ([Momba: JANI meets Python](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7984561/)). HyST exists because hybrid model checkers share semantics but have "drastically different model description languages" ([HyST record](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/1690)).

Three readings of a guard circulate **[I]**: a Boolean becoming true (Modelica, Antimony), a signed zero crossing (XPPAUT, ModelingToolkit), and a region that enables an edge with urgency separate (SpaceEx, CIF, Ptolemy); they differ when a trajectory starts inside the guard, so a new language must say which it means. Resets are everywhere assignments to the same variables. **No source was found for any hybrid format in which a mode change alters the dimension or type of the state** (a body that becomes two fragments, a rewrite that adds cells); Ptolemy's refinements come nearest, with an untyped list of set actions between them **[I]**. Modelica's `elsewhen` and Antimony's `priority` are the only explicit orderings of simultaneous events found **[I]**.

### 1.3 The interpretation of noise sits in the solver, and random variables in another family of languages

Random quantities are declared in two unrelated ways **[I]**. Probabilistic languages name a draw inside otherwise arbitrary code: Stan's `y[t] ~ normal(mu[t], sigma)`, with dynamics as opaque functions handed to `ode_rk45` and no mention of SDEs, delays or events in its ODE chapter ([Stan user's guide, ODEs](https://mc-stan.org/docs/stan-users-guide/odes.html)); Turing's `x ~ Normal(a, 1)`, an observation if conditioned and an assumption otherwise ([Turing core functionality](https://turinglang.org/docs/core-functionality/)); Gen's addressed choices recorded in a trace ([Gen tutorial](https://www.gen.dev/tutorials/bottom-up-intro/tutorial)); and PRISM's guarded commands with probabilities or rates, `[] x=0 -> 0.8:(x'=0) + 0.2:(x'=1)` ([PRISM language](https://www.prismmodelchecker.org/manual/ThePRISMLanguage/Example1)). Dynamical languages name a noise process inside an equation: Brian 2's `xi`, XPPAUT's `wiener`, Catalyst's `@brownians`. **In no surveyed language is the Itô or Stratonovich reading part of the model's text** **[I]**. In DifferentialEquations.jl it is a property of the solver: `EM` is Itô, `EulerHeun` its Stratonovich counterpart, and `RKMil(interpretation=:Stratonovich)` switches a method ([SciML SDE solvers](https://docs.sciml.ai/DiffEqDocs/dev/solvers/sde_solve/)); Brian 2 states an interpretation only for its `heun` method ([Brian 2 numerical integration](https://brian2.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/numerical_integration.html)). Since the two readings of one expression are different processes under multiplicative noise, a language that keeps the model apart from the method must state the interpretation in the model, or state the noise as a generator, where the question does not arise **[I]**. The dynamical languages also cannot say that a parameter or an initial condition is a random variable with a law; Antimony's `normal(...)` and SBML `distrib` are the partial exception, without inference **[I]**. Whether Stan has any SDE facility, and whether ModelingToolkit's `SDESystem` declares an interpretation, were not confirmed on the pages read.

### 1.4 Rules with rates are already dynamical systems; actions and quantum generators have no declarative middle

A grammar is written as a dynamical system exactly where a rate is attached to a rule **[I]**. BNGL rules are graph transformations on molecules with components, states and bonds ([BNGL in MCell4](https://mcell.org/mcell4_documentation/bngl.html)), and one BNGL model runs as a generated network (ODE, SSA), network-free in NFsim, or as a hybrid ([BioNetGen 2.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5079481)). Kappa's simulator picks a rule with probability proportional to its activity, the number of matches of its left side times its rate ([Kappa tutorial](https://kasim.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorial.html)). L-systems in L-Py are parallel string rewriting mixed with Python ([L-Py paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2012.00076/text)), and GROOVE gives graph rules nested quantification, typing, control and exhaustive exploration with no stochastic or timed semantics in its introduction ([GROOVE manual](https://groove.cs.utwente.nl/manual_introduction.html)). The two communities solve disjoint halves: rates with flat site-graph rules, or rich rules without rates; neither rewrites a cell complex or carries continuous state on what is rewritten, beyond Kappa's tokens **[I]**. **Mjolsness and Yosiphon's dynamical grammars are the nearest prior statement of the objective**: rules on multisets of typed, parameterised terms with a rate clause, `solving` clauses for ODEs and SDEs, and a semantics in an algebra of creation and annihilation operators with master equation dPr/dt = H·Pr, whose time-ordered expansion yields Gillespie's algorithm; the implementation described is "a preliminary implementation … in the form of a Mathematica notebook" ([arXiv:cs/0511073](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cs/0511073)), extended to dynamical graph grammars in 2022 ([Frontiers in Systems Biology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsysb.2022.898858)). No current general implementation was verified; Plenum and a C++ library, DGGML, are recalled and unconfirmed **[R]**.

For actions, the particle-physics tools take a Lagrangian as a symbolic expression and derive the perturbative expansion for a sharply restricted class. FeynRules takes "a list of fields, parameters and a Lagrangian", requires locality and Lorentz and gauge invariance, and derives Feynman rules and mass matrices ([arXiv:1310.1921](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1921)); SARAH takes the symmetries and a potential and generates kinetic terms, gauge interactions, ghosts, vertices and one- and two-loop renormalisation group equations ([arXiv:1309.7223](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1309.7223)). Cadabra and xAct vary an action to field equations and produce no simulation; a 2026 package notes that xAct's `VarD` ignores variational relations ([xCPS, arXiv:2606.05204](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05204)). At the other end, Grid is a data-parallel C++ library for lattice QCD ([arXiv:1512.03487](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03487v1)), and sl2cfoam-next computes one EPRL vertex from ten spins, with amplitudes over several vertices assembled by hand in nested loops and cut-offs chosen without a known rule ([arXiv:2202.04360](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.04360)). **Between "action in, Feynman rules out" and "C++ in, configurations out" there is no declarative middle** **[I]**: no surveyed format states a lattice action over a complex, a group field theory's kernels and interaction pattern, or a spin-foam model's amplitudes as data. A search for software specific to group field theory returned only general Feynman-amplitude tools; that absence is a search result, not a published statement.

Open quantum systems are stated everywhere as host-language objects: QuTiP's `mesolve(H, psi0, times, c_ops)`, each collapse operator the square root of a rate times an operator ([QuTiP master equation guide](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-master.html)), and likewise QuantumOptics.jl and QuantumToolbox.jl. No textual model format for quantum dynamics was found. The one symbolic layer is QuantumCumulants.jl, which derives equations of motion for averages from commutation relations, truncates by a cumulant expansion at a stated order, and hands a ModelingToolkit `System` to the solvers ([QuantumCumulants.jl](https://qojulia.github.io/QuantumCumulants.jl/stable/)). The data of a Markovian open system is small and uniform (a tensor product of named factors, H, pairs of rate and jump operator, a state, observables), so a textual notation is straightforward and absent only because the packages are libraries **[I]**.

### 1.5 Four lessons recur, and six unions are missing

Composition has a stated algebra in one place only. AlgebraicDynamics.jl composes systems along wiring diagrams "follow[ing] the mathematics of operads and operad algebras", distinguishing resource sharers on undirected diagrams from machines on directed ones ([AlgebraicDynamics.jl](https://algebraicjulia.github.io/AlgebraicDynamics.jl/dev/)), but its primitives are opaque Julia functions, so nothing is derived from the composite **[I]**. Decapodes.jl keeps the equations symbolic and typed by where they live on a complex (`Form0`, `Form1`, `Form2`, primal and dual) and composes the syntax before compiling, while warning that "a stable API/behavior cannot yet be assumed" ([Decapodes.jl](https://algebraicjulia.github.io/Decapodes.jl/dev/)). Elsewhere composition is flattening (Modelica, ModelingToolkit, Antimony) with no stated law **[I]**.

Four design lessons recur across the survey **[I]**: keep the model apart from the numerical method (Brian 2's `method` keyword, Catalyst's and BNGL's several simulations of one model; Dedalus, NESTML's `integrate_odes()` and solver-chosen SDE interpretation are the counter-examples); keep laws as symbolic data, since derivation happens only where they are (ModelingToolkit, UFL, FeynRules, SARAH, QuantumCumulants) and never where the right-hand side is opaque (Stan, AlgebraicDynamics, QuTiP's matrices); carry dimensions in every declaration; and treat events as first-class with an explicit rule for simultaneity. Reading the coverage table by columns gives the six unions that no language states **[I]**: an action together with a time evolution to be integrated; rewriting rules together with continuous fields over what is rewritten; a quantum generator and a classical one in one notation; the law of a random variable together with the interpretation of a stochastic integral in the model; a hybrid mode change that alters the state space; and anything declarative for lattice actions on a complex, group field theories or spin foams.

Most of the list nonetheless has one semantic target **[I]**. Reaction networks, rate-carrying rules, jump processes, SDEs through their generators and Lindblad dynamics are all linear evolutions generated by a sum of operators, one per rule, reaction, noise term or jump operator; dynamical grammars and Doi–Peliti state this for the classical cases and QuantumCumulants uses the analogous algebra for the quantum one. Collapse operators with rates are to the Lindblad generator what reactions with rates are to a master equation's generator, and the choice of unravelling (jumps or diffusion) corresponds to the choice between a jump process and an SDE; no package states that correspondence in its interface. A flow is the deterministic limit. An action is the outlier: it determines a generator only after Euler–Lagrange or a transfer matrix, which is the derivation the Lagrangian tools automate for a narrow class.

## 2. Canonical models give exact checks once the convention is stored with the number

### 2.1 Maps and low-dimensional flows agree across sources after parameters and logarithms are matched

The maps have exact or well-converged values. For the logistic map the period-doubling points run r₁ = 3, r₂ = 1 + √6, r₃ = 3.5440903 to r₈ = 3.5699340, with Feigenbaum's δ = 4.669201609… and α = 2.502907875… ([Feigenbaum constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feigenbaum_constants), a secondary source quoting Alligood, Sauer and Yorke); at r = 4 the exponent is ln 2 and the Kaplan–Yorke dimension exactly 1 ([Sprott, Common chaotic systems](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/comchaos.htm)). **The Hénon map in the form x′ = 1 − a x² + y, y′ = b x at (1.4, 0.3) has exponents 0.41922 and −1.62319 and D_KY = 1.25827**, confirmed by a second algorithm at 0.419217, −1.623190 ([Sprott, Lyapunov spectrum software](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/lespec.htm)). The standard map at K = 1 has ±0.10497 in its chaotic sea ([Sprott](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/comchaos.htm)), and Greene's last invariant circle breaks at K_c = 0.971635 ([PredefinedDynamicalSystems.jl](https://juliadynamics.github.io/PredefinedDynamicalSystems.jl/dev/)). The Lozi (0.47023, −1.16338) and Ikeda (0.50760, −0.71832) values come from Sprott's Appendix A and were not read **[R]** ([book site](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaostsa/)). An internal check needs no table: the exponents sum to the logarithm of the Jacobian determinant, and Hénon (ln 0.3), Lozi (ln 0.5) and Ikeda (2 ln 0.9) all pass to five decimals, which supports the recalled figures without replacing a reading of the appendix **[I]**. The standard map's exponent is a property of one chaotic component, so a check must fix the initial condition **[I]**. The tent map at μ = 2 collapses every binary floating-point orbit to zero after about 53 iterations, so its ln μ check belongs slightly below 2 or in exact arithmetic **[R]**.

For flows, **Lorenz at σ = 10, r = 28, b = 8/3 has exponents 0.9056, 0, −14.5723 and D_KY = 2.06215**, with a second computation giving 0.90563 and −14.57219 ([Sprott](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/comchaos.htm); [Sprott, software](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/lespec.htm)). Its bifurcation values are firm: a pitchfork at r = 1, a subcritical Hopf bifurcation at r = σ(σ + b + 3)/(σ − b − 1) ≈ 24.74 ([Lorenz system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_system), secondary), the homoclinic bifurcation at 13.926 ([arXiv:chao-dyn/9710014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/chao-dyn/9710014)) and the boundary crisis at about 24.06 ([arXiv:1705.08178](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08178)). Rössler at a = b = 0.2, c = 5.7 has 0.0714, 0, −5.3943 ([Sprott](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/comchaos.htm)), and dysts agrees at 0.07120 and −5.39373 ([dysts data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/williamgilpin/dysts/master/dysts/data/chaotic_attractors.json)). Three Rössler parameter sets circulate, (0.2, 0.2, 5.7), (0.1, 0.1, 14) and Wolf's (0.15, 0.2, 10); with bits against nats this accounts for the "different Rössler exponents" 0.0714, 0.090 and 0.13 **[I]**. Wolf, Swift, Swinney and Vastano's widely copied table uses base-2 logarithms and σ = 16, r = 45.92, b = 4 for Lorenz ([Sprott, software](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/lespec.htm)); its individual values are recalled only **[R]** ([Wolf et al. 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(85)90011-9)). The sum of exponents equals the mean divergence, giving exact checks: −(σ + 1 + b) = −13.6667 for Lorenz, −k for Ueda's oscillator, −N for Lorenz 96 **[I]**.

Two higher-dimensional references were read at the primary source. For Lorenz 96 with 40 variables and F = 8, Lorenz and Emanuel give a doubling time of 0.42 time units, 13 positive exponents and a Kaplan–Yorke dimension near 27.1; they give the doubling time, not λ₁, and the value 1.68 quoted in later literature was not verified ([Lorenz and Emanuel 1998](https://texmex.mit.edu/ftp/pub/emanuel/PAPERS/lorenz98.pdf)). For the Kuramoto model K_c = 2/(π g(0)) for unimodal symmetric g ([Kuramoto model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuramoto_model), secondary), and for a Lorentzian of half-width Δ, K_c = 2Δ with R = √(1 − 2Δ/K) beyond it ([Bick et al., arXiv:1902.05307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.05307)). For Hamiltonian templates (Hénon–Heiles, the restricted three-body problem with the Arenstorf orbit, the double pendulum) the checkable quantities are integrals of motion, the pairing of exponents and special orbits, not spectra; the Arenstorf initial data and period are recalled from Hairer, Nørsett and Wanner **[R]** ([Solving ODEs I](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-78862-1)). The double pendulum has no canonical Lyapunov value.

### 2.2 PDE and fluid templates rest on exact solutions, linear thresholds and one tabulated chaotic case

The one well-tabulated chaotic PDE is Kuramoto–Sivashinsky in derivative form. Edson, Bunder, Mattner and Roberts tabulate the twelve largest exponents for periodic domains from L = 12 to 100; **at L = 22 the leading exponent is 0.043 and D_KY = 5.198, and D_KY ≈ 0.226 L − 0.160 for periodic boundaries** against 0.226 L − 2.106 for odd-periodic ones; with the mean left free there are two zero exponents ([arXiv:1902.09651](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09651)). The linear theory σ(k) = k² − k⁴, with fastest growth at k = 1/√2, checks a spectral implementation before any chaos is computed **[I]**. Pearson's Gray–Scott set-up was read in full: D_u = 2 × 10⁻⁵, D_v = 10⁻⁵, a 2.5 × 2.5 periodic domain on 256², with the saddle-node and Hopf curves meeting at F = k = 1/16 ([Pearson 1993](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/patt-sol/9304003)). Every other exact solution in the PDE catalogue is recalled from textbooks **[R]**: KdV and NLS solitons, the sine-Gordon kink and breather, the Fisher–KPP minimal speed 2√(rD), the Cahn–Hilliard fastest-growing wavenumber, the Swift–Hohenberg and Brusselator thresholds from [Cross and Hohenberg](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.65.851), the Benjamin–Feir–Newell line of the complex Ginzburg–Landau equation from [Aranson and Kramer](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.74.99), and the Burgers benchmark of [Basdevant et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0045-7930(86)90036-8).

Fluid thresholds are exact to the digits quoted. Rayleigh–Bénard convection sets in at **Ra_c = 1707.762 with k_c = 3.117 between rigid walls** ([arXiv:comp-gas/9612001](https://arxiv.org/pdf/comp-gas/9612001)), at about 1100.65 rigid–free and at 27π⁴/4 ≈ 657.511 free–free, where the marginal curve is Ra(k) = (π² + k²)³/k² ([Nektar++ tutorial](https://doc.nektar.info/tutorials/latest/incns/rbc/rbcch3.html)). The Taylor–Couette thin-gap value 1707.76 ([Taylor–Couette flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor%E2%80%93Couette_flow), secondary) is the same number, since the two linear problems coincide in that limit, so one eigenvalue solver checks both templates **[I]**. Kolmogorov flow's R_c → √2 holds in Iudovich's normalisation in the long-domain limit ([arXiv:2007.06513](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.06513)). The Taylor–Green vortex at Re = 1600 has a 512³ pseudo-spectral reference, case C3.5 of the high-order CFD workshop ([HiOCFD C3.5](https://cfd.ku.edu/hiocfd/case_c3.5.pdf)). The lid-driven cavity is the weak point: Ghia, Ghia and Shin's table was read only through a public transcription ([gist](https://gist.github.com/ivan-pi/3e9326d18a366ffe6a8e5bfda6353219)), a search summary of the same page reported a conflicting entry at Re = 1000, y = 0.4531 (−0.86636 against −0.10648), and the Chebyshev solution of Botella and Peyret, which supersedes it at Re = 1000, is recalled **[R]** ([Botella and Peyret 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0045-7930(98)00002-4)).

**Nothing in the networks and lattice catalogue was verified in the session** **[R]**. Its exact checks are combinatorial: Game of Life census values, the 88 inequivalent elementary cellular automata, the sandpile's Abelian property with det Δ recurrent configurations ([Dhar 1990](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.64.1613)), the critical line 2p(1 − p)K = 1 of random Boolean networks ([Derrida and Pomeau 1986](https://doi.org/10.1209/0295-5075/1/2/001)), the harmonic frequencies of the Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou chain, and the identity of the Frenkel–Kontorova Aubry transition with Greene's K_c ([Braun and Kivshar 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0370-1573(98)00029-5)). ### 2.3 Neural models offer closed forms and one published theory-against-simulation table

About half the neural templates have results sharp enough for automatic checks **[I]**. The Siegert formula for the noisy leaky integrate-and-fire neuron and the map between the quadratic integrate-and-fire neuron and the theta model were read in Gerstner et al. ([Neuronal Dynamics §8.4](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch8.S4.html); [§5.3](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch5.S3.html)). The Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin firing-rate equations, ṙ = Δ/π + 2rv, v̇ = v² + η̄ + Jr + I − π²r², are exact for Lorentzian heterogeneity in the thermodynamic limit, with figure parameters and N = 10⁴ microscopic simulations ([arXiv:1506.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06581)). Izhikevich's model, its cell types and its 1,000-neuron network were read in the paper ([Izhikevich 2003](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/spikes.pdf)). For random rate networks the fixed point loses stability at g = 1 and λ_max = ½(g − 1)² near onset, as restated by Engelken, Wolf and Abbott ([arXiv:2006.02427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02427)).

Brunel's network is the fullest fixture. His four states (g = 3, 6, 5, 4.5 at external drive ratios 2, 4, 2, 0.9; N_E = 10,000, N_I = 2,500, J = 0.1 mV, delay 1.5 ms) come with a table of simulation against theory: rates of 60.7 against 55.8 Hz, 37.7 against 38.0 Hz and 5.5 against 6.5 Hz ([Brunel 2000, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10809012/); [paper PDF](https://neuro.bstu.by/ai/To-dom/My_research/Papers-2.1-done/LIF/Recurrent-not-copy/0/Ref/brunel00JCNS.pdf)). The notes solved his self-consistent equations as transcribed and obtained 55.84, 37.95 and 6.52 Hz, reproducing the theory column, so the transcription and its convention (no factor √2 in the argument) are right **[I]**. Theory and simulation differ by up to about 20% in rate in the slow state, so a network simulation is compared with the simulation column and only a mean-field solver with the theory column **[I]**.

The conductance models are weaker ground. The book's Hodgkin–Huxley table is a cortical pyramidal refit, not the squid axon ([Neuronal Dynamics §2.2](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch2.S2.html)); the 1952 squid-axon set and its bifurcation currents (9.78, 6.26 and about 154 µA/cm²) are recalled **[R]** ([Hodgkin and Huxley 1952](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1952.sp004764); [Rinzel and Miller 1980](https://doi.org/10.1016/0025-5564(80)90041-X)), and the published currents belong to the squid set only **[I]**. The Morris–Lecar Hopf, SNIC and homoclinic parameter sets rest on a secondary lecture file ([ICTS lecture](https://www.icts.res.in/sites/default/files/DCS2017-2017-05-11-Pranay-Goel-2.pdf)). FitzHugh–Nagumo, AdEx, Wilson–Cowan, Amari's bump condition and Hopfield's capacity are recalled **[R]**. The Virtual Brain's Jansen–Rit defaults were read in its source, with v₀ = 5.52 mV where its own docstring calls 6.0 the usual value ([TVB jansen_rit.py](https://docs.thevirtualbrain.org/_modules/tvb/simulator/models/jansen_rit.html)).

### 2.4 Stochastic processes and diffusion generative models can be checked without any training

SciML's `SDEProblemLibrary` is a standing test set with pathwise analytic solutions, including geometric Brownian motion in both calculi (Itô solution X₀ exp(0.63155 t + 0.87 W_t), with 0.63155 = 1.01 − 0.87²/2) and a matched Itô/Stratonovich pair whose drifts differ by exactly ½ b b′ ([SDEProblemLibrary.jl](https://github.com/SciML/DiffEqProblemLibrary.jl/blob/master/lib/SDEProblemLibrary/src/SDEProblemLibrary.jl)); the pair verifies the conversion rule by itself **[I]**. The exponential Hawkes process has branching ratio n = α/β, mean intensity λ/(1 − n), a closed covariance density and an O(k) likelihood recursion ([Laub, Taimre and Pollett](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02822)). The noisy Kuramoto model of identical oscillators has non-trivial stationary states iff K > 1 at unit noise, with r = I₁(2Kr)/I₀(2Kr) ([Giacomin, Pakdaman and Pellegrin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.4501)), and ε_c = 2(γ + σ²) for Lorentzian frequencies ([Munyaev et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01468)); the two agree as K_c = 2D once the noise is normalised **[I]**. Ornstein–Uhlenbeck and Cox–Ingersoll–Ross moments were confirmed only at Wikipedia ([OU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornstein%E2%80%93Uhlenbeck_process); [CIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox%E2%80%93Ingersoll%E2%80%93Ross_model)). Kramers' full turnover formulas, the master-equation closed forms and stochastic resonance are recalled **[R]** ([Hänggi, Talkner and Borkovec](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.62.251)); nothing was verified for Lévy noise.

Score-based models, DDPM and DDIM, flow matching, rectified flow, stochastic interpolants and consistency models are one family: a Gaussian conditional path x_t = α_t x₀ + σ_t ε with an SDE or ODE transporting its marginals **[I]**. The forward, reverse-time and probability-flow equations and the VE, VP and sub-VP kernels were read in [Song et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456); the discrete chains in [Ho, Jain and Abbeel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239) and [Song, Meng and Ermon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502); the conditional fields in [Lipman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747), [Liu, Gong and Liu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003) and [Albergo, Boffi and Vanden-Eijnden](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08797); the consistency function in [Song et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469). For Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture data the score, the velocity and, for an isotropic Gaussian, the probability-flow map x(t) = x(0)√(v_t/v₀) are closed forms, so every sampler can be held to an exact answer and its order measured **[I]**. A library therefore needs one "Gaussian path" object from which score, noise prediction, denoiser and velocity convert **[I]**. That training on two-dimensional mixtures is a CPU task is a judgement from the size of the cited toy problems; no source read states timings. TorchCFM (MIT) ships such tutorials ([conditional-flow-matching](https://github.com/atong01/conditional-flow-matching)). No named benchmark suite for closed-form checks of diffusion models was found.

### 2.5 Open quantum systems have closed-form spectra and steady states, and dissipator conventions that differ by a factor of two

For a two-level atom with H = (ω_x/2)σ_x and decay γ₋ the Liouvillian eigenvalues are 0, −γ₋/2 and −¾γ₋ ± ¼√(γ₋² − 16ω_x²), with the Liouvillian exceptional point at γ₋ = 4ω_x and the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian's at 2ω_x, a different place ([Minganti et al., arXiv:1909.11619](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11619)). **The Kerr resonator with one- and two-photon driving has an exact steady state for every parameter value** ([Bartolo et al., arXiv:1607.06739](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06739)); the notes reproduced it numerically for one parameter set (⟨a†a⟩ = 1.6674967708532 from a dense Liouvillian against the series, to 10⁻¹³), confirming the formula and convention as read **[I]**. The Dicke model's critical coupling with cavity loss and collective decay is λ_c = ½√((ω_z² + γ²)(ω_c² + κ²)/(ω_z ω_c)) ([Kirton et al., arXiv:1805.09828](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09828)); the boundary-driven XXZ chain has an exact matrix-product steady state ([Prosen, arXiv:1106.2978](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2978)); and the quantum van der Pol oscillator tends to ⅔|0⟩⟨0| + ⅓|1⟩⟨1| in its quantum limit ([Walter, Nunnenkamp and Bruder](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.7044)). That last limit depends only on a ratio of rates, so it is the best first check of an implementation's dissipator **[I]**. General spectral facts and the two kinds of dissipative transition are in [Minganti et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.11293).

Sources write the dissipator in two normalisations: QuTiP and Walter et al. use the standard form with rate γ, while Kirton et al. and Lee and Sadeghpour multiply a factor-2 dissipator by γ, making their κ an amplitude decay rate ([QuTiP guide](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-master.html); [arXiv:1306.6359](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6359)). The Mollow spectrum's weights, the damped oscillator's Liouvillian spectrum, the lossy Jaynes–Cummings eigenvalues, superradiance and the exactly solvable non-Markovian cases are recalled **[R]** ([Breuer and Petruccione](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199213900.001.0001)). All these systems are small enough for a dense Liouvillian on a CPU: 40 to 80 Fock states give dimension 1,600 to 6,400 **[I]**.

### 2.6 Only one collection is curated, and each template must carry nine conventions

The collections are of two kinds. Among catalogues of low-dimensional systems, **Sprott's web table of eight systems is curated, cross-checked by a second algorithm, and warns that the last digit is "only a best estimate"** ([Sprott](https://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/chaos/comchaos.htm)). dysts stores, per system, parameters, an initial condition on the attractor, `dt`, `period`, a citation and Lyapunov spectra averaged over runs of 1,000 periods, under Apache-2.0 ([dysts](https://github.com/williamgilpin/dysts)). Its values are automatic estimates **[I]**: its Lorenz entry writes β as 2.667 and gives 0.90504 against Sprott's 0.9056; its correlation dimensions (1.994 for Lorenz, 1.797 for Rössler) sit below accepted values; its "Lorenz 96" entry is four variables at F = 20 with a description of the two-scale model; and its README counts 10 maps where its JSON lists 26 ([dysts data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/williamgilpin/dysts/master/dysts/data/chaotic_attractors.json); [maps](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/williamgilpin/dysts/master/dysts/data/discrete_maps.json)). PredefinedDynamicalSystems.jl (MIT, 67 systems) says it "does not have any actual tests" and is for demonstration only ([repository](https://github.com/JuliaDynamics/PredefinedDynamicalSystems.jl)). The PDE collections hold trajectories for machine learning, not invariants: PDEBench (code MIT, data CC BY 4.0; [repository](https://github.com/pdebench/PDEBench), [DaRUS](https://darus.uni-stuttgart.de/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.18419/darus-2986)), PDEArena ([repository](https://github.com/pdearena/pdearena)), The Well with 16 datasets and 15 TB ([repository](https://github.com/PolymathicAI/the_well)) and APEBench, which generates 46 PDEs procedurally ([repository](https://github.com/tum-pbs/apebench)). The data licences of The Well and PDEArena were not found. Collections of packaged models carry their own discrepancies: SciML's noisy Lorenz problem codes β = 2.66 where its docstring says 8/3. A fixture generated from a package records the package's choice, not the paper's **[I]**.

dysts' JSON schema is a usable model for a template's metadata, with reference values recomputed and pinned to primary sources **[I]**. Almost every disagreement between quoted values traces to a convention, so each template stores **[I]**: the equations in full as the cited source writes them; parameters as exact rationals where the source has them (8/3, not 2.667); the unit of exponents (base e, per iteration or per time unit); the phase-space convention (mod 2π or mod 1; a forcing phase counted or not); domain, boundary conditions and constraints for PDEs (mean fixed or free); the nondimensionalisation of thresholds ("1708" without its boundary conditions is three numbers); the initial condition or energy for mixed phase spaces; the kind of dimension and its estimator; and a tolerance. Each value also carries a class: exact, rigorous (as Tucker's proof), converged numerical, or estimated. Scholarpedia could not be read on the day (its certificate had expired), so no value here rests on it.

## 3. Closed-form induction is cheap to own, and every induced model is known only up to an equivalence

*Caveat.* In this part the bibliography is mostly recalled: the notes fetched the 2022–2026 benchmark and criticism papers and the licence files, and gave the classical method papers from memory. The **[R]** marks below are therefore frequent, and they mark the bibliographic detail, not doubt about the method.

### 3.1 Sparse regression depends on its library, and the weak form is its most reported improvement

SINDy poses Ẋ = Θ(X)Ξ over a library Θ of candidate terms and finds a sparse Ξ by sequentially thresholded least squares, assuming few active terms in the chosen basis **[R]** ([Brunton, Proctor and Kutz 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.03580)). Its cost is iterated least squares, milliseconds to seconds for ODEs on a laptop **[I]**. Its result is conditional on the library: the Common Task Framework evaluation states that SINDy does well when its candidate library includes the true terms ([Wyder et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)), and a review names derivative approximation from noisy data as a major source of error ([North, Wikle and Schliep 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10663)). The weak form integrates against compactly supported test functions so that no derivative of the data is taken; Messenger and Bortz claim identification with signal-to-noise ratio "approaching one in many well-known cases" and cost O(N^{D+1} log N) through FFT-based test functions ([arXiv:2007.02848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02848)), against the 1% noise level of PDE-FIND's tests **[R]** ([Rudy et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06401)). Both figures are author-reported on their own problems **[I]**. A benchmark by PySINDy's developers on dysts found strong performance from the original thresholded least squares and from mixed-integer MIOSR, and that the weak formulation "significantly improves" on the traditional method even on clean data ([Kaptanoglu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10787)); no independent head-to-head at matched noise was found. Ensemble SINDy bags over data and library terms and returns inclusion probabilities, its authors calling standard SINDy "sensitive to noise, especially in the low-data limit" ([Fasel et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10992)).

The variants that matter for a typed library are recalled **[R]**: linear constraints on coefficients ([Loiseau and Brunton](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03271)), trapping regions ([Kaptanoglu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01843)), dimensionally consistent libraries by Buckingham Pi ([Bakarji et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04643)), and SPIDER's libraries built from irreducible representations of an assumed symmetry group, regressed in weak form ([Gurevich et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00048)). The published methods treat dimension and symmetry as add-ons, which is where a library typed by both would differ **[I]**. PySINDy is MIT and at v2.1.0 (8 January 2026 on PyPI), with Bayesian SINDy, trapping, MIOSR (needing Gurobi), SINDy-PI (needing cvxpy) and ensembling ([PySINDy releases](https://github.com/dynamicslab/pysindy/releases); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pysindy/)). The authors' MATLAB WSINDy repositories state "All Rights Reserved" and the PDE-FIND repository has no licence file, so those algorithms must be reimplemented from the papers ([WSINDy_ODE README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MathBioCU/WSINDy_ODE/HEAD/README.md); [PDE-FIND](https://github.com/snagcliffs/PDE-FIND)).

### 3.2 Operator methods are linear algebra, with proved limits on what spectra can be learned

DMD and EDMD are singular value decompositions and eigenproblems, practical for state dimensions up to 10⁵–10⁶ through a rank-r SVD **[I]**. Exact DMD is sensitive to noise, and the PyDMD paper recommends bagged, optimised DMD as the "all-purpose DMD method for noisy data" ([Ichinaga et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07463)); forward–backward and total-least-squares DMD remove the bias in closed form **[R]** ([Dawson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02264)). EDMD converges to a Galerkin projection of the Koopman operator, with eigenvalues converging only along subsequences and weakly **[R]** ([Korda and Mezić](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04680)). Residual DMD computes a residual for every candidate eigenpair from the same snapshots plus one more matrix, removing spectral pollution and giving pseudospectra with convergence guarantees **[R]** ([Colbrook and Townsend](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14889)); its reference MATLAB code is BSD-2-Clause ([licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MColbrook/Residual-Dynamic-Mode-Decomposition/HEAD/LICENSE)), and the PyDMD paper names residual DMD as a candidate for future extension, not a feature. **Colbrook, Mezić and Stepanenko prove that for some smooth systems on tori, non-trivial Koopman eigenfunctions cannot be determined by any sequence of algorithms, even randomised and with unlimited data** ([arXiv:2407.06312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06312)). Ulam's method on a box partition, coherent sets from singular vectors of a transfer operator, and generator EDMD, which identifies drift and diffusion and reduces to SINDy-like regression with the identity observable, are closed-form relatives **[R]** ([Klus et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10638)). On the hidden test sets of the Common Task Framework the operator methods forecast chaos poorly (optimised DMD 20.62 on Lorenz and −11.04 on Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, where 100 is perfect; [Wyder et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)); a linear model with a finite spectrum cannot reproduce a continuous one, and the scores do not speak to modal analysis **[I]**.

### 3.3 Drift, diffusion and jumps are read from conditional moments, with finite-sampling bias and an embedding problem

The Kramers–Moyal coefficients give drift and diffusion of a Langevin model from conditional moments by binning or kernel regression, assuming the Markov property above a Markov–Einstein time scale **[R]** ([Siegert, Friedrich and Peinke](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9803250)). Finite sampling intervals bias the estimates; corrections of arbitrary order follow in closed form from a power-series expansion of the generator, for diffusions and jump-diffusions ([Rydin Gorjão et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12813)). Strong measurement noise destroys the Markov property of the observed series, and a non-vanishing fourth coefficient signals jumps **[R]** ([Böttcher et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.97.090603); [Anvari et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep35435)). Markov state models and hidden Markov models are mature and cheap; hidden Markov parameters are generically identifiable from three consecutive observations up to relabelling **[R]** ([Allman, Matias and Rhodes](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032)). For a jump process observed at discrete times the generator may not exist or be unique, the embedding problem **[R]** ([Bladt and Sørensen](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2005.00508.x)). The two main Markov-model libraries, deeptime and PyEMMA, are LGPL-3.0, which is a reason to own Markov-model estimation ([deeptime licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deeptime-ml/deeptime/HEAD/LICENSE.txt); [PyEMMA licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markovmodel/PyEMMA/HEAD/LICENSE.txt)); `kramersmoyal` is MIT ([licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LRydin/KramersMoyal/HEAD/LICENSE)). Estimation of Hawkes and other self-exciting processes was not researched.

### 3.4 Symbolic search rarely recovers first-principles laws, and causal-state methods have finite-data pathologies

Symbolic regression is NP-hard **[R]** ([Virgolin and Pissis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01018)) and runs for minutes to hours per problem on a CPU through PySR (Apache-2.0; [licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MilesCranmer/PySR/HEAD/LICENSE)) or Operon, a C++ genetic-programming code under MIT ([repository](https://github.com/heal-research/operon)) **[I]**. The 2025 SRBench update ran 25 algorithms on 24 datasets, single-core with 10 GB per job: **"no single algorithm dominates", most top performers use constant optimisation, and only one method, ITEA, retrieved a first-principles equation on the new track** ([Aldeia et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03977)). Above a noise threshold the true closed-form model cannot be learned by any method ([Fajardo-Fontiveros et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02704)). Dimensional typing prunes the search, in PhySO (MIT; [licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WassimTenachi/PhySO/HEAD/LICENSE)) and AI Feynman **[R]** ([Udrescu and Tegmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11481)). The published evidence says that what a dimension-typed evolutionary search lacks for dynamics is a nonlinear least-squares constant fitter inside the search and a Pareto front by complexity, not a new engine **[I]**. The SRBench harness is GPL-3.0 ([licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cavalab/srbench/HEAD/LICENSE)).

For symbolic series, the ε-machine is the minimal unifilar hidden Markov model on causal states **[R]** ([Shalizi and Crutchfield](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176)). CSSR builds it by splitting on hypothesis tests and converges for processes with finitely many causal states **[R]** ([Shalizi and Klinkner](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0406011)), but on finite data it does not guarantee a minimal machine and state explosion occurs ([Paulson and Griffin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01300)). Bayesian structural inference enumerates unifilar topologies and computes each one's evidence in closed form with Dirichlet priors, returning a posterior over machines ([Strelioff and Crutchfield 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1392)); it enumerates, so it is limited to few states. The criticism of CSSR in Crutchfield's essay comes from the group that proposed the alternative ([arXiv:1710.06832](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06832)). The mirrored CSSR code has no licence file ([stites/CSSR](https://github.com/stites/CSSR)), and no licence-clean reference for either method was found, so both are candidates to implement; the need for a generating partition must be stated wherever a continuous series is symbolised **[R]** ([Bollt et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.85.3524)).

### 3.5 Structure-preserving networks owe little to symplecticity, and the strongest cheap forecaster needs the right nonlinearity

Hamiltonian and Lagrangian networks train in minutes on a CPU for a few degrees of freedom **[I]**, but Gruver et al. show their advantage comes from modelling acceleration directly, "rather than symplectic structure or energy conservation" ([arXiv:2202.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04836)). What they learn carries a gauge **[I]**: a Hamiltonian up to a constant, a Lagrangian up to scaling and a total derivative, and a map trained through a symplectic integrator identifies that integrator's shadow Hamiltonian **[R]** ([Offen and Ober-Blöbaum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02492)). A discrete Lagrangian can be learned directly from positions, giving a variational integrator, with degenerate solutions excluded by normalisation **[R]** ([Ober-Blöbaum and Offen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12619)). The closed-form alternatives a C++ library can own are a Hamiltonian or Lagrangian expanded in a term library and fitted by sparse regression, a discrete Lagrangian fitted to a vanishing discrete Euler–Lagrange residual, and physics-informed DMD **[I]**.

Among black-box surrogates, next-generation reservoir computing is a nonlinear vector autoregression fitted by one ridge regression **[R]** ([Gauthier et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07688)). It reconstructs basins from a single transient when given the exact nonlinearities, and a tiny uncertainty in the nonlinearity makes its predictions no better than chance ([Zhang and Cornelius 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10211)). With a polynomial library it differs from SINDy only in the regression target and the penalty, so one library-and-regression core serves both and the same dependence on the library applies **[I]**. On the Common Task Framework's hidden test sets, reservoirs lead on Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (18.88) while **a zero prediction scores 0.00 and beats most of the methods**; LSTM leads on Lorenz (64.54), neural ODEs score 3.38 and −33.46, and the authors state that "no model scores high across all tasks" ([Wyder et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)). Gradients of recurrent models trained on chaotic data necessarily diverge with the unrolled length **[R]** ([Mikhaeil, Monfared and Durstewitz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07238)). Delay embedding, singular spectrum analysis and empirical dynamic modelling are closed-form or nearest-neighbour methods; convergent cross mapping can report wrong causal directions **[R]** ([Cobey and Baskerville](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169050)), and pyEDM's University of California licence allows only educational, research and non-profit use ([pyEDM licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SugiharaLab/pyEDM/HEAD/LICENSE)), which is a reason to own those methods **[I]**.

### 3.6 Lindbladian learning cannot reach the Heisenberg limit, and the generator has a gauge to fix

A 2026 result, accepted to SODA 2027, learns all Hamiltonian and dissipative coefficients of local Lindbladians with about Õ(Λ²/ε²) experiments and proves matching lower bounds from single-qubit dephasing: **Heisenberg-limited scaling is information-theoretically impossible once dissipative coefficients must be estimated** ([Arad et al., arXiv:2606.20535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20535)). Hamiltonians alone can be learned at total evolution time O(ε⁻¹ log(1/δ)) **[R]** ([Huang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03030)). The learned objects carry gauge freedoms **[R]**: the GKSL decomposition is invariant under unitary mixing of jump operators and shifts L_k ↦ L_k + a_k·1, fixed by a traceless operator basis in which the Kossakowski matrix is unique ([Gorini, Kossakowski and Sudarshan](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.522979)); gate-set tomography is defined up to a similarity ([Nielsen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07301)); and deciding whether a channel has any time-independent Lindbladian generator requires searching branches of the matrix logarithm and is NP-hard in general ([Wolf et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.3172)). A C++ fit of a Lindbladian by linear least squares on the generator in a traceless basis returns the canonical pair and so fixes the gauge by construction, checkable against a simulated open two-level system **[I]**. A 2026 senior thesis claiming the first rigorous characterisation of gauge in SPAM-noisy Lindbladian learning was seen only as a search summary ([Sinha 2026](https://thesis.caltech.edu/18488/)). Learning from continuous measurement records was not researched.

### 3.7 Evaluation needs invariants beside forecasts, and no benchmark scores topology

Most induced models are identified only up to a transformation, and a report should name it **[I]**: similarity for linear state-space models, diffeomorphism for delay reconstructions, permutation for hidden Markov models, conjugacy for Koopman models, gauge for Lagrangians and Lindbladians, and a branch for generators from maps. Structural identifiability of rational ODE models is decidable by differential algebra, and StructuralIdentifiability.jl is the maintained tool under an MIT-style licence ([licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SciML/StructuralIdentifiability.jl/HEAD/LICENSE.md)); STRIKE-GOLDD is GPL-3.0 and SIAN has no licence file. PDE discovery is non-unique when the data satisfy more than one PDE in the library **[R]** ([Scholl et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08342)).

For chaotic systems the Common Task Framework scores a short-time error and a long-time statistic (a log power spectrum for Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, histograms for Lorenz) on hidden test sets, and calls self-reporting "a flawed premise" ([Wyder et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166); [Kutz et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04001)). Topological analysis is the sharpest and least automated test: for three-dimensional flows, linking numbers and relative rotation rates of unstable periodic orbits determine a template, and a model whose orbits' invariants differ from the data's is rejected; the test falsifies and cannot confirm **[R]** ([Gilmore 1998](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.70.1455)). **No benchmark found scores periodic-orbit or template invariants**, so integer-valued topological scoring would be a distinguishing contribution **[I]**. The honest score for equation recovery has two parts, structural and parametric, reported apart from forecast skill **[I]**.

## 4. Exact braid algorithms are short; soundness under sampling has one certified precedent

### 4.1 Three independent solvers decide the word problem, and nine conventions must be fixed

Artin's presentation of B_n, the Garside element Δ with centre ⟨Δ²⟩, and torsion-freeness are in González-Meneses's survey ([arXiv:1010.0321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.0321)). Three exact solutions of the word problem are practical. The left-greedy normal form writes every braid uniquely as Δ^p a₁⋯a_r with left-weighted simple factors, in O(ℓ² n log n) for a word of length ℓ ([arXiv:1010.0321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.0321)). Dehornoy's handle reduction always terminates and a reduced word is trivial iff empty, but its complexity "remains unknown" though it "is very efficient in practice" ([Dehornoy, arXiv:math/0703666](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703666)); it is believed cubic, "but this is currently only a conjecture" ([Wiest, arXiv:math/0211169](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0211169)). Dynnikov's coordinates map a word to 2n integers by max-plus updates; a word is trivial iff the coordinates return to (0,1,…,0,1), in quadratic time with only addition and maximum ([arXiv:math/0703666](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703666)). **The three share no code, so each checks the others** **[I]**; the suggested order of implementation is Dynnikov coordinates over unbounded integers, then handle reduction (which also decides Dehornoy's ordering), then the greedy normal form, which is needed anyway for gcd, lcm and conjugacy **[I]**. For conjugacy the standard is the set of sliding circuits of Gebhardt and González-Meneses, a finite invariant contained in the ultra summit set, whose worst case is no better than the classical approach ([arXiv:0808.1430](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1430)) and whose size can be exponential in the number of strands ([arXiv:1008.0238](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0238), as summarised in search results). No polynomial bound is known, so conjugacy should be offered with its cost stated **[I]**. The automaton on n! simple braids recognising normal forms by descent sets is recalled **[R]**, and its left/right convention must be fixed by tests.

The sources disagree on conventions, and the two sign conventions in circulation are mirror images **[I]**: Thiffeault, braidlab and Bigelow take σᵢ clockwise (the left point passes above); González-Meneses and Guillemot–Lairez take it counterclockwise. An implementer fixes and records nine things **[I]**: the sign of σᵢ; the reading order of words and whether braids act on the left or right; the form of Artin's action on the free group (González-Meneses's differs from SageMath's); the Burau matrices and which reduced basis; the Lawrence–Krammer variables (Krammer's t is Bigelow's −t, per [arXiv:1404.0724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.0724)); the normalisation of Dynnikov coordinates (2n−4 in Thiffeault, 2n in Dehornoy); the projection axis and tie-break for configurations; the direction of Dehornoy's ordering; and indexing from 0 or 1. No published table of normal forms or summit sets was found, so reference values must be generated.

### 4.2 Burau at four strands is no longer simply "open"

Burau is faithful for n ≤ 3 and unfaithful for n ≥ 5 ([Burau representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burau_representation), secondary); Bigelow gives explicit kernel elements of length 120 for n = 5 and 44 for n = 6 ([arXiv:math/9904100](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9904100)). For n = 4, **Bharathram, Birman and Brendle posted a claimed proof of faithfulness in July 2026, revised in September 2026**, with the consequence that the Jones representation is also faithful for n = 4 ([arXiv:2607.05283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05283)); it "has not yet been verified by the mathematical community", and the proof was not read. Code and notes should rely on it in neither direction **[I]**. The Lawrence–Krammer–Bigelow representation, of dimension n(n−1)/2, is faithful for all n ([Bigelow, arXiv:math/0005038](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0005038)). Bigelow's kernel words are the best joint test: Burau must return the identity, Lawrence–Krammer must not, and every word-problem solver must report nontrivial **[I]**. The Alexander polynomial of a closure follows from the reduced Burau matrix, and SageMath computes Burau in four forms, Lawrence–Krammer and Temperley–Lieb matrices, and the Alexander and Jones polynomials ([SageMath braid groups](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/groups/sage/groups/braid.html)). The entrywise Lawrence–Krammer formula and the exact Temperley–Lieb normalisation for the Jones polynomial were not retrieved. Pairwise linking numbers are the abelianisation of the pure braid group and so do not determine a braid **[R]**.

### 4.3 Entropy comes from exact integer coordinates, bounded below by a Burau spectral radius

B_n modulo its centre is the mapping class group of the n-punctured disc, and every braid is periodic, reducible or pseudo-Anosov ([arXiv:1010.0321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.0321)). Thiffeault's piecewise-linear update rules for Dynnikov coordinates of a lamination, in 2n−4 integers with σᵢ clockwise, were copied in the notes through a summarising fetch and must be checked character by character before use; the entropy of a braid is the growth rate of a loop's length under iteration, converging as 1/m ([arXiv:0906.3647](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3647)). The rules use only +, −, max and min, so they are exact over unbounded integers, and the entropy is a limit of logarithms of exact integers, to be reported with its iteration count **[I]**. The Burau estimate bounds the topological entropy below by the logarithm of the spectral radius of B(η) over |η| = 1; for a pseudo-Anosov braid it can be sharp at a root of unity only at η = −1, under a condition on the singularities of the invariant foliations ([Band and Boyland, arXiv:math/0612716](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612716)). At η = −1 the matrix is integral and the bound an algebraic number **[I]**. Bestvina–Handel train tracks are exact and, per the braidlab guide, impractical for large braids ([arXiv:1410.0849](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0849)). The standard first test, σ₁σ₂⁻¹ with dilatation (3 + √5)/2, is recalled **[R]**. flipper (MIT) computes Nielsen–Thurston types and dilatations ([flipper](https://github.com/MarkCBell/flipper)).

### 4.4 The braid of an open trajectory is a morphism in a groupoid, and a projection is a trivialisation

The standard construction projects particles on a line, records each exchange of neighbours as σᵢ^{±1} with the sign read from the perpendicular coordinate, and orders crossings by time found by linear interpolation; anything beyond pair and the two triple crossings raises an error asking for finer data ([Thiffeault 2010, arXiv:0906.3647](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3647)). braidlab judges coincident projections by an absolute tolerance of 10⁻¹⁰ ([braidlab guide](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0849)). For periodic motions a change of projection line conjugates the braid; for open trajectories it "changes the braid beyond simple conjugation", by a small, finite number of generators ([arXiv:0906.3647](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3647)), and repeated application of such a braid is unjustified, hence the non-iterated finite-time braiding exponent ([Budišić and Thiffeault, arXiv:1502.02162](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02162)). A 2026 study of polar active matter reports braid measures changing with the projection angle through crossings at the two ends ([arXiv:2609.04062](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04062)).

The clean treatment is outside the fluid-dynamics literature. Fox and Neuwirth's stratification of the unordered configuration space has exactly one top cell, the configurations with pairwise distinct real parts, and its codimension-1 and codimension-2 cells give Artin's generators and relations ([arXiv:1010.0321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.0321)). Guillemot and Lairez define the braid of an arbitrary path γ through straight-line canonical paths from the base configuration, matched in lexicographic order, which never collide; the braid of a concatenation is the product of the braids ([arXiv:2601.23073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23073)). The top cell is convex in sorted coordinates, hence contractible; the sources read do not use that word, and the argument is the notes' own **[I]**. It follows that **an open trajectory has a well-defined invariant, a morphism in the fundamental groupoid of the configuration space between its endpoint configurations, and the word in B_n is its coordinate in the trivialisation a projection direction gives** **[I]**. Another direction changes the word by a permutation braid at each end, of at most n(n−1)/2 generators, determined by the endpoint configuration alone; for a closed path the two corrections are inverse and the result is conjugation. This makes Thiffeault's "small, finite number of generators" exact **[I]**. braidlab's closure options are choices, not canonical **[I]**. Applications read in the session include coherent structures from loops of negligible growth ([Allshouse and Thiffeault, arXiv:1106.2231](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2231)), stirring protocols classified by Thurston–Nielsen theory ([Boyland, Aref and Stremler](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/boyland/files/stir.pdf)) and the figure-eight orbit's braid (b₁b₂⁻¹)³ ([arXiv:2601.09843](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09843v2)); Moore's and Montgomery's papers were read only through index records.

### 4.5 The dynamics literature proves no sampling condition; the certified theory was written for polynomial roots

**No paper found in the trajectory-braid literature proves a sufficient condition on sampling for the computed braid to equal the true one, and none propagates measurement uncertainty to a set or distribution of braids** **[I]**. What exists there is qualitative: Thiffeault gives no quantitative requirement and says "missing a few crossings is acceptable if the trajectories are long enough" ([arXiv:0906.3647](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3647)); Budišić and Thiffeault require that strands cross only between samples and any two at most once per step, and find braiding exponents "more resilient to large time steps than word length", with no error bound ([arXiv:1502.02162](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02162)).

The rigorous treatment is Guillemot and Lairez, *Computing braids from approximate data* (January 2026). They note that exact algorithms rely on a lexicographic order "which is unstable under numerical uncertainty". Their input is a separation predicate: for any two strands and a time, an axis (real or imaginary) and a later time up to which the strands are strictly ordered along that axis. Arrangement cells, defined by pairs of strict partial orders, are nonempty, convex and open and cover the ordered configuration space; two paths covered by one sequence of cells are homotopic; and their on-the-fly algorithm returns a braid β with a theorem that the path through β is homotopic to the true one ([arXiv:2601.23073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23073)). Their predicate for certified root tracking halves a time step until interval boxes separate. Prior certified work they cite includes Marco-Buzunariz and Rodríguez's SIROCCO; their own implementation is in Rust, and its licence was not checked.

A sound method for sampled trajectories can be built on that theorem, and this step is the notes' inference, not a published result **[I]**. If every particle's speed is at most v and 2vΔt < |z_j − z_i| at the start of a step for every pair, each relative vector and its linear interpolant stay in one convex disc missing the origin, so the straight-line homotopy stays in the configuration space and the braid read from the linear interpolant is the true braid of that step. The argument must be a homotopy in configuration space, since pairwise winding numbers do not determine a braid. Where the condition fails, the honest answer is three-valued: undecided at this step for this pair, or the finite set of braids compatible with the enclosures; measurement noise of radius ε enlarges the discs. The robotics and topological-data-analysis literatures were not searched beyond one result, so a guarantee may exist there; no probabilistic treatment was found.

### 4.6 Separate structures model strands that vanish, merge or are inserted, and none models merging and splitting together

| Structure | Source | Appear | Disappear | Merge | Split |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inverse braid monoid IB_n | Easdown and Lavers 2004 | as the reverse of deletion | yes | no | no |
| Vines | Lavers 1997 | no | no | yes, permanently | no (the opposite category) |
| Braided injections | Schlichtkrull and Solberg | yes | no | no | no |
| Singular braid monoid | Baez; Birman | no | no | touch at an instant | no |
| Braid category | Joyal and Street | no | no | no | no |
| Tangle categories | recalled **[R]** | in pairs | in pairs | no | no |

Sources: [Vershinin, arXiv:0704.3002](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.3002); [nLab, vine](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/vine); [arXiv:1403.1101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1101); [nLab, braid category](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/braid+category).

The inverse braid monoid consists of partial braids with an explicit presentation, and its word problem is solved by an embedding into partial isomorphisms of a free group ([arXiv:0704.3002](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.3002)). Vines are arcs that, once met, stay together; their category is the free braided strict monoidal category containing a braided monoid ([nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/vine)); Lavers's paper itself was not read. Braided injections form a braided monoidal category whose endomorphism monoids are the braid groups ([arXiv:1403.1101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1101)). **None of the structures found models merging and splitting together with braiding in one presented category** **[I]**; that would be a braided category generated by a monoid and a comonoid object, and nothing was read on it. A singular crossing may serve to represent an undecided crossing **[I]**. On networks the invariants are graph braid groups, computed from Abrams's discretised configuration space by Farley and Sabalka's discrete Morse theory ([arXiv:math/0410539](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410539)). In dimension three and above, for a simply connected manifold the pure braid group is trivial and the braid group is the symmetric group ([arXiv:1311.5642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5642)), so trajectories carry only their permutation; loop braid groups, the motion groups of unlinked circles, replace braids there ([Damiani, arXiv:1605.02323](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02323)).

### 4.7 The operator lifts sort into theorems, exact constructions and speculation

In this subsection most classical statements are recalled **[R]** with their standard citations; the notes opened the recent papers and the SageMath manual.

**Operator-algebraic lifts are theorems and are exactly computable.** Jones's index theorem and the Temperley–Lieb relations of the Jones projections give the representation σᵢ ↦ (t + 1)eᵢ − 1, unitary on a Hilbert space exactly at t = e^{±2πi/r} **[R]** ([Jones 1983](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01389127); [Jones 1987](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971403)). Hecke algebras with Ocneanu's trace give HOMFLY, Birman–Murakami–Wenzl algebras the Kauffman polynomial, and any Yang–Baxter operator a representation on V^{⊗n} **[R]** ([Turaev 1988](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01393746)). Verified recent results: every unitary R-matrix defines an endomorphism of a Cuntz algebra and a homomorphism of B_∞ into a II₁ factor ([Conti and Lechner, arXiv:1909.04127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04127)), and a March 2026 preprint treats unitary R-matrices with two eigenvalues ([arXiv:2603.20158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20158), abstract level); no classification of all unitary R-matrices in dimension three or more was found. For generic parameters every matrix has entries in a Laurent polynomial ring over ℤ, and at roots of unity in a cyclotomic field if a seminormal basis is used; unitarity can be certified exactly through a positive-definite Gram matrix, as Squier does for Burau **[I]**. For a fixed number of strands the Jones polynomial of a word of length L costs L multiplications of matrices of Catalan size; the #P-hardness lives in the growth of n **[I]**, so trajectory braids with few strands and long words are in the cheap regime. A ribbon category's fusion rules, F-symbols and R-symbols give the unitary action of a braid on fusion spaces, σᵢ ↦ F⁻¹RF **[R]** ([Kitaev 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0506438)); the quantum 6j symbols a Turaev–Viro implementation evaluates are the F-matrices of SU(2)_k, so braid matrices need only bookkeeping of fusion trees **[I]**. Density of the braid image at r = 5 **[R]** ([Freedman, Larsen and Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103200)) does not imply faithfulness **[I]**.

**In quantum gravity, kinematics is rigorous, deformation is postulated in four dimensions, and braids-as-particles is speculative.** The holonomy–flux algebra, its unique diffeomorphism-invariant state and the discrete area spectrum are theorems within the canonical framework **[R]** ([Lewandowski et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0504147)). Turaev–Viro is the q-deformation of Ponzano–Regge in three dimensions; in four, Dupuis and Girelli write that "there is no Chern–Simons theory to guide us. Hence, it is postulated that the cosmological constant should also be introduced through a quantum group structure" ([arXiv:1311.6841](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6841)). The identification of braided ribbon networks with Standard Model particles is a programme with a kinematical classification and no derived masses or interactions; its 2012 review only suggests that the trivalent and tetravalent schemes may converge ([Bilson-Thompson et al., arXiv:1109.0080](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0080v1)). That the programme has been largely dormant since is an inference from search results **[I]**. The reusable part for trajectory braids is the framed calculus, twists and braidings evaluated in a ribbon category **[I]**.

**In noncommutative geometry the established results on braid groups are K-theoretic.** Baum–Connes holds for the braid groups ([Schick, arXiv:math/0209165](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0209165v1)); K₀ and K₁ of the reduced C\*-algebra of the pure braid group P_n are each ℤ^{n!/2}, and of B₃ each ℤ, with the direct computation for more strands stated to remain open ([Azzali et al., arXiv:2105.07848](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.07848)). **No spectral-triple or cyclic-cohomology treatment specific to braid groups or to configuration spaces of points was found**; "configuration space" in the spectral-triple literature found means spaces of gauge connections ([Aastrup and Grimstrup, arXiv:2504.03391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03391)). Braided L∞-algebras organise noncommutative gauge theories ([Dimitrijević Ćirić et al., arXiv:2103.08939](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.08939)), with the caveat that their braiding is triangular, so the symmetric group, not the braid group, acts on tensor powers **[R]**.

**Dynamical systems lift to operators in three compatible ways** **[R]**: Koopman and transfer operators; C\*-dynamical systems with crossed products and KMS states; and, after symbolic coding, Cuntz–Krieger algebras, whose K₀ is the Bowen–Franks group coker(1 − Aᵀ) (verified through surveys: [arXiv:2212.11188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11188); [arXiv:1603.06174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.06174)). For a subshift of finite type the zeta function 1/det(1 − zA) is exact and rational **[R]**. Creation and annihilation of strands with a braiding has an operator model in Bożejko and Speicher's Fock space deformed by a self-adjoint contraction satisfying the Yang–Baxter equation **[R]** ([Bożejko and Speicher 1994](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01450478)), now studied as twisted Araki–Woods algebras ([Correa da Silva and Lechner 2023](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00220-023-04773-y.pdf), search-summary level); its Gram operator sums over the symmetric group, and non-involutive unitary braidings need the anyonic constructions instead **[I]**. Three items are the notes' own assemblies, found in no paper **[I]**: the decomposition ℂ[IB_n] ≅ ⊕_k M_{C(n,k)}(ℂ[B_k]) from Steinberg's theorem, to be checked before use; the chain from a pseudo-Anosov braid's train-track matrix to a Cuntz–Krieger algebra whose unique gauge-KMS state sits at inverse temperature equal to the braid's entropy; and the observation that no paper found combines Koopman operators with braid representations, the Burau estimate being the one theorem that links a braid's operator spectrum to a dynamical invariant. No C\*-algebra of the inverse braid monoid was found in the literature.

## 5. Existing tools cover the parts, under licences that mostly allow comparison and forbid borrowing

Versions, dates and licences in this part were read on 2026-11-12 from package registries, release feeds and licence files unless marked.

### 5.1 Continuation tools divide on normal-form coefficients, and analysis libraries lack continuation and manifolds

**BifurcationKit.jl (MIT, v0.8.9, pushed 2026-11-11) is the only continuation tool found that is permissively licensed, scriptable and actively released** ([licence](https://github.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationKit.jl/blob/HEAD/LICENSE.md)). It detects and computes normal forms for fold, Hopf and branch points and for the codimension-2 points Bogdanov–Takens, Bautin, cusp, zero-Hopf and Hopf–Hopf, and continues periodic orbits by shooting and collocation ([capabilities](https://docs.sciml.ai/BifurcationKit/dev/capabilities)). Its Lorenz-84 tutorial prints usable numbers: Hopf points at F = 2.85996783, 2.46723305 and 1.61975642, a Bogdanov–Takens point at (F, T) ≈ (1.44672, 0.02094) with coefficients a = 0.21442 and b = 0.60651; the tutorial says the model is also treated in MatCont and does not state that the numbers agree ([Lorenz-84 tutorial](https://docs.sciml.ai/BifurcationKit/stable/tutorials/ode/lorenz84/)). MatCont (MATLAB; 7p6 of February 2026) is the reference for normal-form coefficients, periodic ones included, and now imports delay equations ([arXiv:2504.12785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12785)). AUTO-07p reports types and locations; its newest tag is v0.9.3 of 2021, GitHub detects no licence file, and SourceForge lists BSD, GPLv2 and LGPLv2 together ([repository](https://github.com/auto-07p/auto-07p); [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/projects/auto-07p/)), so it should be treated as encumbered until checked file by file **[I]**. COCO, DDE-BIFTOOL and pde2path (GPL) also need MATLAB; the licences of MatCont, COCO and DDE-BIFTOOL were not shown on the pages read. A new core that reports coefficients can therefore be held to MatCont's derivations and to BifurcationKit, and to AUTO only for locations **[I]**.

DynamicalSystems.jl (MIT, v3.7.0) is the broadest library for chaos and nonlinear time series: Lyapunov spectra, chaos tests, Poincaré maps, basins and their fractions, global continuation of attractors, recurrence quantification, embeddings, dimensions, complexity measures and surrogates ([contents](https://juliadynamics.github.io/DynamicalSystemsDocs.jl/dynamicalsystems/stable/contents/)). It has no local continuation yet and does not mention invariant manifolds. **No tool in this group computes invariant manifolds of fixed points and periodic orbits as a first-class result** **[I]**: PyDSTool lists them as missing ([overview](https://pydstool.github.io/PyDSTool/ProjectOverview.html)) and XPPAUT computes one-dimensional invariant sets only ([XPPAUT](https://sites.pitt.edu/~phase/bard/bardware/xpp/whatis.html)). TISEAN is frozen at version 3.0.1 of 2007 under GPL ([TISEAN](https://www.pks.mpg.de/tisean/)). The integrator stacks are permissive (SUNDIALS v7.9.0 and JiTC\*DE under BSD-3-Clause, PETSc under BSD-2-Clause, DifferentialEquations.jl under MIT), with Dedalus under GPL-3.0 ([SUNDIALS licence](https://github.com/LLNL/sundials/blob/HEAD/LICENSE); [Dedalus licence](https://github.com/DedalusProject/dedalus/blob/HEAD/LICENSE.txt)).

### 5.2 Rigorous numerics is GPL at its centre, with MIT homology and set-oriented pieces around it

CAPD (C++, v6.0.0, GPL-3.0 in its `COPYING`) certifies trajectories, Poincaré maps and their derivatives with interval arithmetic and Lohner-type set representations ([CAPD](https://github.com/CAPDGroup/CAPD); [arXiv:2010.07097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07097)). The MIT pieces are CHomP and pychomp2 for homology, CMGDB for Conley–Morse graphs, DSGRN for the combinatorial dynamics of switching networks ([DSGRN](https://pypi.org/project/DSGRN/)), GAIO.jl for set-oriented numerics with transfer operators by Ulam's method, Morse graphs and Conley indices ([GAIO.jl](https://gaioguys.github.io/GAIO.jl/)), IntervalArithmetic.jl, and Ripser and GUDHI for persistent homology. Ariadne and Flow\* (GPL-3.0), teaspoon (GPL-3.0), giotto-tda (AGPL v3) and TBarrier (GPL-3.0) are compare-only; the MATLAB LCS Tool has no licence file. What existing code certifies is enclosures of trajectories and Poincaré maps, periodic orbits and symbolic dynamics by covering relations, and Morse graphs on box coverings, all in low dimension **[I]**. Tucker's proof of the Lorenz attractor combined normal forms near the origin with rigorous computation ([Tucker](https://www2.math.uu.se/~watuc425/main/papers/icses08l.pdf)). No wall-clock or box-count figures for standard computer-assisted proofs were found, so "at what cost" is unanswered.

### 5.3 QuTiP is the quantum oracle, and sampler libraries agree on three separations

QuTiP 5.3.1 (BSD-3-Clause, with a Windows wheel for Python 3.12) has every standard solver, and its paper tabulates their memory scaling: `mesolve` d⁴, `mcsolve` N_T·d², `smesolve` N_T·d⁴, with Bloch–Redfield noted as possibly not completely positive ([QuTiP 5, arXiv:2412.04705](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04705v2); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/qutip/)). That d⁴ against N_T·d² trade is the reason to offer trajectories beside the master equation **[I]**. QuantumToolbox.jl (BSD-3-Clause) claims in its paper to outperform QuTiP, QuantumOptics.jl and dynamiqs "across all tested scenarios", with results given only as a figure ([arXiv:2504.21440](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21440v1)). No numerical CPU timings by Hilbert-space dimension were found in text for any package. The safest quantum fixtures come from running QuTiP with tight tolerances, comparing stochastic solvers in distribution, not path by path **[I]**.

Every mature sampling library separates a source of bits, a distribution object, and an inference algorithm that needs only a log density up to a constant **[I]**: Stan's NUTS on a potential "up to a constant" ([Stan reference manual](https://mc-stan.org/docs/reference-manual/mcmc.html)), BlackJAX's kernels with `init` and `step` over an explicit key and state ([BlackJAX](https://blackjax-devs.github.io/blackjax/)), Distributions.jl's `Sampleable` against `Distribution` ([types](https://juliastats.org/Distributions.jl/stable/types/)). Counter-based generators are now standard for keyed streams: NumPy's Philox, JAX's Threefry, and from C++26 `std::philox_engine`, whose `philox4x32` constants are fixed by the standard ([cppreference](https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/numeric/random/philox_engine)); Boost.Random's reference lists none. Portable variates are a separate problem from portable bits: NumPy guarantees only the integer stream, so a C++ core that needs identical normals everywhere owns its transformation from bits to variates **[I]**. **No mainstream library was found that represents a distribution by its score as a first-class object**; only specialised packages take a score function. StochasticDiffEq.jl documents strong and weak orders per solver (Euler–Maruyama strong 0.5, `RKMil` 1.0, `SOSRI` strong 1.5 and weak 2.0), on a page that contradicts itself about whether its high-weak-order methods are adaptive ([SciML SDE solvers](https://docs.sciml.ai/DiffEqDocs/stable/solvers/sde_solve/)).

### 5.4 Braid software is almost all GPL, and nothing runs from trajectories to operator invariants

braidlab (MATLAB with C++ MEX files, GPL-3.0) builds braids from trajectory data and computes Dynnikov coordinates, entropy, finite-time braiding exponents, Burau and Lawrence–Krammer matrices ([braidlab](https://github.com/jeanluct/braidlab); [guide](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0849)); **it is the only tool found that computes topological entropy of braids built from data**. SageMath's `BraidGroup` (GPL) with libbraiding supplies normal forms, gcd and lcm, summit sets, sliding circuits, Thurston type, Burau in four forms, Lawrence–Krammer and Temperley–Lieb matrices, Markov traces and the Alexander and Jones polynomials ([SageMath](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/groups/sage/groups/braid.html)). CBraid, Braiding and libbraiding are GPL; Magma is proprietary; flipper is MIT; braidpy is MPL-2.0, an alpha project built by one person with AI coding tools ([braidpy](https://braidpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)). Braids.jl does algebra (composition, Burau, Dehornoy reduction) and mentions neither entropy nor trajectories. For categories and anyons, TensorCategories.jl is MIT by its citation metadata but depends on GPL OSCAR, the TensorKit family covers fusion and braiding of sectors (licence recalled as MIT **[R]**), and Anyonica needs Mathematica ([AnyonWiki software list](https://anyonwiki.github.io/pages/Lists/Software.html)). There is no maintained general-purpose library for spectral triples. **No package was found that takes trajectory data to operator-algebraic invariants end to end**: braidlab stops at words, entropy and loop coordinates, and SageMath starts at words **[I]**.

GPL code may not be copied or linked into an MIT library; running it to produce fixtures is permitted, since output values are data **[I]**. The fixtures by oracle are then **[I]**: normal forms, summit sets, Thurston types and all representation matrices from SageMath with libbraiding under WSL, each with its convention map recorded; Dynnikov coordinates and entropy from closed forms and from flipper, since braidlab needs MATLAB; trajectory braids from synthetic trajectories of known braid (a rigid rotation gives Δ² per turn), not from another program's output; quantum references from QuTiP; continuation from BifurcationKit.jl; SDE convergence from SciML's analytic problems, which need no oracle; enclosures from CAPD under WSL; spiking networks from Brian 2 and NEST and neural masses from The Virtual Brain, all copyleft and used only as external oracles.

### 5.5 Only one environment is project-based, and no single tool covers the five requirements

Among interactive environments, only The Virtual Brain (GPL v3 or later) organises work as projects with a history of operations and a tree of derived results ([TVB user guide](https://docs.thevirtualbrain.org/manuals/UserGuide/UserGuide-UI_Project.html)); COPASI (Artistic License 2.0; ODE, SDE and Gillespie simulation with parameter estimation; [copasi.org](https://copasi.org/)), Berkeley Madonna (commercial), XPPAUT and MatCont are organised around one model file. The documented complaints give three requirements **[I]**: text-based, mergeable project files, since "by default Git cannot merge automatically with .slx files" ([MathWorks Answers](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/308058-how-do-i-use-git-for-version-handling-simulink-files)); input validated with reasons; and results that leave the tool easily, since XPPLORE exists only to move XPPAUT's output into MATLAB ([arXiv:2507.02709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02709v1)), and XPPAUT's author himself calls its interface "somewhat idiosyncratic and primitive".

**No single tool was found that combines classical and open quantum dynamics, stochastic systems, model discovery, braids and topological tools, and a project-based graphical interface.** A search for such a platform returned only PySINDy and its GUI wrapper SEED 2.0, which cover discovery for classical data alone ([SEED 2.0](https://github.com/M-Vause/SEED2.0)). The closest are ecosystems **[I]**: Julia's SciML with JuliaDynamics and QuantumToolbox.jl covers the first three as separate packages with different state types, has persistent homology and intervals but no braids from trajectories, and no project interface was found; MATLAB with MatCont, braidlab and Simulink has braids under GPL, no rigorous numerics and no single environment, on a commercial base; XPPAUT, QuTiP, COPASI and The Virtual Brain each cover one column. This is a claim of absence resting on searches that found nothing; commercial suites were not examined feature by feature. The gap a new application fills is therefore the conjunction: one model description with classical, stochastic and open quantum evolutions under one project and provenance model, discovery in the same project, and topological readings beside the numerical ones **[I]**.

## 6. Corrections, conflicts and gaps, stated plainly

### 6.1 Three earlier assumptions need amending

The research brief carried claims from memory, and three need changing. First, "Burau is open for n = 4" is out of date: a proof of faithfulness has been claimed and is unverified ([arXiv:2607.05283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05283)). The notes are not consistent on this: the note on operator lifts still records the case as open, from memory, while the note on algorithms records the preprint from the arXiv record. The latter governs. Second, "point braids are trivial in dimension three and above" is imprecise: the braid group of a simply connected manifold of dimension at least three is the symmetric group, so the permutation survives ([arXiv:1311.5642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5642)). Third, the expectation that a soundness theory for sampled trajectory braids exists in the dynamics literature is wrong as far as the searches reached; the certified theory is Guillemot and Lairez's, for root paths. Smaller corrections: Moore's 1993 paper is titled "Braids in classical dynamics" and concerns n-body orbits, not point vortices; no package named "pybraids" was found; and all other citations in the brief (sliding circuits, Dynnikov, Band and Boyland, Thiffeault, Budišić and Thiffeault, Easdown and Lavers, Lavers's vines, Schlichtkrull and Solberg, Farley and Sabalka) exist as given.

### 6.2 Sources conflict in at least a dozen places

Conflicts between sources, none resolved in the session: the Ghia transcription against a search summary of the same page; dysts' README (10 maps) against its JSON (26); Antimony's BSD-style licence file against "MIT" on PyPI; FeynRules' GPL-3 repository against "Licensing provisions: None" in its 2014 paper; CAPD as GPL on its own pages against LGPLv3 on a SourceForge listing; Wolfram's pages naming System Modeler 15 and 14.3 as latest; SciML's SDE page on adaptivity; and The Virtual Brain's Jansen–Rit default against its own docstring. The notes also disagree with each other on three registry facts: StochasticDiffEq.jl appears as v6.100.0 in one note and 7.2.0 in another; braidlab appears with "no releases" in one and with release 3.4.1 of 2026-09-29 in another; and ModelingToolkit's documentation version 11.46.1 sits beside a latest release tag named `ModelingToolkitBase-v1.80.0`. Each should be re-read at a pinned date before it is written into a design. The "context parroting" criticism of time-series foundation models is attributed by a search summary to a group other than the authors of the paper under which it was found, and its primary reference was not retrieved.

### 6.3 The gaps cluster in four places

The largest gaps follow the access failures of the day. Scholarpedia was unreachable, so several neural and map values are recalled. Primary tables were not read for Wolf et al., Ghia et al., Chandrasekhar, Sprott's Appendix A, Hodgkin and Huxley, and Hänggi, Talkner and Borkovec. Several specification pages returned errors: the CellML 2.0 informative specification (its `reset` element and mandatory units are recalled), NESTML's pages on units and random functions, the Kappa manual host, and Karras et al.'s EDM paper. The whole networks-and-lattices catalogue and nearly all classical method papers in Part 3 are recalled.

Four substantive questions remain open. For languages, no published comparative criticism of these languages was retrieved, and AGG, cpfg, lattice-code input formats and classical Lagrangian tools were not researched. For induction, learning from continuous quantum measurement records, estimation of self-exciting processes, LLM-guided equation discovery and false-discovery rates under library misspecification were not researched or not found. For braids, the robotics and topological-data-analysis literatures may hold a sampling guarantee; the Rust implementation's licence, the entrywise Lawrence–Krammer formula, the Temperley–Lieb normalisation and tangle categories were not read; the status of the n = 4 preprint will change. For tools, no CPU timings were found in text for any quantum package or any computer-assisted proof, the licences of MatCont, COCO and DDE-BIFTOOL were not shown, and the CPU cost of training small diffusion models is unsourced judgement.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the shape of the design problem. The foundation's language does not need to be a union of existing notations; it needs a semantics in which a reaction, a rule, a jump, a noise term and a collapse operator are summands of one generator, a flow is its deterministic limit, and an action is admitted with the derivation that turns it into a generator stated as a step. The six unions no language states are then cases of one design, and the two prior statements nearest to it, dynamical grammars and the Lindblad–master-equation parallel, become its precedents. The same reading applies to data: every method the library should own is closed-form, every one returns a model up to an equivalence the library can name, and the benchmark evidence says the report of a fit matters as much as the fit, since a zero prediction beat most published methods on a hidden test set and one of twenty-five symbolic methods recovered one first-principles law.

The braid part offers the clearest contribution of its own. Treating the braid of an open trajectory as a groupoid morphism, reading it through a separation predicate, and answering "undecided at this step for this pair" where a speed bound does not separate two particles would give the dynamics literature the soundness statement it lacks, on a theorem that already exists for another kind of data. That statement, the typed mode change, the declarative lattice or group-field-theory action, the score as a first-class distribution, and topological invariants as a benchmark score are five places where the notes found nothing published. Each is an absence established by search, and each should be searched once more, in the literatures the notes did not reach, before it is claimed.
