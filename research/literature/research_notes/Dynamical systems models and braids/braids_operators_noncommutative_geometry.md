# Braids, Trajectories and Dynamical Systems Lifted to Operators: Operator-Algebraic, Quantum-Gravity and Noncommutative-Geometric Treatments (state of knowledge, November 2026)

*Reading key.* Each finding is tagged by kind and by how it was checked.

- Kind: **(D)** definition; **(T)** theorem; **(C)** standard construction computable exactly in finite terms; **(S)** speculative physical proposal or conjecture.
- Check: `[v]` the source was opened or its abstract/record confirmed by search in this session (2026-11-12); `[r]` the statement is given from the standard literature with its citation, but the source was **not** re-opened in this session, so exact normalisations and theorem numbers should be checked against the source before being quoted verbatim.
- Conventions differ between sources in almost every formula below (the sign and power of $q$, $t = A^{-4}$ versus $q = A^{2}$ versus $q=A^4$, left- versus right-handed crossings, colours $a = 2j$ versus spins $j$, and complex-conjugate choices of $R$). ## 1. Braid Group Representations from Operator Algebras (Jones, Hecke, BMW, Yang–Baxter)

### Takeaway
Operator algebras give finite-dimensional representations of $B_n$ through the Temperley–Lieb, Hecke and BMW algebras and through Yang–Baxter operators, with Markov traces yielding the Jones, HOMFLY and Kauffman polynomials; the representations are unitary on a Hilbert space exactly at the roots of unity $t=e^{\pm2\pi i/r}$. Everything is exactly computable: over $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$ or $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm1},z]$ for generic parameters, and over cyclotomic fields at roots of unity (square roots of quantum integers appear only if an orthonormal basis is insisted on).

### Cited Findings

**Subfactors and Temperley–Lieb**

- **(T)** Jones's index theorem: for a subfactor $N \subset M$ of a type II$_1$ factor, $[M:N] \in \{4\cos^2(\pi/n) : n = 3,4,\dots\} \cup [4,\infty]$, and every such value occurs. The proof builds the tower $N\subset M\subset M_1\subset\cdots$ whose Jones projections $e_i$ satisfy $e_i^2=e_i=e_i^*$, $e_ie_{i\pm1}e_i=\tau e_i$, $e_ie_j=e_je_i$ ($|i-j|\ge2$), with $\tau = [M:N]^{-1}$, and a trace with $\mathrm{tr}(w e_{n}) = \tau\,\mathrm{tr}(w)$ for $w$ in the algebra generated by $e_1,\dots,e_{n-1}$. `[r]` — [Jones 1983, Invent. Math. 72, 1–25](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01389127)
- **(D/C)** The Jones representation: with $\tau^{-1} = 2+t+t^{-1}$, $\sigma_i \mapsto g_i = t e_i - (1-e_i) = (t+1)e_i - 1$ satisfies the braid relations (direct check: the braid relation holds iff $\tau = t/(1+t)^2$); $g_i$ has eigenvalues $t$ and $-1$, so it is unitary iff $|t|=1$. The Jones polynomial of the closure of $b\in B_n$ is a normalisation of $\mathrm{tr}(\pi(b))$: $V_{\hat b}(t) = \bigl(-(t+1)/\sqrt t\bigr)^{n-1}\,\mathrm{tr}(r_t(b))$ in Jones's normalisation with $r_t(\sigma_i)=\sqrt t\,(te_i-(1-e_i))$. `[r]` (normalisation to be checked against the source) — [Jones 1985, Bull. AMS 12, 103–111](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-1985-15304-2)
- **(T)** Positivity: the Markov trace on the Temperley–Lieb algebra is positive (so that the $e_i$ are represented by projections on a Hilbert space and the algebra is a finite-dimensional C\*-algebra) exactly for $\tau^{-1}\ge 4$ or $\tau^{-1}=4\cos^2(\pi/r)$; in the variable $t$: $t>0$ real, or $t=e^{\pm2\pi i/r}$, $r=3,4,5,\dots$. The braid representation is unitary only in the second family. `[r]` — [Jones 1983](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01389127); [Jones 1987, Ann. Math. 126, 335–388](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971403)
- **(D/C)** Kauffman's diagrammatic form: $TL_n(\delta)$ has generators $U_i$ with $U_i^2=\delta U_i$, $U_iU_{i\pm1}U_i=U_i$, far commutation; dimension the Catalan number $C_n$; $\rho(\sigma_i)=A\cdot 1 + A^{-1}U_i$ with $\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}$ gives the Kauffman bracket, and $V(t)$ at $t=A^{-4}$ after the writhe factor $(-A^3)^{-w}$. `[r]` — [Kauffman 1987, Topology 26, 395–407](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-9383(87)90009-7); [Kauffman and Lins 1994, Ann. Math. Studies 134](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400882533)
- **(T)** Alexander's and Markov's theorems (every link is a closed braid; two braids close to isotopic links iff related by conjugation and stabilisation $b\leftrightarrow b\sigma_n^{\pm1}$) are what make a "Markov trace" a link invariant. `[r]` — [Kassel and Turaev 2008, *Braid Groups*, GTM 247, ch. 2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-68548-9)

**Hecke algebras and the Ocneanu trace**

- **(D/T)** $H_n(q)$: generators $g_1,\dots,g_{n-1}$, braid relations and $g_i^2=(q-1)g_i+q$; dimension $n!$; semisimple for generic $q$ (fails only at roots of unity of small order). For every $z$ there is a unique trace with $\mathrm{tr}(1)=1$, $\mathrm{tr}(ab)=\mathrm{tr}(ba)$, $\mathrm{tr}(xg_n)=z\,\mathrm{tr}(x)$ for $x\in H_n(q)$ (Ocneanu). Suitably normalised it is the two-variable HOMFLY(-PT) polynomial. $TL_n$ is the quotient of $H_n(q)$ by the ideal generated by $g_1g_2g_1+g_1g_2+g_2g_1+g_1+g_2+1$, and the trace factors through it at $z=-1/(1+q)$. `[r]` — [Jones 1987](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971403); [Freyd, Yetter, Hoste, Lickorish, Millett, Ocneanu 1985, Bull. AMS 12, 239–246](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-1985-15361-3)
- **(T)** Wenzl: at $q=e^{\pm2\pi i/l}$ the Hecke algebra has C\*-representations $\pi_\lambda^{(k,l)}$ indexed by $(k,l)$-diagrams (Young diagrams with at most $k$ rows and $\lambda_1-\lambda_k\le l-k$), giving unitary braid representations and subfactors of index $\sin^2(k\pi/l)/\sin^2(\pi/l)$. `[r]` — [Wenzl 1988, Invent. Math. 92, 349–383](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01404457)
- **(C)** The Hecke-algebra method computes HOMFLY of a closed braid in time polynomial in the number of crossings for a fixed number of strands. `[r]` — [Morton and Short 1990, J. Algorithms 11, 117–131](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-6774(90)90033-B)

**Birman–Murakami–Wenzl**

- **(D/T)** The BMW algebra $C_n(l,m)$ is generated by invertible $g_i$ and $e_i$ with the braid relations, a cubic relation for $g_i$ (three eigenvalues), and tangle relations; it carries a Markov trace giving the two-variable Kauffman polynomial; generically it is semisimple of dimension $(2n-1)!!$ and is the centraliser algebra for quantum orthogonal and symplectic groups (as Hecke is for $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_N)$ and TL for $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$). `[r]` — [Birman and Wenzl 1989, Trans. AMS 313, 249–273](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1989-0992598-X)

**Yang–Baxter operators**

- **(D/T)** An invertible $R\in\mathrm{End}(V\otimes V)$ with $(R\otimes 1)(1\otimes R)(R\otimes 1)=(1\otimes R)(R\otimes1)(1\otimes R)$ gives $\rho_n:B_n\to GL(V^{\otimes n})$, $\sigma_i\mapsto 1^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes R\otimes 1^{\otimes(n-i-1)}$. Turaev: an "enhanced" Yang–Baxter operator $(R,\mu,\alpha,\beta)$ gives a link invariant $\alpha^{-w(b)}\beta^{-n}\,\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_n(b)\,\mu^{\otimes n})$; the $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_N)$ R-matrices recover HOMFLY specialisations. `[r]` — [Turaev 1988, Invent. Math. 92, 527–553](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01393746)
- **(T)** Jimbo: the R-matrix of the vector representation of $U_q(\mathfrak{gl}_N)$ satisfies the Hecke quadratic relation, and $H_n(q)$ maps onto the commutant of $U_q(\mathfrak{gl}_N)$ on $V^{\otimes n}$ (quantum Schur–Weyl duality). `[r]` — [Jimbo 1986, Lett. Math. Phys. 11, 247–252](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00400222)
- **(T)** Conti and Lechner: every unitary R-matrix in dimension $d$ is a unitary of the Cuntz algebra $\mathcal O_d$ and so defines an endomorphism $\lambda_R$ of $\mathcal O_d$; it defines a homomorphism $\rho_R: B_\infty \to \mathcal U(\mathcal N)$, $b_n\mapsto\varphi^{n-1}(R)$, a II$_1$ factor associated with an extremal character of $B_\infty$, and subfactors with index bounds; left and right partial traces of an R-matrix coincide; $d=2$ is fully analysed. `[v]` — [Conti and Lechner 2019/2020, arXiv:1909.04127 (J. London Math. Soc.)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04127)
- **(T)** Involutive unitary R-matrices are classified (up to the equivalence relevant to these representations) by extremal characters of $S_\infty$ (Lechner, Pennig, Wood); a March 2026 preprint extends the analysis to unitary R-matrices with two eigenvalues, a family stated to include all unitary Yang–Baxter representations of Temperley–Lieb and Hecke type. `[v]` (abstract level) — [Conti–Lechner, intro, citing LPW19](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04127); [arXiv:2603.20158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20158)
- **(T)** Not every unitary braided-category representation is "localisable" to a unitary R-matrix: Rowell–Wang conjecture that localisability corresponds to finite braid image (weakly integral dimensions); Jones representations at $r=5$ (Fibonacci) are not localised by a unitary R-matrix on a fixed $V$. `[v]` (existence and topic of the survey confirmed; statement `[r]`) — [Delaney, Rowell, Wang 2016, arXiv:1604.06429](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.06429); [Rowell and Wang 2010, arXiv:1009.0241](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.0241)

**Homological and faithful representations (operators over Laurent rings)**

- **(T)** The Burau representation is unitary with respect to an explicit Hermitian form over $\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$ (Squier); it is faithful for $n\le3$, unfaithful for $n\ge5$ (Bigelow), open for $n=4$. The Lawrence–Krammer–Bigelow representation over $\mathbb Z[q^{\pm1},t^{\pm1}]$ is faithful for all $n$: braid groups are linear. `[r]` — [Squier 1984, Proc. AMS 90, 199–202](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1984-0727232-8); [Bigelow 1999, arXiv:math/9904100](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9904100); [Bigelow 2000, arXiv:math/0005038](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0005038); [Krammer 2002, arXiv:math/0405198](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405198)
- **(T)** For a braid of trajectories, the spectral radius of the Burau matrix on $|t|=1$ bounds the topological entropy of the braid from below ("Burau estimate"). `[r]` — [Band and Boyland 2007, arXiv:math/0612716](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612716)

**Unitarity, density and complexity at roots of unity**

- **(T)** Freedman, Larsen, Wang answer Jones's question on the closed images of the irreducible sectors of the Jones representation (and of the $SU(N)$ generalisation) by solving the "two-eigenvalue problem" (each braid generator has exactly the eigenvalues $\{-1,q\}$): outside a short list of exceptional roots of unity ($r\in\{3,4,6\}$, and $r=10$ for small $n$), the closed image contains the special unitary group of each sector. `[v]` (paper, aim and method confirmed; exact exception list `[r]`) — [Freedman, Larsen, Wang 2002, arXiv:math/0103200, CMP 228, 177–199](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103200)
- **(T)** Exact evaluation of the Jones polynomial is #P-hard except at a few special values; additive approximation at roots of unity is in BQP (Aharonov–Jones–Landau) and BQP-complete for suitable roots. `[r]` — [Jaeger, Vertigan, Welsh 1990](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100068936); [Aharonov, Jones, Landau 2005, arXiv:quant-ph/0511096](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511096); [Kuperberg 2009, arXiv:0908.0512](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.0512)

### Inferences
- What is finite and exact: (i) for generic parameter, every matrix of the TL, Hecke, BMW, Burau and LKB representations has entries in a Laurent polynomial ring over $\mathbb Z$, and traces/polynomials are exact; (ii) at $q=e^{2\pi i/r}$ entries lie in $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ for a suitable root of unity $\zeta$ if a seminormal (non-orthonormal) basis is used; an orthonormal basis (needed for literal unitary matrices) introduces square roots of ratios of quantum integers $[m]_q$, which are real algebraic numbers that are in general not cyclotomic individually but generate small abelian or multiquadratic extensions. Unitarity can instead be certified exactly as "$\rho(b)^\dagger G\rho(b)=G$ for an explicit positive-definite Gram matrix $G$ over the real cyclotomic field", as Squier does for Burau.
- For a fixed number $n$ of strands, the Jones/HOMFLY polynomial of a braid word of length $L$ costs $L$ multiplications of matrices of size at most $C_n$ (TL) or $n!$ (Hecke); the #P-hardness lives in the growth of $n$, not of $L$. Trajectory braids with few strands and long words are in the cheap regime.
- A foundation that already holds exact cyclotomic fields, Young's seminormal form and group algebras with Wedderburn blocks (as the infrastructure's morphe does) has the ingredients of $H_n(q)$ at roots of unity: the seminormal form of $H_n(q)$ is the $q$-analogue of Young's (Hoefsmit/Wenzl).

### Gaps
- The exact exception list in Freedman–Larsen–Wang (which $(r,n)$ give finite or non-dense images) was not re-read; quote from the paper.
- I did not verify whether SageMath currently ships a BMW-algebra class; only the TL, Iwahori–Hecke and cubic Hecke implementations are known to me (see §7).
- No source was found that classifies *all* unitary R-matrices in dimension $d\ge3$; the classification remains open beyond the involutive and (2026) two-eigenvalue cases.

## 2. Quantum Groups, Braided Monoidal Categories, Recoupling Theory and Anyons

### Takeaway
A ribbon (modular) category packages the same finite data, fusion rules $N_{ab}^c$, $F$-symbols (quantum 6j) and $R$-symbols, that defines both the Reshetikhin–Turaev/Turaev–Viro invariants and the unitary action of a braid on fusion spaces, $\sigma_i\mapsto F^{-1}RF$; for $SU(2)_k$ this is exactly Kauffman–Lins recoupling theory at $A=e^{i\pi/2r}$, and Fibonacci anyons are the case $r=5$ where the braid image is dense.

### Cited Findings

- **(D/T)** $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is a quasi-triangular (ribbon) Hopf algebra in a completed sense; its universal R-matrix makes its finite-dimensional modules a braided monoidal category; on $V\otimes V$ ($V$ the 2-dimensional module) the braiding satisfies a Hecke quadratic relation and generates $TL_n$ as the commutant of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ on $V^{\otimes n}$. `[r]` — [Kassel 1995, *Quantum Groups*, GTM 155](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0783-2); [Jimbo 1986](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00400222)
- **(D/T)** Braided monoidal categories and their coherence (the braid group $B_n$ acts on $X^{\otimes n}$ for any object; the free braided monoidal category on one object is the braid category) are due to Joyal and Street. `[r]` — [Joyal and Street 1993, Adv. Math. 102, 20–78](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1993.1055)
- **(T)** Reshetikhin–Turaev: a ribbon category gives an isotopy invariant functor from coloured framed tangles (ribbon graphs) to the category; a modular category (finitely many simples, invertible $S$-matrix) gives invariants of closed 3-manifolds by surgery and a 3d TQFT, hence projective representations of mapping class groups, in particular of braid groups on the fusion spaces of a punctured disc. `[r]` — [Reshetikhin and Turaev 1990, CMP 127, 1–26](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02096491); [Reshetikhin and Turaev 1991, Invent. Math. 103, 547–597](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01239527); [Turaev, *Quantum Invariants of Knots and 3-Manifolds*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110435221)
- **(T)** Turaev–Viro: a state sum over colourings of the edges of a triangulation by labels $0,\dots,r-2$ weighted by quantum dimensions and quantum 6j symbols at a $2r$-th (or $4r$-th, by convention) root of unity is a 3-manifold invariant; it equals $|RT|^2$ (Turaev, Walker; skein proof by Roberts). `[r]` — [Turaev and Viro 1992, Topology 31, 865–902](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-9383(92)90015-A); [Roberts 1995, Topology 34, 771–787](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-9383(94)00053-0)
- **(C)** Temperley–Lieb recoupling theory (Kauffman and Lins): Jones–Wenzl projectors $f_n\in TL_n$ by the recursion $f_{n+1}=f_n-\frac{\Delta_{n-1}}{\Delta_n}f_ne_nf_n$ with $\Delta_n=(-1)^n[n+1]$ (quantum integers in $A^2$), defined while $\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_{n-1}\neq0$; trivalent vertices from projectors; closed formulas for $\theta(a,b,c)$, the tetrahedral network and the 6j symbols; the half-twist coefficient $\lambda^{ab}_c=(-1)^{(a+b-c)/2}A^{[a(a+2)+b(b+2)-c(c+2)]/2}$ (colours $a=2j$). At $A=e^{i\pi/2r}$ the admissible colours are $0,\dots,r-2$ and the theory yields the Turaev–Viro and Witten–RT invariants. `[r]` — [Kauffman and Lins 1994](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400882533)
- **(C)** Action of a braid on fusion spaces: for anyons $a_1,\dots,a_n$ with total charge $c$, a basis of $V^{a_1\cdots a_n}_c$ is the set of admissible labellings of a fusion tree; $\sigma_i$ acts by $R$ (diagonal, entries $R^{ab}_c$) in a basis where $a_i,a_{i+1}$ fuse first, hence by $B=F^{-1}RF$ in the standard basis; the pentagon and hexagon equations are exactly the conditions making this a representation. `[r]` — [Kitaev 2006, arXiv:cond-mat/0506438, App. E](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0506438); [Nayak, Simon, Stern, Freedman, Das Sarma 2008, arXiv:0707.1889](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1889)
- **(C)** Fibonacci anyons ($\tau\times\tau=1+\tau$, $d_\tau=\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$): $F=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi^{-1}&\varphi^{-1/2}\\ \varphi^{-1/2}&-\varphi^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$, $R=\mathrm{diag}(e^{-4\pi i/5},e^{3\pi i/5})$ (one chirality; the mirror theory has the complex conjugates); $\dim V^{\tau^{\otimes n}}_1$ is a Fibonacci number. Ising anyons: $F=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $R=e^{-i\pi/8}\,\mathrm{diag}(1,i)$; braiding generates only a finite (Clifford-type) group and is not universal. `[r]` — [Trebst, Troyer, Wang, Ludwig 2008, arXiv:0902.3275](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3275); [Nayak et al. 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1889)
- **(T)** Topological quantum computation: the Witten–Chern–Simons modular functor at $r=5$ is universal for quantum computation by braiding alone (the braid image is dense in each relevant $SU(N)$), and TQFTs can be simulated efficiently by a quantum computer. `[r]` — [Freedman, Larsen, Wang 2002, arXiv:quant-ph/0001108](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0001108); [Freedman, Kitaev, Wang 2002, arXiv:quant-ph/0001071](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0001071); [Freedman, Kitaev, Larsen, Wang 2003, arXiv:quant-ph/0101025](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101025); [Kitaev 1997/2003, arXiv:quant-ph/9707021](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707021)
- **(C)** Exact arithmetic for braid compilation: Fibonacci braid matrices have entries in the ring of integers of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{10})$ (with one fixed square root), and exactly synthesisable unitaries are characterised number-theoretically; explicit braid words found by search. `[r]` — [Kliuchnikov, Bocharov, Svore 2013, arXiv:1310.4150](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4150); [Bonesteel, Hormozi, Zikos, Simon 2005, arXiv:quant-ph/0505065](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0505065)
- **(T)** Arithmetic of the data: in a modular category the twists are roots of unity and the modular representation has a congruence kernel, so $S$ and $T$ are defined over a cyclotomic field (Ng–Schauenburg); a fusion category is defined over a number field (Ocneanu rigidity, Etingof–Nikshych–Ostrik), but that field need not be cyclotomic (Morrison–Snyder). `[r]` — [Ng and Schauenburg 2008, arXiv:0806.2493](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.2493); [Etingof, Nikshych, Ostrik 2002, arXiv:math/0203060](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0203060); [Morrison and Snyder 2010, arXiv:1002.0168](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0168)
- **(T)** Turaev–Viro codes: the Levin–Wen/Turaev–Viro lattice model for a fusion category $\mathcal C$ has anyonic excitations described by the Drinfeld centre $Z(\mathcal C)$, and braiding them is universal for suitable $\mathcal C$. `[r]` — [Koenig, Kuperberg, Reichardt 2010, arXiv:1002.2816](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.2816)
- **(C)** Centres are computable: TensorCategories.jl computes the Drinfeld centre of a fusion category explicitly, with F- and R-symbols for multiplicity-free categories up to rank 5. `[v]` — [Mäurer and Thiel 2024, arXiv:2406.13438](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13438v2)

### Inferences
- The quantum 6j symbols a Turaev–Viro implementation already evaluates are the $F$-matrices of $SU(2)_k$; adding the diagonal $R^{ab}_c=\lambda^{ab}_c$ gives every braid matrix on every fusion space. No new special functions are needed, only bookkeeping of fusion trees. In the non-unitary (Kauffman–Lins "network") normalisation all entries are rational functions of $A$, hence exact in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{4r})$; the unitary gauge needs square roots of $\theta$- and $\Delta$-evaluations.
- For classical trajectories, the map "braid of $n$ trajectories $\mapsto$ unitary on $V^{a^{\otimes n}}_c$" is a family of finite-dimensional invariants of the braid's conjugacy class (via the character/trace) and of its closure (via the Markov trace); density at $r=5$ means these unitaries separate braids as well as any finite-dimensional unitary representation of that dimension can, but faithfulness of these representations of $B_n$ is not implied by density and is not known in general.

### Gaps
- Whether the Jones representations at a fixed root of unity are faithful on $B_n$ for $n\ge4$ is, to my knowledge, open; no source was checked in this session.
- Conventions for $R^{\tau\tau}$ of the Fibonacci theory differ between sources by complex conjugation and by an overall phase; the notes give Trebst et al.'s.

## 3. Quantum Gravity: Operators for Geometry, q-Deformation, and Braids as Matter

### Takeaway
Loop quantum gravity's kinematics (holonomy–flux algebra, its unique diffeomorphism-invariant representation, discrete area and volume spectra on spin networks) is rigorous mathematics; quantum-group deformation is rigorous in three dimensions (Turaev–Viro) and a well-developed but postulated ingredient in four; "braids of framed spin networks are particles" (Bilson-Thompson, Markopoulou, Smolin; Wan; Hackett) is a speculative programme with a kinematical classification and no derived masses, gauge interactions or experimental support.

### Cited Findings

**Established kinematics (theorems within the canonical framework)**

- **(D/T)** Classical quantities are lifted as follows: connections $A$ enter only through holonomies $h_e[A]=\mathcal P\exp\int_eA\in SU(2)$ along edges and the conjugate triads through fluxes $E(S,f)$ across surfaces; cylindrical functions of holonomies form a commutative C\*-algebra whose spectrum is the space $\bar{\mathcal A}$ of generalised connections, with the Ashtekar–Lewandowski measure; $\mathcal H_{kin}=L^2(\bar{\mathcal A},d\mu_{AL})$ has the spin-network states as an orthonormal basis. `[r]` — [Ashtekar, Lewandowski, Marolf, Mourão, Thiemann 1995, arXiv:gr-qc/9504018](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504018); [Rovelli and Smolin 1995, arXiv:gr-qc/9505006](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9505006); [Ashtekar and Lewandowski 2004 review, arXiv:gr-qc/0404018](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0404018)
- **(T)** Uniqueness (LOST; Fleischhack): the holonomy–flux \*-algebra admits a unique diffeomorphism-invariant state, so the kinematical representation is forced. `[r]` — [Lewandowski, Okołów, Sahlmann, Thiemann 2005, arXiv:gr-qc/0504147](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0504147); [Fleischhack 2004, arXiv:math-ph/0407006](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0407006)
- **(T)** Area and volume are self-adjoint operators with discrete spectra; for a surface crossed transversally by edges with spins $j_i$, $A=8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2\sum_i\sqrt{j_i(j_i+1)}$ (the full spectrum includes edges ending on or lying in the surface); volume acts only at vertices (non-trivially from valence four) and exists in two inequivalent regularisations. `[r]` — [Rovelli and Smolin 1994, arXiv:gr-qc/9411005](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9411005); [Ashtekar and Lewandowski 1996, arXiv:gr-qc/9602046](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9602046); [Ashtekar and Lewandowski 1997, arXiv:gr-qc/9711031](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9711031); textbooks: [Rovelli 2004](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511755804), [Thiemann 2007](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511755682)
- **(S, relative to physics)** The dynamics (Thiemann's Hamiltonian constraint; spin-foam amplitudes) is mathematically defined but its physical correctness and continuum limit are open; this is the standard assessment in the monographs above. `[r]` — [Thiemann 2001/2007, arXiv:gr-qc/0110034](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0110034)

**q-deformation and the cosmological constant**

- **(T)** In three dimensions the Turaev–Viro model is the $q$-deformation of the Ponzano–Regge model at $q=e^{i\pi/r}$, with $r$ tied to the cosmological constant, and the asymptotics of the $q$-deformed 6j symbol reproduce the Regge action with a cosmological term (Mizoguchi–Tada; Taylor–Woodward). `[v]` (as summarised by the sources below) — [Dupuis and Girelli 2013, arXiv:1311.6841, PRD 90, 104037](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6841); [Khavkine and Christensen 2007, arXiv:0704.0278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.0278); primary `[r]`: [Mizoguchi and Tada 1991, arXiv:hep-th/9110057](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9110057); [Taylor and Woodward 2003, arXiv:math/0305113](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305113)
- **(S)** In four dimensions "there is no Chern–Simons theory to guide us. Hence, it is postulated that the cosmological constant should also be introduced through a quantum group structure"; $q$-deformation does not arise naturally in canonical LQG, whose kinematical states are ordinary $SU(2)$ spin networks with $\Lambda$ entering through the Hamiltonian constraint. Dupuis and Girelli put the quantum group in by hand and construct length, area and angle observables for 3d hyperbolic discrete geometry. `[v]` — [Dupuis and Girelli 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6841); [arXiv:hep-th/0307251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0307251)
- **(S)** Framed ($q$-deformed) spin networks: Major and Smolin propose that with $\Lambda\neq0$ the spin networks are replaced by framed, quantum spin networks at $q=e^{i\pi/(k+2)}$ with level $k\propto 1/(G\hbar\Lambda)$ (Smolin: $k=6\pi/(G\hbar\Lambda)$), motivated by the Chern–Simons (Kodama) state. Edges become ribbons; twists and braids of ribbons become meaningful. `[r]` — [Major and Smolin 1995, arXiv:gr-qc/9512020](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9512020); [Smolin 1995, arXiv:gr-qc/9505028](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9505028); [Smolin 2002, arXiv:hep-th/0209079](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0209079)
- **(C/S)** $q$-deformed four-dimensional spin-foam models exist as explicit, finite state sums: the $q$-Barrett–Crane model (Noui–Roche, Lorentzian, "meant to describe quantum gravity with positive cosmological constant"), numerically studied $q$-deformed models (Khavkine–Christensen), and the $q$-deformed EPRL/FK models (Fairbairn–Meusburger; Han), finite by construction at a root of unity; Haggard, Han, Kamiński, Riello and later Han derive $\Lambda$-amplitudes from $SL(2,\mathbb C)$ Chern–Simons theory with a proved semiclassical limit of the vertex amplitude. That any of these is the quantum theory of gravity is conjectural. `[v]` for Noui–Roche and Khavkine–Christensen, `[r]` for the rest — [Noui and Roche 2002, arXiv:gr-qc/0211109](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0211109); [arXiv:0704.0278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.0278); [Fairbairn and Meusburger 2010, arXiv:1012.4784](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.4784); [Han 2010, arXiv:1012.4216](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.4216); [Haggard, Han, Kamiński, Riello 2014, arXiv:1412.7546](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.7546); [Han 2021, arXiv:2109.00034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00034)

**Braids as particle states (speculative)**

- **(S)** Bilson-Thompson's helon model: the first-generation leptons and quarks as braids of three ribbons, with twists of $\pm2\pi$ on a ribbon carrying electric charge $\pm e/3$. `[r]` — [Bilson-Thompson 2005, arXiv:hep-ph/0503213](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0503213)
- **(S)** Bilson-Thompson, Markopoulou, Smolin: in LQG with framed (ribbon) spin networks, braidings and twists of ribbons that are invariant under the local evolution moves are conserved "noiseless subsystems"; the simplest such states match the helon model. `[v]` (citation and role confirmed via the review) — [Bilson-Thompson, Markopoulou, Smolin 2007, arXiv:hep-th/0603022, CQG 24, 3975–3993](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0603022); background `[r]`: [Kribs and Markopoulou 2005, arXiv:gr-qc/0510052](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0510052)
- **(S)** The review by Bilson-Thompson, Hackett, Kauffman, Wan describes two schemes: the trivalent scheme "has been quite successful at establishing a correspondence between braids and Standard Model particles", whereas the tetravalent scheme (Wan; Smolin–Wan; Hackett–Wan), with dual Pachner moves, "has naturally substantiated a rich, dynamical theory of interactions and propagation of braids"; the authors only *suggest* that the two may converge. `[v]` — [Bilson-Thompson, Hackett, Kauffman, Wan 2012, SIGMA 8, 014, arXiv:1109.0080](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0080v1)
- **(S)** Components of the tetravalent scheme: braid excitations of embedded 4-valent spin networks and their equivalence moves (Wan); propagation and interaction of chiral braids (Smolin–Wan); conserved quantities of interacting braids (Hackett–Wan); locality and translations, showing that trivalent braids cannot be created or destroyed by the standard moves (Hackett); invariants of braided ribbon networks (Hackett, 2011). `[r]` for the first four, `[v]` for the existence of the 2011 papers — [Wan 2007, arXiv:0710.1312](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.1312); [Smolin and Wan 2007, arXiv:0710.1548](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.1548); [Hackett and Wan 2008, arXiv:0803.3203](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3203); [Hackett 2007, arXiv:hep-th/0702198](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0702198); [Hackett 2011, arXiv:1106.5096 and 1106.5095, as cited in the review](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0080v1)
- **(S)** A later algebraic strand relates the helon braids to normed division algebras and Clifford-algebra ideals (Gresnigt). `[r]` — [Gresnigt 2018, arXiv:1803.02202](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02202)

**Braiding and quantum-group symmetry in spin foams and group field theory**

- **(T, within the model)** Freidel and Livine: integrating out 3d quantum gravity (Ponzano–Regge) coupled to particles gives an effective scalar field theory on a noncommutative space with $\kappa$-deformed/quantum-double $D(SU(2))$ symmetry, in which Feynman diagrams carry a non-trivial braiding. `[r]` — [Freidel and Livine 2005, arXiv:hep-th/0512113](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0512113); [Freidel and Livine 2005, arXiv:hep-th/0502106](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0502106)
- **(D)** Braided quantum field theory (perturbation theory for fields in a braided category, with braided Wick theorem) is Oeckl's; group field theories admit a noncommutative ("metric") representation with a star product (Baratin–Oriti). `[r]` — [Oeckl 1999, arXiv:hep-th/9906225](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9906225); [Baratin and Oriti 2010, arXiv:1002.4723](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.4723); [Oriti 2011 review, arXiv:1110.5606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.5606)

### Inferences
- Established: the lift "classical observable $\to$ operator" for holonomies, fluxes, areas and volumes; Turaev–Viro as the $q$-deformed 3d state sum and its semiclassical limit; the appearance of quantum-double braiding for point particles in 3d gravity. Postulated but well-defined: 4d $q$-deformed spin foams. Conjectural: that $q$-deformation is forced by $\Lambda$ in 4d canonical LQG; all identifications of braids with Standard Model particles.
- For the purpose of representing trajectory braids by operators, the reusable part of the quantum-gravity literature is not the particle proposal but the *framed* (ribbon) calculus: twists of ribbons are operators (multiplication by the twist eigenvalue $\theta_a$), braids act by $R$, and the invariants of braided ribbon networks are evaluations in a ribbon category, i.e. §2's data.
- The braided-ribbon-network programme appears largely dormant after about 2012 (the SIGMA review is the last comprehensive account found); this is an inference from the search results, not a sourced statement.

### Gaps
- No post-2020 paper was found that advances the Bilson-Thompson–Markopoulou–Smolin identification to dynamics, masses or gauge interactions; absence in two searches is not proof of absence.
- I found no source in which group field theory itself (beyond the 3d effective-field-theory result and coloured/tensorial combinatorics) uses a braiding in an essential way; treat "braided GFT" as sparse.
- The precise relation $k \leftrightarrow \Lambda$ varies with conventions (factors of $\gamma$, $G$, $\hbar$, signature); quote from the specific paper.

## 4. Noncommutative Geometry: Spectral Triples, Cyclic Cohomology, Braided Geometry, and Braid Groups

### Takeaway
Noncommutative geometry recovers metric and topology from operators (the distance from $\|[D,a]\|\le1$, invariants from index pairings of K-theory with cyclic cocycles), and Majid's braided geometry with the braided $L_\infty$-algebras of Dimitrijević Ćirić, Giotopoulos, Radovanović and Szabo gives field theories whose symmetry is a triangular quantum group; for braid groups themselves the established results are K-theoretic (Baum–Connes holds; $K_*(C^*_r(P_n))\cong\mathbb Z^{n!/2}$ in each degree; $K_*(C^*_r(B_3))\cong\mathbb Z$ in each degree), and no spectral-triple geometry of $B_n$ or of configuration spaces of points was found.

### Cited Findings

**Spectral triples and reconstruction**

- **(D)** A spectral triple $(\mathcal A,\mathcal H,D)$: a \*-algebra represented on a Hilbert space and a self-adjoint $D$ with compact resolvent and bounded commutators $[D,a]$. Connes's distance on states: $d(\varphi,\psi)=\sup\{|\varphi(a)-\psi(a)|:\|[D,a]\|\le1\}$, which for the Dirac operator of a Riemannian spin manifold is the geodesic distance. `[r]` — [Connes 1994, *Noncommutative Geometry*](https://alainconnes.org/wp-content/uploads/book94bigpdf.pdf); [Connes 1989, Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 9, 207–220](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385700004934)
- **(T)** Reconstruction: a commutative spectral triple satisfying Connes's axioms (with a strong regularity hypothesis) is the triple of a smooth compact oriented manifold. `[r]` — [Connes 2008, arXiv:0810.2088](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2088)
- **(T)** Topological invariants as index pairings: cyclic cohomology $HC^*(\mathcal A)$ pairs with $K_*(\mathcal A)$; the Chern character of a finitely summable Fredholm module computes the index map $K_*(\mathcal A)\to\mathbb Z$, and the Connes–Moscovici local index formula expresses it by residues of zeta functions of $D$. `[r]` — [Connes 1985, Publ. Math. IHES 62, 41–144](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02698807); [Connes and Moscovici 1995, GAFA 5, 174–243](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01895667)
- **(T, application)** Integrality from an index pairing: the quantised Hall conductance is the pairing of a cyclic 2-cocycle with the Fermi projection in a crossed-product (noncommutative Brillouin zone) algebra. `[r]` — [Bellissard, van Elst, Schulz-Baldes 1994, arXiv:cond-mat/9411052](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9411052)
- **(D/T)** Noncommutative tori $A_\theta$: universal C\*-algebra of unitaries $U,V$ with $VU=e^{2\pi i\theta}UV$, equal to $C(\mathbb T)\rtimes_\theta\mathbb Z$ (the rotation's Koopman lift, §5); $K_0(A_\theta)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ with trace range $\mathbb Z+\theta\mathbb Z$; $A_\theta\cong A_{\theta'}$ iff $\theta'\equiv\pm\theta \pmod 1$, and Morita equivalent iff the $\theta$'s are in one $GL_2(\mathbb Z)$ orbit. `[r]` — [Rieffel 1981, Pacific J. Math. 93, 415–429](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1981.93.415)
- **(D)** Spectral triples on group C\*-algebras: for a discrete group $\Gamma$ with a length function $\ell$, $D=M_\ell$ on $\ell^2(\Gamma)$ gives a spectral triple over $C^*_r(\Gamma)$ (Connes 1989); whether the induced metric on the state space gives the weak-\* topology is Rieffel's "compact quantum metric space" question, settled for $\mathbb Z^d$ and for hyperbolic groups. `[r]` — [Connes 1989](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385700004934); [Rieffel 2002, arXiv:math/0205195](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205195); [Ozawa and Rieffel 2003, arXiv:math/0302310](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0302310)
- **(C)** Finite spectral triples (fuzzy geometries, Barrett's matrix geometries) are finite matrices and are studied by computer: spectral dimension and variance estimators, Dirac spectra of fuzzy spheres and tori, random Dirac ensembles as multi-matrix models with the spectral action. `[v]` — [Druce, Fuzzy-Spectral-Estimators repository](https://gh.r-mj.com/pauldruce/Fuzzy-Spectral-Estimators); [arXiv:2304.13002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13002); [Khalkhali and Pagliaroli 2025 survey, arXiv:2512.08694](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2512.08694); spectral action `[r]`: [Chamseddine and Connes 1996, arXiv:hep-th/9606001](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9606001); [Connes and Marcolli 2008](https://doi.org/10.1090/coll/055)

**Braided geometry and braided field theory**

- **(D/T)** Majid's braided groups are Hopf algebra objects in a braided monoidal category, where the tensor product algebra is the braided one, $(a\otimes b)(c\otimes d)=a\,\Psi(b\otimes c)\,d$; transmutation turns a quasi-triangular Hopf algebra into a braided group; bosonisation goes back; braided matrices, braided linear spaces and braided differentiation give $q$-deformed geometry. `[r]` — [Majid 1993, J. Pure Appl. Algebra 86, 187–221](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-4049(93)90103-Z); [Majid 1995, arXiv:q-alg/9509023](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-alg/9509023); [Majid 1995, *Foundations of Quantum Group Theory*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511613104); [Beggs and Majid 2020, *Quantum Riemannian Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-30294-8)
- **(D/T)** Braided $L_\infty$-algebras: Dimitrijević Ćirić, Giotopoulos, Radovanović and Szabo introduce them (by Drinfel'd twist deformation of classical $L_\infty$-algebras) "as a homotopy-algebra structure for organising the symmetries and dynamics of noncommutative gauge theories"; braided field theories have gauge symmetries realising a braided Lie algebra, with Noether identities that are inhomogeneous extensions of the classical ones; the noncommutative gravity so obtained reduces to classical gravity, with no extra fields, as the deformation vanishes. `[v]` — [Dimitrijević Ćirić, Giotopoulos, Radovanović, Szabo 2021, arXiv:2103.08939](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.08939)
- **(D)** Review with new examples, including braided noncommutative Yang–Mills theory for arbitrary gauge algebras. `[v]` — [Giotopoulos and Szabo 2021, arXiv:2112.00541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00541v2)
- **(T, perturbative; preliminary)** Braided electrodynamics: preliminary calculations suggest the braided theory has no non-planar diagrams and no UV/IR mixing in the correlators computed, in contrast to conventional (star-product) noncommutative QFT. `[v]` (abstract level; author list not confirmed in this session) — [arXiv:2302.10713](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.10713); related braided BV quantisation `[r]`: [Nguyen, Schenkel, Szabo 2021, arXiv:2107.02532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02532)
- *Caveat.* In this line the braiding is that of a *triangular* (twist) quasi-Hopf/Hopf algebra, so $\Psi^2=\mathrm{id}$: the category is symmetric monoidal, and it is the symmetric group, not the braid group, that acts on tensor powers. Genuinely braided (non-symmetric) cases belong to Majid's and Oeckl's settings. `[r]` — [Giotopoulos and Szabo 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00541v2); [Oeckl 1999](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9906225)

**Braid groups and configuration spaces in noncommutative geometry**

- **(T)** The Baum–Connes conjecture holds for the full braid groups $B_n$ (Schick, via finite extensions of the pure braid groups) and for the pure braid groups $P_n$ (Oyono-Oyono; Chabert–Echterhoff). `[v]` — [Schick 2002/2007, arXiv:math/0209165](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0209165v1); [Baum and Sánchez-García survey, arXiv:0908.1066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0908.1066)
- **(T)** Azzali, Browne, Gomez Aparicio, Ruth, Wang: $K_0(C^*_r(P_n))\cong\mathbb Z^{n!/2}$ and $K_1(C^*_r(P_n))\cong\mathbb Z^{n!/2}$, with the same for K-homology of the classifying space; for $P_4$ the Baum–Connes correspondence between generators of the two sides is described explicitly ($\mathbb Z^{12}$ in each degree); $K_0(C^*_r(B_3))\cong K_1(C^*_r(B_3))\cong\mathbb Z$ by direct computation; the direct computation for the full braid group on more strands is stated to remain open. `[v]` — [Azzali, Browne, Gomez Aparicio, Ruth, Wang 2021, arXiv:2105.07848; New York J. Math. 28 (2022) 1256–1294](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.07848)
- **(T)** Semigroup C\*-algebras of Artin–Tits monoids of finite type, which include the positive braid monoids $B_n^+$, are analysed (K-theory, structure) by Li, Omland and Spielberg, complementing the right-angled case. `[v]` (title and scope) — [Li, Omland, Spielberg 2018, arXiv:1807.08288](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.08288)
- **(S/D)** "Configuration space" in the spectral-triple literature found means the configuration space of *gauge connections* (Aastrup and Grimstrup: a candidate spectral triple over a space of connections; an almost-commutative triple emerging in a semiclassical limit; an earlier construction where the Dirac operator "does not fully comply with the axioms"), not configuration spaces of points. `[v]` — [Aastrup and Grimstrup 2025, arXiv:2504.03391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03391); [arXiv:2309.06374](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2309.06374); [arXiv:hep-th/0503246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0503246)

### Inferences
- $B_n$ is torsion-free with classifying space the unordered configuration space $UC_n(\mathbb C)$, so by Baum–Connes $K_*(C^*_r(B_n))\cong K_*(UC_n(\mathbb C))$ (K-homology); the homology of $UC_n(\mathbb C)$ is classical (Arnol'd, Fuks, F. Cohen), so the *groups* are in principle computable by a spectral sequence, while explicit generators and the direct C\*-side computation are what remain open. Consistency check on the cited results: $P_n$'s Poincaré polynomial $\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+kt)$ has total rank $n!$, split evenly, and $B_3$ is the trefoil group, whose knot complement has $H_0=H_1=\mathbb Z$.
- Every unitary representation in §1–§2 is a \*-representation of the full group C\*-algebra $C^*(B_n)$ factoring through a finite-dimensional C\*-quotient (TL or Hecke at a root of unity); a Markov trace is a tracial state on that quotient. This is the precise sense in which "topological features of braids are studied through operator algebras" in established mathematics. $C^*_r(B_n)$ is not simple, because $B_n$ has infinite cyclic centre $\langle\Delta^2\rangle$; it fibres over the circle $\widehat{Z(B_n)}$.
- A spectral triple on $C^*_r(B_n)$ from a word or Garside length exists by Connes's general construction; whether it is a compact quantum metric space is not known to me (braid groups are not hyperbolic, so Ozawa–Rieffel does not apply).
- For trajectories, the commutative starting point is $C(\text{configuration space})$ with the flow acting, and the braid group enters as $\pi_1$; the noncommutative replacement is a groupoid or crossed-product algebra (§5), not a deformation of the configuration space itself.

### Gaps
- No noncommutative-geometric treatment (spectral triple, cyclic cohomology computation) specific to braid groups or to configuration spaces of points was found; the K-theory results above are the whole of what the searches returned.
- Whether braid groups have the Haagerup property for $n\ge4$ (which would give Baum–Connes with coefficients by Higson–Kasparov) was not checked; I recall it as open but have no source from this session.
- The author list of arXiv:2302.10713 and the status of its "no UV/IR mixing" claim beyond the orders computed were not verified.

## 5. Dynamical Systems Lifted to Operators: Koopman, Crossed Products, Transfer Operators, Cuntz–Krieger and Groupoids

### Takeaway
A dynamical system lifts to operators in three standard and compatible ways: linearly on functions (Koopman and its adjoint transfer operator), as an automorphism group of a commutative C\*-algebra with its crossed product and KMS states, and, after symbolic coding, as a Cuntz–Krieger algebra whose K-theory $\mathrm{coker}(1-A^{T})$ is the Bowen–Franks invariant; when the number of strands changes, the right objects are inverse semigroups, partial actions and their groupoid C\*-algebras, but no paper on the C\*-algebra of the inverse braid monoid was found.

### Cited Findings

**Koopman and Koopman–von Neumann**

- **(D/T)** For a measure-preserving map or flow $\varphi_t$ on $(X,\mu)$, $U_tf=f\circ\varphi_t$ is a unitary group on $L^2(\mu)$ (Koopman 1931); ergodicity, weak mixing and discrete spectrum are spectral properties of $U$. `[r]` — [Koopman 1931, PNAS 17, 315–318](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.17.5.315)
- **(D)** Modern Koopman theory: the operator acts linearly on the space of all measurement functions of the state, trading nonlinearity for infinite dimension; dynamic mode decomposition and its variants are the leading finite approximations; "obtaining finite-dimensional coordinate systems and embeddings in which the dynamics appear approximately linear remains a central open challenge". `[v]` — [Brunton, Budišić, Kaiser, Kutz 2022, SIAM Review 64(2), 229–340, arXiv:2102.12086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12086v1)
- **(T/C)** Finite-data approximations can converge to the wrong spectrum; residual-based methods compute spectra and pseudospectra of Koopman operators with error control. `[r]` — [Colbrook and Townsend 2021, arXiv:2111.14889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14889)
- **(D)** Koopman–von Neumann mechanics: classical mechanics on a Hilbert space of complex wavefunctions $\psi(q,p)$ with $\rho=|\psi|^2$, evolved by the Liouvillian $\hat L=-i\{\cdot,H\}$, with *commuting* position and momentum operators; quantum and classical mechanics are obtained from the same Ehrenfest-type axioms with $[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar$ or $0$. `[r]` — [Mauro 2001, arXiv:quant-ph/0105112](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0105112); [Bondar, Cabrera, Lompay, Ivanov, Rabitz 2012, arXiv:1105.4014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.4014)
- **(context)** Operator-theoretic (Koopman eigenfunction) methods and braid methods are listed side by side as alternative routes to the complexity of flows; the braid of trajectories gives a lower bound on topological entropy (Thiffeault), computed in finite time by braiding exponents (Budišić–Thiffeault); one paper applies Burau eigen-analysis of trajectory braids to material coherence. No paper found *combines* Koopman operators with braid representations. `[v]` — [Thiffeault 2009, arXiv:0906.3647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.3647); [Budišić and Thiffeault 2015, arXiv:1502.02162](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1502.02162); [Yeung, Cohen-Steiner, Desbrun, "Material coherence from trajectories via Burau eigenanalysis of braids" (bibliographic record only)](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1604239-material-coherence-from-trajectories-via-burau-eigenanalysis-braids)

**C\*-dynamical systems, crossed products, KMS states**

- **(D/T)** A C\*-dynamical system $(A,G,\alpha)$ is a strongly continuous action by automorphisms; for $A=C(X)$ these are exactly topological dynamical systems (Gelfand duality), and the Koopman map is $\alpha$. The crossed product $A\rtimes_\alpha G$ is universal for covariant pairs; a state $\omega$ is $(\alpha,\beta)$-KMS if $\omega(a\,\alpha_{i\beta}(b))=\omega(ba)$ for analytic elements. `[r]` — [Bratteli and Robinson, *Operator Algebras and Quantum Statistical Mechanics 2*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-03444-6)
- **(T)** For the gauge action on a Cuntz–Krieger algebra the KMS condition is governed by a Ruelle–Perron–Frobenius operator; for irreducible $A$ there is a unique KMS state, at inverse temperature $\beta=\log\rho(A)$, the topological entropy of the shift (for $\mathcal O_n$, $\beta=\log n$). `[r]` — [Exel and Laca 2000, arXiv:math/0006169](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0006169); [Olesen and Pedersen 1978, Math. Scand. 42, 111–118](https://doi.org/10.7146/math.scand.a-11740)
- **(D/T)** Quantum dynamical semigroups: a norm-continuous semigroup of completely positive unital maps on $B(\mathcal H)$ has generator $L(X)=i[H,X]+\sum_j\bigl(V_j^*XV_j-\tfrac12\{V_j^*V_j,X\}\bigr)$ (Lindblad; Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan for matrices). On a commutative algebra, complete positivity is positivity and the semigroups are exactly classical Markov semigroups. `[r]` — [Lindblad 1976, CMP 48, 119–130](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01608499); [Gorini, Kossakowski, Sudarshan 1976, J. Math. Phys. 17, 821–825](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.522979)

**Transfer operators and zeta functions**

- **(D/T)** The Ruelle transfer operator $(\mathcal L_\phi f)(x)=\sum_{Ty=x}e^{\phi(y)}f(y)$ is the adjoint of the Koopman operator for $\phi=-\log|\det DT|$ (Perron–Frobenius operator); for Hölder $\phi$ on a mixing subshift of finite type it has a simple leading eigenvalue $e^{P(\phi)}$ with a spectral gap (Ruelle's Perron–Frobenius theorem); dynamical zeta functions are Fredholm-type determinants of such operators. `[r]` — [Ruelle 1976, Invent. Math. 34, 231–242](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01403069); [Baladi 2000, *Positive Transfer Operators and Decay of Correlations*](https://doi.org/10.1142/3657)
- **(C)** For a subshift of finite type with matrix $A$, the Artin–Mazur zeta function is $\zeta(z)=\exp\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{z^n}{n}\mathrm{tr}(A^n)=1/\det(1-zA)$ (Bowen–Lanford): exact, rational, computable. `[r]` — [Lind and Marcus, *An Introduction to Symbolic Dynamics and Coding*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511626302)

**Cuntz–Krieger algebras and symbolic dynamics**

- **(D/T)** For a $0$–$1$ matrix $A$ (condition (I)), $\mathcal O_A$ is the universal C\*-algebra of partial isometries $S_i$ with $\sum_iS_iS_i^*=1$, $S_i^*S_i=\sum_jA_{ij}S_jS_j^*$; Cuntz and Krieger found that $K_0(\mathcal O_A)$ coincides with the Bowen–Franks group $\mathrm{coker}(1-A^{T})$ of the flow-equivalence classification (and $K_1=\ker(1-A^T)$), and that flow equivalence of the shifts implies isomorphism of the stabilised algebras together with their diagonals. `[v]` (as summarised by the survey sources) — [Brix 2022, arXiv:2212.11188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11188); [Eilers, Ruiz et al. survey, arXiv:1603.06174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.06174); primary `[r]`: [Cuntz and Krieger 1980, Invent. Math. 56, 251–268](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01390048); [Cuntz 1981, Invent. Math. 63, 25–40](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01389192)
- **(T)** Franks: $(\mathrm{coker}(1-A^T),\ \mathrm{sgn}\det(1-A^T))$ is a complete invariant of flow equivalence for irreducible non-trivial shifts of finite type. Rørdam: $K_0$ is a complete invariant for stable isomorphism of simple Cuntz–Krieger algebras (and with the class of the unit, for isomorphism), so the sign of the determinant is invisible to the algebra alone. `[v]` — [arXiv:1603.06174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.06174); primary `[r]`: [Franks 1984](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385700002261); [Rørdam 1995, K-Theory 9, 31–58](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00965458)
- **(T)** Matsumoto–Matui: the pair $(\mathcal O_A,\ \mathrm{sgn}\det(1-A))$, equivalently $\mathcal O_A$ with its diagonal $C(X_A)$, is a complete invariant of continuous orbit equivalence of one-sided topological Markov shifts; conversely (Arklint–Eilers–Ruiz, Carlsen–Eilers–Ortega–Restorff, 2016) diagonal-preserving stable isomorphism is equivalent to flow equivalence; conjugacy corresponds to isomorphisms that also intertwine the gauge actions (Carlsen–Rout). `[v]` for the first two, `[r]` for the last — [arXiv:2212.11188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11188); [Matsumoto and Matui 2013, arXiv:1307.1299](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1299); [Carlsen and Rout 2016, arXiv:1610.00692](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.00692)
- **(T)** Finer K-theoretic conjugacy invariant: Krieger's dimension triple ($K_0$ of the AF core with its order and the automorphism induced by the shift) is a complete invariant of shift equivalence; shift equivalence does not imply conjugacy (Kim–Roush, the Williams conjecture is false). `[r]` — [Krieger 1980, Invent. Math. 56, 239–250](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01390047); [Kim and Roush 1999, arXiv:math/9907095](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9907095)
- **(D)** Hyperbolic systems beyond shifts have C\*-algebras from their stable and unstable equivalence relations (Putnam's Smale-space algebras). `[r]` — [Putnam 1996, Canad. J. Math. 48, 175–195](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1996-008-2)

**Groupoids, inverse semigroups and a changing number of strands**

- **(D/T)** Exel: a representation of an inverse semigroup $S$ is *tight* if it is supported on the tight spectrum of the idempotent semilattice (the closure of the ultrafilters); tight representations correspond to representations of the C\*-algebra of the groupoid of germs of $S$ acting on its tight spectrum; the naive (universal) groupoid gives the Toeplitz extension rather than the Cuntz algebra, which is why tightness is needed. Exel–Pardo give algebraic conditions for the tight groupoid to be Hausdorff, minimal, effective; Starling shows the universal C\*-algebra of a Boolean inverse monoid equals Exel's tight C\*-algebra. `[v]` — [Exel 2007/2008, arXiv:math/0703182](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703182); [Exel and Pardo 2014, arXiv:1408.5278](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/1408.5278); [Starling 2016, arXiv:1605.05189](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1605.05189)
- **(D)** Background: groupoid C\*-algebras (Renault); the universal groupoid of an inverse semigroup with $C^*(S)\cong C^*(G_u(S))$ (Paterson); partial actions and their crossed products (Exel). `[r]` — [Renault 1980, LNM 793](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0091072); [Paterson 1999](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-1774-9); [Exel 2015/2017, arXiv:1511.04565](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04565)
- **(T)** For an inverse semigroup whose idempotents satisfy a finiteness condition (in particular finite $E(S)$), the semigroup algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of its underlying groupoid, hence to a direct sum of matrix algebras over the group algebras of its maximal subgroups (Steinberg, generalising Munn and Solomon). `[r]` — [Steinberg 2009, arXiv:0903.3456](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3456)
- **(D/T)** The inverse braid monoid $IB_n$ (Easdown and Lavers) consists of partial braids, obtained by "deleting one or several strings"; Vershinin embeds it in a monoid of partial monomorphisms of a free group, solving the word problem; related objects are the inverse braid-permutation monoid and inverse mapping class monoids. `[v]` — [Vershinin 2007, arXiv:0704.3002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.3002); [Kudryavtseva and Mazorchuk 2006, arXiv:math/0610730](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610730); primary `[r]`: [Easdown and Lavers 2004, Adv. Math. 186, 438–455](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2003.07.014)

### Inferences
- *Not found in the literature, but following from the cited theorems:* the idempotents of $IB_n$ are the partial identities on subsets of the $n$ strands (a finite Boolean lattice of $2^n$ elements) and the maximal subgroup at a subset of size $k$ is $B_k$. Steinberg's theorem then gives $\mathbb C[IB_n]\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^n M_{\binom nk}\bigl(\mathbb C[B_k]\bigr)$, the braid analogue of Munn's $\mathbb C[I_n]\cong\bigoplus_kM_{\binom nk}(\mathbb C[S_k])$, and correspondingly $C^*(IB_n)\cong\bigoplus_kM_{\binom nk}(C^*(B_k))$. Consequently its K-theory reduces to that of the braid groups (§4), and its unitary-type representations are induced from those of $B_k$ in §1–§2. This should be checked carefully (Steinberg's hypotheses; full versus reduced norms) before being relied upon.
- A trajectory system with births and deaths of strands is therefore, at the algebraic level, a representation of a groupoid whose objects are subsets (or numbers) of strands and whose arrows are partial braids; a "typed" action in the sense of the question is a groupoid (or category) action, and Exel's tightness matters only when the idempotent semilattice is infinite (e.g. unboundedly many strands).
- A pseudo-Anosov braid has a train-track transition matrix $A$ (Bestvina–Handel), hence a subshift of finite type, a Cuntz–Krieger algebra $\mathcal O_A$, a Bowen–Franks group, a rational zeta function $1/\det(1-zA)$, and a unique gauge-KMS state at $\beta=\log\rho(A)$ equal to the braid's topological entropy. Each step is standard; I found no paper that assembles them into an "operator algebra of a braid type", so the combination is an inference. Train tracks: [Bestvina and Handel 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-9383(94)E0009-9).
- Koopman and braid lifts are complementary: Koopman lifts the *dynamics on the plane* to a linear operator on functions; the braid lift takes the *isotopy class* of the motion of finitely many orbits to an operator on a finite-dimensional space. The Burau estimate (§1) is the one theorem found that links a braid's operator spectrum to a dynamical invariant.

### Gaps
- No source defines or studies a C\*-algebra of the inverse braid monoid; the one search run returned only the monoid's algebraic theory and general inverse-semigroup C\*-theory.
- No paper found that combines Koopman or transfer operators with braid-group representations (e.g. a Koopman operator on a configuration space decomposed under $B_n$ or its Hecke quotients).
- The Yeung–Cohen-Steiner–Desbrun paper was seen only as a bibliographic record; its method and claims were not read.
- KMS and transfer-operator statements were cited from Exel–Laca and Olesen–Pedersen without re-reading; the original Enomoto–Fujii–Watatani (1984) reference has no stable URL known to me.

## 6. Partial Commutation, Event Structures and Deformed Fock Spaces

### Takeaway
Partial commutation has an exact operator-algebraic theory (trace monoids, right-angled Artin monoids and groups, their Toeplitz-type C\*-algebras with K-theory computed and classified, graph products of operator algebras), and creation and annihilation of strands with a braiding has one too: for a self-adjoint contraction $T$ satisfying the Yang–Baxter (braid) equation, Bożejko and Speicher's $T$-deformed Fock space carries operators with $a_ia_j^*-\sum t\,a^*a=\delta_{ij}$, and the associated von Neumann algebras are now understood as twisted Araki–Woods algebras.

### Cited Findings

**Trace monoids, right-angled Artin groups and their operator algebras**

- **(D/T)** The trace (free partially commutative) monoid $M(\Sigma,I)$ is the quotient of $\Sigma^*$ by $ab=ba$ for $(a,b)\in I$; Cartier–Foata: in $\mathbb Z\langle\langle M\rangle\rangle$, $\bigl(\sum_{c\ \text{clique of}\ I}(-1)^{|c|}c\bigr)^{-1}=\sum_{t\in M}t$ (the Möbius function of the monoid), with unique normal forms as sequences of cliques. `[r]` — [Cartier and Foata 1969, LNM 85](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0079468); [Diekert and Rozenberg (eds.) 1995, *The Book of Traces*](https://doi.org/10.1142/2563)
- **(D)** The right-angled Artin group $A_\Gamma$ is the group with the same presentation; it interpolates between free and free abelian groups and is the group of fractions of the trace monoid $A_\Gamma^+$. `[r]` — [Charney 2006, arXiv:math/0610668](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610668)
- **(T)** Toeplitz algebras of right-angled Artin groups (Crisp–Laca), special cases of Nica's Toeplitz algebras of quasi-lattice ordered groups: Ivanov computes their K-theory with the Pimsner–Voiculescu sequence and shows the algebras are nuclear, in the bootstrap class, and (in the purely infinite simple cases, by Kirchberg–Phillips) tensor products of Cuntz algebras $\mathcal O_n$, $1\le n\le\infty$. `[v]` — [Ivanov 2007, arXiv:0708.2944; Trans. AMS 362 (2010)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.2944); primary `[r]`: [Crisp and Laca 2002, J. Aust. Math. Soc. 72, 223–245](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700003876)
- **(T)** Isomorphism problem: for finite graphs, $C^*(A^+_\Gamma)\cong C^*(A^+_\Lambda)$ iff explicit graph invariants agree ($t(\Gamma)$ and the numbers $N_k(\Gamma)$ of co-irreducible components of given Euler characteristic), iff an ordered filtered K-theory invariant agrees; many of these algebras are semiprojective. `[v]` — [Eilers, Li, Ruiz 2014, arXiv:1409.6632; Doc. Math. 21 (2016) 309–343](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.6632)
- **(D/T)** Graph products of operator algebras (reduced and universal), interpolating between free and tensor products, with stability results (exactness, Haagerup property and others). `[r]` — [Caspers and Fima 2014, arXiv:1411.2799](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2799)
- **(D/T)** The probabilistic counterpart is $\Lambda$- or $\varepsilon$-independence, "mixtures of classical and free independence" prescribed by a graph, with its own cumulants and central limit theorem. `[r]` — [Młotkowski 2004](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219025704001517); [Speicher and Wysoczański 2016, arXiv:1603.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08758)

**Deformed and braided Fock spaces**

- **(T)** $q$-Fock space: for $-1<q<1$ the sesquilinear form on the full Fock space given on $\mathcal H^{\otimes n}$ by $P_q^{(n)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}q^{\mathrm{inv}(\sigma)}U_\sigma$ is strictly positive, so there are operators with $a_ia_j^*-q\,a_j^*a_i=\delta_{ij}$; $q=1,-1,0$ give Bose, Fermi and free (full, Cuntz–Toeplitz) Fock space. `[r]` — [Bożejko and Speicher 1991, CMP 137, 519–531](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02100275)
- **(C)** Zagier's exact determinant: $\det P_q^{(n)}=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1-q^{k(k+1)}\bigr)^{\frac{n!\,(n-k)}{k(k+1)}}$, so positivity fails only at roots of unity. `[r]` — [Zagier 1992, CMP 147, 199–210](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099535)
- **(T)** Yang–Baxter deformation: let $T=T^*$ on $\mathcal H\otimes\mathcal H$ with $\|T\|\le1$ and $T_1T_2T_1=T_2T_1T_2$. Then $P_T^{(n)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}T_\sigma$ (with $T_\sigma$ defined through reduced words; well defined by the braid relation) is positive, strictly if $\|T\|<1$. On the resulting Fock space, $a^*(f)\xi=f\otimes\xi$ and $a(f)$ acts on $n$-particle vectors as $\ell(f)\,(1+T_1+T_1T_2+\cdots+T_1T_2\cdots T_{n-1})$, giving relations $a_ia_j^*-\sum_{r,s}t^{\,ir}_{js}a_r^*a_s=\delta_{ij}$; the case $T=$ flip times scalars $q_{ij}$ gives $a_ia_j^*-q_{ij}a_j^*a_i=\delta_{ij}$. The proof uses a completely positive map on Coxeter groups. `[r]` (index placement varies by source) — [Bożejko and Speicher 1994, Math. Ann. 300, 97–120](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01450478); [Speicher 1993, Lett. Math. Phys. 27, 97–104](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00750677)
- **(T)** Wick algebras: Jørgensen, Schmitt and Werner study \*-algebras with relations $d_id_j^*=\delta_{ij}+\sum T_{ij}^{kl}d_l^*d_k$ (which allow Wick ordering), give conditions for positivity of the Fock representation (the braid condition with $\|T\|\le1$; small $\|T\|$), and prove stability: for small coefficients the C\*-algebra is that of the undeformed ($T=0$) relations, the Cuntz–Toeplitz algebra. `[r]` — [Jørgensen, Schmitt, Werner 1995, arXiv:funct-an/9312004, J. Funct. Anal. 134, 33–99](https://arxiv.org/abs/funct-an/9312004)
- **(T)** Fock representations with generalised (exchange-algebra, anyonic) statistics: Liguori–Mintchev; for anyon commutation relations with an operator-valued or function-valued $Q(x,y)$: Bożejko, Lytvynov, Wysoczański. `[r]` — [Liguori and Mintchev 1995, arXiv:hep-th/9403039](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9403039); [Bożejko, Lytvynov, Wysoczański 2016, arXiv:1603.03075](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03075)
- **(T)** von Neumann algebras: the $q$-Gaussian algebras $\Gamma_q(\mathcal H)$ generated by $a_i+a_i^*$ are II$_1$ factors (Ricard) and are isomorphic to free group factors for small $|q|$ (Guionnet–Shlyakhtenko, free monotone transport). `[r]` — [Bożejko, Kümmerer, Speicher 1996, arXiv:funct-an/9604010](https://arxiv.org/abs/funct-an/9604010); [Ricard 2003, arXiv:math/0311413](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311413); [Guionnet and Shlyakhtenko 2012, arXiv:1204.2182](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.2182)
- **(T, 2022–2025)** Twisted Araki–Woods algebras $\mathcal L_T(H)$ (a twist $T$ and a standard subspace $H$): the vacuum is separating iff $T$ is crossing symmetric and satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation (Correa da Silva and Lechner); a family of examples from set-theoretic solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation; for finite-dimensional one-particle space of dimension $\ge2$ they are non-amenable factors, and for small $\|T\|$ isomorphic to free Araki–Woods factors (Yang); mixed $q$-deformed Araki–Woods algebras and operator-valued versions (Kumar and Wirth) extend the setting. `[v]` (search summaries and abstracts) — [Correa da Silva and Lechner 2023, CMP](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00220-023-04773-y.pdf); [review, arXiv:2311.14610](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14610); [Yang 2025, CMP](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00220-025-05285-7); [mixed $q$-deformed Araki–Woods, J. Noncommut. Geom.](https://ems.press/doi/10.4171/jncg/513); [Kumar and Wirth 2024, arXiv:2406.06179](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06179v2)

### Inferences
- The Bożejko–Speicher construction is the direct operator model for "creating and removing strands with braiding": the $n$-particle space is $\mathcal H^{\otimes n}$ with the Gram operator $P_T^{(n)}$ built from the Yang–Baxter representation of $B_n$ restricted along a section $S_n\to B_n$ (reduced words). Three caveats bound what it captures: (i) $T$ must be a self-adjoint *contraction*, whereas unitary R-matrices of §1 are self-adjoint only if involutive ($T^2=1$), in which case $\|T\|=1$ and $P_T^{(n)}$ is positive but degenerate (a quotient is needed, as for bosons and fermions); (ii) the Gram operator sums over $S_n$, not over $B_n$, so the braid group enters only through the Yang–Baxter equation that makes $T_\sigma$ well defined; (iii) non-involutive unitary braidings (true anyons in two dimensions) need the exchange-algebra/anyon constructions (Liguori–Mintchev; Bożejko–Lytvynov–Wysoczański), not a positive-cone deformation.
- In finite dimension everything is computable exactly: $P_T^{(n)}$ is a $d^n\times d^n$ matrix over the field of $T$'s entries, its kernel and signature decide the $n$-particle space, and $a(f),a^*(f)$ are explicit block matrices between levels $n$ and $n\pm1$; Zagier's formula is a closed-form check for the scalar case.
- Partial commutation and deformation combine: $q_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$ in Speicher's mixed relations gives generators that are free where $q_{ij}=0$ and commuting (bosonic) where $q_{ij}=1$, the Fock-space face of a trace monoid/right-angled Artin structure and of $\varepsilon$-independence. An event structure's independence relation is a commutation graph in exactly this sense; its conflict relation has no counterpart in these algebras that I found.
- A structure already present in the infrastructure, Doi–Peliti operators (creation/annihilation for reaction networks, in physis) and traces of records (in morphe), is the $q=1$, fully commutative end of this family.

### Gaps
- No operator-algebraic treatment of (Winskel) event structures as such, with conflict as well as concurrency, was searched for or found; only the commutation part (trace monoids, graph products) is covered.
- Whether $\Gamma_q(\mathcal H)\cong L(\mathbb F_n)$ for all $|q|<1$ is, to my knowledge, still open; not checked in this session.
- The exact hypotheses and constants of the Jørgensen–Schmitt–Werner positivity and stability theorems (the bound on $\|T\|$) were not re-read; I recall $\sqrt2-1$ for stability but give it only as a recollection.
- Attribution of the individual results on twisted Araki–Woods algebras to arXiv numbers was made from search summaries; check authors and numbers (arXiv:2304.11108, 2311.14610) before citing.
