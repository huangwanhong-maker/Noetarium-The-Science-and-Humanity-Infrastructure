## 1. Early-Warning Signals of Critical Transitions (Critical Slowing Down)

### Takeaway

The classical indicators are rolling-window estimates of variance, lag-1 autocorrelation (equivalently the AR(1) coefficient), skewness, spectral ratio and the DFA exponent on detrended residuals, with Kendall's τ of the indicator against time as the trend statistic and surrogate series (ARMA fits or phase randomisation) as the null; all rest on one closed form, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck linearisation with variance σ²/(2λ) and autocorrelation exp(−λΔt). They detect loss of linear stability only: they are silent for noise-induced tipping, unreliable for rate-induced tipping, and give false alarms when the noise's variance or colour changes, when a trend is under- or over-removed, and when series are selected after the fact.

### Cited Findings

**1.1 The closed form beneath all indicators (OU process).**

- [R] For the OU process $dX = r(\theta - X)\,dt + \sigma\,dB$ the transition density over an interval $t$ is Gaussian with conditional mean $X_{0}e^{-rt} + \theta(1-e^{-rt})$ and conditional variance $\frac{\sigma^2}{2r}(1-e^{-2rt})$ — [Boettiger and Hastings 2012, arXiv:1204.6231 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1204.6231). The stationary variance $\sigma^2/(2r)$ and the lag-$\Delta$ autocorrelation $e^{-r\Delta}$ are the $t\to\infty$ and regression-slope readings of these two lines.
- [M] Ditlevsen and Johnsen state the same pair for the linearised dynamics near a fold, variance $\sigma^2/(2\alpha)$ and autocorrelation $c(t)=e^{-\alpha t}$, and argue from the fluctuation–dissipation relation that **both** must rise before a bifurcation-induced transition; a rise of one alone is not evidence. They find neither rising before the Dansgaard–Oeschger events and conclude these were noise-induced — [Ditlevsen and Johnsen 2010, Geophys. Res. Lett. 37, L19703, doi:10.1029/2010GL044486](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010GL044486) (fetch refused, 403).
- [R] The same relation in discrete time: a driven branching (AR(1)-like) process has autocorrelation time $\tau = -\Delta t/\ln m$, diverging as $m\to1$ — [Wilting and Priesemann 2018, arXiv:1608.07035](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1608.07035).
- [M] The return time to equilibrium scales universally near a threshold (the origin of "critical slowing down" in ecology) — [Wissel 1984, Oecologia 65, 101, doi:10.1007/BF00384470](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00384470). Scaling laws of variance for every codimension-one fast–slow bifurcation are in [Kuehn 2011, Physica D 240, 1020, doi:10.1016/j.physd.2011.02.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2011.02.012).

**1.2 The estimators (Dakos et al. 2012, the methods paper behind the R package `earlywarnings`).** All [R] from [Dakos et al. 2012, PLoS ONE 7(7): e41010, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0041010](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0041010); the page's equations are images, so the algebraic forms below marked [M] are the standard ones and must be compared with the PDF.

- [R] Lag-1 autocorrelation is the first value of the autocorrelation function; the AR(1) model is $z_{t+1} = \alpha_1 z_t + \varepsilon_t$ with Gaussian white $\varepsilon_t$, fitted by ordinary least squares; $\rho_1$ and $\alpha_1$ are "mathematically equivalent". [M] The estimator: $\hat\rho_1 = \sum_{t=1}^{n-1}(z_t-\bar z)(z_{t+1}-\bar z)\big/\sum_{t=1}^{n}(z_t-\bar z)^2$.
- [R] Return rate is given as the inverse of the AR(1) coefficient, $1/\alpha_1$; the paper also gives the form $1-\alpha_1$.
- [R] Standard deviation (second moment about the mean), coefficient of variation, skewness (standardised third moment), kurtosis (standardised fourth moment). [M] $\gamma = \frac{1}{n}\sum (z_t-\bar z)^3 / \big(\frac1n\sum (z_t-\bar z)^2\big)^{3/2}$, $\kappa$ likewise with the fourth power and $\sigma^4$.
- [R] Spectral reddening: the *spectral ratio* of the spectral density at a low frequency (e.g. 0.05) to that at a high frequency (e.g. 0.5), and the *spectral exponent*, the slope of log density against log frequency.
- [R] DFA indicator: the fluctuation function $F(s)\sim s^{a}$; the indicator is rescaled so that it "reaches value 1 (rescaled from 1.5) at a critical transition"; usually estimated over 10 to 100 time units and needing more than 100 points.
- [R] Conditional heteroskedasticity: a Lagrange multiplier test; squared residuals of a selected AR($p$) are regressed on their own lag, and $r^2$ is compared with $\chi^2_1$ (χ² divided by sample size is comparable to $r^2$); rolling window 10% of the series.
- [R] BDS test on detrended residuals (after first-differencing, AR(1) or GARCH(0,1)) with embedding dimensions 2 and 3 and ε = 0.5, 0.75, 1 standard deviations; rejection of i.i.d. residuals suggests nonlinearity left over.
- [R] Rolling window: half the series in the worked examples (485 points for the slowing-down set, 5,000 for the flickering set); sensitivity analysis over windows of 25%–75% of the length and Gaussian bandwidths of 5–200.
- [R] Detrending: Gaussian kernel smoothing with bandwidth 10% of the series length in the example; linear detrending, loess and first-differencing are named as options "to use with care".
- [R] Trend statistic: Kendall's τ of the indicator against time; oscillating trends give weak or negative τ; the example's lag-1 autocorrelation has τ = 0.911. [M] $\tau = (C-D)/\binom{n}{2}$ with $C$, $D$ the concordant and discordant pairs.
- [R] Null model: fit the best ARMA($p$,$q$) by AIC to the residuals, generate 1,000 surrogates of the same length, and report $P(\tau^* \ge \tau)$ as the fraction of surrogate trends at least as large as the observed one (the fetch tool wrote the inequality the other way; check the PDF). In the worked example autocorrelation was significant (P < 0.025), standard deviation marginal (P = 0.073) and skewness not (P = 0.8).
- [R] Model-based methods in the same paper: nonparametric drift–diffusion–jump models (conditional variance by nonparametric regression); time-varying AR($p$) with coefficients as random walks, fitted by a Kalman filter and tested by a likelihood ratio against a 50:50 mixture of χ² distributions (variances are bounded at zero); threshold AR($p$); potential analysis ($dU/dz$ a polynomial of even order: 2nd for one well, 4th for two).
- [R] **The published test model.** Logistic growth with saturating harvest and noise; fold near $c \approx 2.604$; $r=1$, $h=1$, $K=10$; slowing-down set: $\sigma = 0.03$, $c$ rising linearly from 1 to 2.6771 over 1,000 steps, the shift near step 970; flickering set: $\sigma = 0.15$ over 10,000 steps with a red-noise inflow ($T=20$, $\beta=0.07$); measurement error $\sigma_{obs}=0.1$. [M] The equation (image e028 on the page) is $dx = \big[r x(1-x/K) - c\,x^2/(x^2+h^2)\big]dt + \sigma x\,dW$.

**1.4 False alarms and missed alarms.**

- [R] Boettiger and Hastings quantify the trade-off of sensitivity and reliability by a model-based test. Null model: OU with constant parameters. Test model (linearised saddle-node): $dX = \sqrt{r_t}\,(\phi(r_t) - X_t)\,dt + \sigma\sqrt{\phi(r_t)}\,dB_t$, $\phi(r_t)=\sqrt{r_t}+\theta$, $r_t = r_0 - m t$. Likelihood is the product of transition densities (closed form for OU; moment equations $dE/dt = 2\sqrt{r}(\sqrt r + \theta - x)$ as printed by the fetch tool, $dV/dt = -2\sqrt{r}\,V + \sigma^2(\sqrt r+\theta)$ integrated numerically for the test model; check the first against the PDF). Statistic: deviance $\delta = -2(\log L_0 - \log L_1)$; its null and test distributions by parametric bootstrap, 500 replicates from each fitted model, each refitted by both models; ROC curves by sweeping a threshold on δ — [arXiv:1204.6231](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1204.6231).
- [R] Their numbers: at a 5% false-alarm rate, variance's Kendall τ detected 25% (simulation), 5.0% (chemostat) and 5.4% (glaciation) of crashes against 61%, 34% and 100% for the likelihood statistic; the summary-statistic ROC curves "often sit near the diagonal". Simulation: a birth–death process with $b_n = eKn^2/(n^2+h^2)$, $d_n = en + a_t$, $e=0.5$, $K=1000$, $h=200$, $X_0=730$, $a_t$ rising from 100 at 0.09 per unit time, 40 samples over $t\in[0,500]$ — [arXiv:1204.6231](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1204.6231). Published as J. R. Soc. Interface 9, 2527 (2012), [doi:10.1098/rsif.2012.0125](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2012.0125) [M for the journal details].
- [M] Selecting records because a shift occurred biases the indicators upward ("the prosecutor's fallacy"): chance fluctuations that cause a noise-induced shift look like slowing down — [Boettiger and Hastings 2012, Proc. R. Soc. B 279, 4734, doi:10.1098/rspb.2012.2085](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2012.2085).
- [R] Indicators of critical slowing down are not specific to catastrophic transitions (smooth, reversible transitions show them too), assume small noise about an equilibrium, fail for noise-induced tipping ("generally unpredictable"), may be changed by merged sensors (a changed signal-to-noise ratio inflates autocorrelation), and may come too late for slow systems; of 229 empirical studies (from 887 publications, 2004 to April 2023) 67.8% reported positive performance, 3.4% negative, 24.7% mixed, which the authors attribute partly to publication bias and hindsight; 74.9% of the 65 distinct indicators were slowing-down based; 77.7% of studies used temporal and 8% spatial data — (recent) [Dakos et al. 2024, Earth Syst. Dynam. 15, 1117, doi:10.5194/esd-15-1117-2024](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/15/1117/2024/).
- [R] Recommended safeguards from the same review: compute the indicators on the drivers as a false-positive check; test for anticorrelation between autocorrelation and variance (a sign that signal-to-noise changed, not stability); combine several indicators; state the test and threshold — (recent) [Dakos et al. 2024](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/15/1117/2024/).
- [R, abstract level] With time-correlated driving noise, variance and lag-1 autocorrelation are reliable only if the noise is white and stationary: a strengthening noise correlation gives spurious alarms, a weakening one masks real ones; the authors propose two estimators of the linear restoring rate that separate the two — (recent) [Morr and Boers 2024, Phys. Rev. X 14, 021037, doi:10.1103/PhysRevX.14.021037](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_29837). In several dimensions, one noise source driving several components gives false negatives and false positives even with stationary noise; variance is more vulnerable than AC(1) — (recent) [Morr, Boers and Ashwin 2024, arXiv:2311.18597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18597v2); [Morr et al. 2024, Phys. Rev. Research 6, 033251](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_30135_2/component/file_30193/30135oa.pdf).
- [M] Further documented failures: no warning at all in some models ([Hastings and Wysham 2010, Ecology Letters 13, 464, doi:10.1111/j.1461-0248.2010.01439.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2010.01439.x)); warnings before non-catastrophic transitions ([Kéfi et al. 2013, Oikos 122, 641, doi:10.1111/j.1600-0706.2012.20838.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0706.2012.20838.x)); false alarms for sea ice from a changing heat capacity ([Wagner and Eisenman 2015, Geophys. Res. Lett. 42, 10333, doi:10.1002/2015GL066297](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015GL066297)); extrapolated tipping times carry uncertainties too large to use ((recent) [Ben-Yami et al. 2024, Science Advances 10, eadl4841, doi:10.1126/sciadv.adl4841](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adl4841)), against the point estimate of (recent) [Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen 2023, Nature Communications 14, 4254, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-39810-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39810-w).

**1.5 Rate-induced tipping.**

- [R] Definitions (Section 1): B-tipping, the output changes "due to a bifurcation of a quasi-static attractor"; N-tipping, "noisy fluctuations result in the system departing from a neighbourhood of a quasi-static attractor"; R-tipping, "the system fails to track a continuously changing quasi-static attractor". Setup: $\dot x = f(x,\lambda(t))$, quasi-static equilibrium $\tilde x(\lambda)$ — [Ashwin, Wieczorek, Vitolo and Cox 2012, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 370, 1166, doi:10.1098/rsta.2011.0306; arXiv:1103.0169](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1103.0169).
- [R] Linear model with tipping radius $R$: $\dot x = M(x-\tilde x(\lambda))$ for $|x-\tilde x|<R$; drift $r = (d\tilde x/d\lambda)\dot\lambda$; steady lag $|x-\tilde x| = |M^{-1}r|$; no R-tipping if $\|M^{-1}\|\,|r| < R$, R-tipping if $\|M\|^{-1}|r| > R$ — [arXiv:1103.0169, Eqs. 4–11](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1103.0169).
- [R] **Exact test.** $\dot x = (x+\lambda)^2 - \mu$, $\dot\lambda = r$, $\mu>0$: the critical rate is $r_c = \mu$ (for an initial condition with $\lambda_0 \le -x_0$; otherwise $r_c = \mu - (\lambda_0+x_0)^2$); there is no bifurcation of the frozen system at any $\lambda$ — [arXiv:1103.0169, Section 3.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1103.0169).
- [R] The authors state that the detrended autoregressive indicators designed for B-tipping "should not be expected" to predict N- or R-tipping, since R-tipping needs no change of stability; cases that reduce to a bifurcation in a co-moving frame (steady drift) may be predictable — [arXiv:1103.0169, Section 5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1103.0169).
- [M] Later work finds that autocorrelation and variance do rise before R-tipping in some settings, but with a delay that makes them late — [Ritchie and Sieber 2016, Chaos 26, 093116, doi:10.1063/1.4963012](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4963012).

### Inferences

- [D] **Fixture for the estimators.** Simulate the OU process exactly as AR(1): $x_{k+1} = \phi x_k + \eta_k$, $\phi = e^{-\lambda\Delta t}$, $\eta_k\sim N(0,\ \tfrac{\sigma^2}{2\lambda}(1-\phi^2))$. With $\lambda=0.5$, $\sigma=0.1$, $\Delta t=1$: variance $0.01$, $\rho_1 = e^{-0.5} = 0.60653$. Both follow from the [R] transition density above.
- [D] **Near a fold.** For $\dot x = \mu - x^2$ ($\mu>0$) the stable state is $x^*=\sqrt\mu$ with $\lambda = 2\sqrt\mu$, so under additive noise the linearised variance is $\sigma^2/(4\sqrt\mu)$ and $\rho_1 = \exp(-2\sqrt\mu\,\Delta t)$: variance diverges as $\mu^{-1/2}$. Skewness is zero in the linearisation; its rise is a nonlinear effect and cannot be tested against the OU form.
- [D] **Observation noise mimics subsampling.** Adding independent measurement noise of variance $s^2$ to an AR(1) series leaves the lag-$k$ covariance and raises the variance, so $\rho_k^{obs} = \rho^k\cdot \mathrm{Var}/(\mathrm{Var}+s^2)$: the same geometric form $b\,m^k$ with $b<1$ as Wilting and Priesemann's subsampling bias (§3). A multistep fit therefore recovers $\lambda$ under measurement noise where $\hat\rho_1$ alone is biased low; one module can serve both uses.
- [D] Kendall's τ of a rolling-window indicator cannot be tested against the textbook null (variance $2(2n+5)/(9n(n-1))$ under independence [M]), because overlapping windows make successive indicator values strongly dependent; this is why every source above uses surrogates. tyche already holds surrogates and red-noise nulls (ADR 0060) and can supply the null.
- [D] The three safeguards that the literature converges on can each be a check in a library: (i) both variance and autocorrelation rise (Ditlevsen and Johnsen); (ii) the estimate of the restoring rate is robust to the noise's colour (Morr and Boers); (iii) the alarm's error rates are stated by parametric bootstrap from fitted null and test models (Boettiger and Hastings).

### Gaps

- The algebraic forms of the estimators in Dakos et al. 2012 were not readable (equation images); the forms given are the standard ones [M]. The direction of the surrogate P-value inequality should be checked in the PDF.
- Ditlevsen and Johnsen 2010, Held and Kleinen 2004, Dakos et al. 2008, Scheffer et al. 2009 and 2012 and Lenton 2011 were not re-opened; every statement about them is [M].
- The two restoring-rate estimators of Morr and Boers 2024 were not read in full (abstract only); their formulas are missing here.
- No closed form was found for the null distribution of Kendall's τ of a rolling-window indicator; it appears to be obtained only by simulation.
- The finite-sample bias of $\hat\rho_1$ (about $-(1+3\rho)/n$, Kendall 1954; Marriott and Pope 1954) is recalled without a source opened; no URL is given, so it is listed here and not as a finding.

## 2. Developments of 2020–2026: Learned Classifiers, Eigenvalues from Data, Spatial Signals, Reliability

### Takeaway

Since 2020 the field has moved from generic indicators toward (i) classifiers trained on simulated bifurcations that also name the bifurcation's type, (ii) direct estimates of the dominant eigenvalue or restoring rate from data, and (iii) explicit accounting of error; the classifiers have a documented dependence on preprocessing identical to that of their training set, and the eigenvalue methods are sensitive to embedding parameters and transients.

### Cited Findings

**2.1 Deep-learning classifiers.**

- [R] Bury et al. train a CNN–LSTM (a convolutional layer for local features, an LSTM over the series) on random two-dimensional systems with polynomial terms to third order (parameters normal, half set to zero), simulated with additive noise up to a bifurcation; 500,000 series of length 500 and 200,000 of length 1,500; four classes: fold, Hopf, transcritical, neutral; preprocessing by Lowess detrending (span 0.2, degree 1) and normalisation by the mean absolute residual; 1,500 epochs at learning rate 0.0005; an ensemble of ten models. Held-out F1: 88.2% (length 1,500), 84.2% (length 500). It outperformed variance and lag-1 autocorrelation (rolling windows with Kendall τ) in six of eight comparisons on model and empirical systems (anoxia sediments, a Rijke tube, palaeoclimate records). Code: `github.com/ThomasMBury/deep-early-warnings-pnas` — [Bury et al. 2021, PNAS 118, e2106140118, doi:10.1073/pnas.2106140118 (PMC8488604)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8488604/).
- [R, via search summary of the PNAS letters] Criticism: the classifier was trained only on series detrended by Lowess with span 0.20, and its performance "drops substantially" when a Gaussian filter is used instead; the authors' reply accepts that the classifier learned features of the preprocessed series as well as of the bifurcation — (recent) [Dablander and Bury 2022, PNAS letter (PMC9477405)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9477405) and [reply (PMC9477383)](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/748101ff2911b5030ff5e806f8877fca/PMC9477383.pdf).
- [R, via search summary] A classifier trained on phase transitions of a two-dimensional Ising lattice is reported to transfer to out-of-sample systems — (recent) [arXiv:2206.00060](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.00060); a 2026 preprint pairs a binary CNN–LSTM with energy-based out-of-distribution detection, an acknowledgement that inputs unlike the training set are the open problem — (recent) [EGUsphere preprint 2026-2223](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-2223/); a 2024 preprint on predicting when a tipping point occurs — (recent) [arXiv:2407.18693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.18693).
- [R] The 2024 review's summary: convolutional models outperform variance and AR(1) "in some case studies" and their performance "depends on training data" — (recent) [Dakos et al. 2024](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/15/1117/2024/).
- [M] Extension to discrete-time (period-doubling, Neimark–Sacker, fold, transcritical, pitchfork) bifurcations — (recent) [Bury et al. 2023, Nature Communications 14, 6331, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-42020-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-42020-z).

**2.2 Spectral indicators that name the bifurcation.**

- [M] Fit the detrended residuals' power spectrum to the normal-form spectra and compare by AIC weights: fold $S(\omega) = \frac{\sigma^2}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\omega^2+\lambda^2}$; Hopf $S(\omega) = \frac{\sigma^2}{4\pi}\Big[\frac{1}{(\omega-\omega_0)^2+\mu^2}+\frac{1}{(\omega+\omega_0)^2+\mu^2}\Big]$; null $S=\sigma^2/2\pi$ — [Bury, Bauch and Anand 2020, J. R. Soc. Interface 17, 20200482, doi:10.1098/rsif.2020.0482](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0482). Implemented in `ewstools` (§6).

**2.3 The dynamical eigenvalue (DEV) and S-map Jacobians.**

- [R] Definition. Delay-embed one variable, $X_t = (x_t, x_{t-\tau},\dots,x_{t-(E-1)\tau})$. At each target time fit the S-map local linear model $x_{t^*+\tau} = C_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{E} C_j\,x_{t^*-(j-1)\tau}$ with weights $w(d) = \exp(-\theta d/\bar d)$ ($d$ the distance to the target, $\bar d$ the mean distance, $\theta\ge0$), solved by SVD. The local Jacobian is the companion matrix with the S-map coefficients in its first row and ones on the subdiagonal. DEV is the dominant eigenvalue, averaged over a moving window — (recent) [Grziwotz et al. 2023, Science Advances 9, eabq4558, doi:10.1126/sciadv.abq4558 (PMC9821862)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9821862/).
- [R] Criteria: $|\mathrm{DEV}|\to1$ at the bifurcation; fold: real part → +1, imaginary part 0; period doubling: real part → −1; Neimark–Sacker: a conjugate pair with nonzero imaginary part and modulus → 1. Parameters: $E$ and $\tau$ screened over 1–12 at $\theta=0$ by forecast skill ρ; θ chosen per window in $[0, 2.5]$ — (recent) [Grziwotz et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9821862/).
- [R] Tested on the Noy-Meir model (fold), the Hénon map (period doubling), the Rosenzweig–MacArthur model (Neimark–Sacker), each 10,000 points with noise, and on five empirical records. Stated limitations: noise pushes $|\mathrm{DEV}|$ below 1; transients push it above 1 without a transition; univariate DEV failed on one spatial model; efficacy falls in high-dimensional networks; "the mathematical basis is not fully established" since it is not evaluated at equilibria. Code on Zenodo ([doi:10.5281/zenodo.7379358](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7379358)), in R with `rEDM`; no code licence stated on the page — (recent) [Grziwotz et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9821862/).
- [R, via search summary] Alternatives: Dynamic Mode Decomposition gives one set of eigenvalues for a global fit in a window, where S-map gives pointwise eigenvalues then averaged — (recent) [arXiv:2506.23946 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23946); a dominant eigenvalue extracted from the autocorrelation function, claimed more sensitive and specific than DEV, AC(1) and variance for period doubling (a preprint, unreviewed) — (recent) [arXiv:2603.05523 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05523v1).

**2.4 Spatial indicators.**

## 3. Criticality in the Statistical-Physics and Neural Sense

### Takeaway

The operational tests are: maximum-likelihood power-law fits with a goodness-of-fit bootstrap and likelihood-ratio comparisons (Clauset, Shalizi and Newman), the crackling-noise relation $\gamma = (\alpha-1)/(\tau-1)$ with collapse of avalanche shapes, the branching ratio estimated by multistep regression (invariant under subsampling), DFA exponents of amplitude envelopes, and finite-size scaling. Power laws, the scaling relation's ingredients and even shape collapse are reproduced by non-critical models, so no single test establishes criticality; the branching ratio in vivo is found slightly below one ($m\approx0.98$).

### Cited Findings

**3.1 Power-law fitting (Clauset, Shalizi, Newman 2009).** All [R] from [arXiv:0706.1062 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0706.1062), published as SIAM Review 51, 661, [doi:10.1137/070710111](https://doi.org/10.1137/070710111).

- Continuous density (Eq. 3): $p(x) = \frac{\alpha-1}{x_{min}}\big(\frac{x}{x_{min}}\big)^{-\alpha}$, $\alpha>1$. Discrete (Eqs. 5–6): $p(x) = x^{-\alpha}/\zeta(\alpha,x_{min})$, $\zeta(\alpha,x_{min}) = \sum_{n\ge0}(n+x_{min})^{-\alpha}$.
- Continuous MLE (Eqs. 9–10): $\hat\alpha = 1 + n\big[\sum_{i=1}^n \ln(x_i/x_{min})\big]^{-1}$, standard error $\sigma = (\hat\alpha-1)/\sqrt n + O(1/n)$.
- Discrete approximation (Eq. 15): $\hat\alpha \simeq 1 + n\big[\sum_i \ln\frac{x_i}{x_{min}-1/2}\big]^{-1}$, accurate to about 1% for $x_{min}\gtrsim6$; otherwise maximise the discrete likelihood numerically.
- $x_{min}$ (Eq. 17): the value minimising the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance $D = \max_{x\ge x_{min}}|S(x)-P(x)|$ between the empirical CDF of the tail and the fitted CDF.
- Goodness of fit (Section 4.1): (1) fit, record $D$; (2) generate synthetic sets of $n$ points: with probability $n_{tail}/n$ draw from the fitted power law, otherwise draw uniformly from the observed points below $\hat x_{min}$; (3) fit each synthetic set **anew** (its own $x_{min}$ and α) and record its $D$; (4) $p$ = fraction of synthetic $D$ larger than the observed. At least $\frac14\varepsilon^{-2}$ sets for accuracy ε in $p$ (2,500 for ε = 0.01). The power law is ruled out if $p\le0.1$.
- Likelihood-ratio test after Vuong (Section 5, Appendix C, Eqs. 42–45): $\mathcal R = \sum_i[\ell_i^{(1)}-\ell_i^{(2)}]$; $\sigma^2 = \frac1n\sum_i\big[(\ell_i^{(1)}-\ell_i^{(2)}) - (\bar\ell^{(1)}-\bar\ell^{(2)})\big]^2$; $p = \big|\mathrm{erfc}\big(\mathcal R/(\sqrt{2n}\,\sigma)\big)\big|$; the sign of $\mathcal R$ is trusted only if $p<0.1$.
- Generators (Appendix D, Eqs. 49, 51): continuous $x = x_{min}(1-r)^{-1/(\alpha-1)}$, $r$ uniform on $[0,1)$; discrete approximation $x = \big\lfloor (x_{min}-\tfrac12)(1-r)^{-1/(\alpha-1)} + \tfrac12\big\rfloor$; exactly, by doubling then bisection on the CDF. The paper's synthetic comparison uses $x_{min}=5$, $\alpha=2.5$ (Table 7).

**3.2 Neuronal avalanches and the crackling-noise relation.**

- [M] An avalanche is a run of consecutive time bins with activity, bounded by empty bins; sizes (electrodes active, or summed LFP amplitude) follow a power law with exponent −3/2 in cortical cultures and slices, and the branching parameter σ (descendants per ancestor between the first two bins) is near 1 at a bin of the mean inter-electrode interval (4 ms); the fitted exponent depends on the bin width — [Beggs and Plenz 2003, J. - [R] The tests as stated by a critical reading: $P(s)\sim s^{-\tau}$, critical branching gives $\tau=3/2$; $P(t)\sim t^{-\alpha}$, critical branching gives $\alpha=2$; mean size at a duration scales as $t^{\gamma}$; the crackling-noise relation $\gamma = (\alpha-1)/(\tau-1)$, which gives $\gamma=2$ for the mean-field values; and avalanche shapes of different durations collapse onto one function when rescaled — [Touboul and Destexhe 2017, Phys. Rev. E 95, 012413; arXiv:1503.08033](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1503.08033). [M] In Sethna's notation $\gamma = 1/(\sigma\nu z)$ and the mean shape is $V(t,T) = T^{1/(\sigma\nu z)-1}\,f(t/T)$ — [Sethna, Dahmen and Myers 2001, Nature 410, 242, doi:10.1038/35065675; arXiv:cond-mat/0102091](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0102091) (abstract read only).
- [R, abstract] Friedman et al. report, on neuron-level culture data, that "a single universal scaling function" describes the mean temporal profiles over a wide range of durations, with approximate power laws of size and duration and scaling laws between the exponents — [Friedman et al. 2012, Phys. Rev. Lett. 108, 208102, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.208102](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.208102). **3.3 The branching ratio under subsampling (Wilting and Priesemann 2018).** All [R] from [arXiv:1608.07035 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1608.07035), published as Nature Communications 9, 2325, [doi:10.1038/s41467-018-04725-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04725-4).

- Model: $\langle A_{t+1}\mid A_t\rangle = mA_t + h$; stationary for $m<1$, critical at $m=1$. Stationary mean $\langle A\rangle = h/(1-m)$; stationary variance $\mathrm{Var}[A] = \frac{1}{1-m^2}\big(\xi^2 + \sigma^2\frac{h}{1-m}\big)$ with $\sigma^2$ the offspring variance and $\xi^2$ the drive's.
- Subsampling condition: $\langle a_t\mid A_t\rangle = \alpha A_t + \beta$ (binomial $a_t\sim\mathrm{Bin}(A_t,\alpha)$, or hypergeometric with $\alpha=n/N$).
- Conventional estimator: the slope $r_1$ of the regression of $a_{t+1}$ on $a_t$; under subsampling $r_1 = \frac{\alpha^2\mathrm{Var}[A_t]}{\mathrm{Var}[a_t]}\,m$, biased unless the factor is 1.
- Multistep regression: $r_k = b\,m^k$ for all $k$; estimate $r_k = \mathrm{Cov}(a_{t+k},a_t)/\mathrm{Var}[a_t]$ for $k=1..k_{max}$ and fit $b$ and $m$ by least squares on $\sum_k (r_k - b m^k)^2$; $b$ absorbs the bias without knowledge of α.
- Applicability tests: reject if an exponential with offset fits much better ($2R^2_{off} < R^2_{exp}$, $R^2$ the residual sum of squares), if the two autocorrelation times disagree ($|\tau_{exp}-\tau_{off}|/\min > 2$), if a linear trend in $k$ fits better, or if the slopes are not significantly positive (Poisson activity, $m=0$).
- Results: in vivo spiking (rat hippocampus, cat visual cortex, monkey prefrontal cortex) gives $m$ between 0.963 and 0.998, median $m\approx0.984$, i.e. $\epsilon = 1-m\approx 0.016$, and with $\Delta t = 4$ ms an autocorrelation time $\tau = -\Delta t/\ln m\approx 250$ ms. A branching network with $m=0.9$ gives conventional estimates 0.312 when 10% of units are sampled and 0.047 at 1%; cat visual cortex, 50 units: conventional 0.271, multistep 0.984.
- [R] The toolbox: [Spitzner et al. 2021, PLoS ONE 16(4): e0249447, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0249447; arXiv:2007.03367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03367).

**3.4 Long-range temporal correlations by DFA.** [R] from [Hardstone et al. 2012, Frontiers in Physiology 3:450, doi:10.3389/fphys.2012.00450](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2012.00450/full) (formulas did not survive extraction; the algorithm is given in words).

## 4. Dynamics Landscapes: Definitions, Algorithms, and Exact Cases

### Takeaway

There are four inequivalent "landscapes": the Freidlin–Wentzell quasi-potential (a zero-noise object, a Lyapunov function, computed by ordered upwind or ordered line integral methods and by minimum action paths), the stationary landscape $U=-\ln P_{ss}$ with its flux (defined at a given noise level, not a Lyapunov function in general), the potential of an orthogonal ("normal") decomposition (half the quasi-potential within a basin), and landscapes fitted from data (the pairwise maximum entropy energy, attractor graphs of Boolean networks). They coincide, up to stated factors of 2 and of the noise strength, for gradient systems with isotropic noise, and exactly in one dimension.

Its Example 1, a plankton–consumer model ($\dot x = \alpha x(1-x/\beta) - \delta x^2y/(\kappa+x^2)$, $\dot y = \gamma x^2y/(\kappa+x^2) - \mu y^2$; $\alpha=1.54$, $\beta=10.14$, $\gamma=0.476$, $\delta=1$, $\kappa=1$, $\mu=0.112509$), has equilibria (0,0) source, (1.4049, 2.8081) stable spiral with eigenvalues $-0.047\pm0.458i$, (4.2008, 4.0039) saddle, (4.9040, 4.0619) stable node with eigenvalues −0.377 and −0.093, (10.14, 0) saddle — [Moore et al. 2016, The R Journal, RJ-2016-031](https://journal.r-project.org/archive/2016/RJ-2016-031/index.html).

**4.2 The landscape definitions compared (Zhou, Aliyu, Aurell, Huang 2012).** All [R] from [J. R. Soc. Interface 9, 3539, doi:10.1098/rsif.2012.0434 (PMC3481575)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3481575/); the equations are images on the page, so the forms are as described in its text.

- Stationary landscape (Wang's): from the Fokker–Planck equation with drift $F$ and diffusion $D$, the steady flux $J_{ss}$ is divergence-free and $F = -D\nabla U_{prob} + F_c$ with $U_{prob} = -\ln P_{ss}$, $F_c = J_{ss}/P_{ss}$. $F_c$ is not divergence-free in general, so this is not the Helmholtz decomposition; $(\nabla U_{prob}, F_c) = D\,\nabla\cdot F_c$, so the two parts are orthogonal only as $D\to0$ or when $\nabla\cdot F_c = 0$; $dU_{prob}/dt\le0$ along deterministic trajectories is **not** guaranteed.
- Normal decomposition: $F = -\nabla U_{norm} + F_\perp$ with $(\nabla U_{norm}, F_\perp)=0$; then $U_{norm}$ decreases along trajectories; it solves a Hamilton–Jacobi equation (solved there by Newton–Raphson).
- Ao's decomposition: $F = -(S+T)\nabla U_{ST}$-type form with $S$ symmetric and $T$ antisymmetric; it equals the normal decomposition when $D=I$; for $D\ne I$ it remains a Lyapunov function but, the paper argues, does not give transition rates.
- Relation to Freidlin–Wentzell: within one basin the Freidlin–Wentzell quasi-potential is "exactly twice" $U_{norm}$; the action is zero on downhill segments and counts only the uphill part. For several attractors the paper takes $U_{norm}$ as a candidate global function and **conjectures** a transitivity condition, which holds in its four-attractor example but "is not guaranteed in general".
- The Helmholtz decomposition is rejected: its potential need not decrease along trajectories.
- Numerical remark: $-D\ln P_{ss}$ approached $U_{norm}$ poorly at the noise levels that could be simulated ($D$ = 0.015 to 0.09 in the first example; $D$ = 20 was needed for a usable landscape in the second).
- [M] The flux landscape's source — [Wang, Xu and Wang 2008, PNAS 105, 12271, doi:10.1073/pnas.0800579105](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0800579105) (a search in this session did not confirm this DOI; a companion paper by the same authors on the circadian clock describes the limit cycle's landscape as a closed-ring valley whose flux drives the motion round the ring: [PMC2667439](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2667439/), read as a search summary). The review — [Wang 2015, Advances in Physics 64, 1, doi:10.1080/00018732.2015.1037068](https://doi.org/10.1080/00018732.2015.1037068). Ao's construction — [Ao 2004, J. Phys. A 37, L25, doi:10.1088/0305-4470/37/3/L01](https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/37/3/L01).

**4.3 The Waddington landscape (all [M]).**

- Waddington's picture is a metaphor without a formula; its quantitative readings are the three above: $-\ln P_{ss}$ with flux and least-action paths between cell fates ([Wang et al. 2011, PNAS 108, 8257, doi:10.1073/pnas.1017017108](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1017017108)); the quasi-potential, with the argument that a true potential does not exist for non-gradient gene networks ([Zhou et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3481575/), [R] above); and a potential drawn per bifurcation, where "valleys" split at pitchforks or vanish at folds ([Ferrell 2012, Current Biology 22, R458, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2012.03.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2012.03.045)).
- A geometric reading classifies decision landscapes as gradient-like Morse–Smale systems by their catastrophes — [Rand et al. 2021, Cell Systems 12, 547, doi:10.1016/j.cels.2021.08.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2021.08.013) (the DOI is recalled and unchecked; see Gaps).

**4.4 Basin stability and other global measures.**

- [R] Definition: $S_B(A) = \mu(B(A)) = \int_R \mathbf 1_{B(A)}\,d\mu\in[0,1]$, the measure under a chosen distribution μ on a reference region $R$ of the basin of attractor $A$. Estimator: draw $N$ independent initial states from μ, integrate each until its attractor is decided, $\hat S_B = M/N$. Standard error $\sqrt{S_B(1-S_B)/N}$, "independently of the system's dimension"; for small $S_B$ the relative error scales as $1/\sqrt{NS_B}$ — [Schultz, Menck, Heitzig and Kurths 2017, New J. Phys. 19, 023005, doi:10.1088/1367-2630/aa5a7b; arXiv:1603.01844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.01844).
- [R] Limits: estimation stays meaningful for fractal (even Wada) boundaries (for the pendulum $\dot\varphi=\omega$, $\dot\omega = X\cos t - \alpha\omega - K\sin\varphi$, $\alpha=0.1$, $K=1$, $X=7/4$, region $[-\pi,\pi]\times[-2,4]$, four limit cycles, estimates stayed within one standard error across arithmetic precisions) but fails for riddled or intermingled basins (for the map $F_\lambda(z) = z^2-(1+\lambda i)\bar z$, $\lambda = 1.02871376822$, three attractors, estimates varied by up to 50% with precision). Practical check: compare $e_p = |\hat S_B(p_h)-\hat S_B(p_l)|$ at two precisions (double and single) with the sampling error; rounding matters only if $e_p$ exceeds it. Uncertainty exponent: the fraction of ε-uncertain initial states scales as $f(\varepsilon)\propto\varepsilon^{\alpha}$, $\alpha<1$ for fractal boundaries — [arXiv:1603.01844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.01844).
- [R, via search] The founding paper: basin stability is "a new measure related to the volume of the basin of attraction", "non-local, nonlinear and easily applicable, even to high-dimensional systems" — [Menck, Heitzig, Marwan and Kurths 2013, Nature Physics 9, 89, doi:10.1038/nphys2516](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_18411).
- [R] The present reference implementation covers attractors, basins and their fractions, exit basins, basin boundaries and edge states with their fractal properties, "nonlocal stability, also called global stability or resilience", and global continuation of attractors and basins over a parameter — (recent) [Attractors.jl](https://github.com/JuliaDynamics/Attractors.jl), paper [Datseris, Rossi and Wagemakers 2023, Chaos 33, 073151, doi:10.1063/5.0159675](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0159675).
- [M] Other global measures: the minimal distance to the basin boundary ("stability threshold", "minimal fatal shock") — [Klinshov, Nekorkin and Kurths 2016, New J. Phys. 18, 013004, doi:10.1088/1367-2630/18/1/013004](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/18/1/013004); survivability, the fraction of initial states whose trajectories stay in a desirable region — [Hellmann et al. 2016, Sci. Rep. 6, 29654; arXiv:1506.01257](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1506.01257); integral stability, combining basin volume with return rates — [Mitra, Kurths and Donner 2015, Sci. Rep. 5, 16196, doi:10.1038/srep16196](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep16196).

**4.5 Energy landscapes fitted from data (pairwise maximum entropy).** All [R] from [Ezaki, Watanabe, Ohzeki and Masuda 2017, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 375, 20160287; arXiv:1611.05137](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.05137).

- Binarise each region's signal at its own time average: $\sigma_i\in\{-1,+1\}$. Model: $P(\sigma\mid h,J) = e^{-E(\sigma)}/Z$, $E(\sigma) = -\sum_i h_i\sigma_i - \frac12\sum_i\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j$, $J_{ij}=J_{ji}$, $J_{ii}=0$; fitted so that $\langle\sigma_i\rangle$ and $\langle\sigma_i\sigma_j\rangle$ match the data. With $\tilde\sigma\in\{0,1\}$: $\tilde h_i = 2h_i - 2\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}$, $\tilde J_{ij} = 4J_{ij}$.
- Fitting: exact likelihood ascent, $h_i\leftarrow h_i + \epsilon(\langle\sigma_i\rangle_{emp}-\langle\sigma_i\rangle_{mod})$, $J_{ij}\leftarrow J_{ij}+\epsilon(\langle\sigma_i\sigma_j\rangle_{emp}-\langle\sigma_i\sigma_j\rangle_{mod})$, the model's moments by summing over all $2^N$ patterns; pseudo-likelihood, with $\langle\sigma_i\rangle_{\tilde P} = \frac{1}{t_{max}}\sum_t\tanh[h_i+\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}\sigma_j(t)]$ and $\langle\sigma_i\sigma_j\rangle_{\tilde P} = \frac{1}{t_{max}}\sum_t\sigma_j(t)\tanh[\cdot]$, converging to the maximum-likelihood estimate as $t_{max}$ grows; minimum probability flow with rates $W(\sigma\mid\sigma') = \exp[-\frac12(E(\sigma)-E(\sigma'))]$ between patterns differing at one region.
- Accuracy: $I_2/I_N = (S_1-S_2)/(S_1-S_N)$ with $S_1$, $S_2$, $S_N$ the entropies of the independent model, the pairwise model and the empirical distribution; and $r = [D_{KL}(P_1\|P_N) - D_{KL}(P_2\|P_N)]/D_{KL}(P_1\|P_N)$ (as printed by the fetch tool; check the order of the arguments in the PDF); the two agree for the maximum-likelihood estimate.
- Landscape: a local minimum is a pattern whose energy is below that of its $N$ single-flip neighbours; its basin is the set of patterns reaching it by steepest descent over single flips. The barrier between minima α, α′ is $\bar E(\alpha,\alpha') - E(\alpha)$ with $\bar E$ the minimax energy over paths, computed by a Dijkstra-like sweep; minima joined at the same $\bar E$ share a branch of the disconnectivity graph.
- Data needed: accuracy scales with $t_{max}/2^N$; about 5 visits per pattern for accuracy 0.8 and about 16 for 0.9; typical scans support $N\approx5$ for one participant and $N\approx 8$ pooled over ten. Matlab code in the supplement, no licence stated.
- [R, via search] Earlier applications — [Watanabe et al. 2014, Frontiers in Neuroinformatics 8:12, doi:10.3389/fninf.2014.00012](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2014.00012/full).

**4.6 Attractor landscapes of Boolean networks (all [M]).**

- A synchronous Boolean network on $n$ nodes is a map on $2^n$ states; its state-transition graph is a functional graph, each component one cycle (the attractor) with in-trees (its basin); basin sizes as fractions of $2^n$ are the discrete analogue of basin stability — [Kauffman 1969, J. Theor. Biol. 22, 437, doi:10.1016/0022-5193(69)90015-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5193(69)90015-0). Under asynchronous update the attractors are the terminal strongly connected components.
- A published fixture: the yeast cell-cycle network (11 nodes, 2,048 states) has 7 fixed points, the largest basin holding 1,764 states — [Li et al. 2004, PNAS 101, 4781, doi:10.1073/pnas.0305937101](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0305937101).

### Inferences

- [D] **Which landscape exists for which system.**

| System | Quasi-potential $U_{FW}$ (noise $\sqrt\varepsilon\,dW$) | $-\ln P_{ss}$ and flux | Normal decomposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gradient, $b=-\nabla V$, isotropic noise | $2V$ (exact) | $P_{ss}\propto e^{-2V/\varepsilon}$, flux zero | $U_{norm}=V$ |
| One dimension, additive noise, no flux at the boundaries | $2V$, $V=-\int b$ (exact) | exact, flux zero | $V$ |
| $b = -\nabla V + l$, $\nabla V\cdot l=0$, $\nabla\cdot l = 0$ | $2V$ | $P_{ss}\propto e^{-2V/\varepsilon}$ exactly, flux $lP_{ss}\neq0$ | $V$ |
| Linear, $b=Ax$, $A$ stable | $\tfrac12x^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}x$ with $A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top} = -I$ | Gaussian with covariance $\varepsilon\Sigma$; equal for every ε | $\tfrac14x^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}x$ |
| General non-gradient, one attractor | exists (may be non-smooth) | exists at each ε; converges only as ε → 0 | exists where the Hamilton–Jacobi solution is smooth |
| Several attractors | local ones, glued through saddles; global one needs Freidlin–Wentzell's graph construction | one function at each ε | transitivity only conjectured (Zhou et al.) |
| Chaotic attractor, limit cycle | defined with respect to the attractor (zero on it) | ring-shaped valley, flux round it | zero on the attractor |
| Discrete states (Boolean, Ising) | not defined | energy $E=-\ln P$ up to a constant (data-fitted) | not defined; attractor graph instead |

  The first, second and fourth rows follow from the [R] Hamilton–Jacobi equation and decomposition; the linear row was checked against the [R] example ($\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac14,\tfrac12)$ satisfies the Lyapunov equation for $A = \begin{pmatrix}-2&-10\\20&-1\end{pmatrix}$ and gives $U=2x_1^2+x_2^2$).
- [D] **Conventions differ by factors that must be declared.** With $dx = b\,dt+\sqrt\varepsilon\,dW$ the quasi-potential of a gradient system is $2V$ (Dahiya and Cameron; Zhou et al.'s "exactly twice"); QPot halves it; with $dx = -\nabla V dt + \sqrt{2D}\,dW$ the stationary density is $e^{-V/D}$. A library should fix one (the potential's, i.e. QPot's, agrees with the 1D and gradient cases) and name it in every result.
- [D] **Exact one-dimensional tests.** For $dx = -V'(x)dt + \sqrt{2D}\,dW$ with $V = x^4/4 - x^2/2$: $P_{ss}\propto e^{-V/D}$, barrier $\Delta V = 1/4$, and the Kramers time $\tau\approx\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{V''(a)|V''(b)|}}e^{\Delta V/D} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt2}e^{1/(4D)}$ ($V''(\pm1)=2$, $V''(0)=-1$); the exact mean first passage time is the double integral $\tau(x_0) = \frac1D\int_{x_0}^{b}dy\,e^{V(y)/D}\int_{-\infty}^{y}dz\,e^{-V(z)/D}$ (standard results, recalled, e.g. Hänggi, Talkner and Borkovec 1990, [doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.62.251](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.62.251), not opened). physis's phase-type first passage and master equations can check a discretised version.
- [D] **A two-dimensional gradient test with a known barrier**: the Maier–Stein field $b = (x - x^3 - \beta xy^2,\ -(1+x^2)y)$ is gradient exactly when $\beta=1$, with $V = -x^2/2 + x^4/4 + y^2/2 + x^2y^2/2$, so the quasi-potential barrier from $(\pm1,0)$ to the saddle $(0,0)$ is $2\cdot\tfrac14 = \tfrac12$ in the $\sqrt\varepsilon$ convention; a minimum action path solver must return the straight segment along the $x$-axis and this action.
- [D] **Sampling error of basin stability.** With $N = 500$ the standard error is at most $\sqrt{0.25/500} = 0.0224$. Near 0 or 1 the normal interval is poor; a Wilson or Clopper–Pearson interval should be reported, and the error of *differences* between parameter values is reduced by common random initial states. An exact fixture: $\dot x = x - x^3$ with initial states uniform on $[-a,b]$ has $S_B(+1) = b/(a+b)$.
- [D] **Exact fixtures for the maximum entropy model.** For $N=2$ with $\langle\sigma_i\rangle=0$: $\langle\sigma_1\sigma_2\rangle = \tanh J$, so $J = \mathrm{artanh}\,c$; the independent model has $h_i = \mathrm{artanh}\langle\sigma_i\rangle$. For $N\le 15$ or so the partition function can be enumerated and the fit checked to rounding. The Potts and Ising machinery of physis and the exact enumeration fit the "reuse before writing" rule; the disconnectivity graph is a minimax-path computation on the hypercube, i.e. a bottleneck shortest path, close to what morphe's graphs already hold.
- [D] The quasi-potential field (OLIM), a minimum action path (gMAM) and Monte Carlo basin fractions answer three different questions (how improbable is each state; how does the system most probably escape and at what cost; how large is each basin) and none substitutes for another; a drawing should say which it shows.

## 5. Topological and Geometric Features of Dynamics That Are Drawn

### Takeaway

Each feature has a standard definition and a standard way of being computed; all references here are recalled, not re-opened, and this section is deliberately brief.

### Cited Findings (all [M] unless marked)

- **Stable and unstable manifolds.** $W^s(p) = \{x: \varphi_t(x)\to p,\ t\to\infty\}$, $W^u(p)$ with $t\to-\infty$; tangent at $p$ to the stable and unstable eigenspaces. Computed by growing from a small sphere in the eigenspace (geodesic level sets, boundary value problems for orbit families) — [Krauskopf et al. 2005, Int. J. Bifurcation and Chaos 15, 763, doi:10.1142/S0218127405012533](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218127405012533).
- **Separatrices and basins.** A basin boundary in the plane is generically the stable manifold of a saddle; boundaries may be fractal, measured by the uncertainty exponent $f(\varepsilon)\sim\varepsilon^{\alpha}$ ([R] in [Schultz et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.01844)), boundary dimension $d-\alpha$ — [Grebogi et al. 1983, Phys. Lett. A 99, 415, doi:10.1016/0375-9601(83)90945-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0375-9601(83)90945-3). Drawn by colouring a grid of initial states by attractor.
- **Poincaré sections.** The first-return map $P:\Sigma\to\Sigma$ of a flow to a transversal surface; computed accurately by interchanging the roles of time and the section coordinate at a crossing — [Hénon 1982, Physica D 5, 412, doi:10.1016/0167-2789(82)90034-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(82)90034-3).
- **Recurrence plots and quantification.** $R_{ij} = \Theta(\varepsilon - \|x_i-x_j\|)$ — [Eckmann, Kamphorst and Ruelle 1987, Europhys. Lett. 4, 973, doi:10.1209/0295-5075/4/9/004](https://doi.org/10.1209/0295-5075/4/9/004). Measures: recurrence rate $RR = N^{-2}\sum R_{ij}$; determinism $DET = \sum_{l\ge l_{min}}lP(l)/\sum_{l\ge1}lP(l)$ over diagonal lines; mean and longest diagonal $L$, $L_{max}$; entropy of diagonal lengths; laminarity $LAM$ and trapping time $TT$ over vertical lines — [Marwan et al. 2007, Physics Reports 438, 237, doi:10.1016/j.physrep.2006.11.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2006.11.001). [R] `pyunicorn` provides recurrence quantification, recurrence networks and visibility graphs — [pyunicorn](https://github.com/pik-copan/pyunicorn).
- **Persistence diagrams of delay embeddings.** The Vietoris–Rips persistence of the sliding-window point cloud $SW_{M,\tau}f(t) = (f(t),\dots,f(t+M\tau))$; the largest 1-dimensional persistence scores periodicity — [Perea and Harer 2015, Found. Comput. Math. 15, 799, doi:10.1007/s10208-014-9206-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-014-9206-z). Stability: bottleneck distance between diagrams is at most the sup-norm distance of the functions — [Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner and Harer 2007, Discrete Comput. Geom. 37, 103, doi:10.1007/s00454-006-1276-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-006-1276-5).
- **Conley–Morse graphs.** Cover phase space by a grid, enclose the map's image of each box (an outer approximation, a directed graph on boxes); the recurrent strongly connected components are the Morse sets, the reachability order between them the Morse graph, each node carrying its Conley index; computed over a grid of parameters as a database — [Arai et al. 2009, SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 8, 757, doi:10.1137/080734935](https://doi.org/10.1137/080734935).
- **Lagrangian coherent structures and FTLE.** With flow map $\Phi_{t_0}^{t_0+T}$ and Cauchy–Green tensor $C = (\nabla\Phi)^{\top}\nabla\Phi$: $\sigma_T(x_0,t_0) = \frac{1}{|T|}\ln\sqrt{\lambda_{max}(C)}$; LCS were defined as ridges of this field — [Shadden, Lekien and Marsden 2005, Physica D 212, 271, doi:10.1016/j.physd.2005.10.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2005.10.007); ridges of FTLE need not be material barriers, and a variational definition replaces them — [Haller 2015, Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 47, 137, doi:10.1146/annurev-fluid-010313-141322](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-fluid-010313-141322). Standard test flow, the double gyre: $\psi = A\sin(\pi f(x,t))\sin(\pi y)$, $f = \epsilon\sin(\omega t)x^2 + (1-2\epsilon\sin\omega t)x$, $A=0.1$, $\omega = 2\pi/10$, $\epsilon=0.25$ on $[0,2]\times[0,1]$ (Shadden et al. 2005).

### Inferences

- [D] Exact checks: for the linear saddle $\dot x = x$, $\dot y=-y$ the FTLE is 1 everywhere for every $T$, and $W^s$, $W^u$ are the axes; for a noiseless sine the recurrence plot is a set of diagonals at spacing one period ($DET=1$, $LAM=0$); for a periodic signal the sliding-window cloud is a closed curve with one long-lived 1-cycle.
