# Inducing Structured Dynamical Models from Data: System Identification and Equation Discovery on a CPU (as of November 2026)

*Provenance key.* **[V]** = the page was fetched or searched in this session (2026-11-12) and the claim was read there. **[R]** = recalled from the primary source; the identifier (arXiv number or DOI) is given from memory and was not re-fetched in this session, so the writer should treat the bibliographic detail as very likely right but unverified. **[recent]** = published 2024–2026. Licences marked "file read" were read from the repository's licence file in this session. Cost classes: **CF** closed form (one linear solve, SVD or eigenproblem); **CVX** convex programme or iterated least squares; **MIP** mixed-integer; **GRAD** iterative gradient training (non-convex); **COMB** combinatorial or evolutionary search.

## 1. Sparse Regression: SINDy and Its Variants

### Takeaway
SINDy reduces discovery to sparse linear regression of derivatives on a fixed term library, so it is cheap (iterated least squares) and laptop-friendly, but its result is conditional on the library containing the true terms and on derivative estimates; the weak (integral) form is the single most consistently reported improvement, on noisy and on clean data. PySINDy (MIT, v2.1.0) implements nearly the whole family, so a C++ library need only own the regression core, the weak form and a typed library builder.

### Cited Findings

**The base method and its optimisers**
- SINDy poses $\dot X = \Theta(X)\,\Xi$ with a library $\Theta$ of candidate functions and finds sparse $\Xi$ by sequentially thresholded least squares (STLSQ); the assumption is that the dynamics have few active terms in the chosen basis and coordinates. Cost class CF/CVX: repeated least-squares solves. [R] — [Brunton, Proctor, Kutz 2016, PNAS 113:3932, arXiv:1509.03580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.03580)
- STLSQ converges in finitely many steps to a local minimiser of the $\ell_0$-penalised least-squares objective (not necessarily the global one). [R] — [Zhang and Schaeffer 2019, arXiv:1805.06445](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06445)
- SR3 relaxes the sparse problem with an auxiliary variable so that constraints and non-convex regularisers fit one framework (CVX-like alternating steps). [R] — [Champion, Zheng, Aravkin, Brunton, Kutz 2020, arXiv:1906.10612](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10612)
- MIOSR solves the $\ell_0$-constrained problem to provable optimality by mixed-integer optimisation (cost class MIP; needs Gurobi in PySINDy). [R] — [Bertsimas and Gurnee 2023, arXiv:2206.00176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00176)
- A benchmark of four SINDy optimisers on the dysts database of chaotic systems found "strong performance" from the original STLSQ and from MIOSR; ensembling was used in all cases; the weak formulation "significantly improves" on the traditional method even on clean data; and Pareto-optimal performance showed no significant dependence on the amount of chaos, scale separation, degree of nonlinearity or syntactic complexity. [V] — [Kaptanoglu, Zhang, Nicolaou, Fasel, Brunton 2023, arXiv:2302.10787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10787)

**PDEs**
- PDE-FIND extends SINDy to PDEs: the library holds spatial derivatives and their products, the regression is sequential thresholded ridge (STRidge), derivatives of noisy data are taken by polynomial interpolation; the paper's noisy tests are at the 1% noise level. [R] — [Rudy, Brunton, Proctor, Kutz 2017, Sci. Adv. 3:e1602614, arXiv:1609.06401](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06401)
- Weak SINDy for PDEs integrates against compactly supported test functions so that no derivative of the data is taken; the abstract claims "robust identification of PDEs in the large noise regime" with signal-to-noise ratio "approaching one in many well-known cases", near machine-precision recovery from noise-free data, and worst-case cost $O(N^{D+1}\log N)$ for $N$ points in each of $D+1$ dimensions via FFT-based separable test functions. [V] — [Messenger and Bortz 2021, J. Comput. Phys. 443:110525, arXiv:2007.02848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02848)
- Weak SINDy for ODEs (Galerkin form, generalised least squares for the correlated residual). [R] — [Messenger and Bortz 2021, Multiscale Model. Simul. 19:1474, arXiv:2005.04339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04339)
- WSINDy is asymptotically consistent in the continuum-data limit under stated conditions on the noise and the library; the result also exposes a bias from nonlinear library terms evaluated on noisy data. [R] — [Messenger and Bortz, arXiv:2211.16000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16000)
- An independent weak-form line (integration over space-time subdomains) recovered the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation from very noisy data and handled latent (unobserved) variables. [R] — [Reinbold, Gurevich, Grigoriev 2020, Phys. Rev. E 101:010203](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.101.010203)
- Weak-PDE-LEARN **[recent]** learns a neural-network surrogate of the solution and applies a weak-form sparse regression, for noisy and limited data. Cost class GRAD (network fit) plus CVX. [R] — [Stephany and Earls 2024, J. Comput. Phys., arXiv:2309.04699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04699)

**Robustness, implicit and controlled forms**
- Ensemble SINDy applies bootstrap aggregating over data and over library terms; standard SINDy is "sensitive to noise, especially in the low-data limit"; inclusion probabilities give uncertainty quantification; the abstract claims PDE recovery with more than twice the measurement noise previously reported, and Lotka–Volterra from the 1900–1920 lynx–hare record. Cost: CF times the number of bags (trivially parallel). [V] — [Fasel, Kutz, Brunton, Brunton 2022, Proc. R. Soc. A 478:20210904, arXiv:2111.10992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10992)
- SINDy-PI handles implicit and rational dynamics by solving one convex problem per candidate left-hand-side term in parallel, replacing the noise-sensitive null-space computation of implicit SINDy. Cost class CVX (needs cvxpy in PySINDy). [R] — [Kaheman, Kutz, Brunton 2020, arXiv:2004.02322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02322)
- SINDy with control (SINDYc) adds inputs $u$ to the library, giving models usable for model-predictive control. [R] — [Brunton, Proctor, Kutz 2016, arXiv:1605.06682](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06682)
- Constrained SINDy imposes linear equality constraints on coefficients (e.g. energy-preserving quadratic nonlinearity in Galerkin models of fluids). [R] — [Loiseau and Brunton 2018, J. Fluid Mech. 838:42, arXiv:1611.03271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03271)
- Trapping SINDy enforces a Schlegel–Noack trapping region so that quadratic models are globally bounded. [R] — [Kaptanoglu et al. 2021, Phys. Rev. Fluids 6:094401, arXiv:2105.01843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01843)
- Bayesian SINDy with sparsifying priors (spike-and-slab, regularised horseshoe) gives posterior inclusion probabilities at MCMC cost. [R] — [Hirsh, Barajas-Solano, Kutz 2022, arXiv:2107.02107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02107)

**Library, symmetry and dimensions**
- The CTF evaluation states that SINDy does well when its candidate library includes the true terms. [V] — [Wyder et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.23166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)
- Buckingham-Pi constraints can be imposed so that discovered models are dimensionally consistent (the library is built from dimensionless groups, or the groups are learned). [R] — [Bakarji, Callaham, Brunton, Kutz 2022, arXiv:2202.04643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04643)
- SPIDER builds the library from the irreducible representations of the assumed symmetry group (rotations, translations, Galilean boosts), so every candidate relation is equivariant by construction, and regresses in weak form on turbulent data. [R] — [Gurevich, Golden, Reinbold, Grigoriev 2024, J. Fluid Mech., arXiv:2105.00048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00048)
- A review of data-driven discovery notes that derivative approximation from noisy data is a major source of error and recasts discovery as Bayesian inference over structure and parameters. [V] — [North, Wikle, Schliep 2022, arXiv:2210.10663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10663)

**What PySINDy implements**
- Licence: MIT (file read; portions copyright Markus Quade's `sparsereg`). Latest release v2.1.0 (dated "08 Jan" on the releases page, year not shown; the repository was last pushed 2026-10-15). [V] — [PySINDy LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dynamicslab/pysindy/HEAD/LICENSE); [PySINDy releases](https://github.com/dynamicslab/pysindy/releases)
- v2.0.0 added Bayesian SINDy (`SBR`), trapping extended to enstrophy and local trapping (`TrappingSR3` a subclass of `ConstrainedSR3`), `MIOSR`, an `EnsemblingOptimizer` for data and library bagging, and STLSQ that can sparsify a subset of indices; v2.1.0 split discrete-time models into `DiscreteSINDy` and raised the minimum Python to 3.10; v1.7.3 added `ParameterizedLibrary` and `StableLinearSR3`; SINDy-PI requires cvxpy and MIOSR requires gurobipy (both optional extras). [V] — [PySINDy releases](https://github.com/dynamicslab/pysindy/releases)
- The package paper lists: weak-form and PDE libraries, SINDy-PI, ensembling, trapping, constrained SR3, SSR and FROLS greedy optimisers, control inputs, and differentiation through the `derivative` package (finite differences, smoothed, spectral, spline, total-variation). [R] — [Kaptanoglu et al. 2022, JOSS 7(69):3994, arXiv:2111.08481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08481)
- Other implementations: DataDrivenDiffEq.jl (MIT, file read) for Julia; the authors' MATLAB WSINDy repositories carry "Copyright 2020, All Rights Reserved" in the README and no licence file; the original PDE-FIND repository has no licence file. [V] — [DataDrivenDiffEq.jl licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SciML/DataDrivenDiffEq.jl/HEAD/LICENSE.md); [WSINDy_ODE README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MathBioCU/WSINDy_ODE/HEAD/README.md); [PDE-FIND repository](https://github.com/snagcliffs/PDE-FIND)

### Inferences
- Cost classes: STLSQ, STRidge, SR3, weak form, ensembling are all CF/CVX and run in milliseconds to seconds for ODEs and seconds to minutes for 1D–2D PDE fields on a laptop. MIOSR (MIP, commercial solver) and Bayesian SINDy (MCMC) are the only members that are not trivially cheap; Weak-PDE-LEARN is GRAD.
- For a C++ library the natural own-implementation set is: the library builder (typed by physical dimension and by symmetry representation, which the published methods treat as add-ons), STLSQ/STRidge, SR3 with linear constraints, the weak form with test functions and FFT convolution, and bagging. These are a few hundred lines each on existing linear algebra and are the methods for which exact checks exist (recovery of coefficients on noise-free data to near machine precision in weak form).
- MIOSR, SINDy-PI's convex programmes, SBR and neural variants are best borrowed through PySINDy (MIT, compatible).
- The WSINDy and PDE-FIND reference codes cannot be copied (no licence granted); the algorithms must be reimplemented from the papers.
- The 1% noise figure for PDE-FIND versus "SNR approaching one" for WSINDy is the clearest published statement of how much the weak form buys; both are author-reported on their own test problems.

### Gaps
- I did not find an independent (non-author) head-to-head of weak SINDy, ensemble SINDy and Weak-PDE-LEARN at matched noise levels; the Kaptanoglu benchmark is by PySINDy's developers.
- Exact noise thresholds at which STLSQ fails for specific systems (e.g. Lorenz) were not retrieved as numbers in this session.
- The year of PySINDy's v2.0.0 and v2.1.0 releases is not shown on the fetched page (inferred 2025 and 2026).

## 2. Operator Methods: DMD, Koopman, Transfer Operators

### Takeaway
DMD and EDMD are closed-form linear algebra (SVD and eigenproblems) and are the cheapest methods surveyed, but plain DMD is biased by sensor noise and EDMD suffers spectral pollution and only weak, subsequence convergence of spectra; residual DMD (Colbrook and Townsend) adds one extra matrix and gives error-controlled spectra, and is not in PyDMD's published feature list. Since 2024 there are proved impossibility results for learning Koopman spectra from trajectory data.

### Cited Findings

**DMD family**
- DMD fits a best linear operator $A$ with $Y \approx AX$ from snapshot pairs; "exact DMD" defines modes from the SVD of $X$ and is CF. [R] — [Tu, Rowley, Luchtenburg, Brunton, Kutz 2014, arXiv:1312.0041](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0041); [Schmid 2010, J. Fluid Mech. 656:5](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112010001217)
- Sensor noise biases DMD eigenvalues (towards spurious damping); forward–backward DMD and total-least-squares DMD remove the bias at CF cost. [R] — [Dawson, Hemati, Williams, Rowley 2016, arXiv:1507.02264](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02264); [Hemati, Rowley, Deem, Cattafesta 2017, arXiv:1502.03854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03854)
- Optimised DMD fits exponentials to all snapshots at once by variable projection (nonlinear least squares, cost class iterative but small), allowing uneven sampling. [R] — [Askham and Kutz 2018, arXiv:1704.02343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02343)
- BOP-DMD bags optimised DMD for stability and uncertainty; the PyDMD paper calls it a "generally recommended all-purpose DMD method for noisy data" and says exact DMD is sensitive to noise. [V] — [Ichinaga et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.07463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07463); method in [R] [Sashidhar and Kutz 2022, arXiv:2107.10878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10878)
- Higher-order DMD (HODMD) applies DMD to time-delayed snapshots, for data whose spatial rank is smaller than the number of frequencies. [R] — [Le Clainche and Vega 2017, SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 16:882](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1054924)
- Physics-informed DMD restricts $A$ to a matrix manifold (unitary, symmetric, circulant, triangular, …) by a Procrustes problem, giving closed-form solutions for several constraints. [R] — [Baddoo, Herrmann, McKeon, Kutz, Brunton 2023, arXiv:2112.04307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04307)
- HAVOK models chaos as a linear system in delay coordinates forced by the last coordinate. [R] — [Brunton, Brunton, Proctor, Kaiser, Kutz 2017, arXiv:1608.05306](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05306)

**What PyDMD implements**
- Before v1.0: exact DMD, DMD with control, multiresolution, sparsity-promoting, compressed, time-delay, higher-order, forward–backward, total least squares, optimal closed-form and subspace DMD. Added in v1.0: optimised DMD, BOP-DMD (`BOPDMD`), CoSTS, parametric DMD, randomised DMD, physics-informed DMD, extended DMD including kernel variants, HAVOK, LANDO and centring. The paper names mpEDMD, residual DMD and rigorous Koopman spectral computation as "strong candidates for future extensions", i.e. not implemented as of that paper. [V] — [Ichinaga et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.07463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07463)
- Licence MIT (GitHub API, repository pushed 2026-10-03). [V] — [PyDMD repository](https://github.com/PyDMD/PyDMD)
- PyKoopman (MIT, file read) is a separate package for Koopman regression with dictionaries and neural observables. [V] — [PyKoopman licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dynamicslab/pykoopman/HEAD/LICENSE); paper [R] [Pan et al. 2024, arXiv:2306.12962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12962)

**EDMD, kernel EDMD and convergence**
- EDMD lifts the state by a dictionary $\Psi$ and solves a least-squares problem for a matrix approximating the Koopman operator on $\mathrm{span}\,\Psi$ (CF: Gram matrices and an eigenproblem). [R] — [Williams, Kevrekidis, Rowley 2015, J. Nonlinear Sci. 25:1307, arXiv:1408.4408](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.4408)
- Kernel EDMD replaces the dictionary by a kernel so the cost scales with the number of snapshots, not of features. [R] — [Williams, Rowley, Kevrekidis 2015, arXiv:1411.2260](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2260)
- As the number of samples grows EDMD converges to the Galerkin projection of the Koopman operator onto the dictionary's span; as the dictionary grows the operators converge strongly, but eigenvalues converge only along subsequences and in a weak sense. [R] — [Korda and Mezić 2018, arXiv:1703.04680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04680)
- A finite-dimensional Koopman-invariant subspace containing the state cannot exist for systems with several isolated fixed points (no such linear representation is topologically conjugate). [R] — [Brunton, Brunton, Proctor, Kutz 2016, arXiv:1510.03007](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.03007)

**Residual DMD and spectral pollution**
- ResDMD computes, from the same snapshot data plus one additional matrix ($\Psi_Y^* W \Psi_Y$), a residual for every candidate eigenpair, which removes spurious eigenvalues (spectral pollution), and computes pseudospectra and spectral measures with convergence guarantees. Cost: CF per candidate; pseudospectra need a small eigen- or singular-value problem per grid point. [R] — [Colbrook and Townsend 2024, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 77:221, arXiv:2111.14889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14889); [Colbrook, Ayton, Szőke 2023, J. Fluid Mech., arXiv:2205.09779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09779)
- Reference MATLAB code for ResDMD is BSD-2-Clause (file read). [V] — [Residual-Dynamic-Mode-Decomposition licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MColbrook/Residual-Dynamic-Mode-Decomposition/HEAD/LICENSE)
- mpEDMD enforces measure preservation by an orthogonal Procrustes problem and converges for spectral measures and spectra of measure-preserving systems. [R] — [Colbrook 2023, arXiv:2209.02244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02244)
- **[recent]** "Limits and Powers of Koopman Learning" proves that there are smooth, well-behaved systems on tori for which non-trivial Koopman eigenfunctions cannot be determined by any sequence of algorithms, even randomised and with unlimited data, and classifies Koopman spectral problems in the Solvability Complexity Index hierarchy; it says of EDMD that convergence is weak and only along subsequences. [V] — [Colbrook, Mezić, Stepanenko 2024, arXiv:2407.06312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06312)
- **[recent]** A review organises the many DMD variants and their convergence theory. [R] — [Colbrook 2024, "The multiverse of dynamic mode decomposition algorithms", arXiv:2312.00137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00137)

**Transfer operator, Ulam's method, coherent sets, generators**
- Ulam's method discretises the Perron–Frobenius operator on a box partition (a row-stochastic matrix of transition counts); almost-invariant sets are read from eigenvectors near eigenvalue 1. Cost CF (sparse eigenproblem) but boxes grow exponentially with dimension. [R] — [Dellnitz and Junge 1999, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 36:491](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036142996313002)
- Finite-time coherent sets are obtained from the second singular vectors of a normalised transfer operator between two times. [R] — [Froyland, Santitissadeekorn, Monahan 2010, Chaos 20:043116](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3502450); [Froyland 2013, Physica D 250:1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2013.01.013)
- Ulam's method is EDMD's adjoint with indicator functions as the dictionary; the two operators' approximations are unified. [R] — [Klus, Koltai, Schütte 2016, arXiv:1512.05997](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05997)
- Generator EDMD (gEDMD) approximates the Koopman generator (for an SDE, the backward Kolmogorov operator) from data and so identifies drift and diffusion; with the identity observable it reduces to SINDy-like regression. CF. [R] — [Klus, Nüske, Peitz, Niemann, Clementi, Schütte 2020, Physica D 406:132416, arXiv:1909.10638](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10638)
- VAMP gives a variational score for Koopman models of non-reversible dynamics, usable for model selection and cross-validation. [R] — [Wu and Noé 2020, arXiv:1707.04659](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04659)
- Implementations: deeptime (LGPL-3.0, file read: weak copyleft) has MSMs, HMMs, Koopman models (TICA, VAMP, EDMD, kernel methods) and SINDy; Klus's `d3s` is LGPL-3.0 (file read). [V] — [deeptime licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deeptime-ml/deeptime/HEAD/LICENSE.txt); [d3s licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sklus/d3s/HEAD/LICENSE.txt); paper [V] [Hoffmann et al. 2021, arXiv:2110.15013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15013)
- In the CTF evaluation the operator methods scored low on forecasting composites: OptDMD 20.62 (Lorenz) and −11.04 (KS), Higher-Order DMD 0.94 and −25.23, PyKoopman 8.33 and −20.11, where 100 is perfect and a zero prediction scores 0.00 on KS. [V] — [Wyder et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.23166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)

### Inferences
- Everything in this family except BOP-DMD's variable projection and neural dictionaries is CF and practical on a laptop for state dimensions up to $10^5$–$10^6$ through the rank-$r$ SVD; kernel EDMD is limited by the snapshot count ($O(M^3)$).
- For a C++ library: exact DMD, TLS/forward–backward DMD, EDMD with a user dictionary, ResDMD residuals and pseudospectra, Ulam's matrix with almost-invariant and coherent sets, and gEDMD are all short linear-algebra routines with exact checks (linear systems, circle rotations, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator) and are good candidates to own. ResDMD is the notable gap in PyDMD's published list and its reference code is BSD (compatible).
- BOP-DMD, CoSTS, LANDO, parametric DMD and neural Koopman models are better borrowed from PyDMD/PyKoopman (both MIT).
- deeptime and d3s are LGPL: usable as an optional Python dependency called at arm's length, but not to be copied into an MIT code base.
- The low CTF scores of DMD-type methods are for autoregressive forecasting of chaotic data, where a linear model with a finite spectrum cannot reproduce a continuous spectrum; they do not speak to DMD's use for modal analysis.

### Gaps
- Whether PyDMD has added residual DMD since the 2024 paper was not checked against the current repository.
- I found no reported wall-clock comparison of ResDMD against EDMD at scale.

## 3. Stochastic Models: Kramers–Moyal, Drift and Diffusion, Markov Models, Jumps

### Takeaway
Drift and diffusion are estimated in closed form from conditional moments, but the estimates are biased at finite sampling interval and by measurement noise, and published corrections exist to arbitrary order; Markov-state and hidden Markov models are cheap, mature and well served by existing libraries, while the generator of a jump process observed at discrete times is not always identifiable (the embedding problem).

### Cited Findings
- The Kramers–Moyal coefficients $D^{(n)}(x)=\lim_{\tau\to0}\frac{1}{n!\,\tau}\langle (X_{t+\tau}-X_t)^n \mid X_t=x\rangle$ give drift ($n=1$) and diffusion ($n=2$) of a Langevin model directly from data by binning or kernel regression (CF); the method assumes the Markov property above a Markov–Einstein time scale. [R] — [Siegert, Friedrich, Peinke 1998, arXiv:cond-mat/9803250](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9803250); review [Friedrich, Peinke, Sahimi, Tabar 2011, Phys. Rep. 506:87](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2011.05.003)
- Finite sampling intervals bias the estimates; a first-order correction to the diffusion estimate was given by Ragwitz and Kantz, different estimators' definitions were compared by Gottschall and Peinke, exact finite-interval expressions by Lade, and a low-sampling-rate optimisation method by Honisch and Friedrich. [R] — [Ragwitz and Kantz 2001, Phys. Rev. Lett. 87:254501](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.87.254501); [Gottschall and Peinke 2008, New J. Phys. 10:083034](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/10/8/083034); [Lade 2009, arXiv:0905.4324](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.4324); [Honisch and Friedrich 2011, Phys. Rev. E 83:066701](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.83.066701)
- Corrections of arbitrary order follow from a power-series expansion of the Kramers–Moyal operator's generator, in closed form in the conditional moments, for diffusions and jump-diffusions; they help tell the two apart. [V] — [Rydin Gorjão, Witthaut, Lehnertz, Lind 2021, Entropy 23:517, arXiv:2104.12813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12813)
- Strong measurement noise destroys the Markov property of the observed series and must be modelled explicitly. [R] — [Böttcher et al. 2006, Phys. Rev. Lett. 97:090603](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.97.090603)
- Jump-diffusion processes are separated from pure diffusions through the higher coefficients: a non-vanishing fourth coefficient signals jumps (Pawula's theorem), and jump rate and amplitude follow from $D^{(4)}$ and $D^{(6)}$. [R] — [Anvari, Tabar, Peinke, Lehnertz 2016, Sci. Rep. 6:35435](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep35435); [Lehnertz, Zabawa, Tabar 2018, New J. Phys. 20:113043](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/aaf0d7)
- Reference implementation `kramersmoyal` (kernel-based, $n$-dimensional): MIT (file read). [V] — [KramersMoyal licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LRydin/KramersMoyal/HEAD/LICENSE); paper [R] [Rydin Gorjão and Meirinhos 2019, JOSS 4(44):1693](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.01693)
- Stochastic SINDy regresses the Kramers–Moyal conditional averages on a library with cross-validation to choose sparsity. [R] — [Boninsegna, Nüske, Clementi 2018, J. Chem. Phys. 148:241723, arXiv:1712.02432](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02432)
- Langevin regression corrects finite-sampling effects by matching Kramers–Moyal averages through the adjoint Fokker–Planck equation and enforces consistency with the stationary density (iterative optimisation around CF pieces). [R] — [Callaham, Loiseau, Rigas, Brunton 2021, Proc. R. Soc. A, arXiv:2009.01006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01006)
- For discretely sampled diffusions, closed-form expansions of the transition density give approximate maximum likelihood. [R] — [Aït-Sahalia 2002, Econometrica 70:223](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00274)
- Markov state models: discretise state space, count transitions at lag $\tau$, estimate a (reversible) transition matrix by maximum likelihood, and validate by implied time scales and the Chapman–Kolmogorov test; the discretisation error is bounded by how well the partition resolves the slow eigenfunctions. Cost CF/CVX. [R] — [Prinz et al. 2011, J. Chem. Phys. 134:174105](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3565032); [Sarich, Noé, Schütte 2010, Multiscale Model. Simul. 8:1154](https://doi.org/10.1137/090764049)
- Hidden Markov models are fitted by Baum–Welch (EM, local optima; cost linear in sequence length and quadratic in states per iteration). [R] — [Rabiner 1989, Proc. IEEE 77:257](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.18626)
- HMM parameters are generically identifiable from the joint distribution of three consecutive observations, up to relabelling of states. [R] — [Allman, Matias, Rhodes 2009, Ann. Statist. 37:3099, arXiv:0809.5032](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032)
- For a Markov jump process observed at discrete times the generator's maximum-likelihood estimate may not exist or be unique (the embedding problem: a transition matrix can have none or several generators); EM and MCMC algorithms are given. [R] — [Bladt and Sørensen 2005, J. R. Stat. Soc. B 67:395](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2005.00508.x)
- Implementations: deeptime and PyEMMA are LGPL-3.0 (files read); hmmlearn is BSD-3-Clause (file read). [V] — [deeptime licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deeptime-ml/deeptime/HEAD/LICENSE.txt); [PyEMMA licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markovmodel/PyEMMA/HEAD/LICENSE.txt); [hmmlearn licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hmmlearn/hmmlearn/HEAD/LICENSE.txt)

### Inferences
- Kramers–Moyal estimation with finite-time corrections, MSM estimation with its two validation tests, and gEDMD (section 2) are all CF and small; they are natural own implementations, each checkable against the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process and exactly solvable chains.
- The infrastructure's tyche already holds HMMs with Gaussian emissions and Markov chains observed at irregular times by their panel likelihood (per the repository's CLAUDE.md), so the Markov side is largely a matter of reuse; the new content is drift–diffusion and jump estimation and the link from a transition matrix to a generator with its non-uniqueness reported.
- The copyleft status of the two main Markov-model libraries (deeptime, PyEMMA: LGPL) is a reason to own MSM estimation rather than borrow it.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve quantitative guidance on the minimum sample size for reliable $D^{(4)}$ estimation (jump detection); the literature states it qualitatively.
- Estimation of Hawkes and other self-exciting jump processes was not researched.

## 4. Symbolic and Grammar-Based Discovery; Computational Mechanics

### Takeaway
Symbolic regression is combinatorial search (NP-hard in general), feasible on a CPU through PySR (Apache-2.0) or Operon (MIT, C++); the 2025 SRBench update with 25 methods finds that no single algorithm dominates and that only one method retrieved a first-principles equation on its new track. For symbolic time series, CSSR is fast but has known finite-data pathologies; Bayesian structural inference over unifilar HMM topologies is the more principled alternative but enumerates topologies and so is limited to few states.

### Cited Findings

**Symbolic regression**
- Symbolic regression is NP-hard. [R] — [Virgolin and Pissis 2022, arXiv:2207.01018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01018)
- Free-form laws (Hamiltonians, Lagrangians, invariants) were distilled from motion-tracking data by genetic programming with a partial-derivative-pair criterion. [R] — [Schmidt and Lipson 2009, Science 324:81](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1165893)
- PySR/SymbolicRegression.jl: multi-population evolutionary search with constant optimisation, simulated annealing and a complexity-vs-accuracy Pareto front; multi-threaded CPU code in Julia; supports dimensional-analysis constraints on expressions. Licence Apache-2.0 (files read). [V] licence — [PySR licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MilesCranmer/PySR/HEAD/LICENSE); [SymbolicRegression.jl licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MilesCranmer/SymbolicRegression.jl/HEAD/LICENSE); paper [R] [Cranmer 2023, arXiv:2305.01582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01582)
- AI Feynman combines a neural-network fit with dimensional analysis, symmetry and separability tests to split a problem recursively; it solved all 100 equations of its Feynman set where prior software solved 71. Licence MIT (GitHub API); last pushed 2024-07-09. [R] paper — [Udrescu and Tegmark 2020, Sci. Adv. 6:eaay2631, arXiv:1905.11481](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11481); [V] licence — [AI-Feynman repository](https://github.com/SJ001/AI-Feynman)
- Operon: C++ genetic programming with nonlinear least-squares tuning of constants; MIT (GitHub API; pushed 2026-11-02). [V] — [Operon repository](https://github.com/heal-research/operon); paper [R] [Burlacu, Kronberger, Kommenda 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3377929.3398099)
- PhySO: reinforcement-learning search in which every partial expression is checked for consistency of physical units; MIT (file read). [V] licence — [PhySO licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WassimTenachi/PhySO/HEAD/LICENSE); paper [R] [Tenachi, Ibata, Diakogiannis 2023, arXiv:2303.03192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03192)
- Probabilistic context-free grammars as the prior over expressions for equation discovery (ProGED): the grammar encodes domain knowledge and makes the search space a sampled language. [R] — [Brence, Todorovski, Džeroski 2021, arXiv:2012.00428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00428)
- A Bayesian "machine scientist" samples expressions by MCMC with a prior learned from a corpus of formulae. [R] — [Guimerà et al. 2020, Sci. Adv. 6:eaav6971, arXiv:2004.12157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12157)
- **[recent]** ODEFormer: a transformer that outputs ODE systems from one trajectory, with a benchmark (ODEBench) of 63 ODEs. [R] — [d'Ascoli et al. 2024, arXiv:2310.05573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05573)
- SRBench (2021): 14 SR methods and 7 ML baselines on 252 problems; genetic-programming methods with constant optimisation (Operon) led black-box accuracy, AI Feynman led exact recovery on noise-free physics problems and degraded with noise. [R] — [La Cava et al. 2021, arXiv:2107.14351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14351)
- **[recent]** SRBench 2025 ("Call for Action"): 25 algorithms, 24 datasets in two tracks (12 black-box, 12 phenomenological and first-principles), 30 runs each, single core and 10 GB of memory per job; "no single algorithm dominates across all datasets"; most top performers use constant optimisation; ITEA was the only method to retrieve a first-principles equation (Leavitt's law); the two most energy-hungry methods were Python-based; uDSR was not compatible during the experiments; noise in first-principles data can make higher-$R^2$ models fit noise. The fetched page did not give per-method rankings for PySR or Operon in text. [V] — [Aldeia et al. 2025, arXiv:2505.03977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03977)
- The SRBench repository itself is GPL-3.0 (file read): copyleft, relevant only if its harness code were reused. [V] — [srbench licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cavalab/srbench/HEAD/LICENSE)
- There is a noise-driven transition from a phase where the true closed-form model can be learned to one where no method can recover it; probabilistic model selection is optimal in both phases, and generalisation is hard for all approaches near the transition. [V] — [Fajardo-Fontiveros et al. 2023, Nat. Commun. 14:1043, arXiv:2204.02704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02704)

**Computational mechanics and automaton induction**
- The ε-machine is the minimal unifilar hidden Markov model whose states (causal states) are the equivalence classes of pasts with the same conditional distribution of futures; statistical complexity is the entropy of its state distribution. [R] — [Crutchfield and Young 1989, Phys. Rev. Lett. 63:105](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.63.105); [Shalizi and Crutchfield 2001, arXiv:cond-mat/9907176](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176)
- CSSR builds causal states by splitting on hypothesis tests of next-symbol distributions for suffixes up to a maximum length $L_{\max}$; it is proved to converge on the true states for processes with finitely many causal states, and runs in time linear in the data length for fixed alphabet and $L_{\max}$. [R] — [Shalizi and Klinkner 2004, arXiv:cs/0406011](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0406011)
- For finite-length input CSSR does not guarantee a minimal-state machine, and state explosion can occur. [V] — [Paulson and Griffin, arXiv:1501.01300](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01300)
- Crutchfield's historical essay states that causal-state splitting methods often give a diverging set of states as more data are presented, because of the determinisation step's exponential blow-up, and that Bayesian structural inference was introduced because of these problems. (A Crutchfield-group source, with a stake in the comparison.) [V] — [Crutchfield 2017, arXiv:1710.06832](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06832)
- Bayesian structural inference enumerates candidate unifilar HMM topologies, computes each one's evidence in closed form with Dirichlet priors (unifilarity makes the state path a function of the start state and the data), and returns a posterior over topologies rather than a single machine. Cost: CF per topology, but the number of topologies grows super-exponentially with states. [V] — [Strelioff and Crutchfield 2014, Phys. Rev. E 89:042119, arXiv:1309.1392](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1392)
- The Shalizi–Klinkner C++ CSSR code mirrored at `stites/CSSR` has no licence file in the repository root. [V] — [stites/CSSR](https://github.com/stites/CSSR)
- Regular languages are not identifiable in the limit from positive data alone (Gold); with membership and equivalence queries they are learnable in polynomial time (Angluin's L*); stochastic regular languages can be learned by state merging (ALERGIA). [R] — [Gold 1967](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(67)91165-5); [Angluin 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/0890-5401(87)90052-6); [Carrasco and Oncina 1994](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-58473-0_144)
- Symbolic dynamics from data depends on the partition: threshold-crossing partitions that are not generating give degenerate, misleading symbolic dynamics and entropy estimates; data-driven estimates of good partitions exist (symbolic false nearest neighbours). [R] — [Bollt, Stanford, Lai, Życzkowski 2000, Phys. Rev. Lett. 85:3524](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.85.3524); [Kennel and Buhl 2003, arXiv:nlin/0304054](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0304054)
- Sequitur infers a context-free (straight-line) grammar from a sequence in linear time by digram uniqueness and rule utility. [R] — [Nevill-Manning and Witten 1997, arXiv:cs/9709102](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9709102)

### Inferences
- Symbolic regression is COMB; minutes to hours per problem on a laptop CPU with PySR or Operon, which are multi-threaded and need no GPU. The transformer-based methods (ODEFormer, TPSR, E2E) need pretrained weights and gain little from being reimplemented.
- The infrastructure's heuresis already holds programs typed by physical dimensions and grammatical evolution (CLAUDE.md). The published evidence (constant optimisation is what the top SRBench performers share; dimensional typing as in PhySO and AI Feynman prunes the space) says the missing ingredient for dynamics is a nonlinear least-squares constant fitter inside the search and a Pareto front by complexity, not a new search engine. PySR and Operon can serve as borrowed references for cross-checks.
- CSSR and Bayesian structural inference are small exact algorithms on strings and counts (linear-time suffix statistics; closed-form Dirichlet evidence) with exact test cases (the even process, the golden-mean process), and no licence-clean reference implementation was found: both are candidates to implement in C++ on existing word and automaton machinery. The need for a generating partition should be stated wherever a continuous series is symbolised.
- The SRBench 2025 finding that only one of 25 methods recovered one first-principles law is the strongest recent evidence against claiming "discovery" from fit quality alone.

### Gaps
- Per-method SRBench 2025 rankings (PySR, Operon) are in figures that the fetched text did not describe.
- I did not find an independent benchmark comparing CSSR, Bayesian structural inference and spectral HMM learning on common data.
- LLM-guided equation discovery (LLM-SR, LLM-SRBench, 2024–2025) was not researched in this session; it is not CPU-local in any case.

## 5. Structure-Preserving Learning

### Takeaway
Hamiltonian, Lagrangian and symplectic networks are small MLPs trained by gradient descent and are feasible on a CPU for low-dimensional systems, but the evidence since 2022 is that their advantage comes mostly from the second-order (acceleration) structure rather than from symplecticity or energy conservation; what they learn is identified only up to gauge (a Hamiltonian up to a constant and canonical transformations, a Lagrangian up to scaling and total derivatives).

### Cited Findings
- Hamiltonian neural networks parametrise $H(q,p)$ by a network and train on $\dot q = \partial_p H$, $\dot p = -\partial_q H$; they need canonical coordinates. Reference code Apache-2.0 (file read). [R] — [Greydanus, Dzamba, Yosinski 2019, arXiv:1906.01563](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01563); [V] [hamiltonian-nn licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/greydanus/hamiltonian-nn/HEAD/LICENSE)
- Lagrangian neural networks parametrise $L(q,\dot q)$ and solve the Euler–Lagrange equations for $\ddot q$ through the Hessian $\partial^2 L/\partial\dot q^2$, which must be inverted (ill-conditioning is a known training difficulty); no canonical momenta are needed. Reference code Apache-2.0 (file read). [R] — [Cranmer et al. 2020, arXiv:2003.04630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04630); [V] [lagrangian_nns licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MilesCranmer/lagrangian_nns/HEAD/LICENSE)
- Deep Lagrangian Networks impose the mechanical form $L = \tfrac12 \dot q^\top M(q)\dot q - V(q)$ with $M$ positive definite by a Cholesky factor. [R] — [Lutter, Ritter, Peters 2019, arXiv:1907.04490](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490)
- SympNets compose shear-like symplectic layers so the learned map is exactly symplectic, with a universal approximation theorem for symplectic maps. [R] — [Jin, Zhang, Zhu, Tang, Karniadakis 2020, arXiv:2001.03750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03750)
- Training through a symplectic integrator (leapfrog) rather than on derivatives improves robustness to noise and stiffness (symplectic recurrent networks). [R] — [Chen, Zhang, Arjovsky, Bottou 2020, arXiv:1909.13334](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13334)
- **Negative result.** The improved generalisation of HNNs comes from modelling acceleration directly and avoiding artificial complexity from the coordinate system, "rather than symplectic structure or energy conservation"; relaxing the biases matches performance on conservative systems and improves it on non-conservative ones. [V] — [Gruver, Finzi, Stanton, Wilson 2022 (ICLR), arXiv:2202.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04836)
- Using Cartesian coordinates with explicit holonomic constraints makes HNNs and LNNs far easier to train than generalised coordinates. [R] — [Finzi, Wang, Wilson 2020, arXiv:2010.13581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13581)
- When a Hamiltonian is learned through a symplectic integrator, the network learns a modified (shadow) Hamiltonian of the integrator; correcting for this recovers the true one to higher order. [R] — [Offen and Ober-Blöbaum 2022, Chaos 32:013122, arXiv:2108.02492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02492); [David and Méhats 2023, arXiv:2106.11753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11753)
- A discrete Lagrangian (i.e. a discrete action) can be learned directly from position data without velocities, so that the learned object is a variational integrator; the Lagrangian is not unique and degenerate solutions must be excluded by normalisation. [R] — [Ober-Blöbaum and Offen 2023, J. Comput. Appl. Math., arXiv:2112.12619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12619); [Qin 2020, Sci. Rep. 10:19329, arXiv:1910.10147](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10147)
- Conserved quantities can be found from trajectories without a model (AI Poincaré estimates the number of conservation laws from the dimension of the trajectory manifold); Noether networks meta-learn conserved quantities as an inductive bias. [R] — [Liu and Tegmark 2021, Phys. Rev. Lett. 126:180604, arXiv:2011.04698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04698); [Alet et al. 2021, arXiv:2112.03321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03321)
- Port-Hamiltonian neural networks add dissipation and external forcing to the Hamiltonian form, $\dot x = (J - R)\nabla H + F(t)$. [R] — [Desai, Mattheakis, Sondak, Protopapas, Roberts 2021, Phys. Rev. E 104:034312, arXiv:2107.08024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08024)
- Port-Hamiltonian DMD identifies a linear port-Hamiltonian system from data by constrained least squares (iterative but convex-like subproblems). [R] — [Morandin, Nicodemus, Unger 2023, SIAM J. Sci. Comput., arXiv:2204.13474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13474)
- Gaussian processes and kernel methods give Hamiltonians from data with closed-form linear solves instead of gradient training. [R] — [Bertalan, Dietrich, Mezić, Kevrekidis 2019, Chaos 29:121107, arXiv:1907.12715](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12715)

### Inferences
- Cost class GRAD for all network variants; for systems of a few degrees of freedom the networks have $10^3$–$10^5$ parameters and train in minutes on a laptop CPU with PyTorch. They are natural "borrow through Python" items.
- The closed-form alternatives are the ones a C++ library can own: (i) a Hamiltonian or Lagrangian expanded in a term library and fitted by sparse regression (SINDy on the energy rather than on the vector field), which is linear in the coefficients for $H$ and linear after clearing the mass matrix for $L$; (ii) a discrete Lagrangian fitted so that the discrete Euler–Lagrange residual vanishes, which composes with a variational integrator; (iii) piDMD and port-Hamiltonian least squares for linear systems. The infrastructure's physis already integrates motion from a discrete action (CLAUDE.md), so (ii) reuses that machinery in the inverse direction.
- Every result of this section should be reported with its gauge: $H$ up to an additive constant (and only the vector field is observable); $L$ up to $L \mapsto cL + \tfrac{d}{dt}F(q,t)$; a learned symplectic map identifies the shadow Hamiltonian of the integrator used in training.

### Gaps
- No standard benchmark comparing sparse-regression Lagrangians or Hamiltonians with HNN/LNN at matched noise was found.
- I did not verify a single authoritative recent (2024–2026) review of port-Hamiltonian identification.

## 6. Black-Box Surrogates Feasible on a CPU

### Takeaway
Reservoir computers (and their closed-form relative, next-generation reservoir computing, which is a nonlinear vector autoregression fitted by ridge regression) are the strongest CPU-cheap forecasters of chaos in the 2025 Common Task Framework and in dysts benchmarks at small data, but NG-RC is only as good as its chosen nonlinearity; neural ODEs are gradient-trained, harder to train on chaotic data, and scored poorly in the CTF. Delay embedding, SSA and empirical dynamic modelling are closed-form or nearest-neighbour methods that are easy to own, and the reference EDM code is not open-source-licensed.

### Cited Findings

**Reservoir computing**
- Echo state networks: a fixed random recurrent reservoir with only the linear readout trained, by ridge regression (CF). [R] — [Jaeger and Haas 2004, Science 304:78](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091277)
- A parallel reservoir scheme forecast the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky system for several Lyapunov times and reproduced its Lyapunov spectrum. [R] — [Pathak, Hunt, Girvan, Lu, Ott 2018, Phys. Rev. Lett. 120:024102, arXiv:1710.07313](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07313)
- Next-generation reservoir computing replaces the reservoir by time-delayed states and their polynomial products (a nonlinear vector autoregression), needs no random matrices, far fewer hyperparameters and shorter warm-up and training data. Cost CF (one ridge regression). [R] — [Gauthier, Bollt, Griffith, Barbosa 2021, Nat. Commun. 12:5564, arXiv:2106.07688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07688)
- A reservoir with linear activation and a quadratic readout is equivalent to a nonlinear VAR, linking reservoir computing to DMD and VAR. [R] — [Bollt 2021, Chaos 31:013108, arXiv:2008.06530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06530)
- **Negative result.** For basin prediction in multistable systems, standard reservoirs need a warm-up containing almost the entire transient; NG-RC needs negligible warm-up and can reconstruct basins from a single transient when it is given the exact nonlinearities of the true equations, but a tiny uncertainty in the nonlinearity can make its predictions no better than chance. [V] — [Zhang and Cornelius 2023, Phys. Rev. Research 5:033213, arXiv:2210.10211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10211)
- Adding dynamical invariants (Lyapunov spectrum, fractal dimension) to the training objective improves the long-term statistics of reservoir forecasts. [R] — [Platt, Penny, Smith, Chen, Abarbanel 2023, arXiv:2304.12865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12865)
- ReservoirPy: MIT (file read). [V] — [reservoirpy licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/reservoirpy/reservoirpy/HEAD/LICENSE)

**Neural ODEs, universal differential equations, recurrent networks**
- Neural ODEs parametrise the vector field by a network and train through an ODE solver by the adjoint method (GRAD). torchdiffeq: MIT (file read). [R] — [Chen, Rubanova, Bettencourt, Duvenaud 2018, arXiv:1806.07366](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366); [V] [torchdiffeq licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rtqichen/torchdiffeq/HEAD/LICENSE)
- Universal differential equations embed a small network inside a known mechanistic model, then recover the missing term symbolically by sparse regression on the network. DiffEqFlux.jl: MIT (file read). [R] — [Rackauckas et al. 2020, arXiv:2001.04385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04385); [V] [DiffEqFlux.jl licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SciML/DiffEqFlux.jl/HEAD/LICENSE)
- **Negative result.** When the underlying system is chaotic, loss gradients of a recurrent model trained by backpropagation through time necessarily diverge with the unrolled length (tied to the largest Lyapunov exponent); teacher-forcing variants are the proposed remedy. [R] — [Mikhaeil, Monfared, Durstewitz 2022, arXiv:2110.07238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07238); [Hess, Monfared, Brenner, Durstewitz 2023, arXiv:2306.04406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04406)
- **[recent]** CTF composite scores (100 is perfect; twelve tasks averaged; Lorenz, then Kuramoto–Sivashinsky): LSTM 64.54, 15.61; DeepONet 57.80, 6.99; reservoir 54.87, 18.88; KAN 47.28, 6.54; ODE-LSTM 41.67, 11.85; SINDy 19.19, −3.00; OptDMD 20.62, −11.04; FNO 20.51, −18.51; PyKoopman 8.33, −20.11; neural ODE 3.38, −33.46; Higher-Order DMD 0.94, −25.23; SpaceTime 39.46, −45.58; PINN −8.48 on Lorenz. On KS a zero prediction scores 0.00 and beats most methods; reservoir computing leads KS, with a short-time forecast score of 99.97. The authors state "no model scores high across all tasks", that generic RNN-style models struggle with little data, and that reservoirs suit chaotic series. [V] — [Wyder et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.23166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)
- On 135 chaotic systems (dysts) and 24 methods, large domain-agnostic models (NBEATS, transformers) forecast best when training data are plentiful, staying accurate up to about two dozen Lyapunov times, while physics-based hybrids (reservoirs, nVAR, neural ODEs) keep an edge when data are limited. [V] — [Gilpin 2023, Phys. Rev. Research 5:043252, arXiv:2303.08011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08011)
- **[recent]** Pretrained models for chaos: time-series foundation models give zero-shot forecasts competitive with custom-trained models on 135 chaotic systems (Zhang and Gilpin); DynaMix, a mixture-of-experts of about 10k parameters trained on 34 systems, reports better long-term statistics than time-series foundation models; Panda (21M parameters) is trained on $2\times10^4$ synthetic chaotic systems. A Gilpin-group study reports that such models can be explained by a "context parroting" strategy. [V] — [Zhang and Gilpin 2024, arXiv:2409.15771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15771); [Hemmer and Durstewitz 2025, arXiv:2505.13192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13192); [Lai, Bao, Gilpin 2025, arXiv:2505.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13755)

**Delay embedding, SSA, empirical dynamic modelling**
- A generic scalar observable's delay vectors of dimension $m > 2d$ embed a $d$-dimensional attractor (Takens; extended to box-counting dimension by Sauer, Yorke and Casdagli): the reconstruction is diffeomorphic to the attractor, not equal to it. [R] — [Takens 1981](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0091924); [Sauer, Yorke, Casdagli 1991](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01053745)
- Embedding dimension by false nearest neighbours; delay by the first minimum of mutual information; Cao's method avoids the FNN threshold. [R] — [Kennel, Brown, Abarbanel 1992](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.45.3403); [Fraser and Swinney 1986](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.33.1134); [Cao 1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-2789(97)00118-8)
- PECUZAL chooses non-uniform delays and the dimension automatically, for multivariate input. DelayEmbeddings.jl and DynamicalSystems.jl: MIT (files read). [R] — [Kraemer et al. 2021, New J. Phys. 23:033017, arXiv:2011.07040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07040); [V] [DelayEmbeddings.jl licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliaDynamics/DelayEmbeddings.jl/HEAD/LICENSE); [DynamicalSystems.jl licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliaDynamics/DynamicalSystems.jl/HEAD/LICENSE.md)
- Singular spectrum analysis: SVD of the trajectory (delay) matrix separates trend, oscillations and noise (CF). [R] — [Broomhead and King 1986](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(86)90031-X); [Vautard and Ghil 1989](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(89)90077-8)
- Empirical dynamic modelling forecasts by nearest neighbours in the delay embedding (simplex projection, S-maps); convergent cross mapping infers coupling from whether one variable's shadow manifold predicts another's, with skill converging in library length. Cost: nearest-neighbour search. [R] — [Sugihara and May 1990](https://doi.org/10.1038/344734a0); [Sugihara et al. 2012, Science 338:496](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1227079)
- **Negative result.** CCM's causal conclusions are sensitive to periodicity, synchrony, noise and embedding choices, and gave wrong directions of causation in simulated infectious-disease systems. [R] — [Cobey and Baskerville 2016, PLoS ONE 11:e0169050](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169050)
- pyEDM's licence is a University of California text that grants use "for educational, research and non-profit purposes" and requires contacting the university for commercial use: not an open-source licence and not MIT-compatible for redistribution. [V] — [pyEDM licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SugiharaLab/pyEDM/HEAD/LICENSE)

### Inferences
- CF and ownable in C++: echo state networks (sparse random matrix, ridge readout), NG-RC (delay monomials, ridge), delay embedding with FNN, mutual information and Cao's criterion, SSA, simplex projection, S-maps and CCM. All are short, deterministic given a seed, and checkable. Owning EDM is advisable because the reference implementation's licence restricts commercial use.
- NG-RC with a polynomial library and SINDy with the same library differ only in the regression target (next state versus derivative) and the penalty (ridge versus sparse); a single library-and-regression core can serve both, and Zhang and Cornelius's result applies to both: the library must contain the true nonlinearity.
- GRAD and borrowable: neural ODEs, UDEs, LSTMs, piecewise-linear RNNs with generalised teacher forcing, DeepONet. At Lorenz or KS scale these train in minutes to tens of minutes on a laptop CPU; the CTF's low neural-ODE scores and the diverging-gradient theorem argue against making them a centrepiece.
- Pretrained models (Panda at 21M parameters, DynaMix at about 10k) run inference on a CPU; they are comparators, not models that are written down.

## 7. Quantum: Hamiltonian, Lindbladian and Process Learning

### Takeaway
Hamiltonian learning can reach Heisenberg-limited total evolution time ($\propto 1/\varepsilon$), while a 2026 result states that this is information-theoretically impossible once dissipative coefficients must be estimated ($1/\varepsilon^2$); the learned objects carry gauge freedoms (the GKSL form's unitary mixing and shifts of jump operators, gate-set tomography's similarity gauge, the non-unique logarithm of a channel) that any implementation must fix and report. The classical post-processing is linear algebra or small convex problems and is laptop-feasible for few-qubit or local models.

### Cited Findings
- Standard quantum process tomography reconstructs a channel from an informationally complete set of input states and measurements by linear inversion (later maximum likelihood); the number of settings grows as $16^n$ for $n$ qubits. [R] — [Chuang and Nielsen 1997, arXiv:quant-ph/9610001](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9610001)
- Gate set tomography characterises state preparation, gates and measurement together and is defined only up to a gauge: the gate set $\{\rho, G_i, E\}$ and $\{T\rho, T G_i T^{-1}, E T^{-1}\}$ give identical data for any invertible $T$, so gauge-variant quantities (individual gate fidelities) are not directly observable. pyGSTi: Apache-2.0 (file read). [R] — [Nielsen et al. 2021, Quantum 5:557, arXiv:2009.07301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07301); [V] [pyGSTi licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sandialabs/pyGSTi/HEAD/LICENSE)
- The GKSL generator's decomposition into $H$ and jump operators is not unique: it is invariant under unitary mixing of the jump operators and under $L_k \mapsto L_k + a_k \mathbb{1}$ with a compensating change of $H$; uniqueness is restored by requiring traceless $H$ and traceless orthonormal operator basis, where the Kossakowski matrix is unique. [R] — [Gorini, Kossakowski, Sudarshan 1976, J. Math. Phys. 17:821](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.522979); [Breuer and Petruccione 2002, §3.2](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199213900.001.0001)
- Deciding whether a measured channel is generated by any time-independent Lindbladian (the quantum embedding problem) requires searching the branches of the matrix logarithm; the problem is NP-hard in general. [R] — [Wolf, Eisert, Cubitt, Cirac 2008, Phys. Rev. Lett. 101:150402, arXiv:0711.3172](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.3172); [Cubitt, Eisert, Wolf 2012, Commun. Math. Phys. 310:383, arXiv:0908.2128](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2128)
- Local Hamiltonians can be learned from local measurements of a single eigenstate or steady state through a linear "correlation matrix" whose null vector is the Hamiltonian (CF: one eigenproblem); the same idea gives Lindbladians from steady states by linear constraints. The Hamiltonian is recovered up to scale. [R] — [Bairey, Arad, Lindner 2019, Phys. Rev. Lett. 122:020504, arXiv:1807.04564](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04564); [Bairey, Guo, Poletti, Lindner, Arad 2020, New J. Phys. 22:032001, arXiv:1907.11154](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11154)
- Bayesian online Hamiltonian learning by sequential Monte Carlo is robust to noise and model error for few-parameter models. [R] — [Granade, Ferrie, Wiebe, Cory 2012, New J. Phys. 14:103013, arXiv:1207.1655](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1655)
- Many-body Hamiltonians can be learned with Heisenberg-limited scaling: total evolution time $O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta))$ for low-intersection Hamiltonians, robust to state-preparation and measurement error. [R] — [Huang, Tong, Fang, Su 2023, Phys. Rev. Lett. 130:200403, arXiv:2210.03030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03030)
- From high-temperature Gibbs states, sample complexity $O(\log N/(\beta^2\varepsilon^2))$ is optimal; learning at any constant temperature is possible in polynomial time. [R] — [Haah, Kothari, Tang 2022, arXiv:2108.04842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.04842); [Bakshi, Liu, Moitra, Tang 2023, arXiv:2310.02243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02243)
- Lindblad tomography extracts Hamiltonian, jump operators and SPAM errors of a superconducting device by maximum likelihood over time-resolved measurements. [R] — [Samach et al. 2022, Phys. Rev. Applied 18:064056, arXiv:2105.02338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02338)
- Many-qubit Hamiltonians and Lindbladians can be estimated robustly from derivatives of expectation values at short times with Lieb–Robinson-bounded error. [R] — [Stilck França, Markovich, Dobrovitski, Werner, Borregaard 2024, Nat. Commun., arXiv:2205.09567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09567)
- **[recent]** "Near-Optimal Learning of Local Lindbladians" (v1 June 2026, v3 3 November 2026; accepted to SODA 2027): a non-adaptive, ancilla-free protocol with random product-state inputs and random Pauli measurements learns all Hamiltonian and dissipative coefficients to accuracy $\varepsilon$ with about $\tilde O(\Lambda^2/\varepsilon^2)$ experiments and $\tilde O(\Lambda/\varepsilon^2)$ total evolution time, logarithmic in the number of qubits, without knowing the structure in advance; lower bounds from single-qubit dephasing show $\Omega(\Lambda^2/\varepsilon^2)$ experiments and $\Omega(\Lambda/\varepsilon^2)$ time even for adaptive, ancilla-assisted algorithms, so Heisenberg-limited scaling is information-theoretically impossible once dissipative coefficients must be estimated. Assumes low-intersection dissipators and local strength at most $\Lambda$. [V] — [Arad, Chen, Guo, Rebentrost, Yu 2026, arXiv:2606.20535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20535)
- **[recent]** "Ansatz-Free Learning of Lindbladian Dynamics In Situ" (March 2026, revised October 2026): learns the full generator of sparse Markovian Lindbladians without assumed structure, ancilla-free with product-state preparations and Pauli measurements; v2 adds "provably stable coefficient learning". The abstract page gives no scaling and does not discuss gauge. [V] — [Ivashkov et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.05492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05492)
- **[recent]** A 2026 Caltech senior thesis claims the first rigorous characterisation of gauge degrees of freedom in SPAM-noisy Lindbladian learning and of which components remain learnable (undergraduate thesis, not peer-reviewed; seen only as a search summary). [V] — [Sinha 2026, Caltech thesis 18488](https://thesis.caltech.edu/18488/)
- **[recent]** Ansatz-free Hamiltonian learning with Heisenberg-limited scaling appeared in PRX Quantum 6, 040315 (2025) (seen only as a search result title). [V] — [PRX Quantum 6:040315](https://journals.aps.org/prxquantum/abstract/10.1103/j7b8-pb77)
- QuTiP (simulation of Lindblad dynamics and trajectories, for generating synthetic records): BSD-3-Clause (file read). [V] — [QuTiP licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qutip/qutip/HEAD/LICENSE.txt)

### Inferences
- Cost classes for the classical side: linear-inversion tomography and the correlation-matrix method are CF; maximum-likelihood tomography and gate-set tomography are iterative (convex for process tomography with CPTP constraints, non-convex for GST); Bayesian sequential Monte Carlo is sampling. All are laptop-feasible for one to a few qubits, and for local models on more qubits where the number of coefficients is polynomial.
- A C++ implementation can own, with exact checks against a simulated open two-level system (which the infrastructure's physis already has, per CLAUDE.md): (i) fitting a Lindbladian to a measured channel or to time-resolved expectation values by linear least squares on the generator in a traceless operator basis, returning the canonical $(H, \text{Kossakowski matrix})$ and so fixing the gauge by construction; (ii) the Markovianity test by enumerating logarithm branches for small dimensions; (iii) linear-inversion process tomography with its SPAM gauge stated. GST should be borrowed (pyGSTi, Apache-2.0).
- The measurement record (quantum trajectories, continuous monitoring) case is a hidden-Markov-like inference problem; it connects to section 3's estimators but its specific literature was not surveyed here.

### Gaps
- Learning from continuous measurement records (quantum trajectories, quantum-jump records, Bayesian filtering of stochastic master equations) was not researched; no source is given here.
- Sample-complexity bounds for full process tomography (optimal rates in the diamond norm) were not verified in this session.
- The Sinha thesis and the PRX Quantum paper were not read; only their existence and summaries are reported.

## 8. Identifiability and Evaluation

### Takeaway
Most induced models are identified only up to a stated equivalence (coordinates, hidden-state similarity, gauge), and there are mature MIT-licensed tools that decide structural identifiability for rational ODE models; for chaotic systems pointwise long-horizon error is meaningless and the field now reports a short-horizon score together with invariant-measure, spectral and Lyapunov statistics, while topological invariants (linking numbers of unstable periodic orbits, templates) are the sharpest but least automated test and are absent from every benchmark found.

### Cited Findings

**What is identified only up to a transformation**
- Linear state-space models are identified from input–output data only up to a similarity transformation of the hidden state (the realisation is unique up to a change of basis), which is why subspace identification returns a basis-dependent realisation. [R] — [Van Overschee and De Moor 1996, *Subspace Identification for Linear Systems*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4613-0465-4)
- A delay reconstruction is diffeomorphic to the attractor: metric quantities change, while dimensions, Lyapunov exponents and topological invariants are preserved. [R] — [Sauer, Yorke, Casdagli 1991](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01053745)
- HMM parameters: generically identifiable up to permutation of states. [R] — [Allman, Matias, Rhodes 2009, arXiv:0809.5032](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032)
- Markov generators from discrete-time transition matrices: possibly none or several (embedding problem). [R] — [Bladt and Sørensen 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2005.00508.x)
- A linear or linear-in-parameters ODE is identifiable from a single trajectory if and only if the trajectory is not confined to a proper invariant subspace. [R] — [Stanhope, Rubin, Swigon 2014, SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 13:1792](https://doi.org/10.1137/130937913)
- PDE discovery is non-unique when the data satisfy more than one PDE in the library (e.g. a travelling-wave or algebraic solution); conditions for uniqueness can be stated and tested. [R] — [Scholl, Bacho, Boche, Kutyniok 2023, arXiv:2210.08342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08342)
- Koopman eigenfunctions and spectra are invariants of conjugacy classes, so a Koopman model identifies dynamics up to conjugacy of the chosen observables' span. [R] — [Budišić, Mohr, Mezić 2012, Chaos 22:047510, arXiv:1206.3164](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.3164)
- Quantum: GKSL gauge, GST gauge and non-unique channel logarithms (section 7). [R] — [Nielsen et al. 2021, arXiv:2009.07301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07301); [Wolf et al. 2008, arXiv:0711.3172](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.3172)

**Structural identifiability tools**
- Structural (a priori) global identifiability of rational ODE models with outputs is decidable by differential algebra: the input–output equations' coefficients are compared (Ljung and Glad; DAISY implements this in REDUCE). [R] — [Ljung and Glad 1994, Automatica 30:265](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(94)90029-9); [Bellu, Saccomani, Audoly, D'Angiò 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2007.07.002)
- STRIKE-GOLDD tests local structural identifiability as generalised observability by Lie derivatives; MATLAB, GPL-3.0 (file read: copyleft). [R] paper — [Villaverde, Barreiro, Papachristodoulou 2016, PLoS Comput. Biol. 12:e1005153](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005153); [V] [strike-goldd licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/afvillaverde/strike-goldd/HEAD/LICENSE)
- SIAN decides global identifiability by a randomised (Monte Carlo, with stated error probability) algorithm on Taylor coefficients; the Maple repository has no licence file. [R] paper — [Hong, Ovchinnikov, Pogudin, Yap 2019, Bioinformatics 35:2873, arXiv:1812.10180](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.10180); [V] [SIAN repository](https://github.com/pogudingleb/SIAN)
- StructuralIdentifiability.jl computes input–output equations by differential elimination via projections, assesses local and global identifiability and returns identifiable functions of the parameters; MIT-style licence (file read: "Permission is hereby granted, free of charge…", copyright Dong, Goodbrake, Harrington, Pogudin). [R] paper — [Dong, Goodbrake, Harrington, Pogudin 2023, arXiv:2111.00991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00991); [V] [StructuralIdentifiability.jl licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SciML/StructuralIdentifiability.jl/HEAD/LICENSE.md)
- Practical (finite, noisy data) identifiability is assessed by the profile likelihood: a parameter is practically non-identifiable when its profile-likelihood confidence interval is unbounded. [R] — [Raue et al. 2009, Bioinformatics 25:1923](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp358)

**Scoring chaotic systems**
- **[recent]** The CTF scores each method by $E = 100(1 - S)$ on twelve tasks: short-time ("weather") relative error on the first $k$ steps; long-time ("climate") error, which for Kuramoto–Sivashinsky compares the log power spectrum over the first 100 wavenumbers and for Lorenz compares histograms of $x, y, z$ (41 bins, $\ell_1$ difference) over the last 500 steps; plus reconstruction and forecasting under medium and high noise, limited data with and without noise, and parametric interpolation and extrapolation. Scores are held on hidden test sets by a referee; failures score −100. For chaotic Lorenz "predict-the-mean" is described as the rational long-horizon baseline. [V] — [Wyder et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.23166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)
- **[recent]** The CTF position paper (Kutz, Battaglia, Brenner, Carlberg, Hagberg, Ho, Hoyer, Lange, Lipson, Mahoney, Noé, Welling, Zanna, Zhu, Brunton; 6 November 2025) names forecasting, state reconstruction, generalisation and control under limited data and noisy measurements as the objectives of a growing set of challenge data sets. ctf4science code: MIT (file read). [V] — [Kutz et al. 2025, arXiv:2511.04001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04001); [ctf4science licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CTF-for-Science/ctf4science/HEAD/LICENSE)
- Zero-shot chaos forecasting is evaluated by short-term error (sMAPE), valid prediction time, correlation dimension and a KL divergence between attractor distributions. [V] — [Zhang and Gilpin 2024, arXiv:2409.15771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15771)
- In the long-horizon regime forecast accuracy stops tracking classical predictability measures such as the largest Lyapunov exponent. [V] — [Gilpin 2023, arXiv:2303.08011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08011)
- The dynamical-systems-reconstruction literature scores attractor geometry by a state-space divergence $D_{\mathrm{stsp}}$ (KL divergence between binned or Gaussian-mixture occupation measures) and temporal structure by a Hellinger distance between power spectra. [R] — [Koppe et al. 2019, arXiv:1902.07186](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07186); [Brenner et al. 2022, arXiv:2207.02542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02542)
- Lyapunov exponents and correlation dimension from time series are classical invariants used to compare model and data. [R] — [Wolf, Swift, Swinney, Vastano 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(85)90011-9); [Grassberger and Procaccia 1983](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.50.346)
- Topological analysis: for three-dimensional chaotic flows, unstable periodic orbits extracted from data by close returns have linking numbers and relative rotation rates that determine a branched manifold (template); a model is rejected if its orbits' invariants differ from the data's, a test that is robust to noise and parameter change and can falsify but not confirm. [R] — [Gilmore 1998, Rev. Mod. Phys. 70:1455](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.70.1455); [Mindlin, Hou, Solari, Gilmore, Tufillaro 1990, Phys. Rev. Lett. 64:2350](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.64.2350); [Gilmore and Lefranc, *The Topology of Chaos*, 2nd ed. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/9783527639403)
- The template approach is restricted to attractors embedded in three dimensions (knots unknot in four); homology-based extensions (branched-manifold cell complexes with a flow, "templex") aim beyond it. [R] — [Charó, Letellier, Sciamarella 2022, Chaos 32:083108](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0092933)

**Benchmarks**
- dysts: a curated database of over a hundred chaotic systems (135 in the forecasting study) with precomputed trajectories and invariants (Lyapunov exponents, dimensions); Apache-2.0 (file read). [R] paper — [Gilpin 2021, arXiv:2110.05266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05266); [V] [dysts licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/williamgilpin/dysts/HEAD/LICENSE.md)
- PDEBench: time-dependent PDE datasets (advection, Burgers, diffusion–reaction, Navier–Stokes, shallow water, Darcy) for surrogate learning with baselines (FNO, U-Net, PINN); code MIT "except where otherwise stated" (file read). Its task is surrogate accuracy, not equation recovery. [R] paper — [Takamoto et al. 2022, arXiv:2210.07182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07182); [V] [PDEBench licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pdebench/PDEBench/HEAD/LICENSE.txt)
- **[recent]** The Well: a large collection of physics simulation datasets for surrogate models. [R] — [Ohana et al. 2024, arXiv:2412.00568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00568)
- ODEBench (63 ODEs) for symbolic recovery of ODEs. [R] — [d'Ascoli et al. 2024, arXiv:2310.05573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05573)
- Classical nonlinear system-identification benchmarks (Silverbox, Wiener–Hammerstein and others) and a user-oriented map of model classes. [R] — [Schoukens and Ljung 2019, arXiv:1902.00683](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00683)

### Inferences
- An evaluation suite for the new library can be assembled from closed-form pieces: valid prediction time and short-horizon relative error; histogram or KL distance between occupation measures; log-power-spectrum distance; Lyapunov spectrum (physis already computes Lyapunov spectra and Floquet multipliers, per CLAUDE.md); correlation dimension. All are CF or nearest-neighbour and CPU-cheap.
- No benchmark found scores topological invariants. Periodic-orbit extraction by close returns, linking numbers and relative rotation rates, and the comparison of a model's template with the data's would be a distinguishing contribution, and it is integer-valued and exact: well suited to the infrastructure's algebraic foundations and to a research note titled with braids.
- For equation recovery the honest score is two-part: structural (were the right terms selected, counted against a known truth) and parametric (coefficient error), reported separately from forecast skill, since CTF and SRBench both show that good fit and correct structure diverge.
- Structural identifiability should be borrowed: StructuralIdentifiability.jl (MIT) is the maintained tool; DAISY, SIAN (no licence file) and STRIKE-GOLDD (GPL-3.0) should not be vendored. A C++ library can still report the cheap local test (rank of the observability–identifiability Jacobian) and the profile likelihood itself.
- Every induced model's report should name its equivalence class: similarity (state-space), diffeomorphism (delay embeddings), permutation (HMM), conjugacy (Koopman), gauge (Lagrangian, GKSL, GST), branch (generators from maps).

### Gaps
- I found no published comparative benchmark that includes periodic-orbit or template metrics, so no comparative numbers exist to report.
- The exact number of systems currently in dysts and the current PDEBench data licence were not verified.
- The CTF's leaderboard state as of November 2026 (beyond the December 2025 paper revision) was not checked.

## 9. Reported Negative Results and Criticisms

### Takeaway
The recurring criticisms are: results depend on the library or dictionary containing the truth; derivative estimation makes strong-form methods fragile to noise; many models fit equally well (non-uniqueness) and above a noise threshold the true model is unrecoverable by any method; and self-reported benchmarks overstate performance, with simple baselines beating many published methods on hidden test sets.

### Cited Findings
- Self-reporting is "a flawed premise"; on hidden test sets, a zero prediction beats most of thirteen methods on Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, and "no model scores high across all tasks". [V] — [Wyder et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.23166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166)
- Standard SINDy is "sensitive to noise, especially in the low-data limit". [V] — [Fasel et al. 2022, arXiv:2111.10992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10992)
- Derivative approximation from noisy data is a major source of error in sparse-regression discovery. [V] — [North, Wikle, Schliep 2022, arXiv:2210.10663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10663)
- Above a noise threshold the true closed-form model cannot be learned by any method, and near the threshold generalisation is hard for all approaches. [V] — [Fajardo-Fontiveros et al. 2023, arXiv:2204.02704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02704)
- Of 25 symbolic-regression methods only one retrieved one first-principles equation in SRBench 2025's new track; noise in first-principles data can make higher-$R^2$ models fit noise; many method repositories are poorly maintained. [V] — [Aldeia et al. 2025, arXiv:2505.03977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03977)
- NG-RC's basin predictions fall to chance under tiny errors in the assumed nonlinearity. [V] — [Zhang and Cornelius 2023, arXiv:2210.10211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10211)
- Some Koopman eigenfunctions cannot be learned by any algorithm from any amount of data; EDMD converges only weakly and along subsequences. [V] — [Colbrook, Mezić, Stepanenko 2024, arXiv:2407.06312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06312)
- Exact DMD is sensitive to noise. [V] — [Ichinaga et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.07463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07463)
- HNNs' benefit is not due to symplecticity or energy conservation. [V] — [Gruver et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04836)
- CSSR may give non-minimal machines and state explosion on finite data. [V] — [Paulson and Griffin, arXiv:1501.01300](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01300); [Crutchfield 2017, arXiv:1710.06832](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06832)
- Time-series foundation models' chaos forecasts can be explained by "context parroting". [V, search summary] — [Hemmer and Durstewitz 2025, arXiv:2505.13192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13192) (the summary attributes the parroting study to the Gilpin group; the primary reference was not retrieved)
- PDE discovery can be non-unique for the data at hand. [R] — [Scholl et al. 2023, arXiv:2210.08342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08342)
- CCM can report wrong causal directions. [R] — [Cobey and Baskerville 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169050)
- Gradients diverge when training recurrent models on chaotic data. [R] — [Mikhaeil, Monfared, Durstewitz 2022, arXiv:2110.07238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07238)
