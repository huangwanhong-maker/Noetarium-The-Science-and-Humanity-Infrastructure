## 1. Neural Dynamics, Non-Smooth and Smooth: Equations, Parameters, Regimes and Exact Results

### Takeaway

Every model in the list has a published parameter set, and about half have closed-form or self-consistent results sharp enough for automatic checks: the LIF and QIF f–I curves, the Siegert rate under white noise, Brunel's self-consistent rates with a published simulation-versus-theory table, the Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin firing-rate equations (exact in the thermodynamic limit), the Sompolinsky–Crisanti–Sommers transition at $g=1$ with $\lambda_{\max}=\tfrac12(g-1)^2$ near onset, and bifurcation types for the conductance models. All of it is checkable on a CPU; the largest item is Brunel's 12,500-neuron network.

### Cited Findings

**Leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF).**
- [V] $\tau_m\,\dot u=-(u-u_{\rm rest})+R\,I(t)$ (Eq. 1.5); spike when $u=\vartheta$ (Eq. 1.12), then reset to $u_r<\vartheta$ (Eq. 1.13); for constant $I_0$ from rest, $u(t)=u_{\rm rest}+RI_0\,[1-e^{-t/\tau_m}]$ (Eq. 1.7); "typical" $\tau_m\approx10$ ms — [Gerstner et al., *Neuronal Dynamics* §1.3](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch1.S3.html)
- [V] Under diffusive white noise (drift $h_0=RI_0$, noise amplitude $\sigma$), the stationary free density is $p(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi\,\sigma}\exp\{-(u-RI_0)^2/\sigma^2\}$ (Eq. 8.46, i.e. variance $\sigma^2/2$), and the mean interspike interval is the Siegert formula $\langle s\rangle=\tau_m\sqrt\pi\int_{(u_r-h_0)/\sigma}^{(\vartheta-h_0)/\sigma}e^{u^2}\,[1+{\rm erf}(u)]\,du$ (Eq. 8.54); the Fokker–Planck equation is Eq. 8.41 with absorbing boundary $p(\vartheta,t)=0$ (Eq. 8.44) — [*Neuronal Dynamics* §8.4](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch8.S4.html)
- [R] Deterministic f–I curve: $T=\tau_m\ln\frac{RI_0-u_r}{RI_0-\vartheta}$ (with $u_{\rm rest}=0$), $\nu=[\Delta_{\rm abs}+T]^{-1}$, rheobase $I_\theta=\vartheta/R$. The page fetched (§1.3) does not contain it; it is in the book's later sections — [*Neuronal Dynamics* (book site)](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch1.S3.html)

**Exponential integrate-and-fire (EIF) and AdEx.**
- [V] $\tau\,\dot u=-(u-u_{\rm rest})+\Delta_T\exp[(u-\vartheta_{rh})/\Delta_T]+RI$ (Eq. 5.6); $\vartheta_{rh}$ is the rheobase threshold, the minimum of $f(u)$; the numerical threshold $\theta_{\rm reset}$ is irrelevant once $\theta_{\rm reset}\gg\vartheta+\Delta_T$; the LIF is the limit $\Delta_T\to0$ (Eqs. 5.7–5.8); refractory time "typically" $0<\Delta_{\rm abs}<5$ ms; the figure plots $\Delta_T=1,0.5,0.25,0.05$ mV — [*Neuronal Dynamics* §5.2](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch5.S2.html)
- [R] AdEx (Brette and Gerstner 2005) parameters: $C=281$ pF, $g_L=30$ nS, $E_L=-70.6$ mV, $V_T=-50.4$ mV, $\Delta_T=2$ mV, $\tau_w=144$ ms, $a=4$ nS, $b=0.0805$ nA — [Brette & Gerstner 2005, J. Neurophysiol. 94, 3637](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00686.2005)

**Quadratic integrate-and-fire (QIF) and the theta neuron.**
- [V] $\tau\,\dot u=a_0(u-u_{\rm rest})(u-u_c)+RI$ (Eq. 5.16); canonical type-I phase model $\dot\phi=[1-\cos\phi]+\Delta I\,[1+\cos\phi]$ (Eq. 5.17); reduced QIF $\dot u=u^2+\Delta I$ (Eq. 5.18); the substitution $u=\tan(\phi/2)$ (Eq. 5.19) maps one to the other, exactly in the limit of threshold $\to\infty$ and reset $\to-\infty$; the two fixed points merge and disappear at a critical current (saddle-node) — [*Neuronal Dynamics* §5.3](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch5.S3.html)
- [R] For $\dot V=V^2+\eta$ with $V_p=-V_r\to\infty$ and $\eta>0$: period $T=\pi/\sqrt\eta$, i.e. $f=\sqrt\eta/\pi$ (type-I, square-root f–I curve) — stated in [Montbrió, Pazó & Roxin 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06581); theta model from [Ermentrout & Kopell 1986](https://doi.org/10.1137/0146017)

**Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin exact mean field.**
- [V] Microscopic model: $\dot V_j=V_j^2+I_j$, reset from $V_p$ to $V_r$ with $V_p=-V_r\to\infty$ (Eq. 1); $I_j=\eta_j+J\,s(t)+I(t)$ (Eq. 2); $\eta_j$ Lorentzian $g(\eta)=\frac1\pi\frac{\Delta}{(\eta-\bar\eta)^2+\Delta^2}$ (Eq. 11) — [Montbrió, Pazó & Roxin, PRX 5, 021028 (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06581)
- [V] Firing-rate equations (Eq. 12): $\dot r=\Delta/\pi+2rv$, $\;\dot v=v^2+\bar\eta+Jr+I(t)-\pi^2r^2$; conformal map to the Kuramoto order parameter $Z=(1-W^*)/(1+W^*)$, $W\equiv\pi r+iv$ (Eq. 18) — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06581)
- [V] Phase diagram for $I=0$: only fixed points; regions of a stable node (low activity), a stable focus (high activity), and bistability; saddle-node boundary in parametric form $(\bar\eta,J)_{SN}=\big[-\pi^2r^2-3\Delta^2/(2\pi r)^2,\;2\pi^2r+\Delta^2/(2\pi^2r^3)\big]$; focus boundary $\bar\eta_f=-[J/(2\pi)]^2-(\pi\Delta/J)^2$ — [same, Fig. 1 caption](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06581)
- [V] Figure parameters: Fig. 2 $J=15$, $\bar\eta=-5$, $\Delta=1$, step $I=3$ until $t=30$ or sinusoid $I_0=3$, $\omega=\pi/20$; Fig. 3 $I_0=3$, $\omega=\pi$, $\bar\eta=-2.5$, $J=10.5$; microscopic simulations $N=10^4$, Euler $dt=10^{-4}$ — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06581)

**Wilson–Cowan, Amari, Hopfield, CTRNN, echo-state networks.**
- [R] Wilson–Cowan: $\tau_e\dot E=-E+(k_e-r_eE)\,S_e(c_1E-c_2I+P)$, $\tau_i\dot I=-I+(k_i-r_iI)\,S_i(c_3E-c_4I+Q)$, $S(x)=[1+e^{-a(x-\theta)}]^{-1}-[1+e^{a\theta}]^{-1}$; limit-cycle set $c_1=16$, $c_2=12$, $c_3=15$, $c_4=3$, $a_e=1.3$, $\theta_e=4$, $a_i=2$, $\theta_i=3.7$, $r_e=r_i=1$, $P=1.25$, $Q=0$ — [Wilson & Cowan 1972, Biophys. - [R] Amari field: $\tau\,\partial_tu(x,t)=-u+\int w(x-y)\,H(u(y,t))\,dy+h+s(x,t)$; with Heaviside firing and $W(x)=\int_0^xw$, a stationary bump of width $a$ satisfies $W(a)+h=0$ and is stable iff $w(a)<0$ — [Amari 1977, Biol. Cybern. 27, 77](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00337259)
- [R] Hopfield: $E=-\tfrac12\sum_{i\ne j}w_{ij}s_is_j$, $w_{ij}=\frac1N\sum_\mu\xi_i^\mu\xi_j^\mu$; asynchronous updates never raise $E$; capacity about $0.15N$ (Hopfield), $\alpha_c\approx0.138$ by replica theory — [Hopfield 1982, PNAS 79, 2554](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.79.8.2554); [Amit, Gutfreund & Sompolinsky 1985, PRL 55, 1530](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.55.1530)
- [R] CTRNN: $\tau_i\dot y_i=-y_i+\sum_jw_{ji}\,\sigma(y_j+\theta_j)+I_i$ — [Beer 1995, Adaptive Behavior 3, 469](https://doi.org/10.1177/105971239500300405)
- [R] Leaky echo-state network: $x(n+1)=(1-\alpha)x(n)+\alpha\tanh(Wx(n)+W_{\rm in}u(n+1))$ with linear trained readout; the echo-state property is usually arranged by scaling the spectral radius of $W$ below 1 (neither necessary nor sufficient in general) — [Lukoševičius & Jaeger 2009, Comput. **Random rate networks (Sompolinsky–Crisanti–Sommers).**
- [V] $\tau\,\dot h_i=-h_i+\sum_jJ_{ij}\phi(h_j)$, $\phi=\tanh$, $J_{ij}\sim\mathcal N(0,g^2/N)$, $J_{ii}=0$ (Eq. 1); the fixed point $h=0$ loses stability at $g_{\rm crit}=1$ and above it the only stable self-consistent solution is chaotic; near onset $\Delta(t')=(g-1)\,{\rm sech}\big(t'(g-1)/\sqrt3\big)+O((g-1)^2)$ (Eq. 12) and $\lambda_{\max}=\tfrac12(g-1)^2+O((g-1)^3)$ (Eq. 13); for large $g$, $\Delta_0\to2(1-2/\pi)$ per unit $g^2$ (Eq. 15); the mean Lyapunov exponent is $-1/\tau$; entropy rate and attractor dimension are extensive, the dimension peaking below 10% of $N$ ($N=1000$, 20 realisations) — [Engelken, Wolf & Abbott, "Lyapunov spectra of chaotic recurrent neural networks"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02427), restating [Sompolinsky, Crisanti & Sommers 1988, PRL 61, 259](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.61.259)

### Inferences

- With $C_E=1000$, $J=0.1$ mV, $\tau=20$ ms, $\theta=20$ mV the threshold rate is $\nu_{\rm thr}=10$ Hz (arithmetic from Brunel's definition), so states A–D are driven at 20, 40, 20 and 9 Hz per external synapse.
- **Checked numerically for this note:** solving Brunel's Eqs. 20–21 as transcribed above (SciPy quadrature and root-finding, under a second on the CPU) gives $\nu_0=55.84$, $37.95$ and $6.52$ Hz at points B, C and D, which are the "Theory" column of his Table 1 (55.8, 38.0, 6.5 Hz). The transcription, including the convention that $\sigma_0$ enters as $(\theta-\mu_0)/\sigma_0$ without a factor $\sqrt2$, is therefore right, and these three numbers are a ready fixture for a mean-field solver.
- MPR's uncoupled case ($J=0$, $I=0$) has the closed-form rate $r=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi}\sqrt{\bar\eta+\sqrt{\bar\eta^2+\Delta^2}}$, obtained by eliminating $v=-\Delta/(2\pi r)$ from Eq. 12; with $J\neq0$ the fixed points are the roots of $\Delta^2/(4\pi^2r^2)+\bar\eta+Jr-\pi^2r^2=0$. Both are exact fixtures for a network simulation in the large-$N$ limit and for the ODE itself.
- The sharpest automatic checks, in order: (i) LIF/QIF f–I in closed form; (ii) the Siegert integral against a simulated noisy LIF; (iii) Brunel's Eqs. 20–21 solved self-consistently against the paper's Table 1; (iv) MPR's ODE against a QIF network of $10^4$ neurons; (v) SCS: $\lambda_{\max}$ changing sign at $g=1$, growing as $\tfrac12(g-1)^2$. For the conductance models the checks are of bifurcation type (Hopf versus SNIC versus homoclinic, read from the f–I curve's onset: discontinuous, $\sqrt{I-I_c}$, or logarithmic).
- Brunel's own Table 1 shows theory and simulation differ by up to about 20% in rate and 30% in oscillation frequency in state D; a check must compare with the *simulation* column within statistical tolerance, or with the *theory* column only for the mean-field solver.
- The book's Hodgkin–Huxley table and the 1952 squid-axon set are different models; a template must name which one it is, since the published bifurcation currents belong to the squid-axon set only.

## 2. Stochastic Processes: Closed Forms, First-Passage Results and the Itô–Stratonovich Conventions

### Takeaway

The linear and square-root diffusions (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, geometric Brownian motion, Cox–Ingersoll–Ross), the exponential Hawkes process and the identical-oscillator noisy Kuramoto model all have exact stationary laws, moments or critical points that an implementation can be held to; a maintained open test set (SciML's `SDEProblemLibrary`) supplies SDEs with analytic pathwise solutions, including matched Itô and Stratonovich pairs.

### Cited Findings

**Ornstein–Uhlenbeck.**
- [V, secondary] $dx_t=-\theta(x_t-\mu)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t$; $x_t=x_0e^{-\theta t}+\mu(1-e^{-\theta t})+\sigma\int_0^te^{-\theta(t-s)}dW_s$; ${\rm cov}(x_s,x_t)=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}\big(e^{-\theta|t-s|}-e^{-\theta(t+s)}\big)$; stationary covariance $\frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}e^{-\theta|t-s|}$; Gaussian transition density with mean $x_0e^{-\theta\Delta}+\mu(1-e^{-\theta\Delta})$ and variance $\frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}(1-e^{-2\theta\Delta})$ — [Wikipedia, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornstein%E2%80%93Uhlenbeck_process)

**Geometric Brownian motion; Itô against Stratonovich.**
- [V] Test problem `prob_sde_linear`: $dX=1.01X\,dt+0.87X\,dW$ (Itô), $X_0=\tfrac12$, $t\in[0,1]$, solution $X_0\exp(0.63155\,t+0.87W_t)$ where $0.63155=1.01-0.87^2/2$; the Stratonovich problem with the same coefficients has solution $X_0\exp(1.01\,t+0.87W_t)$ — [SciML DiffEqProblemLibrary, SDEProblemLibrary.jl](https://github.com/SciML/DiffEqProblemLibrary.jl/blob/master/lib/SDEProblemLibrary/src/SDEProblemLibrary.jl)
- [V] A matched pair in both calculi: diffusion $\beta(1-X^2)$ with Itô drift $-(\alpha+\beta^2X)(1-X^2)$ or Stratonovich drift $-\alpha(1-X^2)$, $\alpha=\beta=1$, $X_0=0.5$, $t\in[0,3]$, common solution $\frac{\tau e^{-2\alpha t+2\beta W}+X_0-1}{\tau e^{-2\alpha t+2\beta W}-X_0+1}$, $\tau=1+X_0$ — [same](https://github.com/SciML/DiffEqProblemLibrary.jl/blob/master/lib/SDEProblemLibrary/src/SDEProblemLibrary.jl)
- [V] Further problems with pathwise analytic solutions: cubic, drift $-\tfrac14X(1-X^2)$, diffusion $\tfrac12(1-X^2)$, solution $\frac{(1+X_0)e^{W}+X_0-1}{(1+X_0)e^{W}+1-X_0}$ (the docstring's drift sign disagrees with the code); wave, drift $-0.01\sin X\cos^3X$, diffusion $0.1\cos^2X$, $X_0=1$, solution $\arctan(0.1W+\tan X_0)$; additive, drift $p_2/\sqrt{1+t}-X/(2(1+t))$, diffusion $p_1p_2/\sqrt{1+t}$, $p=(0.1,0.05)$, solution $X_0/\sqrt{1+t}+p_2(t+p_1W)/\sqrt{1+t}$. Without analytic solutions: Lorenz with additive noise 3.0 (code uses $\beta=2.66$ where the docstring says $8/3$), a bistable chemical system, the stochastic Brusselator, a seven-species oscillating reaction, a 19-species EMT model, a stiff stochastic heat equation — [same](https://github.com/SciML/DiffEqProblemLibrary.jl/blob/master/lib/SDEProblemLibrary/src/SDEProblemLibrary.jl)
- [R] General conversion: the Stratonovich SDE $dX=a\,dt+b\circ dW$ equals the Itô SDE $dX=\big(a+\tfrac12b\,\partial_xb\big)dt+b\,dW$ (in $d$ dimensions, $a_i+\tfrac12\sum_{j,k}b_{kj}\partial_kb_{ij}$) — [Gardiner, *Stochastic Methods* §4.3.6 (publisher page)](https://link.springer.com/book/9783540707127); numerical schemes and strong/weak orders in [Kloeden & Platen 1992](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-12616-5)

**Cox–Ingersoll–Ross.**
- [V, secondary] $dr_t=a(b-r_t)\,dt+\sigma\sqrt{r_t}\,dW_t$; Feller condition $2ab\ge\sigma^2$ (zero is not reached); $E[r_t|r_0]=r_0e^{-at}+b(1-e^{-at})$; ${\rm Var}[r_t|r_0]=\frac{r_0\sigma^2}{a}(e^{-at}-e^{-2at})+\frac{b\sigma^2}{2a}(1-e^{-at})^2$; $2c\,r_{t+T}$ is noncentral $\chi^2$ with $4ab/\sigma^2$ degrees of freedom and noncentrality $2c\,r_te^{-aT}$, $c=\frac{2a}{\sigma^2(1-e^{-aT})}$; stationary law Gamma with shape $2ab/\sigma^2$ and rate $2a/\sigma^2$; primary reference Cox, Ingersoll & Ross, Econometrica 53, 385 (1985) — [Wikipedia, Cox–Ingersoll–Ross model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox%E2%80%93Ingersoll%E2%80%93Ross_model)

**Langevin dynamics and Kramers' escape rate.**
- [V, secondary] Overdamped, low-temperature Kramers prefactor $\omega_0\omega_b/(2\pi\gamma)\,e^{-\beta E_b}$, valid for $\gamma/\omega_b\gg1$ and $\beta E_b\gg1$ (appearing as $D=\frac{\omega_0\omega_bL^2}{2\pi\gamma}e^{-\beta E_b}$ for a periodic potential of period $L$) — [arXiv:0805.0112, "Diffusive transport in periodic potentials: underdamped dynamics"](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0112)
- [V, secondary] The rate is the transition-state-theory expression times a depopulation factor and Kramers' spatial-diffusion factor, which is unity at weak damping and inversely proportional to damping when friction is strong; at very weak friction the rate is limited by energy diffusion and proportional to the friction — [arXiv:1304.1933, "Improvements to Kramers turnover theory"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.1933)
- [R] Moderate-to-strong friction (spatial-diffusion) formula: $k=\frac{\omega_0}{2\pi}\,\frac{\sqrt{\gamma^2/4+\omega_b^2}-\gamma/2}{\omega_b}\,e^{-E_b/k_BT}$; weak friction: $k\simeq\gamma\,\beta I(E_b)\,\frac{\omega_0}{2\pi}e^{-\beta E_b}$ with $I$ the action at the barrier energy — [Hänggi, Talkner & Borkovec, Rev. Mod. Phys. 62, 251 (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.62.251); [Kramers 1940](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0031-8914(40)90098-2)
- [R] Overdamped $\gamma\,dx=-U'(x)\,dt+\sqrt{2\gamma k_BT}\,dW$ has stationary density $\propto e^{-U/k_BT}$; underdamped $dx=v\,dt$, $m\,dv=-(\gamma mv+U'(x))\,dt+\sqrt{2\gamma mk_BT}\,dW$ has $\propto e^{-(mv^2/2+U)/k_BT}$; free particle $\langle\Delta x^2\rangle=\frac{2k_BT}{m\gamma}\big[t-(1-e^{-\gamma t})/\gamma\big]$ — [Risken, *The Fokker–Planck Equation*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-61544-3)

**Hawkes processes.**
- [V] $\lambda^*(t)=\lambda+\int_0^t\mu(t-u)\,dN(u)$ (Eq. 3); exponential kernel $\mu(t)=\alpha e^{-\beta t}$ (Eq. 4); branching ratio $n=\alpha/\beta$ (Eq. 5); stationary iff $0<n<1$; mean intensity $\bar\lambda^*=\lambda/(1-n)=\beta\lambda/(\beta-\alpha)$ (Eq. 7); covariance density for $\tau>0$: $R(\tau)=\frac{\alpha\beta\lambda(2\beta-\alpha)}{2(\beta-\alpha)^2}e^{-(\beta-\alpha)\tau}$ (Theorem 3.2, from Hawkes 1971); log-likelihood $l=\sum_i\log(\lambda+\alpha A(i))-\lambda t_k+\frac\alpha\beta\sum_i[e^{-\beta(t_k-t_i)}-1]$ with $A(i)=e^{-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})}(1+A(i-1))$, $A(1)=0$ (Eqs. 20–21), an $O(k)$ recursion; simulation by Ogata's modified thinning (Algorithm 2) — [Laub, Taimre & Pollett, "Hawkes Processes"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02822)

**Noisy Kuramoto model and its Fokker–Planck description.**
- [V] For identical oscillators with unit noise, the density obeys $\partial_tq=\tfrac12\partial_\theta^2q-K\,\partial_\theta[q\,(J*q)]$, $(J*q)(\theta)=\int\sin(\phi-\theta)\,q(\phi)\,d\phi$ (Eqs. 1.1–1.2); non-trivial stationary solutions exist iff $K>1$; they are $\hat q(\theta)=\frac{e^{2Kr\cos\theta}}{2\pi I_0(2Kr)}$ and rotations, with $r=I_1(2Kr)/I_0(2Kr)$ (Eqs. 4.1–4.2); for $K<1$ the incoherent state attracts exponentially at rate $(1-K)/2$, at $K=1$ not exponentially; for $K>1$ it is unstable with rate $(K-1)/2$ and the circle of synchronised states is stable and normally hyperbolic — [Giacomin, Pakdaman & Pellegrin, "Global attractor and asymptotic dynamics in the Kuramoto model for coupled noisy phase oscillators"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.4501)
- [V] With frequencies distributed by $g(\omega)$ and noise $\sigma\xi$, $\langle\xi\xi'\rangle=2\delta$: $\dot\phi_n=\omega_n+\frac\varepsilon N\sum_{n'}\sin(\phi_{n'}-\phi_n)+\sigma\xi_n$ (Eq. 2 at $\mu=0$); order parameter $r=\int d\nu\,g(\omega_0+\nu)\,\frac{I_{1-i\nu/\sigma^2}(A/\sigma^2)}{I_{-i\nu/\sigma^2}(A/\sigma^2)}$, $A=\varepsilon r$ (Eq. 26); critical coupling $\varepsilon_c=1/C_0$ with $C_0=\tfrac12\int dy\,g(\omega_0+\sigma^2y)/(1+y^2)$ at zero inertia (Eq. 31); for a Lorentzian of half-width $\gamma$, $\varepsilon_c=2(\gamma+\sigma^2)$; noise-free limit $\varepsilon_c=2/(\pi g(\omega_0))$ — [Munyaev et al., "Analytical approach to synchronous states of globally coupled noisy rotators"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01468); original analysis [Sakaguchi 1988](https://doi.org/10.1143/PTP.79.39), stability [Strogatz & Mirollo 1991](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01029202)

**Birth–death and chemical master equations; stochastic resonance; Lévy noise.**
- [R] Immigration–death ($\emptyset\to X$ at rate $k$, $X\to\emptyset$ at rate $\gamma n$): stationary law Poisson($k/\gamma$), and a Poisson initial law stays Poisson with mean $m(t)=m_0e^{-\gamma t}+\frac k\gamma(1-e^{-\gamma t})$; linear birth–death with rates $\lambda n$, $\mu n$: mean $n_0e^{(\lambda-\mu)t}$, extinction probability $(\mu/\lambda)^{n_0}$ for $\lambda>\mu$; exact simulation by Gillespie's direct method — [Gillespie 1977, J. Phys. Chem. 81, 2340](https://doi.org/10.1021/j100540a008); [Gardiner, *Stochastic Methods*, ch. 7 (publisher page)](https://link.springer.com/book/9783540707127)
- [R] Stochastic resonance in $\dot x=ax-bx^3+A\cos\Omega t+\xi$, $\langle\xi\xi'\rangle=2D\delta$: wells at $x_m=\pm\sqrt{a/b}$, barrier $\Delta V=a^2/4b$, Kramers rate $r_K=\frac{a}{\sqrt2\,\pi}e^{-\Delta V/D}$, two-state signal-to-noise ratio ${\rm SNR}=\pi\,(Ax_m/D)^2\,r_K$, maximal at $D=\Delta V/2$ — [Gammaitoni, Hänggi, Jung & Marchesoni, Rev. Mod. Phys. 70, 223 (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.70.223); [McNamara & Wiesenfeld 1989](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.39.4854)

### Inferences

- The two Kuramoto sources agree once noise is normalised: unit noise $\tfrac12\partial_\theta^2$ is $D=\tfrac12$ and gives $K_c=1=2D$; the Lorentzian result $\varepsilon_c=2(\gamma+\sigma^2)$ reduces to $2D$ at $\gamma=0$ with $D=\sigma^2$. So $K_c=2D$ for identical oscillators, and $K_c=2(\gamma+D)$ for Lorentzian frequencies, are safe fixtures; the stationary order parameter is the root of $r=I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$. Computed for this note at unit noise ($D=\tfrac12$): $r=0.5448889866$ at $K=1.2$, $0.7241587176$ at $K=1.5$, $0.8314620248$ at $K=2$ (this note's own numbers, from the fixed-point equation of Giacomin et al.).
- SciML's stiff-quadratic pair verifies the conversion rule by itself: $\tfrac12b\,b'=\tfrac12\beta(1-X^2)(-2\beta X)=-\beta^2X(1-X^2)$, exactly the difference between the two drifts.
- From the OU covariance, the stationary spectrum is $S(\omega)=\sigma^2/(\theta^2+\omega^2)$ (two-sided, $S=\int C(\tau)e^{-i\omega\tau}d\tau$) and the exact update is $x_{t+\Delta}=\mu+(x_t-\mu)e^{-\theta\Delta}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}(1-e^{-2\theta\Delta})}\,\xi$; Wikipedia's printed simulation formula is only the Euler–Maruyama step.
- Pathwise analytic solutions (GBM, cubic, wave, additive) allow *strong*-order convergence tests on the same Brownian path; stationary laws (OU, CIR, Kuramoto) and moments allow *weak* tests. Both kinds run in seconds on a CPU.
- For Kramers' rate, a direct numerical check needs $E_b/k_BT\gtrsim5$ for the asymptotic formula to hold within a few per cent, which makes brute-force simulation slow; the mean first-passage time by quadrature of the backward equation is the cheaper exact reference in one dimension.

## 3. Diffusion Generative Models as Dynamical Systems

### Takeaway

Score-based models, DDPM/DDIM, flow matching, rectified flow, stochastic interpolants and consistency models are all one family: a Gaussian conditional path $x_t=\alpha_tx_0+\sigma_t\varepsilon$ with an SDE or ODE transporting its marginals, so that for Gaussian or Gaussian-mixture data the score, the velocity and (for a single Gaussian) the probability-flow map are known in closed form and every sampler can be checked exactly without training anything. Training on 2-D toy data is a routine CPU task, though no source read here states timings.

### Cited Findings

**The SDE framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021).**
- [V] Forward $dx=f(x,t)\,dt+g(t)\,dw$ (Eq. 5); reverse-time $dx=[f(x,t)-g(t)^2\nabla_x\log p_t(x)]\,dt+g(t)\,d\bar w$ (Eq. 6), attributed to Anderson (1982); probability-flow ODE $dx=[f(x,t)-\tfrac12g(t)^2\nabla_x\log p_t(x)]\,dt$ (Eq. 13) — [Song et al., "Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456); the time-reversal theorem is [Anderson 1982, Stoch. Proc. Appl. 12, 313](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4149(82)90051-5)
- [V] VE: $dx=\sqrt{d[\sigma^2(t)]/dt}\,dw$ (Eq. 9); VP: $dx=-\tfrac12\beta(t)x\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dw$ (Eq. 11); sub-VP: $dx=-\tfrac12\beta(t)x\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)(1-e^{-2\int_0^t\beta})}\,dw$ (Eq. 12), whose variance is bounded by the VP's — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456)
- [V] Perturbation kernels (Eq. 29): VE $\mathcal N(x(0),[\sigma^2(t)-\sigma^2(0)]I)$; VP $\mathcal N(x(0)e^{-\frac12\int_0^t\beta},(1-e^{-\int_0^t\beta})I)$; sub-VP same mean, variance $(1-e^{-\int_0^t\beta})^2I$. With the schedules used: VE variance $\sigma_{\min}^2(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})^{2t}$ (Eq. 31); VP mean factor $e^{-\frac14t^2(\bar\beta_{\max}-\bar\beta_{\min})-\frac12t\bar\beta_{\min}}$, variance $1-e^{-\frac12t^2(\bar\beta_{\max}-\bar\beta_{\min})-t\bar\beta_{\min}}$ (Eq. 33); $\bar\beta_{\min}=0.1$, $\bar\beta_{\max}=20$, $\sigma_{\min}=0.01$; cutoff $\epsilon=10^{-5}$ (VE; VP training) or $10^{-3}$ (VP sampling) — [same, App. B–C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456)
- [V] Training objective (Eq. 7): $\min_\theta E_t\{\lambda(t)E_{x(0)}E_{x(t)|x(0)}\|s_\theta(x(t),t)-\nabla_{x(t)}\log p_{0t}(x(t)|x(0))\|^2\}$; SMLD's chain $x_i=x_{i-1}+\sqrt{\sigma_i^2-\sigma_{i-1}^2}\,z$ (Eq. 8) and DDPM's $x_i=\sqrt{1-\beta_i}\,x_{i-1}+\sqrt{\beta_i}\,z$ (Eq. 10) converge to the VE and VP SDEs as $N\to\infty$ — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456)

**DDPM and DDIM as discretisations.**
- [V] $q(x_t|x_{t-1})=\mathcal N(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\beta_tI)$ (Eq. 2); $q(x_t|x_0)=\mathcal N(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I)$, $\bar\alpha_t=\prod_s(1-\beta_s)$ (Eq. 4); posterior mean $\tilde\mu_t=\frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\beta_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})}{1-\bar\alpha_t}x_t$, variance $\tilde\beta_t=\frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\beta_t$ (Eqs. 6–7); $L_{\rm simple}=E\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,t)\|^2$ (Eq. 14); sampler $x_{t-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\big(x_t-\frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\theta\big)+\sigma_tz$ with $\sigma_t^2\in\{\beta_t,\tilde\beta_t\}$ (Algorithm 2); $T=1000$, $\beta$ linear from $10^{-4}$ to $0.02$ — [Ho, Jain & Abbeel, "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)
- [V] DDIM (its $\alpha_t$ is DDPM's $\bar\alpha_t$): $x_{t-1}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\,\frac{x_t-\sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\epsilon_\theta}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_t^2}\;\epsilon_\theta+\sigma_t\epsilon_t$ (Eq. 12); $\sigma_{\tau_i}(\eta)=\eta\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha_{\tau_{i-1}}}{1-\alpha_{\tau_i}}}\sqrt{1-\frac{\alpha_{\tau_i}}{\alpha_{\tau_{i-1}}}}$ (Eq. 16), $\eta=1$ DDPM, $\eta=0$ deterministic; the deterministic iterate is an Euler step of $d\bar x=\epsilon_\theta\big(\bar x/\sqrt{\sigma^2+1}\big)\,d\sigma$ (Eq. 14), equivalent (Proposition 1) to the probability-flow ODE of the VE SDE, but stepped in $d\sigma$ and not in $dt$ — [Song, Meng & Ermon, "Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502)

**Score-matching objectives.**
- [V] Fisher divergence $L=\tfrac12E_{p_d}\|s_m-s_d\|^2$ (Eq. 1); Hyvärinen's $J=E_{p_d}[{\rm tr}(\nabla_xs_m)+\tfrac12\|s_m\|^2]$ (Eq. 2); sliced $J(\theta;p_v)=E_{p_v}E_{p_d}[v^\top\nabla_xs_m\,v+\tfrac12(v^\top s_m)^2]$ (Eq. 5), with the variance-reduced form replacing the last term by $\tfrac12\|s_m\|^2$ (Eq. 8); denoising (Vincent 2011) $\tfrac12E_{q_\sigma(\tilde x|x)p_d(x)}\|s_m(\tilde x)-\nabla_{\tilde x}\log q_\sigma(\tilde x|x)\|^2$, equal to score matching on the noised density up to a constant — [Song et al., "Sliced Score Matching"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07088)

**Flow matching, rectified flow, stochastic interpolants.**
- [V] $\mathcal L_{FM}=E_{t,p_t(x)}\|v_t(x)-u_t(x)\|^2$ (Eq. 5); $\mathcal L_{CFM}=E_{t,q(x_1),p_t(x|x_1)}\|v_t(x)-u_t(x|x_1)\|^2$ (Eq. 9); for $p_t(x|x_1)=\mathcal N(\mu_t(x_1),\sigma_t(x_1)^2I)$ (Eq. 10) the conditional field is $u_t(x|x_1)=\frac{\sigma_t'}{\sigma_t}(x-\mu_t)+\mu_t'$ (Theorem 3, Eq. 15); optimal-transport path $\mu_t=tx_1$, $\sigma_t=1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t$ (Eq. 20) with $u_t(x|x_1)=\frac{x_1-(1-\sigma_{\min})x}{1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t}$ (Eq. 21); the VE and VP diffusion paths are Eqs. 16–19 — [Lipman et al., "Flow Matching for Generative Modeling"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747)
- [V] Rectified flow: $X_t=tX_1+(1-t)X_0$; $\min_v\int_0^1E\|(X_1-X_0)-v(X_t,t)\|^2dt$ (Eq. 1); minimiser $v^X(x,t)=E[X_1-X_0|X_t=x]$ (Eq. 2); ODE $dZ_t=v(Z_t,t)\,dt$; marginals preserved, ${\rm Law}(Z_t)={\rm Law}(X_t)$ (Theorem 3.3); convex transport costs not increased (Theorem 3.5); reflow $Z^{k+1}={\rm RectFlow}(Z_0^k,Z_1^k)$ with $\min_{k\le K}S(Z^k)\le E\|X_1-X_0\|^2/K$ for the straightness $S(Z)=\int_0^1E\|(Z_1-Z_0)-\dot Z_t\|^2dt$ (Theorem 3.7); toy cases are 2-D mixtures — [Liu, Gong & Liu, "Flow Straight and Fast"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003)
- [V] Stochastic interpolant $x_t=I(t,x_0,x_1)+\gamma(t)z$, $\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=0$ (Eq. 2.1), e.g. $x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1+\sqrt{2t(1-t)}\,z$ (Eq. 1.1); velocity $b=E[\partial_tI+\dot\gamma z|x_t=x]$ (Eq. 2.10); score $s=-\gamma^{-1}E[z|x_t=x]$ (Eq. 2.14); for any $\epsilon\ge0$ the SDEs $dX^F=(b+\epsilon s)\,dt+\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,dW$ and $dX^B=(b-\epsilon s)\,dt+\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,dW^B$ have the interpolant's marginals (Eqs. 2.20–2.23, 2.33–2.34); quadratic losses $\mathcal L_b=\int_0^1E[\tfrac12|\hat b|^2-(\partial_tI+\dot\gamma z)\cdot\hat b]\,dt$ (Eq. 2.13) and $\mathcal L_s=\int_0^1E[\tfrac12|\hat s|^2+\gamma^{-1}z\cdot\hat s]\,dt$ (Eq. 2.16); the paper states that the drifts are available in closed form when both endpoints are Gaussian mixtures (its Appendix A) — [Albergo, Boffi & Vanden-Eijnden, "Stochastic Interpolants"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08797)

**Schrödinger bridges.**
- [V] $\pi^\star=\arg\min\{{\rm KL}(\pi|p):\pi_0=p_{\rm data},\pi_N=p_{\rm prior}\}$ (Eq. 16), solved by iterative proportional fitting, alternating KL projections onto each marginal constraint (Eqs. 20–21); a Gaussian example with analytic bridge ($p_{\rm prior}=\mathcal N(-a,I)$, $p_{\rm data}=\mathcal N(a,I)$, $a=0.1\cdot\mathbf 1$, $d=5$ or 50); 2-D toy experiments with $N=20$ steps of $\gamma_k=0.01$ ($T=0.2$) and a small fully connected network — [De Bortoli et al., "Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01357)

**Consistency models.**
- [V] On the probability-flow ODE $dx_t/dt=-t\,s_\phi(x_t,t)$ (Eq. 3), the consistency function maps $(x_t,t)\mapsto x_\epsilon$ and is constant along a trajectory, with $f(x_\epsilon,\epsilon)=x_\epsilon$; parameterised as $f_\theta=c_{\rm skip}(t)x+c_{\rm out}(t)F_\theta(x,t)$, $c_{\rm skip}(\epsilon)=1$, $c_{\rm out}(\epsilon)=0$ (Eq. 5); distillation loss $E[\lambda(t_n)\,d(f_\theta(x_{t_{n+1}},t_{n+1}),f_{\theta^-}(\hat x^\phi_{t_n},t_n))]$ (Eq. 7); training loss $E[\lambda(t_n)\,d(f_\theta(x+t_{n+1}z,t_{n+1}),f_{\theta^-}(x+t_nz,t_n))]$ (Eq. 10) with $\mathcal L_{CD}=\mathcal L_{CT}+o(\Delta t)$ (Eq. 9); $T=80$, $\epsilon=0.002$, $t_i=(\epsilon^{1/\rho}+\frac{i-1}{N-1}(T^{1/\rho}-\epsilon^{1/\rho}))^\rho$, $\rho=7$, after Karras et al. 2022 — [Song, Dhariwal, Chen & Sutskever, "Consistency Models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469)

**Closed-form scores.**

### Inferences

- **Exact checks that need no training** (each follows in a line or two from the cited kernels):
  1. *Gaussian data.* If $x_0\sim\mathcal N(m,\Sigma)$ and $x_t=\alpha_tx_0+\sigma_t\varepsilon$, then $p_t=\mathcal N(\alpha_tm,\alpha_t^2\Sigma+\sigma_t^2I)$ and $\nabla\log p_t(x)=-(\alpha_t^2\Sigma+\sigma_t^2I)^{-1}(x-\alpha_tm)$.
  2. *Gaussian mixtures.* $p_t=\sum_kw_k\,\mathcal N(\alpha_tm_k,C_{k,t})$, $C_{k,t}=\alpha_t^2\Sigma_k+\sigma_t^2I$, and $\nabla\log p_t(x)=-\sum_kr_k(x)\,C_{k,t}^{-1}(x-\alpha_tm_k)$ with $r_k\propto w_k\mathcal N(x;\alpha_tm_k,C_{k,t})$. A finite dataset is the case $\Sigma_k=0$ (the "ideal denoiser").
  3. *Probability-flow map.* For isotropic Gaussian data $\mathcal N(0,s^2I)$ the marginal variance is $v_t=\alpha_t^2s^2+\sigma_t^2$, and since $\dot v=2fv+g^2$, the ODE of Eq. 13 becomes $\dot x=x\,\dot v_t/(2v_t)$, with exact solution $x(t)=x(0)\sqrt{v_t/v_0}$. Any ODE sampler (Euler, Heun, DDIM) can be held to this map and its order of convergence measured.
  4. *Reverse SDE.* With the exact score, the reverse SDE is linear for Gaussian data; its marginals must be $\mathcal N(\alpha_tm,v_t)$ at every $t$, a weak-convergence test of any stochastic sampler.
  5. *Flow matching.* For $x_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ independent of $x_1\sim\mathcal N(m,s^2I)$ and $x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1$: marginal velocity $u_t(x)=m+\frac{ts^2-(1-t)}{(1-t)^2+t^2s^2}(x-tm)$ (regression of $x_1-x_0$ on $x_t$), the exact target of rectified flow and of Lipman's OT path at $\sigma_{\min}=0$.
  6. *DDPM against VP.* The linear schedule has $\sum_i\beta_i=10.05=\int_0^1(0.1+19.9t)\,dt$, so $\bar\alpha_T\approx e^{-10.1}\approx4\times10^{-5}$ in both; the discrete and continuous kernels agree to $O(1/N)$.
- DDIM with $\eta=0$, the EDM/consistency ODE $\dot x=-t\,s(x,t)$, and flow matching's ODE are reparameterisations of one probability-flow ODE for the same Gaussian path; a library needs one "Gaussian path" object ($\alpha_t,\sigma_t$) from which score, noise-prediction, denoiser and velocity are converted: $s=-\varepsilon/\sigma_t$, $D=(x-\sigma_t\varepsilon)/\alpha_t$, $v=\dot\alpha_tD+\dot\sigma_t\varepsilon$.
- CPU feasibility: all of items 1–6 are deterministic or Monte Carlo checks in $d\le10$ and take seconds. Training a small MLP on 2-D mixtures (the toy settings of Liu et al., De Bortoli et al. and TorchCFM's tutorials) is small enough for a CPU; image-scale models (the CIFAR-10 settings of the cited papers) are not. This is a judgement from the size of the cited toy problems, not a measured figure.
- In this repository such training would sit on tyche's `learning` (networks by backpropagation and Adam) with the closed-form mixture score as the truth, so that a learned score's error is measured and not merely its samples looked at.

## 4. Open Quantum Systems: Master Equations, Closed-Form Steady States and Spectra

### Takeaway

Closed-form references exist for the driven and damped two-level atom (all four Liouvillian eigenvalues, with the exceptional point at $\gamma=4\Omega$), the damped oscillator, the Kerr resonator with one- and two-photon driving (exact steady state for every parameter value, via the complex P-representation), the Dicke model's critical couplings, the boundary-driven XXZ chain (exact matrix-product steady state) and the quantum van der Pol oscillator's quantum limit; all are small enough that a dense Liouvillian on a CPU reproduces them to rounding.

### Cited Findings

**The GKSL equation and conventions.**
- [V] QuTiP: $\dot\rho=-\frac i\hbar[H,\rho]+\sum_n\tfrac12[2C_n\rho C_n^\dagger-\rho C_n^\dagger C_n-C_n^\dagger C_n\rho]$, $C_n=\sqrt{\gamma_n}A_n$ (rate $\gamma_n$ multiplies the standard dissipator) — [QuTiP guide, Lindblad master equation solver](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-master.html)
- [V] Minganti et al. and Bartolo et al. write $\sum_i\frac{\gamma_i}{2}\mathcal D[\Gamma_i]$ with $\mathcal D[\Gamma]\rho=2\Gamma\rho\Gamma^\dagger-\Gamma^\dagger\Gamma\rho-\rho\Gamma^\dagger\Gamma$ (same physical rate as QuTiP's); Kirton et al. write $\sum_i\gamma_i\mathcal D[L_i]$ with the *same* factor-2 dissipator (so their $\kappa$ is an amplitude decay rate); Lee and Sadeghpour write $\kappa_1(2a^\dagger\rho a-\dots)$ likewise; Walter et al. write $\gamma\,\mathcal D[O]$ with $\mathcal D[O]\rho=O\rho O^\dagger-\tfrac12\{O^\dagger O,\rho\}$ — [Minganti et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.11293), [Kirton et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09828), [Lee & Sadeghpour 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6359), [Walter et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.7044)
- [V] General spectral facts: $\mathcal L\rho_{ss}=0$ has at least one solution in finite dimension; ${\rm Re}\,\lambda_i\le0$; eigenvalues come in conjugate pairs with eigenmatrices $\rho_i$, $\rho_i^\dagger$; ${\rm Tr}\,\rho_i=0$ whenever ${\rm Re}\,\lambda_i\ne0$; the Liouvillian gap is $|{\rm Re}\,\lambda_1|$, the asymptotic decay rate; a first-order dissipative transition closes the gap only at the critical point, a second-order one with $\mathbb Z_2$ breaking keeps $\lambda_1=0$ throughout the broken phase — [Minganti, Biella, Bartolo & Ciuti, "Spectral theory of Liouvillians for dissipative phase transitions"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.11293)
- [V] QuTiP's worked examples (usable as regression cases): a spin with $H=2\pi\cdot0.1\,\sigma_x$, collapse operator $\sqrt{0.05}\,\sigma_x$, $t\in[0,10]$; an atom in a leaky cavity, $H=2\pi a^\dagger a+2\pi\sigma_+\sigma_-+2\pi\cdot0.25(\sigma_-a^\dagger+\sigma_+a)$, collapse $\sqrt{0.1}\,a$, cavity initially $|5\rangle$ in a 10-level truncation — [same QuTiP guide](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-master.html)

**Driven, damped two-level system (optical Bloch equations, Mollow triplet).**
- [V] For $H=\frac{\omega_x}{2}\sigma_x$ and decay at rate $\gamma_-$ through $\sigma_-$: Liouvillian eigenvalues $\lambda_0=0$, $\lambda_1=-\gamma_-/2$, $\lambda_{2,3}=-\tfrac34\gamma_-\pm\tfrac14\sqrt{\gamma_-^2-16\omega_x^2}$ (Eq. 47); Liouvillian exceptional point at $\gamma_-=4\omega_x$; the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian $H_{\rm eff}=H-\tfrac i2\sum_\mu\Gamma_\mu^\dagger\Gamma_\mu$ has eigenvalues $\tfrac14(-i\gamma_-\mp\sqrt{4\omega_x^2-\gamma_-^2})$ and its exceptional point at $\gamma_-=2\omega_x$, a different place (Eqs. 44–45) — [Minganti, Miranowicz, Chhajlany & Nori, "Quantum exceptional points of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians and Liouvillians"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11619)
- [V] A second closed-form case: $H=\frac\omega2\sigma_z$ with dissipators $\frac{\gamma_-}{2}\mathcal D[\sigma_-]$, $\frac{\gamma_x}{2}\mathcal D[\sigma_x]$, $\frac{\gamma_y}{2}\mathcal D[\sigma_y]$: $\lambda_{1,2}=-\frac{\gamma_-}{2}-\gamma_x-\gamma_y\pm\sqrt{(\gamma_x-\gamma_y)^2-\omega^2}$; with $\gamma_-=0$ exceptional points at $\gamma_x=\gamma_y\pm\omega$, with no Hamiltonian counterpart (Eqs. 38–40) — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11619)
- [V, secondary] Mollow triplet: central line at the drive frequency with (half-)width $\Gamma/2$, sidebands at $\pm\Omega_R$ with width $3\Gamma/4$; $g^{(2)}(0)=0$ (antibunching); theory by Kimble & Mandel, Phys. Rev. A 13, 2123 (1976) — [Wikipedia, Resonance fluorescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonance_fluorescence)
- [R] Steady-state excited population $\rho_{ee}=\frac{\Omega^2/4}{\Delta^2+\gamma^2/4+\Omega^2/2}$; on resonance $g^{(2)}(\tau)=1-e^{-3\gamma\tau/4}\big[\cosh\kappa\tau+\frac{3\gamma}{4\kappa}\sinh\kappa\tau\big]$, $\kappa=\sqrt{\gamma^2/16-\Omega^2}$; integrated weights of the triplet 1:2:1 and peak heights 1:3:1 for $\Omega\gg\gamma$ — [Mollow 1969, Phys. Rev. 188, 1969](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.188.1969); [Carmichael & Walls 1976](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3700/9/8/007)

**Damped harmonic oscillator; Jaynes–Cummings with loss.**
- [R] $\dot\rho=-i\omega[a^\dagger a,\rho]+\gamma(\bar n+1)\mathcal D[a]\rho+\gamma\bar n\,\mathcal D[a^\dagger]\rho$ (standard dissipator): $\langle a\rangle_t=\langle a\rangle_0e^{-(i\omega+\gamma/2)t}$, $\langle n\rangle_t=\langle n\rangle_0e^{-\gamma t}+\bar n(1-e^{-\gamma t})$, thermal steady state, and Liouvillian spectrum $\lambda_{n,k}=-ik\omega-\gamma(n+|k|/2)$, $n=0,1,\dots$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, independent of $\bar n$ — [Briegel & Englert 1993, Phys. Rev. A 47, 3311](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.47.3311)
- [R] Resonant Jaynes–Cummings with cavity loss $\kappa$ and atomic decay $\gamma$ (energy rates), one-excitation sector of $H_{\rm eff}$: $\lambda_\pm=-\tfrac i4(\kappa+\gamma)\pm\sqrt{g^2-(\kappa-\gamma)^2/16}$; vacuum Rabi splitting resolved for $g>|\kappa-\gamma|/4$, exceptional point at equality — [Breuer & Petruccione, *The Theory of Open Quantum Systems*](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199213900.001.0001)

**Kerr resonator (Drummond–Walls) and its two-photon generalisation.**
- [V] $H=-\Delta a^\dagger a+\frac U2a^{\dagger2}a^2+Fa^\dagger+F^*a+\frac G2a^{\dagger2}+\frac{G^*}2a^2$ (Eq. 4); $i\dot\rho=[H,\rho]+i\frac\gamma2\mathcal D(a)\rho+i\frac\eta2\mathcal D(a^2)\rho$, $\mathcal D(C)\rho=2C\rho C^\dagger-C^\dagger C\rho-\rho C^\dagger C$ (Eqs. 5–6); with $c=\frac{\Delta+i\gamma/2}{U-i\eta}$, $f=\frac{F}{U-i\eta}$, $g=\frac{G}{U-i\eta}$ the steady state is exact: $\langle a^{\dagger i}a^j\rangle=\frac1{\mathcal N}\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{2^m}{m!}\mathcal F_{m+j}\mathcal F^*_{m+i}$ (Eq. 15), $\langle p|\rho_{ss}|q\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal N\sqrt{p!q!}}\sum_m\frac1{m!}\mathcal F_{m+p}\mathcal F^*_{m+q}$ (Eq. 16), $\mathcal N=\sum_m\frac{2^m}{m!}|\mathcal F_m|^2$ (Eq. 11), $\mathcal F_m(f,g,c)=(i\sqrt g)^m\,{}_2F_1(-m,-c-if/\sqrt g;-2c;2)$ (Eq. 13); Wigner function $W(z)=\frac{2}{\pi\mathcal N}\big|\sum_m\frac{(2z^*)^m}{m!}\mathcal F_m\big|^2e^{-2|z|^2}$ (Eq. 20) — [Bartolo, Minganti, Casteels & Ciuti, "Exact steady state of a Kerr resonator with one- and two-photon driving and dissipation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06739)
- [V] One-photon drive only ($g\to0$): $P_{ss}(\alpha,\beta)\propto e^{2\alpha\beta}\,\frac{e^{-2f/\alpha}}{\alpha^{2+2c}}\,\frac{e^{-2f^*/\beta}}{\beta^{2+2c^*}}$ (Eq. 8) and $\mathcal F_m(f,0,c)=\frac{(-2f)^m}{\Gamma(m-2c)}$, the Drummond–Walls result — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06739); original [Drummond & Walls 1980, J. Phys. A 13, 725](https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/13/2/034)
- [V] Thermodynamic-limit scalings and regimes: $U=\tilde U/N$, $F=\tilde F\sqrt N$; first-order transition at $\Delta/\gamma=10$, $\tilde U/\gamma=10$ (one-photon drive); two-photon drive with $\eta=\tilde\eta/N$: second-order transition with $\mathbb Z_2$ breaking at $\Delta/\gamma=-10$, $\tilde U/\gamma=10$, $\tilde\eta/\gamma=1$; first-order with symmetry breaking at $\Delta/\gamma=+10$; the parity superoperator is $\mathcal Z_2=e^{i\pi a^\dagger a}\bullet e^{-i\pi a^\dagger a}$ — [Minganti et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.11293)

**Dicke model and superradiance.**
- [V] $H=\omega_ca^\dagger a+\omega_z\sum_j\sigma_j^z+\frac{2\lambda}{\sqrt N}(a+a^\dagger)\sum_j\sigma_j^x$ (Eq. 1); equilibrium $\lambda_c=\tfrac12\sqrt{\omega_c\omega_z\coth(\beta\omega_z/2)}$ (Eq. 9), i.e. $\tfrac12\sqrt{\omega_c\omega_z}$ at $T=0$; with cavity loss $\kappa$ and collective atomic decay $\gamma$: $\lambda_c=\tfrac12\sqrt{\frac{\omega_z^2+\gamma^2}{\omega_z}\frac{\omega_c^2+\kappa^2}{\omega_c}}$ (Eq. 22); exponents ($\beta$, $\gamma$, $\zeta$): quantum transition $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac13)$; thermal and driven-dissipative steady state $(\tfrac12,1,\tfrac12)$; mean-field Ising class — [Kirton, Roses, Keeling & Dalla Torre, "Introduction to the Dicke model"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09828)
- [R] Dicke superradiance of $N$ inverted atoms decaying collectively at rate $\gamma$ (mean field): $I(t)=\frac{N^2\gamma}{4}{\rm sech}^2\big[\tfrac{N\gamma}{2}(t-t_D)\big]$, $t_D\approx\ln N/(N\gamma)$ — [Gross & Haroche 1982, Phys. Rep. 93, 301](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-1573(82)90102-8)

**Dissipative spin chains.**
- [V] Open XXZ chain $H=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(2\sigma_j^+\sigma_{j+1}^-+2\sigma_j^-\sigma_{j+1}^++\Delta\sigma_j^z\sigma_{j+1}^z)$ with $L_1=\sqrt\epsilon\,\sigma_1^+$, $L_2=\sqrt\epsilon\,\sigma_n^-$: exact steady state $\rho_\infty=S_nS_n^\dagger/{\rm tr}(S_nS_n^\dagger)$ with $S_n$ a matrix-product operator of near-diagonal auxiliary matrices (Eqs. 4–7), a polynomial of degree $2n-2$ in the coupling; spin current independent of $n$ (ballistic) for $|\Delta|<1$, $\langle J\rangle\approx\pi^2\epsilon^{-1}n^{-2}$ at $\Delta=1$ for large $\epsilon$, exponentially small for $|\Delta|>1$ — [Prosen, "Exact nonequilibrium steady state of a strongly driven open XXZ chain"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2978)

**Quantum van der Pol oscillator.**
- [V] $\dot\rho=-i[-\Delta b^\dagger b+i\Omega(b-b^\dagger),\rho]+\gamma_1\mathcal D[b^\dagger]\rho+\gamma_2\mathcal D[b^2]\rho$ (Eq. 1, standard dissipator); classical limit $\dot\beta=i\Delta\beta+\frac{\gamma_1}{2}\beta-\gamma_2|\beta|^2\beta-\Omega$ (Eq. 2), whose phase equation is Adler's; undriven quantum limit $\gamma_2/\gamma_1\to\infty$: $\rho_{ss}\to\tfrac23|0\rangle\langle0|+\tfrac13|1\rangle\langle1|$; figure parameters $\Omega/\gamma_1=1$, $\gamma_2/\gamma_1=0.1$ (semiclassical) and $\Omega/\gamma_1=3$, $\gamma_2/\gamma_1=3$ (quantum) — [Walter, Nunnenkamp & Bruder, "Quantum synchronization of a driven self-sustained oscillator"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.7044)
- [V] The same limit by rate counting, $|0\rangle\xrightarrow{2\kappa_1}|1\rangle\xrightarrow{4\kappa_1}|2\rangle\xrightarrow{4\kappa_2}|0\rangle$, in the factor-2 convention $\dot\rho=\kappa_1(2a^\dagger\rho a-aa^\dagger\rho-\rho aa^\dagger)+\kappa_2(2a^2\rho a^{\dagger2}-a^{\dagger2}a^2\rho-\rho a^{\dagger2}a^2)$; classical model $\ddot x+\omega_o^2x-\epsilon(1-x^2)\dot x=0$ with amplitude equation $\dot\alpha=\frac\epsilon2(1-|\alpha|^2)\alpha$ — [Lee & Sadeghpour, "Quantum synchronization of quantum van der Pol oscillators with trapped ions"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6359)

**Kicked top.**

**Quantum trajectories and continuous measurement.**
- [V] Stochastic Schrödinger equation (homodyne): $d\psi=-iH\psi\,dt-\sum_n\big(\tfrac12S_n^\dagger S_n-\tfrac{e_n}2S_n+\tfrac{e_n^2}8\big)\psi\,dt+\sum_n\big(S_n-\tfrac{e_n}2\big)\psi\,dW_n$, $e_n=\langle S_n+S_n^\dagger\rangle$; stochastic master equation $d\rho=-i[H,\rho]\,dt+\mathcal D[A]\rho\,dt+\mathcal H[A]\rho\,dW$ with $\mathcal H[A]\rho=A\rho+\rho A^\dagger-{\rm tr}(A\rho+\rho A^\dagger)\rho$ (the page prints the trace term without its $\rho$ in one place); $E[dW]=0$, $E[dW^2]=dt$; homodyne current $J_x=\langle x\rangle+dW/dt$; example: cavity of dimension 20, $H=5\cdot2\pi\,a^\dagger a$, monitored $\sqrt2\,a$, initial coherent state of intensity 4, 500 trajectories, $dt=0.00125$; schemes: Platen (default, order 1), Euler–Maruyama, Rouchon (positivity-preserving), explicit 1.5, Milstein, Taylor 1.5 and implicit variants, predictor–corrector; Taylor and Rouchon schemes assume commuting monitored operators — [QuTiP guide, stochastic solvers](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-stochastic.html)

**Beyond the Markov approximation.**
- [V] QuTiP's `HEOMSolver` handles bosonic and fermionic baths, dynamics and steady states, for Drude–Lorentz and underdamped Brownian-motion spectral densities after the bath correlation function is decomposed into exponentials; method of Tanimura & Kubo, J.   - Two-level atom, $H=\tfrac\Omega2\sigma_x$, decay rate $\gamma$ ($\Omega=1$, $\gamma=0.8$): eigenvalues $0$, $-0.4$, $-0.6\pm0.9797958971\,i$, equal to $-\gamma/2$ and $-\tfrac34\gamma\pm\tfrac14\sqrt{\gamma^2-16\Omega^2}$ to rounding.
  - Quantum van der Pol, gain rate 1 and two-photon loss rate 2000 (standard dissipator), 12 Fock states: populations $0.66639$, $0.33344$, approaching $\tfrac23,\tfrac13$.
- The two-level results are mutually consistent and give a complete fixture: eigenvalues $-\gamma/2$ and $-\tfrac34\gamma\pm\tfrac14\sqrt{\gamma^2-16\Omega^2}$ give a Mollow central line of half-width $\gamma/2$ and sidebands of half-width $3\gamma/4$ at $\pm\sqrt{\Omega^2-\gamma^2/16}$, which appear only for $\Omega>\gamma/4$ (the Liouvillian exceptional point), and the same exponents appear in $g^{(2)}(\tau)$.
- The quantum van der Pol limit $\tfrac23,\tfrac13$ is convention-free (it depends only on the ratio of rates) and so is the best first check of an implementation's dissipator; converting between sources, Lee–Sadeghpour's $\kappa_{1,2}$ equal Walter's $\gamma_{1,2}/2$.
- Sizes on a CPU: a Kerr resonator truncated at 40–80 Fock states gives a Liouvillian of dimension 1,600–6,400, a dense eigenproblem of seconds to minutes; Prosen's chain can be checked against a dense solve up to about $n=6$–7 sites ($4^n\le16{,}384$); the Dicke model in the symmetric subspace has dimension $(N+1)\times n_{\rm phot}$. Trajectory methods converge as $1/\sqrt M$ and are needed only beyond these sizes.
- This repository's physis already holds the open two-level system (its `quantum` module), so the two-level fixtures above extend an existing module; the Kerr steady state needs only the Gauss hypergeometric function with a non-positive-integer first argument (a finite sum) and the complex gamma function.

### Gaps

- Drummond and Walls' 1980 paper was not read; their formula is taken from Bartolo et al.'s restatement (confirmed numerically above for one parameter set), and the ${}_0F_2$ form is a derivation of this note. The two-photon-driven case ($G\ne0$, Eq. 13 with ${}_2F_1$) was not checked numerically.
- Mollow's 1969 spectrum, the height and area ratios, the steady-state population, $g^{(2)}(\tau)$, the damped-oscillator spectrum (Briegel–Englert), the Jaynes–Cummings eigenvalues with loss, superradiance, MCWF, the TCL example and collision models are [R]; none of their primary sources was fetched.
- The quantum Rabi model with loss was not researched: beyond the rotating-wave approximation the correct dissipator must be written in the dressed basis, and no closed-form steady state is known to this note; Braak's 2011 integrability result concerns the closed model only.
- The kicked top's map and operator are from search summaries, with signs unchecked against Haake–Kuś–Scharf; no reference numbers (level-spacing statistics, Lyapunov exponents at given $k$) were collected.
- Redfield theory: no closed-form reference case was collected beyond noting that QuTiP provides a Bloch–Redfield solver (not confirmed on the pages fetched).
- No closed form of the quantum van der Pol steady state at finite $\gamma_2/\gamma_1$ was found in the two papers read (Lee and Sadeghpour give only the expansion in $1/\kappa_2$); whether one exists (in confluent hypergeometric functions) was not established.
- Dissipative transverse-field Ising and other interacting chains without exact solutions, and Liouvillian-gap scaling results, were not surveyed.

## 5. Curated Collections and Benchmarks, with Licences

### Takeaway

The reference implementations split by licence: QuTiP (BSD 3-clause), SciML's problem library and TorchCFM (permissive) can be read and their cases reused freely; Brian 2 (CeCILL 2.1), NEST (GPL v2 or later) and The Virtual Brain (GPL v3 or later) are copyleft, so an MIT library may use them only as external oracles that generate fixtures, never as copied code; model databases (ModelDB, NeuroML-DB, Open Source Brain) carry per-model terms.

### Cited Findings

- [V] QuTiP's `LICENSE.txt` is a three-clause BSD licence, copyright "the QuTiP developers and contributors", 2011–2026 — [QuTiP LICENSE.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qutip/qutip/master/LICENSE.txt)
- [V] QuTiP's guide documents, with runnable examples, a Lindblad solver, stochastic Schrödinger and master equation solvers with nine integration schemes, and a HEOM solver for bosonic and fermionic baths — [master equation](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-master.html), [stochastic](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-stochastic.html), [HEOM](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/heom/intro.html)
- [V, from search results] Brian 2 is released under CeCILL 2.1 (version 2.10.1 published 5 December 2025) — [Zenodo record, Brian 2.10.1](https://zenodo.org/records/17833036); [brian2 LICENSE](https://www.github.com/brian-team/brian2/blob/master/LICENSE)
- [V, from search results] NEST "is licensed under the GNU General Public License v2 or later" — [NEST download page](https://www.nest-simulator.org/download); [PyPI nest-simulator](https://pypi.org/project/nest-simulator/)

- [V, from search results] NeuroML-DB hosts over 1,500 previously published models translated to NeuroML, each characterised by standard protocols; the describing paper is CC BY 4.0, and the NeuroML schema is LGPL v3 — [Birgiolas et al., "NeuroML-DB", PLoS Comput. ### Inferences

- For an MIT-licensed library the workable pattern is the one this repository already follows for its fixtures: run the reference package (QuTiP, Brian 2, NEST, TVB, SciML) in a `make_*fixtures` script and commit only the numbers with their source and date. Equations and parameter values are facts taken from the papers; copying a GPL or CeCILL model file would not be compatible with MIT.
- QuTiP is the one collection that is both permissive and broad enough to serve as the oracle for the whole of §4 (steady states, Liouvillian spectra, trajectories, HEOM); for §1, Brian 2 and NEST are oracles for spiking networks and TVB for neural masses; for §2, SciML's analytic problems need no oracle at all.
- Two discrepancies found in the collections themselves are worth carrying as cautions: TVB's Jansen–Rit default $v_0=5.52$ mV against the "usual" 6 mV, and SciML's Lorenz $\beta=2.66$ against the documented $8/3$ and its cubic problem's docstring sign. A fixture generated from a package records the package's choice, not the paper's.
- A "library of candidate models to match against data" needs, per template, the fields this note has tried to fill: equations, parameter set with units and source, regime labels with their bifurcations, and at least one exact or published reference value with its tolerance and whether it is theory or simulation.

### Gaps

- The licences of ModelDB's individual entries and of NeuroML-DB's models were not established; ModelDB's terms-of-use page was not read. The number of models in ModelDB was not found on the page fetched.
- The licence of SciML's DiffEqProblemLibrary (recalled as MIT) and of the *Neuronal Dynamics* exercises was not read.
- QuTiP's test suite and tutorial notebooks (qutip-tutorials) were not enumerated; which closed-form cases its own tests assert (e.g. the Kerr steady state, the Dicke model through PIQS) is unknown to this note. QuTiP's current version number was not shown on the pages fetched.
- NEST's and Brian 2's model catalogues were not enumerated (which of §1's models each ships, and with which default parameters).
- No standard, named benchmark suite was found for closed-form checks of diffusion generative models; the papers cited in §3 use Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture cases ad hoc.
- Standard SDE test sets other than SciML's (Kloeden and Platen's worked examples; test suites of `sdeint`, `torchsde`, `diffrax`) were not examined.
