## 1. Genetic-Algorithm Learning in Economics and Its Relation to Rational Expectations

### Takeaway
Genetic-algorithm (GA) and classifier-system learning reach the rational-expectations equilibrium (REE) where that equilibrium is stable under learning and the operators are designed to converge (Arifovic's election operator; Marimon–McGrattan–Sargent's fundamental equilibrium). They fail to converge, or converge elsewhere, where the equilibrium is indeterminate (exchange rates in overlapping generations), where learning is social and payoffs are relative (Vriend's spite effect), or where learning is fast (the Santa Fe market). Several celebrated results turned on operator details, most notably a biased mutation operator in the Santa Fe market.

### Cited Findings

**Cobweb model (Arifovic 1994).**
- Arifovic, J. (1994), "Genetic algorithm learning and the cobweb model", *Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control* 18(1):3–28 (DOI 10.1016/0165-1889(94)90067-1 [M]). Firms in a single-good competitive market (Muth's 1961 cobweb) update production rules by a GA, compared against other adaptive learning schemes [V record] — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:dyncon:v:18:y:1994:i:1:p:3-28)
- Two variants: a basic GA (reproduction, crossover, mutation) and an augmented GA adding the **election operator**, where an offspring replaces its parent only if it would have done better in the previous period. The GA converged to the REE over a wider range of parameter values than the other algorithms commonly studied in cobweb settings, including the case unstable under naive expectations; the basic GA stays only in a neighbourhood of the REE because mutation keeps injecting variation, while the election operator yields exact convergence [S] — [Wikipedia, GAs in economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_algorithms_in_economics); [EITM lecture notes](https://www.uh.edu/hobby/eitm/_docs/past-lectures/2014-Lectures/Sunny-Wong/Genetic-Algorithm-Learning-and-the-Cobweb-Model.pdf)
- Later work argues that self-adaptation of the mutation rate can replace the election operator and still reach the REE [S] — [Wikipedia, GAs in economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_algorithms_in_economics)
- Chen and Yeh (1996) found the cobweb equilibrium price can also be reached by genetic programming learning [S] — [GP bibliography record](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/chen_1996_aigp2.html)

**Overlapping generations (Arifovic 1995, 1996) [M].**
- Arifovic, J. (1996), "The behavior of the exchange rate in the genetic algorithm and experimental economies", *Journal of Political Economy* 104(3):510–541 (DOI 10.1086/262033 [M]). In a two-currency overlapping-generations economy whose REE exchange rate is indeterminate (Kareken–Wallace), the GA exchange rate keeps fluctuating and never settles, as in laboratory economies with human subjects, while first-period consumption settles near its stationary value [M] — [doi.org/10.1086/262033](https://doi.org/10.1086/262033)
- Arifovic (1995), "Genetic algorithms and inflationary economies", *Journal of Monetary Economics* 36(1):219–243: in an overlapping-generations economy with seigniorage, GA agents select the low-inflation stationary equilibrium, the one stable under adaptive learning, as experimental subjects do [M] — no URL verified.

**Social versus individual learning (Vriend 2000).**
- Vriend, N. J. (2000), "An illustration of the essential difference between individual and social learning, and its consequences for computational analyses", *JEDC* 24(1):1–19 (DOI 10.1016/S0165-1889(98)00064-5 [M]). Two GA variants in a Cournot oligopoly: a **spite effect** separates them [V record] — [Vriend's abstract page](https://nvriend.econ.qmul.ac.uk/abs/abs.jedc1.html)
- Main result as summarised by later work: individual learning (each firm a population of its own rules) converges to the Cournot–Nash output; social learning (firms imitate one another's rules within one population) converges to the Walrasian (competitive) output, since a rule that lowers everyone's profit but one's rivals' more is selected when fitness is compared across firms [S] — [Vallée and Yıldızoğlu 2009, HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00394413v1)
- Arifovic and Maschek (2006) confirm the social-learning result and question the convergence under individual learning; Vallée and Yıldızoğlu (2009), *JEBO* 72(2):670–690, also question Vriend's results [V record] — [doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2009.07.007](https://doi.org/10.1016%2Fj.jebo.2009.07.007); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jeborg:v:72:y:2009:i:2:p:670-690)

**GA learning read as an evolutionary game (Riechmann; Dawid).**
- Riechmann, T. (2001), "Genetic algorithm learning and evolutionary games", *JEDC* 25(6–7):1019–1037, DOI 10.1016/S0165-1889(00)00066-X. Each simple one-population economic GA can be treated as a dynamic game; GA learning yields a sequence of near-Nash equilibria that settles near an evolutionarily stable state; the paper defines evolutionary superiority and evolutionary stability for genetic populations [V record/abstract] — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/dyncon/v25y2001i6-7p1019-1037.html)
- Riechmann (1999, *Journal of Evolutionary Economics*) and Dawid's monograph *Adaptive Learning by Genetic Algorithms* (Springer, 1996; 2nd ed. 1999) analyse the GA as a Markov chain on populations: with positive mutation the chain is ergodic, so "convergence" means concentration of the stationary distribution near an equilibrium as mutation vanishes, not absorption [M] — no URL verified.

**The Santa Fe artificial stock market.**
- Arthur, Holland, LeBaron, Palmer and Tayler (1997), "Asset pricing under endogenous expectations in an artificial stock market", in Arthur, Durlauf and Lane (eds), *The Economy as an Evolving Complex System II*, Addison-Wesley, pp. 15–44 [M]. Traders hold classifier-system forecasting rules (condition bits on fundamental and technical indicators) revised by a GA.
- LeBaron, Arthur and Palmer (1999), "Time series properties of an artificial stock market", *JEDC* 23(9–10):1487–1516, DOI 10.1016/S0165-1889(98)00081-5: for certain parameters agents collectively learn a homogeneous REE, with time series and individual forecasts consistent with the equilibrium parameters [V record/abstract] — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/dyncon/v23y1999i9-10p1487-1516.html)
- The learning speed (how often the GA is invoked, the exploration rate) is the single parameter that shifts the market between a regime near the REE (slow learning) and a "complex" regime with technical trading, fat tails and volatility persistence (fast learning) [S] — [Ehrentreich 2002 working paper](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/comp/papers/0209/0209001.pdf); [Tesfatsion's overview](https://www2.econ.iastate.edu/tesfatsi/SFIStockMarketModel.LTOverview.pdf)
- **Artefact.** Ehrentreich showed the mutation operator was faulty: the bit distribution of the classifier system was systematically biased upward, inflating apparent technical trading at short GA intervals. With a corrected operator the Marimon–Sargent hypothesis (adaptive agents find the REE) is supported for most learning speeds, though the time series still suggest two regimes. Published as "Technical trading in the Santa Fe Institute Artificial Stock Market revisited", *JEBO* 61(4):599–616 (2006) (DOI 10.1016/j.jebo.2004.06.010 [M]), and a Springer book (2008) [V record] — [RePEc 2002](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wpa:wuwpco:0209001); [IDEAS 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v61y2006i4p599-616.html)
- A sensitivity analysis found that Joshi, Parker and Bedau's results replicate with both the original and the corrected operator, but only for specific parameter settings [S] — [search digest of Ehrentreich line](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wpa:wuwpco:0209001)
- LeBaron's survey, "Agent-based computational finance", *Handbook of Computational Economics* vol. 2 (2006), ch. 24 (DOI 10.1016/S1574-0021(05)02024-1 [M]) is the standard review of this line [M].
- Chen and Yeh (2001), "Evolving traders and the business school with genetic programming", *JEDC* 25(3–4):363–393, DOI 10.1016/S0165-1889(00)00030-0: a "business school" is a separate GP population that traders consult (social learning), with traders' search intensity tied to peer pressure by simulated annealing; the market's returns are iid, supporting the efficient-market hypothesis, although the traders do not believe it, and traders often found useful but short-lived signals [V record/abstract] — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/dyncon/v25y2001i3-4p363-393.html); [GP bibliography](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Shu-HengChen_2001_JEDC.html)

**Money (Marimon, McGrattan and Sargent 1990).**
- "Money as a medium of exchange in an economy with artificially intelligent agents", *JEDC* 14(2):329–373 (DOI 10.1016/0165-1889(90)90024-C [M]). Holland's classifier systems (bucket-brigade credit and GA rule discovery) replace the rational agents of Kiyotaki and Wright (1989); the question is whether they learn a Markovian Nash equilibrium [V record] — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:dyncon:v:14:y:1990:i:2:p:329-373)
- Where both a fundamental and a speculative equilibrium exist, the agents select the fundamental one (the good with the lowest storage cost circulates as money); in a variant a good no one consumes emerges as fiat money [S] — [QuantEcon lecture](https://python.quantecon.org/marimon_mcgrattan_sargent.html); [replication project](https://mmcky.github.io/paper-marimon-mcgrattan-sargent-1990/)
- Laboratory economies (Duffy and Ochs 1999, *AER*) also show subjects mostly playing fundamental strategies [M].

**Fitting experiments.**
- GA- and heuristic-switching models have been fitted to learning-to-forecast experiments (Hommes and Lux 2013, *Macroeconomic Dynamics* 17(2):373–401, DOI 10.1017/S1365100511000162 [M]; Anufriev, Hommes and Makarewicz 2019, *JEEA* 17(5):1538–1584, DOI 10.1093/jeea/jvy028 [M]); Arifovic and Ledyard's individual evolutionary learning (IEL) fits public-goods and mechanism experiments [M]. Holland and Miller (1991), "Artificial adaptive agents in economic theory", *AER P&P* 81(2):365–370, is the programmatic statement [M].

### Inferences
- The convergence results line up with E-stability in the adaptive-learning literature: where least-squares learning converges, GA learning with a convergence-favouring operator (election, vanishing mutation) generally converges too; the GA's extra content is in cases where REE is indeterminate or where fitness is relative. A designer should treat "converges to REE" as a check with a known answer only in such E-stable cases (cobweb with election; Kiyotaki–Wright fundamental equilibrium).
- Social versus individual learning is a structural choice of the evolutionary loop (one population of rules shared by agents, or one population per agent), and it changes the equilibrium reached. In an infrastructure with one loop over any genotype, this must be a declared strategy, never an implementation default.
- Operator choices (election, mutation bias) have repeatedly decided published economic results. Every operator should therefore be recorded with its parameters in the run's lineage, and invariance of a result to operators reported.

## 2. Evolved Strategies in Games, Evolved Trading Rules and Genetic Programming That Discovers Economic Relations

### Takeaway
GA-evolved strategies in the repeated prisoner's dilemma reproduced and extended Axelrod's tournament findings (reciprocity, punishment of defectors) once strategies coevolved; GA- and GP-evolved trading rules mostly failed to beat buy-and-hold out of sample after costs on equities, with more positive results on foreign exchange; GP's "rediscovery" of the exchange equation is a demonstration on a known relation, not a discovery.

### Cited Findings
- Axelrod, R. (1987), "The evolution of strategies in the iterated prisoner's dilemma", in L. Davis (ed.), *Genetic Algorithms and Simulated Annealing*, pp. 32–41 [M]. Axelrod was the first to model the evolution of repeated-PD strategies by a GA; strategies conditioned on the last three periods' action profiles and were evaluated against a fixed environment of eight strategies [S] — [Ioannou, Southampton working paper](https://cdn.southampton.ac.uk/assets/imported/transforms/content-block/UsefulDownloads_Download/AFDA102621444620BAC28CD7858BFFD2/1019_ver2_new.pdf)
- Miller, J. H. (1996), "The coevolution of automata in the repeated Prisoner's Dilemma", *JEBO* 29(1):87–112 (DOI 10.1016/0167-2681(95)00052-6 [M]); working paper 1993. Metaplayers submit finite automata updated by a GA in a coevolving (variable) environment, so counting and trigger strategies are expressible; studied under perfect and imperfect reporting; strategies that punish defectors evolve and take over [V record; findings S] — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jeborg/v29y1996i1p87-112.html); [Santa Fe abstract](https://santafe.edu/research/results/working-papers/the-coevolution-of-automata-in-the-repeated-prison)
- Lindgren, K. (1991), "Evolutionary phenomena in simple dynamics", in Langton et al. (eds), *Artificial Life II*, pp. 295–312: genomes of variable memory length grow by gene duplication, giving punctuated equilibria and an open-ended increase of strategic memory [M]; not found by search this session.
- Allen, F. and Karjalainen, R. (1999), "Using genetic algorithms to find technical trading rules", *Journal of Financial Economics* 51(2):245–271 (DOI 10.1016/S0304-405X(98)00052-X [M]). On daily S&P 500 prices 1928–1995, rules found by a GA do not earn consistent excess returns over buy-and-hold out of sample once transaction costs are counted; the GA was motivated as a guard against data snooping by ad hoc rule specification [V abstract] — [JFE PDF](https://www.cs.montana.edu/courses/spring2007/536/materials/Lopez/genetic.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6996)
- The 1995 working paper and 1994 dissertation versions reported significant out-of-sample excess returns in 1970–1989; the published conclusion is more negative [V] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6996)
- Neely, Weller and Dittmar (1997, *JFQA* 32(4):405–426) used GP to discover profitable foreign-exchange rules [S] — [Carleton course paper listing](https://people.scs.carleton.ca/~dmckenne/5704/Papers/2.pdf)
- Koza (1992), *Genetic Programming* (MIT Press), recovered the exchange equation (price level as money supply times velocity over output) from US time series by symbolic regression [M]; Koza also published "A genetic approach to econometric modeling" (1992) [M].
- Shu-Heng Chen edited *Evolutionary Computation in Economics and Finance* (Physica/Springer 2002), a collection of GA/GP applications including trading rules [S] — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-7908-1784-3_16)

### Inferences
- The robust lesson of the trading-rule literature is methodological: a search over rules is itself a data-snooping procedure unless the evaluation of what it kept is held out, and costs are counted. This matches the reusable-holdout and once-only confirmation discipline already used elsewhere in the infrastructure.
- Coevolution (the environment is the population) and a fixed environment (Axelrod's eight strategies) answer different questions: the first models a strategic ecology, the second optimises a best response. The loop should make the fitness environment's dependence on the population explicit.

### Gaps
- Lindgren 1991 and Koza 1992 were not verified; no modern (2015–2026) peer-reviewed study of symbolic regression discovering a new economic law was found in this pass.

## 3. Formal Bridges Between Evolutionary Algorithms and Evolutionary Dynamics

### Takeaway
Four bridges are exact: (i) Price's equation is an identity for any record of parents, offspring and weights, so it applies equally to a GA population and to a population of firms, and the standard productivity decomposition of Foster, Haltiwanger and colleagues is Price's equation with entry and exit; (ii) fitness-proportional selection is a discrete replicator dynamic, and under weak selection population genetics with recombination equals multiplicative-weights learning; (iii) estimation-of-distribution and evolution strategies are natural-gradient ascent on a family of search distributions (information-geometric optimisation); (iv) the schema theorem says little about performance, and Price's covariance makes its hidden assumption explicit. Open-endedness and quality-diversity are models of innovation with measures but no accepted theory.

### Cited Findings

**Price's equation and Fisher's theorem.**
- Frank, S. A. (2012), "Natural selection. IV. The Price equation", *Journal of Evolutionary Biology* 25(6):1002–1019 (DOI 10.1111/j.1420-9101.2012.02498.x [M]), arXiv:1204.1515. Replies to critics (Nowak, van Veelen) who call the equation a tautology: its value is abstract, in the invariances it exposes about selection; Frank traces the "tautology" label to his own 1995 paper [V record/abstract] — [arXiv:1204.1515](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.1515v1); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3354028)
- Fisher's fundamental theorem is a special case of more general Price-type theorems [S] — [Queller 2017 (PDF)](https://www.zoology.ubc.ca/~mmosmond/let/pdfs/Queller2017.pdf)
- Standard form used for firms (Holm 2014, full text): with firm size x_i, character z_i, share s_i = x_i/X, fitness w_i = x′_i/x_i, mean fitness W = X′/X, the identity is W ΔZ = Cov(w_i, z_i) + E(w_i Δz_i) (eq. 3), the first term "selection / inter-firm / reallocation", the second "innovation / learning / intra-firm"; the inner expectation can be expanded by Price's equation again, giving a multilevel decomposition in which each agent is a population in its own right (after Andersen 2004) [V full text] — [Holm 2014, preprint](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/241567339/Holm2014_VBN.pdf); published *Journal of Evolutionary Economics* 24(5):1009–1036, [DOI 10.1007/s00191-014-0380-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00191-014-0380-6)

**Altenberg 1995: the schema theorem and Price's theorem.**
- Altenberg, L. (1995), "The schema theorem and Price's theorem", *Foundations of Genetic Algorithms 3* (Whitley and Vose, eds), Morgan Kaufmann, pp. 23–49. The schema theorem says nothing about how well a GA performs; interpretations implicitly assume a correlation between parental and offspring fitness, an assumption made explicit by Price's covariance and selection theorem; schemata reappear as the natural units once recombination operators are used [V record/abstract] — [GP bibliography](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Altenberg_1995STPT.html); [author's page with full text](https://dynamics.org/Altenberg/PAPERS/STPT/)

**Price's theorem inside evolutionary computation.**
- Langdon and Poli (1997), "Fitness causes bloat": comparing runs with and without fitness selection, backed by Price's theorem, shows the growth of program size is caused by fitness-based selection [V record] — [GP bibliography](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Langdon_1997_bloatMUTet.html); also Langdon (1997), "Price's theorem and the MAX problem" — [record](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Langdon97.html)
- Poli and McPhee rewrote their exact size-evolution equation for GP (2003) in a form showing its direct relation to Price's theorem, and used it to set the parsimony coefficient dynamically ("covariant parsimony pressure", technical report CES-480, 2008; a 2012 Springer chapter revisits it) [V record] — [CES-480 record](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/CES-480.html); [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-642-33206-7_9)
- Exact schema theorems model the expected count of a schema from one generation to the next under selection, crossover and mutation, but are hard to evaluate because many terms contribute [S] — [Poli's GECCO 2008 tutorial](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gecco2008/docs/p2559.pdf)
- **Bassett, Potter and De Jong (2004)**, "Looking under the EA hood with Price's equation", GECCO 2004, LNCS 3102:914–922: an extension of Price's equation used as a tool to look inside production-level EAs (how selection, representation and reproductive operators interact); two EAs with similar black-box behaviour look quite different "under the hood" [V record/abstract] — [author abstract](https://cs.gmu.edu/~mpotter/abstract_gecco04.html); [PDF](https://cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2004/Conference%20proceedings/papers/3102/31020914.pdf). Precursor "Visualizing evolvability with Price's equation" (CEC 2003) — [abstract](https://cs.gmu.edu/~mpotter/abstract_cec03.html); extension "Applying Price's equation to survival selection" (GECCO 2005), noting the original works on population averages — [abstract](https://cs.gmu.edu/~mpotter/abstract_gecco05.html)
- Mühlenbein and Schlierkamp-Voosen (1993), "Predictive models for the breeder genetic algorithm", *Evolutionary Computation* 1(1):25–49 (DOI 10.1162/evco.1993.1.1.25 [M]), import the breeder's equation R = h²S (response to selection = realised heritability × selection differential) from quantitative genetics to predict GA progress under truncation selection [M].

**Selection as a replicator dynamic; evolution as learning.**
- Fitness-proportional selection maps frequencies p_i to p_i f_i / f̄, the discrete-time replicator equation (standard; e.g. Vose, *The Simple Genetic Algorithm*, MIT Press 1999) [M].
- Chastain, Livnat, Papadimitriou and Vazirani (2014), "Algorithms, games, and evolution", *PNAS* 111(29):10620–10623 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1406556111 [M]): under weak selection, the population-genetic equations of selection with sex coincide with genes playing a repeated (coordination) game by multiplicative-weights updates; since multiplicative weights trades cumulative payoff against entropy, this suggests a view of how evolution keeps diversity [V abstract] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4115542). Companion commentary by Barton, Novak and Paixão — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4115508). Meir and Parkes (2015) dispute the diversity reading: sexual dynamics imply no property of the population distribution beyond what convergence already implies [V record] — [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037e-1f02-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)
- The replicator equation is a gradient flow of mean fitness in the Shahshahani (Fisher information) metric, and is formally Bayes's rule with fitness as likelihood (Harper 2009, arXiv:0911.1383) [M].

**Evolutionary algorithms as natural-gradient search over distributions.**
- Ollivier, Arnold, Auger and Hansen (2017), "Information-geometric optimization algorithms: a unifying picture via invariance principles", *JMLR* 18(18):1–65, arXiv:1106.3708. Any smooth parametric family of distributions on any search space yields a continuous-time black-box optimiser (IGO); for Gaussians it recovers a version of CMA-ES and xNES; for Bernoulli distributions on {0,1}^d it recovers PBIL and the compact GA; the cross-entropy method is a large-step special case; the method is invariant under reparametrisation of the search space, of the distribution, and under increasing transformations of the objective [V record/abstract] — [JMLR](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/14-467.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.3708v3)

**Multilevel selection.**
- Price's equation can be substituted into itself, allowing a multilevel study of evolution (Andersen 2004); Holm derives a two-level version for firms within industries (industry selection, firm selection within industry, learning, entry, exit), in which firm selection is assumed to occur only within industries [V full text] — [Holm 2014](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/241567339/Holm2014_VBN.pdf)
- Knudsen (2004), "General selection theory and economic evolution: the Price equation and the replicator/interactor distinction", *Journal of Economic Methodology* 11(2):147–173 [V record]; Metcalfe (1998) and others expressed economic evolution in the Price equation; Nelson and Winter (1982) treated routines as replicators, Metcalfe (1998) as interactors with knowledge the replicator; Hodgson and Knudsen (2004) take the firm as the interactor of routines [S] — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jecmet/v11y2004i2p147-173.html); [MPRA paper with the summary](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/106146/8/MPRA_paper_106146.pdf)
- In EC, cooperative coevolution (Potter and De Jong 2000, *Evolutionary Computation* 8(1):1–29) and island models supply group structure; Traulsen and Nowak (2006, *PNAS* 103(29):10952–10955) give a simple model of multilevel selection [M].

**Open-endedness, quality-diversity and the adjacent possible.**
- Tria, Loreto, Servedio and Strogatz (2014), "The dynamics of correlated novelties", *Scientific Reports* 4:5890, DOI 10.1038/srep05890: Kauffman's adjacent possible formalised as a Pólya urn with triggering (drawing a new element adds new elements to the urn), predicting a Heaps-like law for the rate of novelty and a Zipf-like law for frequencies, tested on Wikipedia edits, tagging, texts and music listening [V record/abstract] — [arXiv:1310.1953](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.1953); [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014NatSR...4E5890T); review: [Loreto et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.00994v1.pdf)
- MAP-Elites (Mouret and Clune 2015, arXiv:1504.04909), novelty search (Lehman and Stanley 2011, *Evolutionary Computation* 19(2):189–223, DOI 10.1162/EVCO_a_00025) and the quality-diversity frame (Pugh, Soros and Stanley 2016, *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* 3:40, DOI 10.3389/frobt.2016.00040) [M].
- Evolution through Large Models (ELM; Lehman et al., arXiv:2206.08896) used a code-diff language model as the mutation operator inside a quality-diversity loop; FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al. 2024, *Nature* 625:468–475, DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06924-6 [M]) uses a frozen language model as the variation operator over island populations with score-weighted parent choice; AlphaEvolve generalises it from functions to codebases [S] — [search digest of 2024–2026 preprints](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14150v3). A 2026 preprint argues that FunSearch's diversity comes only from sampling one model, not from different generative priors [S; attribution among the retrieved preprints uncertain] — [arXiv:2605.27130](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27130)

### Inferences
- The Price identity is the single bridge that needs no modelling assumption: given any census of a population at two times, a size weight, a character and a parent map, it decomposes the change of the weighted mean exactly. It therefore applies to firms (productivity), to rules held by agents (Arifovic's populations), and to candidate solutions in a search (fitness or any descriptor). What differs is only what the weight, the character and the parent map mean.
- Fisher's theorem (change of mean fitness = additive variance / mean) holds exactly in Price form only when the transmission term vanishes; in a GA that term is the operators' bias (mutation and crossover moving offspring away from parents). Langdon and Poli's bloat result and Bassett, Potter and De Jong's operator analysis are both readings of that second term.
- IGO says that a search distribution's update is a natural-gradient step; the replicator equation is a natural-gradient flow on the simplex. Both are "selection as information geometry", which gives a common language for an agent's GA learning (economics as object) and a search algorithm's update (economics as method).
- Quality-diversity archives are formally a structured population with niches; the multilevel Price equation (cells as groups) can decompose an archive's progress into between-cell and within-cell selection, which would make QD runs readable as models of innovation.

- No published work was found that applies the multilevel Price equation to a MAP-Elites archive; this may exist and was not searched for specifically.
- The specific operators analysed by Bassett, Potter and De Jong (2004) could not be confirmed from the abstract.

## 4. Policy Search in Simulated Economies, and Language-Model and Learning Agents as Economic Agents

### Takeaway
Policy design by search in simulated economies is now a two-level (bilevel) problem: a planner searches policies while agents re-learn under each one (the AI Economist by deep RL; the LLM Economist by in-context RL with language-model agents), which answers the Lucas critique inside the model but not outside it. Language-model agents reproduce qualitative findings of classic behavioural experiments and macro stylised facts, but their validation rests largely on believability, they are stochastic and versioned, and their conflicts of interest and closed weights limit reproducibility.

### Cited Findings

**Reinforcement-learned tax policy.**
- Zheng, Trott, Srinivasa, Parkes and Socher (2022), "The AI Economist: taxation policy design via two-level deep multiagent reinforcement learning", *Science Advances* 8(18):eabk2607, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.abk2607: agents and a social planner coadapt; structured curriculum learning stabilises the two-level problem; in one-step economies it recovers the optimal tax policy of economic theory; in complex dynamic economies it substantially improves utilitarian social welfare over baselines [V record/abstract] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9067926); [arXiv:2108.02755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02755v1)
- An earlier preprint (arXiv:2004.13332) reported a 16% better equality–productivity trade-off than the Saez framework; that figure belongs to the earlier version [V/S] — [arXiv:2004.13332](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13332)
- A 2026 survey of RL for economics notes that the welfare ranking depends on the social welfare function chosen and on the absence of behavioural responses outside the modelled action space [S] — [arXiv:2603.08956](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08956)
- Karten, Li, Ding, Kleiner, Bai and Jin (2025), "LLM Economist: large population models and mechanism design in multi-agent generative simulacra", arXiv:2507.15815 (NeurIPS 2025): worker agents are persona prompts sampled from US Census-calibrated income and demographic statistics choosing labour supply; a planner proposes piecewise-linear marginal tax schedules by in-context RL; with up to 100 agents the planner converges near Stackelberg equilibria that improve aggregate welfare over Saez solutions; a periodic persona-level vote adds gains; presented as a pre-deployment testbed [V abstract] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15815v1); [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/129463)
- Koster et al. (2022), "Human-centred mechanism design with Democratic AI", *Nature Human Behaviour* 6:1398–1407 (DOI 10.1038/s41562-022-01383-x [M]): a redistribution mechanism designed by deep RL to win human players' majority votes [M].

**Policy optimisation and the Lucas critique in agent-based models.**
- Munson, Dey and Mak (2026), arXiv:2602.17079: a statistical framework that first tests how sensitive the optimal policy is to state parameters and then searches policies by Bayesian optimisation, on Sugarscape; ensembles of hundreds to thousands of runs take hours, the cost the surrogate addresses [S/V abstract] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17079)
- Dyer, Bishop and Felekis (arXiv:2312.11158) argue a surrogate of an ABM must stay consistent under policy interventions, not only fit the baseline, framing it as a causal abstraction [S] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11158v1)
- ADAGE (arXiv:2501.09429): fixed, hand-specified agent rules expose ABMs to the Lucas critique; it proposes adaptive agents in a Stackelberg (two-layer) game covering calibration, scenario analysis, robust behavioural learning and policy design [S] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09429)
- Surrogate-assisted GA and PSO for ABM calibration: conclusions depend on the interaction of model, optimiser, objective and surrogate [S] — [arXiv:2609.13247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13247)
- Lamperti, Roventini and Sani (2018), "Agent-based model calibration using machine learning surrogates", *JEDC* 90:366–389 (DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2018.03.011 [M]) [M].

**Language-model agents as economic agents.**
- Horton, Filippas and Manning, "Large language models as simulated economic agents: what can we learn from Homo silicus?", NBER WP 31122 (April 2023, revised February 2026), arXiv:2301.07543: LLMs as implicit computational models of humans, given endowments, information and preferences; simulations of Charness and Rabin (2002), Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler (1986) and Samuelson and Zeckhauser (1988) are qualitatively similar to the originals; differences are framed as prompts for research; not peer-reviewed; disclosure that Horton advises Anthropic and all authors have a financial interest in Expected Parrot [V record] — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31122); [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07543v1)
- Li, Gao, Li, Li and Liao (2024), "EconAgent: large language model-empowered agents for simulating macroeconomic activities", ACL 2024, pp. 15523–15536, DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.829, arXiv:2310.10436: perception (heterogeneous decision profiles) and memory (reflection on own experience and market conditions) modules; labour, consumption, government and banking; the authors report more plausible macro phenomena (inflation, unemployment) than rule-based or RL agents; code public [V record] — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.829); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10436v4). Listed with an Outstanding Paper award [S] — [ACL preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2024.acl-long.829)
- Larooij and Törnberg (2025), "Validation is the central challenge for generative social simulation: a critical review of LLMs in agent-based modeling", *Artificial Intelligence Review* (DOI 10.1007/s10462-025-11412-6), arXiv:2504.03274: validation remains insufficiently addressed; LLMs may worsen it (black box, cultural biases, stochastic outputs); many studies rest on face validity or outcomes loosely tied to mechanisms [V record] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11412-6); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03274v1). - Aher, Arriaga and Kalai (2023, ICML) and Argyle et al. (2023, *Political Analysis* 31(3):337–351) are the other standard references on LLMs simulating human subjects [M].

### Inferences
- The AI Economist and the LLM Economist are both bilevel searches with an inner adaptive population. An evolutionary loop can play the outer (planner) role directly, with the inner economy's agents re-adapting under each candidate policy; this is the evolutionary form of the Lucas-critique answer, and its cost structure is exactly the "costly evaluator screened by a surrogate" problem already built in the allocation domain.
- A policy found against one model of agents is an optimum relative to that model; robustness requires an ensemble over agent models (rule-based, GA-learning, RL, language-model) and over parameters the record does not identify, with the policy judged by its worst case or regret across the ensemble.
- Language-model agents fit the role of proposals of grade C (as the infrastructure already treats models): useful to generate candidate behaviours and policies, never as validation of a policy's effect on people.

### Gaps
- No single peer-reviewed source combining evolutionary search over policies, robustness across ensembles of ABMs and an explicit Lucas-critique test was found.
- Reproducibility of LLM-agent economies across model versions was not documented by a dedicated study in this pass; it is asserted generally (stochastic outputs) by Larooij and Törnberg.
- Koster et al. 2022, Lamperti et al. 2018, Aher et al. 2023 and Argyle et al. 2023 not re-read.

## 5. Lineage and Attribution in Evolutionary Records and in the Economics of Routines

### Takeaway
In economics, the genealogy of a population is used through decomposition (Price/productivity decompositions attribute aggregate change to selection, learning, entry and exit) and through spinoff genealogies of firms (inheritance of routines); in evolutionary computation, ancestry is quantified by lineage and phylogeny metrics and used for operator credit. No established method attributes innovation to external proposers in a search; the entry term of the Price decomposition, split by source, is the natural candidate.

### Cited Findings
- Productivity decomposition as Price's equation (Holm 2014, full text): distinguish continuing (C), entering (N) and exiting (X) firms; ΔZ = Σ_{i∈C} Δs_i (z_i − Z) [selection] + Σ_{i∈C} s′_i Δz_i [learning] + Σ_{i∈N} s′_i (z′_i − Z) [entry] − Σ_{i∈X} s_i (z_i − Z) [exit] (eq. 4, signs as in the paper's description: entry contributes positively when entrants are more productive than the pre-evolution mean, exit positively when exiters are less productive); the only difference from Foster et al. (1998) is that they split learning into a within term Σ s_i Δz_i and a cross term Σ Δs_i Δz_i (eq. 5) [V full text] — [Holm 2014](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/241567339/Holm2014_VBN.pdf)
- Holm's caveats: entry and exit are measured against the average incumbent, while Baldwin and Gu (2006b) argue entrants should be compared with exiters; the selection terms of successive short intervals do not sum to the selection term of the whole interval, so results depend on census years (Bottazzi et al.'s strong selection is partly an artefact of summing up to fifteen one-year decompositions); the method is best suited to labour productivity and reallocation [V full text] — [Holm 2014](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/241567339/Holm2014_VBN.pdf)
- Melitz and Polanec (2015) extend Olley–Pakes to survivors, entrants and exiters and note that Griliches–Regev (1995) and Foster–Haltiwanger–Krizan (2001) refine Baily, Hulten and Campbell (1992); a 2023 Springer paper recommends decompositions in levels, since log-based growth decompositions can mislead [S; the search digest names the *Journal of Productivity Analysis*, but the DOI prefix s00191 is that of the *Journal of Evolutionary Economics*; venue unverified] — [Melitz's page](https://melitz.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/pdf-22); [Springer 2023, DOI 10.1007/s00191-023-00816-8](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00191-023-00816-8.pdf)
- Replicator/interactor accounts of routines (Nelson and Winter; Metcalfe; Hodgson and Knudsen) — see §3 [V record/S] — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jecmet/v11y2004i2p147-173.html)
- Klepper (2007), "Disagreements, spinoffs, and the evolution of Detroit as the capital of the U.S. automobile industry", *Management Science* 53(4):616–631 (DOI 10.1287/mnsc.1060.0643 [M]), and Klepper and Sleeper (2005), "Entry by spinoffs", *Management Science* 51(8):1291–1306: firm genealogies in which spinoffs inherit capabilities from parents, the better parents having more and better spinoffs [M].
- Dolson, Lalejini, Jorgensen and Ofria (2020), "Interpreting the tape of life: ancestry-based analyses provide insights and intuition about evolutionary dynamics", *Artificial Life* 26:58–79 (DOI 10.1162/artl_a_00313 [M]): three lineage measures (lineage length, mutation accumulation, phenotypic volatility) and four phylogeny measures adopted from biology, open source in the Empirical library; later tools Phylotrack (MIT licence) and hereditary stratigraphy (inferring phylogenies from heritable annotations instead of tracking every birth) [V record; tools S] — [PeerJ preprint](https://peerj.com/preprints/26883); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10104615)
- Davis (1989), "Adapting operator probabilities in genetic algorithms" (ICGA 3): credit for an improved offspring is passed back to the operators that produced its ancestors, a bucket-brigade over the genealogy [M].
- Bedau, Snyder and Packard's evolutionary activity statistics (1998) count the persistent usage of components along lineages to classify long-term dynamics, and were applied to patent data to compare biological and cultural evolution (Skusa and Bedau 2002) [M].

### Inferences
- A genealogy with sources attached lets the Price entry term be partitioned by source: the contribution of outside proposals (a person, a language model, a rule) to the change of the archive's mean is Σ over entrants from that source of s′_i (z′_i − Z), and their later descendants' contributions can be carried forward through the transmission term along lineages. This is an accounting attribution, exact as an identity, but not causal: it does not say what would have happened without the proposals.
- For causal credit, a counterfactual run (the search re-run without a source) or a Shapley value over sources is needed; both are expensive and stochastic, and neither was found in the literature on evolutionary search.

### Gaps
- No study was found that uses EC genealogies to attribute innovation to external proposers, or Shapley values over proposal sources in an evolutionary run.
- Klepper's spinoff work and Bedau's activity statistics were not verified this session.

## 6. What Is Established, What Is Speculative, and the Known Pitfalls

### Takeaway
Established: Price's identity and its productivity form; IGO's unification of distribution-based search; proportional selection as replicator; GA convergence to REE in E-stable settings with convergent operators; the dependence of equilibrium on social versus individual learning (with the individual case contested). Speculative: language-model agents as human proxies; RL-designed taxes transferring to real economies; quality-diversity as a model of economic innovation. The pitfalls recur: results driven by operator artefacts, data snooping, census-interval dependence, believability as validation.

### Cited Findings
- Operator artefacts decide results: the Santa Fe market's biased mutation inflated technical trading [V record] — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wpa:wuwpco:0209001); the election operator decides exact convergence in the cobweb [S] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_algorithms_in_economics)
- Learning structure decides equilibrium: social learning → Walrasian, individual → Cournot, the latter contested [S/V] — [HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00394413v1)
- Data snooping and costs: GA trading rules lose their edge out of sample after costs; earlier drafts had found an edge [V] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6996)
- The schema theorem has been over-read as a performance guarantee [V] — [Altenberg 1995](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Altenberg_1995STPT.html)
- Price's equation is an identity ("tautology"); its use is in decomposition and invariances, not prediction [V] — [Frank 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.1515v1)
- Decompositions depend on census intervals, on the reference point for entry and exit, and on levels versus logs [V/S] — [Holm 2014](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/241567339/Holm2014_VBN.pdf); [Springer 2023, DOI 10.1007/s00191-023-00816-8](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00191-023-00816-8.pdf)
- Welfare rankings of learned policies depend on the social welfare function and on behaviours outside the modelled action space [S] — [arXiv:2603.08956](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08956)
- Generative ABMs are mostly validated by believability [V/S] — [Larooij and Törnberg](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11412-6)
- Fixed agent rules expose ABM policy results to the Lucas critique [S] — [ADAGE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09429)

### Inferences
- Overfitting to stylised facts (fat tails, volatility clustering) is the ABM analogue of data snooping: a market tuned until it shows the facts has not explained them. Held-out facts (not used in tuning) and invariance to operators are the minimum.
- Unidentified parameters (learning speed, mutation rate, election) should be reported as such, and results shown over their range, which is the same rule the earlier survey set for learned dynamics.

### Gaps
- No systematic replication study across the GA-learning economics literature (2000–2026) was found in this pass.

## 7. What an Implementation Would Need

### Takeaway
Build first a general, exact Price decomposition over any genealogical record with weights, characters, a parent map and sources (multilevel, with entry and exit, split by operator and by source), held to identities and closed forms; it serves firms and candidate solutions alike and costs little. Then GA-learning agents in the cobweb and Cournot economies as models with known answers; the bilevel policy search and language-model agents only after those checks pass.

### Cited Findings
- The firm form and its multilevel recursion, with entry and exit sets and the within/cross split, are fully specified in Holm (2014) eqs. 2–9 [V full text] — [Holm 2014](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/241567339/Holm2014_VBN.pdf)
- Operator-level Price decompositions inside production EAs exist (Bassett, Potter and De Jong 2004, 2005) [V record] — [abstract](https://cs.gmu.edu/~mpotter/abstract_gecco04.html)
- PBIL and cGA are IGO on Bernoulli families; CMA-ES and xNES on Gaussians [V] — [JMLR](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/14-467.html)
- Kiyotaki–Wright with classifier systems has an open replication (QuantEcon) usable as a reference [S] — [QuantEcon](https://python.quantecon.org/marimon_mcgrattan_sargent.html)

### Inferences

**What the formal bridge requires (a Price decomposition on any evolutionary record).**
1. *A census abstraction over the genealogy.* Two populations (at t and t′); for each member a weight x_i (copies in the population, archive share, market share, employment, a budget), a character z_i (fitness, any descriptor, productivity, a price), and a parent map from each member at t′ to its parents at t with transmission weights (1 for clonal descent; ½ each for two-parent crossover, or as declared). Members of t′ with no parent at t are entrants (N), members of t with no descendant at t′ are exiters (X), the rest continuing (C).
2. *The decomposition.* ΔZ = selection + transmission + entry − exit (Holm eq. 4), with the optional within/cross split (Foster et al.), all computed in exact rational arithmetic so that the identity can be checked to equality.
3. *Partitions.* The transmission term split by the operator recorded on each birth (mutation, crossover, a model's variation, a person's edit); the entry term split by the source recorded on each entrant (heuresis's outside proposals, credited to their source); the exit term split by the reason of retirement.
4. *Levels.* A recursive (multilevel) form with declared groups: islands, niches or quality-diversity cells for a search; industries or places for firms. Both levels' sums must equal the single-level ΔZ.
5. *Intervals.* A decomposition over each census pair and over the whole span, reported side by side, since they do not add (Holm §3.3); a declared reference for entry and exit (mean of incumbents, or exiters).
6. *Placement.* The decomposition names no object in nature and serves both the search foundation's genealogies and the economics domain's populations of firms; it belongs in a foundation (beside the genealogies), with the economics domain composing it for firms. This is a recommendation for the designer, not a finding.

**Checks with known answers.**
- Identity: on random records (random weights, characters, parent maps, entrants, exiters), the terms sum to ΔZ exactly in rationals; the two-level sum equals the one-level ΔZ.
- Fisher in Price form: under pure proportional selection with exact copying and z = fitness, transmission = 0 and ΔZ = Var(w)/W (size-weighted), exactly.
- Replicator: one generation of roulette-wheel selection in expectation equals p_i f_i / f̄.
- Neutrality: with fitness-blind selection the selection term has expectation zero; the size change in a GP run then equals the operators' transmission bias (Langdon and Poli's bloat argument).
- Breeder's equation: truncation selection of the top fraction q of a normal character with variance σ² has selection differential S = σ φ(Φ⁻¹(1−q))/q, and response h²S with heritability h² (Mühlenbein) [M for the GA literature; the formula is standard quantitative genetics].
- Firms: a hand-worked panel of five firms (two continuing, one entrant, one exiter, one with zero growth) reproducing Foster–Haltiwanger–Krizan's within, between, cross, entry and exit terms and Holm's equivalence.
- Information geometry: the IGO step on a Bernoulli family equals PBIL's update in closed form; on an isotropic Gaussian it gives the known natural-gradient mean update.
- Weak selection: the replicator step and multiplicative weights agree to first order in the selection strength (Chastain et al.).
- Models with known answers for GA-learning agents: Muth's cobweb REE price (linear demand and supply, closed form), reached exactly with the election operator and approached only in a neighbourhood without it; Vriend's Cournot game with linear demand, Cournot quantity (individual learning) and Walrasian quantity (social learning), both closed forms, the individual case flagged as contested; Kiyotaki–Wright's fundamental equilibrium selected (MMS 1990), against the QuantEcon replication.

**What is worth building first.**
1. The Price decomposition over genealogies, with entry by source and transmission by operator, multilevel and multi-interval, and the checks above. Cheap, exact, general, and it gives at once an accounting of what outside proposals contributed to a search and a productivity decomposition for any panel of firms.
2. GA-learning agents in the cobweb and Cournot models, with social versus individual learning as a declared strategy of the existing loop, and the election operator as one survival strategy; their convergence (and non-convergence) are known answers.
3. A bilevel policy search: the existing loop as the planner over policies, inner agents re-adapting under each candidate, judged across an ensemble of agent models and unidentified parameters, with the existing surrogate screening for the costly inner economy.
4. Language-model agents only as proposers of behaviours and policies (grade C), validated against the same known-answer games before any use as a population.
5. Deferred: a Santa Fe-type market (heavy, operator-sensitive; build only with the corrected mutation operator and operator-invariance reported), open-endedness measures (MODES-style) on quality-diversity runs read as innovation, and causal attribution of innovation by counterfactual runs or Shapley values over sources.

### Gaps
- No published implementation computes Price decompositions over arbitrary EC genealogies with external proposers; the design above is an inference from Holm (2014), Bassett et al. (2004) and Langdon and Poli (1997), not a reported system.
- Causal (counterfactual) attribution of innovation in evolutionary runs has no standard method in the literature found.
