# Agent-Based Evolutionary Models of Industries and Macroeconomies, and Their Validation

Marks: [V] read at the source cited (the page or full text itself); [S] read only in an abstract, an index record or a search summary of the source; [M] from memory, unverified (DOIs so marked must be checked before citation). Where a DOI is given without a mark it carries the mark of its line.

Scope note: stock–flow consistency, money circulation and AB-SFC in general, and the learning of economic dynamics from data, were covered by an earlier survey in this repository; they enter here only where they bear on the evolutionary (innovation, selection, industry dynamics) side.

## 1. The Keynes + Schumpeter (K+S) Family: Structure, Timing and Stylised Facts

### Takeaway
K+S is a two-sector economy (F1 = 50 capital-good firms that search by R&D, F2 = 200 consumption-good firms that buy vintage machines and compete by quasi-replicator dynamics), later given heterogeneous banks, a Taylor-rule central bank, a government with fiscal rules, a decentralised labour market, a climate–energy block (DSK) and several sectors; its code is public in LSD (C++) at the Sant'Anna repository, and its claim to validity rests on jointly reproducing 17 listed macro and micro stylised facts across 100 Monte Carlo runs, judged mostly qualitatively.

**Agents and sectors (baseline 2010 vintage)** [V, OFCE WP 2014-19]
- Capital-good sector of F1 firms (index i): produce heterogeneous machines with labour; invest a fraction of past sales in R&D; process innovation raises their own labour productivity B_i,t, product innovation raises the productivity of the machines they sell, A_i,t. Price = fixed mark-up over unit cost. Imperfect information: they send "brochures" (price, productivity) to a subset of consumption-good firms (new-customer sample parameter 0.50).
- Consumption-good sector of F2 firms (index j): homogeneous good, capital and labour under constant returns; desired production from adaptive demand expectations net of inventories (desired inventories 0.10); expansion investment when capacity falls short; replacement investment by a payback rule; capital is a set of machine vintages Ξ_j,t of heterogeneous productivity.
- LS workers/consumers who consume their wage; a public sector taxing profits (flat rate tr) and paying unemployment benefits.
- Baseline parameters legible in the table: F1 = 50, F2 = 200; R&D propensity ν = 0.04; share of R&D to innovation ξ = 0.50 (rest to imitation); Beta support for innovation draws [x̲1, x̄1] = [−0.15, 0.15]; Beta shape (α1, β1) = (3, 3); search-capability parameters ζ1,2 = 0.30 (experiments use 0.1 and 0.5); 100 Monte Carlo replications; time series of about 600 periods (figure axes). The rest of the extracted parameter table is misaligned in the PDF text and is not reported here.

**Search: innovation and imitation** [V]
- Two-stage draw. Stage 1: Bernoulli success with probability θ^in_i,t = 1 − exp(−ζ1 · IN_i,t), IN the R&D spent on innovation (eq. 1); imitation likewise with ζ2. Stage 2 (innovation): new technology A^in = A_i,t(1 + x^A), B^in = B_i,t(1 + x^B), x^A, x^B independent Beta(α1, β1) draws rescaled to [x̲1, x̄1], x̲1 ∈ [−1, 0], x̄1 ∈ [0, 1] (eq. 2). A draw may be worse than the current vintage. The Beta shape encodes "technological opportunities".
- Imitation: probability of copying a competitor decreases in the Euclidean technological distance (in (A, B) space).
- Because R&D is proportional to past turnover, buoyant demand raises innovation probabilities: the Keynes→Schumpeter feedback.

**Selection: investment, pricing, market shares, entry and exit** [V]
- Replacement by payback: a machine of productivity A_i,τ in the vintage set is scrapped when p*_t / (c(A_i,τ) − c*_t) ≤ b, where p*, c* are price and unit cost of the new machine and b > 0 the payback period (eq. 3); machines older than a physical scrapping age (3 in the table text, possibly misaligned) are also scrapped.
- Supplier choice: a consumption-good firm switches supplier when offered a better combination of lower machine price and lower unit production cost.
- Finance (2010): internal funds first; external funds dearer; borrowing capped at a debt-to-sales ratio Λ, so credit rationing can occur.
- Quasi-replicator dynamics of market shares: f_j,t = f_j,t−1 (1 + χ (E_j,t − Ē_t)/Ē_t), χ > 0 (eq. 4); competitiveness E_j,t = −ω1 p_j,t − ω2 l_j,t, with l_j,t unfilled demand inherited from the previous period (eq. 5).
- Variable mark-up driven by market-share change: μ_j,t = μ_j,t−1 (1 + υ (f_j,t−1 − f_j,t−2)/f_j,t−2) (eq. 6).
- Exit when net liquid assets NW < 0 or market share falls to (near) zero; each exiting firm is replaced, so the number of firms is constant. Entrants are smaller: consumption-good entrants' capital is mean incumbent capital times U[φ1, φ2] (table: [0.10, 0.90]); entrants' liquid assets likewise U[φ3, φ4]; capital-good entrants draw technology from Beta(α2, β2), bounded by the most productive incumbent.

**Labour, consumption and accounts (2010)** [V]
- The wage rate follows an institutional rule in prices, unemployment and average productivity; involuntary unemployment is endogenous when labour demand L^D < L^S. Unemployment benefit w^u = φ w, φ ∈ [0, 1]. Aggregate consumption C_t = w_t L^D_t + w^u_t (L^S − L^D_t). GDP = Σ_i Q_i + Σ_j Q_j = C + I + ΔN.

**Credit and policy block (2013–2015 vintages)** [V]
- B heterogeneous banks with Pareto-distributed numbers of clients (Pareto shape in the table text, misaligned); deposits, loans and sovereign bonds.
- Bank credit supply TC_k,t = NW^b_k,t−1 / (τ^b (1 + β · Bda_k,t−1)), with τ^b a Basel-like capital requirement, Bda the ratio of accumulated bad debt to assets and β the speed of the buffer's adjustment to fragility (eq. 7). Credit allocated by ranking firms on past net worth to sales until supply is exhausted.
- Taylor rule r_t = r^T + γπ (π_t − π^T) + γU (U^T − U_t), γπ > 1, γU ≥ 0 (eq. 8); banks lend at a risk premium over r_t.
- Failed banks are recapitalised by the government (bail-out cost). Deficit Def_t = Debt^cost_t + G^bailout_t + G_t − Tax_t (eq. 9); deficits financed by bonds bought by banks out of net profits, the central bank buying the residual.

**Timing of a period** [V]
1. Capital-good firms do R&D (innovate, imitate) and send brochures.
2. Consumption-good firms decide production and investment, choose suppliers, place orders.
3. Firms in both sectors hire and produce.
4. The consumption-good market opens; shares evolve by the quasi-replicator.
5. Entry and exit (zero share or negative net liquid assets; replacement).
6. Machines ordered at the start are delivered and enter the capital stock at t + 1.
Aggregates are sums of micro variables at the end of the step.

**Stylised facts claimed (Table 1 of the WP)** [V]
- Macro: SF1 endogenous self-sustained growth with persistent fluctuations; SF2 fat-tailed GDP growth-rate distribution; SF3 exponentially distributed recession durations; SF4 relative volatility of GDP, consumption and investment; SF5 cross-correlations of macro variables; SF6 pro-cyclical aggregate R&D; SF7 cross-correlations of credit variables; SF8 lagged correlation between firm debt and loan losses; SF9 right-skewed banking-crisis durations; SF10 fat-tailed fiscal cost of banking crises to GDP.
- Micro: SF11 right-skewed (non-log-normal) firm log-size distribution; SF12 fat-tailed ("tent-shaped") firm growth rates; SF13 productivity heterogeneity; SF14 persistent productivity differentials; SF15 lumpy firm investment; SF16 counter-cyclical bankruptcies; SF17 power-law bad debt at bankruptcy.
- Statistics shown: series bandpass-filtered (6, 32, 12) (Baxter–King) before computing relative volatilities and co-movements; consumption smoother and investment more volatile than GDP; consumption pro-cyclical and coincident; net investment, inventories, productivity, nominal wages, inflation pro-cyclical; unemployment, prices and mark-ups counter-cyclical; real wage acyclical. GDP growth density: 250 bins, 59,900 pooled observations, plotted against a normal fit; recession durations against an exponential fit. The authors state that details of the validation are in the earlier papers and are omitted "due to space limitations"; no numerical acceptance thresholds are stated in the WP.
- The authors' validation stance: matching many very different micro and macro facts at once is "far more demanding than a simple polynomial-fitting exercise", taken as "robust empirical validation" (citing Fagiolo et al. 2007; Fagiolo and Roventini 2012). Footnote 6 concedes an open "puzzle": averaging across runs washes out path dependency.

**Labour-market variant** [S]
- Dosi, Pereira, Roventini and Virgillito 2017, "When more flexibility yields more fragility: the microfoundations of Keynesian aggregate unemployment", JEDC 81:162–186: compares two "archetypes of capitalism" defined by wage determination, firing, labour protection and the sharing of productivity gains (a "Fordist" regime with wages insensitive to labour-market conditions and indexed to productivity, against a "competitive" one); the seemingly more rigid regime yields higher and smoother growth. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:dyncon:v:81:y:2017:i:c:p:162-186); [OFCE blog](https://www.ofce.sciences-po.fr/blog/?p=7589)

Not verified here.

**Climate and energy variant (DSK)** [S]
- Lamperti et al. 2018, "Faraway, so close: coupled climate and economic dynamics in an agent-based integrated assessment model", Ecological Economics 150:315–339 (keywords: climate damages, integrated assessment, macro dynamics). — [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/faraway-so-close-coupled-climate-and-economic-dynamics-in-an-agent-based-integrated-assessment-model-lamperti-francesco/10011991146)

**Other descendants** [S unless marked]

### Inferences
- The K+S core is small enough to state as a handful of decision rules: a Bernoulli–Beta search on a two-coordinate technology (A, B); payback replacement; a supplier switch on price plus unit cost; a quasi-replicator on a linear competitiveness index; a share-driven mark-up; exit at zero share or negative net worth with replacement. It maps naturally onto an agent framework plus an evolutionary library (the replicator and Beta draws are selection and variation operators) and onto a ledger (net worth, deposits, loans, bonds, bail-outs as postings).
- The constant number of firms (replacement of exits) makes entry and exit rates a by-product, not a free stylised fact; any check on entry/exit rates in a reimplementation is weaker than it looks.
- "Reproduces SF_k" in K+S papers mostly means a sign pattern (pro/counter-cyclicality), an ordering of volatilities, and a visual or parametric departure from Gaussianity, not a tested match to empirical magnitudes.

### Gaps
- The 2010 JEDC paper and the 2015 finance paper were not read in full (LEM site's certificate failed); exact cross-correlation tables, relative-volatility values and Subbotin/AEP estimates for K+S are not reported here.
- The legible baseline parameter list is partial; the full table should be taken from the LSD configuration files (`.lsd`) in SantAnnaKS/LSD rather than from PDF text.
- Whether the Sant'Anna repository ships reference outputs (seeded runs with expected statistics) was not checked.
- The labour-market variant's matching protocol and the DSK energy and climate equations were not read at source.

## 2. Other Evolutionary and Schumpeterian ABMs and What Each Adds

### Takeaway
Beyond K+S, the main lines are: history-friendly industry models (sector-specific, calibrated to a narrative of real events: computers, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals); the Eurace@Unibi macro model (spatial regions, vintage capital with skill dynamics, written in FLAME with public code and a transparency paper); the Caiani et al. AB-SFC benchmark (balance-sheet consistency, a Java framework, later given R&D); Ciarli–Lorentz–Savona–Valente structural-change models (multi-sector, consumption classes, demand-led structural change); the Pisa "industry" and "island" models; and Nelson–Winter (1982) Schumpeterian competition, of which modern reimplementations are few.

### Cited Findings

**History-friendly models (HFM)**
- [S] Malerba, Nelson, Orsenigo and Winter 2016, *Innovation and the Evolution of Industries: History-Friendly Models*, Cambridge University Press (DOI 10.1017/CBO9781107280120): formal models that follow the sequence of real events in a specific industry; three industries: computers, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals; the pharmaceutical chapters treat drug-market fragmentation, imitation and patent protection, and the biotechnology revolution. — [Wharton Mack Institute](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/2016/innovation-evolution-industries-history-friendly-models/); [Cambridge chapter 2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/innovation-and-the-evolution-of-industries/historyfriendly-models-methods-and-fundamentals/BB89B7C2CEA2A3EC2EF503DC181F4788)
- [M] The first HFM: Malerba, Nelson, Orsenigo and Winter 1999, "History-friendly models of industry evolution: the computer industry", ICC 8(1):3–40 (DOI 10.1093/icc/8.1.3, unverified): mainframe firms on transistor then microprocessor technologies, a later personal-computer market, firms with "routines" for R&D and marketing, demand by submarkets with bandwagon effects and lock-in; validated by reproducing the qualitative history (IBM's dominance in mainframes, a less concentrated PC market) and then run counterfactually ("history-divergent" runs: weaker lock-in, different timing of the new technology, antitrust).
- [M] What HFM adds: validation against a dated sequence of qualitative events and concentration paths of one industry, and counterfactual history, rather than generic stylised facts; criticism has been that matching one history with many free parameters is weak identification.

**Eurace and Eurace@Unibi**
- [S] Eurace@Unibi is "a closed macroeconomic model with a spatial structure", derived from the EURACE project's platform (ended November 2009) and extended by Dawid, Gemkow, Harting, Neugart and van der Hoog; its mechanisms of technological change are developed in more detail than other parts; it runs in the ETACE Virtual Appliance with the FLAME framework; source code in the Bielefeld repository (pub.uni-bielefeld.de record 2900767) and on GitHub (ETACE/eurace_unibi). — [Bielefeld model page](https://www.uni-bielefeld.de/fakultaeten/wirtschaftswissenschaften/lehrbereiche/etace/eurace@unibi/the-eurace@unibi-model/) [V]; [GitHub ETACE/eurace_unibi](https://github.com/ETACE/eurace_unibi)
- [S] Dawid, Harting, van der Hoog and Neugart 2019, JEE 29:467–538 (DOI 10.1007/s00191-018-0594-0), titled in the repository record "A Heterogeneous Agent Macroeconomic Model for Policy Evaluation: Improving Transparency and Reproducibility": a complete description written because the complexity of such models makes their exact working hard to communicate; Dawid, Gemkow, Harting, van der Hoog and Neugart 2018, "Agent-Based Macroeconomic Modeling and Policy Analysis: The Eurace@Unibi Model", Oxford Handbook on Computational Economics and Finance, pp. 490–519, reviewing applications to labour-market design, banking regulation, growth and inequality. — [Bielefeld PUB 2902981](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2902981); [Bielefeld PUB 2622498](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2622498)
- [M] Structure (unverified here): households, consumption-goods firms, an investment-goods firm offering machine vintages of rising productivity (product innovation), banks, a central bank, a government, a statistical office and regional "malls" as retail points; workers with general skills (fixed, education) and specific skills (learned on the job, converging to the vintage they use), so that the effective productivity of a vintage depends on workers' skills; regions with commuting; calendar of days, weeks and months with firms acting on their own activation days. Its innovation and diffusion of vintages under skill constraints is its main evolutionary contribution, beside spatial policy (cohesion policy, convergence). Dawid's handbook chapter "Agent-based models of innovation and technological change" (Handbook of Computational Economics vol. 2, 2006) surveys the earlier field.
- [M] Dawid and Delli Gatti 2018, "Agent-based macroeconomics", Handbook of Computational Economics vol. 4, ch. 2 (DOI 10.1016/bs.hescom.2018.02.006, unverified): compares seven macro ABM families (including K+S, Eurace@Unibi, Eurace (Genoa), CATS/Delli Gatti, Ashraf–Gershman–Howitt, LAGOM/Wolf et al., JAMEL/Seppecher) on their protocols for production, pricing, investment, credit, labour and timing.

**Caiani et al. benchmark AB-SFC**
- [S] Caiani, Godin, Caverzasi, Gallegati, Kinsella and Stiglitz 2016, "Agent based-stock flow consistent macroeconomics: Towards a benchmark model", JEDC 69:375–408 (DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2016.06.001 [M]): pairs AB and SFC methods; the model matches many empirical regularities, "ranking among the best performers in the related literature", robustly across parameterisations; offers rules and tools to build, calibrate, validate and display AB-SFC models. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:dyncon:v:69:y:2016:i:c:p:375-408); [Columbia PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/Joseph_Stiglitz/Agent%20based-stock%20flow.pdf)
- [M] Its sectors (households, consumption- and capital-goods firms with vintage capital, banks, government, central bank), its calibration of initial balance sheets from an aggregate SFC steady state, and its Java framework JMAB are from memory. Its evolutionary extension with R&D and innovation is Caiani, Russo and Gallegati 2019, "Does inequality hamper innovation and growth? An AB-SFC analysis", JEE (DOI 10.1007/s00191-018-0554-8, unverified).

**Ciarli, Lorentz, Savona and Valente: structural change**
- [S] Ciarli, Lorentz, Valente and Savona 2019, "Structural changes and growth regimes", JEE (DOI 10.1007/s00191-018-0574-4): a multi-sector growth model comparing two regimes differing in labour relations, competition and consumption patterns; income distribution and growth linked through structural change on the demand and supply sides; the demand of the least wealthy classes "particularly relevant". Related: LEM WP 2008/08 "Structural change of production and consumption"; Lorentz et al. SWPS 2015-04 on demand-driven structural transformation (inertia in consumption delays a Kaldorian engine of growth). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/r/sru/ssewps/2017-12.html); [SPRU 2015-04](https://www.sussex.ac.uk/spru/documents/2015-04-swps-lorentz-et-al-demand-driven-structural-transformations.pdf)
- [M] Mechanisms (unverified): firms organised in hierarchical tiers of workers (Lydall-style), wage classes forming consumer classes whose preferences over product characteristics differ and shift with income; product and process innovation in sectors; the published versions are Ciarli et al. 2010 Metroeconomica 61(1):180–218 and Lorentz, Ciarli, Savona and Valente 2016 JEE 26:219–246 (DOI 10.1007/s00191-015-0409-x). Written in LSD (Valente is LSD's author).
- What it adds over K+S: many sectors and consumer classes, so structural change (shares of sectors in output and employment) and the income distribution are endogenous outcomes to be validated (Kaldor's facts, Engel-curve shifts).

**Pisa industry and island models**
- [S] The "Industry model" (Dosi, Pereira and Virgillito 2017, ICC) couples a replicator dynamic with idiosyncratic learning and robustly yields fat-tailed firm growth rates; its global sensitivity analysis is Dosi, Pereira and Virgillito 2018 (Section 4). — [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/on-the-robustness-of-the-fat-tailed-distribution-of-firm-growth-rates-a-global-sensitivity-analysis-dosi-giovanni/10011789715)
- [V] The Islands model of endogenous growth (Fagiolo and Dosi 2003) has 8 input parameters, used with 50 firms and 1,000 periods as a test-bed for surrogate calibration. — [Lamperti, Roventini and Sani, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.10639)
- [S] The Islands model has been statistically model checked with MultiVeStA (arXiv 2604.04543). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04543)

**Nelson–Winter replications**
- [S] Clementino and Barrenechea 2022 (Economia Ensaios) implement in Python the family of Schumpeterian competition models of Nelson and Winter (1982, ch. 12), starting from a simple static industry model; they cite Andersen et al. (1996) and Andersen (2001) as earlier algorithmic versions. No LSD or NetLogo port of the Nelson–Winter growth model was found. — [Economia Ensaios](https://seer.ufu.br/index.php/revistaeconomiaensaios/article/view/66702/34489)
- [M] Other modern uses: Yıldızoğlu 2002 (Computational Economics) replaces fixed R&D rules in a Nelson–Winter industry with genetic-algorithm learning of R&D strategies; Salle and Yıldızoğlu 2014 (Computational Economics, DOI 10.1007/s10614-013-9406-7, unverified) use the Nelson–Winter model as a test case for kriging meta-models with nearly orthogonal Latin hypercubes. Safarzyńska and van den Bergh 2010 (JEE, "Evolutionary models in economics: a survey of methods and building blocks") classify the operators (replicator, GA, Pólya urns, multi-level selection).

### Inferences
- The families differ in what selection acts on: K+S selects firms by a replicator on competitiveness and by exit; Nelson–Winter selects by investment from profits (capital follows profitability) with satisficing search; Eurace@Unibi lets vintages diffuse subject to skill accumulation; HFM selects via demand submarkets with lock-in; Nomaler–Spinola–Verspagen select R&D strategies themselves toward an ESS. A general sub-domain would expose selection as a declared operator (replicator, profit-financed growth, exit threshold, imitation by distance), not hard-code one.
- Eurace@Unibi and Caiani et al. are the reference points for transparency (full descriptions plus public code); K+S is the reference for evolutionary content (search draws, replicator) and for the length of its stylised-fact list.

### Gaps
- The Eurace@Unibi JEE paper was paywalled to the fetcher; its structure above is [M].
- No full text of an HFM chapter was read; parameter counts, the data used to check the computer-industry histories, and their quantitative targets are not reported.
- I found no modern reference implementation of Nelson–Winter (1982) with published outputs to check against, and no ODD description of K+S.

## 3. Stylised Facts Used for Validation, and the Statistics Used to Judge Them

### Takeaway
The canonical list is fixed (Table 1 of K+S, Section 1), but acceptance criteria are rarely formal: authors report signs of cross-correlations and orderings of bandpass-filtered volatilities, visual or parametric (Subbotin/Laplace) fat tails, skewed size distributions, and persistence of productivity gaps; the few explicit thresholds found are Lamperti et al.'s for the Islands model (mean growth above 2%, Subbotin shape b ≤ 1) and KS tests in asset-pricing ABMs.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Explicit acceptance rules in a calibration exercise: for the Islands model a parameter vector is "positive" when the average growth rate exceeds 2% and the growth-rate distribution has fat tails, operationalised as the Subbotin shape parameter b ≤ 1 (only vectors passing the growth test are tested for tails); a robustness variant uses AGR > 0.5% with Monte Carlo averages over 100 seeds. For Brock–Hommes, positive when a two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test of simulated log returns against S&P 500 returns (Dec 2013–Dec 2015, 502 observations) has p > 0.05. — [Lamperti, Roventini and Sani, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.10639)
- [V] K+S judges business-cycle facts on (6, 32, 12) bandpass-filtered series: σ(C) < σ(Y) < σ(I) and the signs and timing of cross-correlations with GDP (Section 1); GDP growth fat tails against a normal fit; recession durations against an exponential fit. — [OFCE WP 2014-19](https://www.ofce.fr/pdf/dtravail/WP2014-19.pdf)
- [S] Firm growth rates: Bottazzi and Secchi 2006, "Explaining the distribution of firm growth rates", RAND J. Econ. 37(2):235–256: a symmetric exponential (Laplace) density, "tent-like" on a log scale, also reported for US manufacturing (Stanley et al. 1996) and pharmaceuticals; explained by a model in which firms' ability to take up business opportunities increases with opportunities already taken. Later work on French manufacturing finds Subbotin shapes significantly below 1, so the Laplace (b = 1) is not universal. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:randje:v:37:y:2006:i:2:p:235-256); [LEM WP 2002/20](https://www.lem.sssup.it/WPLem/files/2002-20.pdf)
- [S] Robustness of fat tails in a model: Dosi, Pereira and Virgillito 2018, JEIC 13(1):173–193 (DOI 10.1007/s11403-017-0193-4): a kriging meta-model of their industry model (GLS trend, ML correlation function; Sobol' decomposition via R's DiceKriging, DiceOptim, DiceEval; nearly orthogonal Latin hypercubes) shows the fat-tailed growth distribution holds across the parameter space. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11403-017-0193-4); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ssa/lemwps/2016-12.html)
- [M] Other standard references and their statistics (unverified here): firm size Zipf law with exponent ≈ 1 for US firms (Axtell 2001, Science 293:1818–1820, DOI 10.1126/science.1062081); Laplace growth rates (Stanley et al. 1996, Nature 379:804–806, DOI 10.1038/379804a0); fat-tailed GDP growth across OECD countries, Subbotin b well below 2 (Fagiolo, Napoletano and Roventini 2008, J. Applied Econometrics 23(5):639–669, DOI 10.1002/jae.1003); productivity dispersion within narrow industries, with a 90th/10th percentile TFP ratio of about 2 in US manufacturing (Syverson 2011, JEL); lumpy investment as spikes above about 20% of capital (Doms and Dunne 1998; Cooper and Haltiwanger 2006); industry life cycle with a shakeout in the number of producers (Gort and Klepper 1982; Klepper 1996 AER).
- [V] The stylised-fact references K+S itself cites for micro facts: Bottazzi and Secchi (2003, 2006) for size and growth, Bartelsman and Doms (2000) and Dosi (2007) for productivity heterogeneity and persistence, Doms and Dunne (1998) for lumpy investment, Jaimovich and Floetotto (2008) for counter-cyclical bankruptcies, Di Guilmi et al. (2004) for power-law bad debt; Stock and Watson (1999) and Napoletano et al. (2006) for business cycles; Wright (2005) and Ausloos et al. (2004) for exponential recession durations; Reinhart and Rogoff (2009) and Laeven and Valencia (2008) for banking crises. — [OFCE WP 2014-19](https://www.ofce.fr/pdf/dtravail/WP2014-19.pdf)
- [S] Guerini and Moneta 2017 argue that matching statistical dependencies is weak because several causal structures generate the same dependencies, and propose comparing causal structures of VARs estimated on artificial and real data (Section 4). — [INET WP 42](https://www.ineteconomics.org/uploads/papers/WP_42-Moneta.pdf)

### Inferences
- Testable forms for a library of checks: (a) Subbotin fit of pooled growth rates with b and a confidence interval, with "fat tails" as b < 2 and "Laplace-like" as b ≈ 1 (the Lamperti threshold b ≤ 1 is the strictest found); (b) right skew and departure from log-normality of log sizes (skewness, a KS or Anderson–Darling test against the fitted log-normal, a Zipf/Pareto tail exponent by Hill or CSN's MLE); (c) σ ratios and cross-correlation signs on Baxter–King (6, 32, 12) or HP-filtered series, each with Monte Carlo intervals; (d) persistence as rank correlation of firm productivity across lags or a transition matrix between quantiles; (e) lumpiness as the share of firm-years with investment spikes; (f) recession durations against an exponential law (likelihood ratio against, e.g., a power law).
- Since thresholds are not standardised, the checks should report the statistic with its interval and leave the verdict to a declared rule, which suits the infrastructure's existing practice of declared schemes.

### Gaps
- No source read gives formal thresholds for Okun's coefficient, Phillips or Beveridge slopes in an evolutionary ABM; those claims are [M].
- No source read gives quantitative acceptance bands for entry/exit rates or for industry life cycles in the macro ABMs.

## 4. Calibration, Estimation, Sensitivity and Ergodicity

### Takeaway
The toolbox is: simulated minimum distance (MSM/indirect inference), Bayesian estimation with simulated likelihoods (kernel density, ABC; neural posterior estimation more recently), information-criterion distances (GSL-div, MIC), surrogate-assisted exploration (XGBoost active learning; kriging with Sobol' indices), causal-structure validation (SVAR plus causal search), and statistical model checking for warm-up, ergodicity and run counts; benchmarks favour likelihood-based Bayesian methods on small models, and all authors concede that large evolutionary macro ABMs like K+S are not yet estimated in full.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Fagiolo, Guerini, Lamperti, Moneta and Roventini 2019, "Validation of agent-based models in economics and finance", in *Computer Simulation Validation* (Springer), pp. 763–787, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-70766-2_31: frames validation in three dimensions, (i) comparison of artificial and real data, (ii) calibration and estimation, (iii) parameter-space exploration, and calls for more work on hypothesis testing, especially stationarity and ergodicity. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/spmain/halshs-02375423.html)
- [V] Lamperti, Roventini and Sani 2018, JEDC 90:366–389, DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2018.03.011: an XGBoost surrogate (hyperparameters tuned automatically; chosen over kriging because it needs no kernel or prior choice; logit as a robustness comparison) trained by active learning on a Sobol'-sampled pool (e.g. 35 initial labels from a pool of 1,000,000 with a 500-evaluation budget), each round adding C·log(budget) points from predicted positives (entropy sampling when none); budgets 250–2,500. Results: Brock–Hommes (12 parameters) binary F1 about 0.80 at 2,500 points, real-valued TPR about 0.95 at 2,500; Islands (8 parameters) TPR about 0.9, F1 about 0.8; precision 94.7% (XGBoost), 99.35% with Platt scaling; prediction about 500× (B&H) and 3,750× (Islands) faster than simulation. Limitations stated: positives (< 1% of the space) must be re-checked on the true model; few parameters; large-scale application "under development"; fixed seeds in main experiments. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.10639)
- [S] Platt 2020, "A comparison of economic agent-based model calibration methods", JEDC 113:103859, DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2020.103859: a simple likelihood-based Bayesian estimator consistently outperforms several popular simulated minimum distance methods; performance degrades as models grow and more parameters are fitted; calibration "requires further development" before large-scale models can be definitively calibrated. — [arXiv 1902.05938](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05938) [V for abstract]; [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/dyncon/v113y2020ics0165188920300294.html)
- [M] Platt's test cases and competitors (unverified): Brock–Hommes and Franke–Westerhoff models; MSM (Franke and Westerhoff 2012 style), GSL-div (Lamperti 2018), MIC (Barde 2017) against KDE-likelihood Bayesian estimation (Grazzini et al. 2017).
- [S] Grazzini, Richiardi and Tsionas 2017, "Bayesian estimation of agent-based models", JEDC 77:26–47, DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2017.01.014: non-parametric likelihood by kernel densities with MCMC; parametric likelihood approximations; ABC to embed SMD in a Bayesian frame; tested on a one-parameter price-discovery model and on De Grauwe's (2012) behavioural macro model with nine parameters. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:dyncon:v:77:y:2017:i:c:p:26-47); [Torino AAM](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/1619141)
- [M] Further estimation methods (unverified): Franke and Westerhoff 2012 (JEDC, MSM on asset-pricing ABMs); Lamperti 2018, GSL-div information-theoretic distance between symbolised time series (Econometrics and Statistics, DOI 10.1016/j.ecosta.2017.01.006); Barde 2017, Markov information criterion; Barde and van der Hoog 2017, an empirical validation protocol applying MIC with kriging to Eurace@Unibi; Delli Gatti and Grazzini 2020 (JEBO, DOI 10.1016/j.jebo.2020.10.021), Bayesian estimation and forecasting of a medium-scale macro ABM; Dyer, Cannon, Farmer and Wolf 2024 (JEDC, DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2024.104827), neural posterior estimation ("black-box Bayesian inference") for ABMs.
- [S] Guerini and Moneta 2017, "A method for agent-based models validation", JEDC 82:125–141 (DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2017.06.001 [M]): estimate SVARs on artificial and on real data, identify each by causal search (graphical-model algorithms), and compare the causal structures; applied to the Dosi et al. (2015) K+S macro model. Establishes agreement of contemporaneous causal orderings among aggregates, not of micro mechanisms. — [LEM WP 2016/16 record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ssa:lemwps:2016/16); [INET WP 42](https://www.ineteconomics.org/uploads/papers/WP_42-Moneta.pdf)
- [S] Global sensitivity by kriging: Dosi, Pereira and Virgillito 2018 (Section 3) fit a kriging meta-model on a near-orthogonal Latin hypercube and compute Sobol' indices with DiceKriging/DiceOptim/DiceEval. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ssa/lemwps/2016-12.html)
- [S] Vandin, Giachini, Lamperti and Chiaromonte, "Automated and distributed statistical analysis of economic agent-based models" (arXiv 2102.05405, v2 Nov 2023; related DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2022.104458 listed on the arXiv page): model-independent techniques in the statistical model checker MultiVeStA that choose the number of runs and steps for a stated confidence, separate transient from steady state, estimate warm-up, compare configurations with statistical tests and give indications of (non-)ergodicity; applied to a large-scale macro-financial ABM and a small prediction-market model, identifying and fixing "some erroneous conclusions" of earlier analyses. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05405) [V for abstract]; [MultiVeStA](https://sysma.imtlucca.it/tools/tool_multivesta/)
- [M] Ergodicity tests: Grazzini 2012, "Analysis of the emergent properties: stationarity and ergodicity", JASSS 15(2):7, runs and KS-type tests across and within runs; the K+S authors' own footnote treats path dependence as an open issue (Section 1).
- [M] Earlier methodological frame: Fagiolo, Moneta and Windrum 2007, "A critical guide to empirical validation of agent-based models in economics", Computational Economics 30:195–226 (indirect calibration, Werker–Brenner, history-friendly approaches as three validation strategies).

### Inferences
- What each method establishes: SMD and Bayesian estimators give parameter values (with intervals for the Bayesian ones) conditional on the chosen moments or likelihood approximation, and only for models of tens of parameters; surrogates locate regions where declared criteria hold, not a best point; GSA says which parameters move which statistic, not whether the model is right; causal-structure matching tests aggregate contemporaneous orderings; statistical model checking certifies the Monte Carlo estimates themselves (enough runs, past warm-up, ergodic or not). None validates micro behavioural rules directly.
- For a K+S-scale model (dozens of parameters, 600 periods, 250 firms, 100 runs per point), the realistic route is: fix the warm-up and run count by sequential statistical tests; screen parameters by Morris; fit a surrogate on a Latin hypercube; compute Sobol' indices on the surrogate; then estimate a reduced set of parameters by MSM or simulated-likelihood Bayes on chosen moments.

### Gaps
- No source read reports a full estimation of K+S parameters on data; I found none.
- Platt's exact competitors and models were not read at source.

## 5. Software, Reproducibility and Reference Implementations

### Takeaway
K+S and the Ciarli–Lorentz line are written in LSD (C++, GPL, by Valente and Pereira); Eurace@Unibi in FLAME (C/XML) with code in Bielefeld's repository and on GitHub; a K+S variant was re-interpreted in Java on JAS-mine; Python (Mesa, ABCE) is used for smaller models and one Nelson–Winter reimplementation. Code is public for the main families, but no source found ships seeded reference outputs that a reimplementation could be checked against.

## 6. Policy Experiments and Their Robustness

### Takeaway
K+S experiments consistently report complementarity between "Schumpeterian" (innovation, opportunity, entry) and "Keynesian" (demand, stabilisers) policies, austerity and labour flexibility as harmful, and active (mission-oriented) state innovation policy as best; robustness is assessed by Monte Carlo t-tests at fixed parameters, occasionally by GSA, and almost never by estimation-based uncertainty or by an independent reimplementation.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Technology experiments (100 runs, values normalised to baseline, simulation t-statistics): low search capabilities (ζ = 0.1) give GDP growth 0.917, high (0.5) 1.063; low technological opportunities (Beta(2.7, 3.3)) 0.774, high (Beta(3.3, 2.7)) 1.250; faster growth goes with lower unemployment. Industrial experiments: longer patents give growth 0.960, broader patents 0.647 and unemployment 1.240; low entrant productivity 0.726, high 1.492; strong antitrust (market share capped at 50%) growth 1.083, volatility 0.628, unemployment 0.781. — [OFCE WP 2014-19](https://www.ofce.fr/pdf/dtravail/WP2014-19.pdf)
- [V] Experiments listed: fiscal stabilisers (tax and benefit ranges), fiscal rules (SGP deficit cap 3%, with an escape clause when growth is negative), income distribution (mark-up range 0.10–0.40), monetary policy (interest-rate range), from Dosi et al. 2010, 2013 and 2014; conclusion: "strong complementarities between Schumpeterian (technological) and Keynesian (demand-related) policies", increasing inequality harmful in short and long run, supporting redistributive fiscal policy. The authors add that most structural regularities hold "across an ample parameter range, under positive technological progress". — [OFCE WP 2014-19](https://www.ofce.fr/pdf/dtravail/WP2014-19.pdf)
- [S] Labour: more rigid labour relations (Fordist regime) yield higher and smoother growth than flexible ones (Dosi et al. 2017). — [OFCE blog](https://www.ofce.sciences-po.fr/blog/?p=7589)
- [S] Innovation policy: all policies tested (R&D subsidies, investment tax discounts, public research-oriented firms, a public lab for radical innovation) raise productivity and growth; active discretionary state policies do best, crowd in private investment and leave positive hysteresis; mission policies outperform "if the government is patient enough and willing to bear the intrinsic risks". — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10175810/)
- [S] Eurace@Unibi applications cover labour-market design, banking regulation, growth and income inequality. — [Bielefeld PUB 2622498](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2622498)
- [M] Climate (DSK): damages at the micro level amplify crises and raise the public costs of climate-induced bank failures (Lamperti et al. 2019 Nature Climate Change); combined carbon pricing and green subsidies, and green macro-prudential policies (Lamperti et al. 2021 J. Financial Stability), are the main experiments. Unverified here.
- [M] Robustness commentary: Fagiolo and Roventini 2017 (JASSS 20(1):1, "Macroeconomic policy in DSGE and agent-based models redux") argue ABMs are better policy laboratories; Haldane and Turrell 2019 (JEE, "Drawing on different disciplines: macroeconomic agent-based models") note limited calibration and the difficulty of separating mechanism from parameter choice. Unverified here.
- [S] Methodological caution relevant to policy comparisons: Vandin et al. show that analyses with a fixed number of runs and unexamined warm-up can reach conclusions that a statistically controlled analysis corrects. — [arXiv 2102.05405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05405)

### Inferences
- Policy results in this literature are comparative statics across Monte Carlo ensembles at one calibrated point, with sign robustness sometimes checked over a grid; claims that a policy "works" therefore inherit the unverified calibration. A sub-domain should report a policy effect with its run-count-controlled interval and over a sampled parameter region (via the surrogate), not at one point.
- Independent replication of a headline K+S policy result (e.g. the antitrust or austerity result) in a second code base would itself be a contribution; none was found.

### Gaps
- No published independent replication of a K+S policy result was found.
- The 2024–2026 literature on robustness of these policy findings (beyond the 2023 mission paper and the 2025 Elements) was not surveyed in depth.

## 7. What an Implementation Would Need

### Takeaway
A minimal K+S-original reimplementation needs about a dozen decision rules over two firm populations, a wage rule and a government, all on balance sheets the ledger already keeps; checks should be the 17 stylised facts expressed as statistics with Monte Carlo intervals plus the published policy signs; the general methods worth building once are a Subbotin/Laplace and tail-exponent fitter, bandpass filters and cross-correlation tables, a statistical model checker for warm-up and run counts, Morris and Sobol' over a kriging or boosted-tree surrogate, MSM and simulated-likelihood Bayesian estimation, and causal-structure comparison of SVARs.

### Cited Findings
- [V] The rules and their equations to reproduce: Bernoulli–Beta innovation and distance-weighted imitation on (A, B); brochures to a sampled set of clients; supplier choice on price plus unit cost; adaptive expectations, inventories, expansion and payback replacement over vintages; debt-to-sales cap on credit; quasi-replicator shares on E = −ω1 p − ω2 l; share-driven mark-up; exit at zero share or negative net worth with replacement by smaller entrants; institutional wage rule; unemployment benefit proportional to the wage; the six-step timing; F1 = 50, F2 = 200, ν = 0.04, ξ = 0.5, Beta(3, 3) on [−0.15, 0.15], ζ = 0.3, 100 runs. Banks (Pareto clients, capital-requirement credit supply with a fragility buffer), Taylor rule, bail-outs and bonds are the second stage. — [OFCE WP 2014-19](https://www.ofce.fr/pdf/dtravail/WP2014-19.pdf)
- [V] Reference code to compare against: SantAnnaKS/LSD, `8.0-stable/Example/SantAnna/K+S-original`; the Islands model in the same repository as a smaller first target, already used with explicit criteria (growth > 2%, Subbotin b ≤ 1). — [Sant'Anna Models](https://www.ews.santannapisa.it/); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.10639)
- [S] Validation methods with reference papers to hold implementations to: surrogate calibration (Lamperti et al. 2018), kriging GSA with Sobol' (Dosi, Pereira and Virgillito 2018), Bayesian KDE likelihood (Grazzini et al. 2017), benchmark comparison (Platt 2020), causal SVAR comparison (Guerini and Moneta 2017), statistical model checking (Vandin et al. 2022). — sources as in Section 4.

### Inferences
- **Minimal model structure (K+S-original as the canonical target).**
  - Agents: capital-good firms (technology (A_i, B_i), price, R&D budget, clients); consumption-good firms (vintage list, expectations, inventories, net worth, debt, share, mark-up, supplier); a household sector of L^S workers (or worker agents for the labour variant); a government; later banks and a central bank.
  - Routines as declared, swappable operators: search (Bernoulli success in R&D, Beta step on technology, imitation kernel by distance), investment (expansion; payback replacement), pricing (fixed mark-up; share-driven mark-up), selection (quasi-replicator with χ; exit thresholds; entry draws), finance (pecking order, debt-to-sales cap), wage rule.
  - Markets: a machine market with brochures and supplier switching (orders delivered next period); a consumption-good market cleared by shares with unfilled demand; a labour market (aggregate rationing in the base model; matching in the labour variant); credit by ranking.
  - Timing: the six-step sequence, with machine deliveries lagged one period.
  - Accounts: every payment (wages, machine sales, consumption, taxes, benefits, R&D, interest, loans, bail-outs) as a posting on the existing ledger, so that GDP = C + I + ΔN and the stock–flow identities hold by construction and can be checked each period.
  - Variation and selection operators live naturally in the evolutionary-computation library (Beta mutation on a two-dimensional genotype; replicator as a selection scheme; imitation as crossover by distance), and agents and scheduling in the existing agent framework.
- **Stylised-fact tests as checks** (each with across-run intervals and a declared rule, never a hidden threshold):
  - growth: positive mean growth of log GDP and of productivity; non-stationarity of log GDP (unit-root test);
  - business cycles: Baxter–King (6, 32, 12) filtered σ(C)/σ(Y) < 1 < σ(I)/σ(Y); signs and leads/lags of cross-correlations with GDP (consumption, investment, inventories, productivity, wages, inflation, unemployment, mark-ups, R&D);
  - GDP growth: Subbotin shape b with interval, b < 2; recession durations against an exponential law;
  - firm sizes: skewness of log size, rejection of log-normality, Pareto/Zipf tail exponent by maximum likelihood;
  - firm growth: Subbotin/Laplace fit of pooled normalised growth rates, b ≤ 1 under the strict reading;
  - productivity: cross-firm dispersion (90/10 ratio) and its persistence (rank autocorrelation, quantile transition matrices);
  - investment: share of firm-years with spikes above a declared rate, and the share of aggregate investment they carry;
  - firm demography: counter-cyclical bankruptcies (correlation with filtered GDP); with banks, power-law bad debt and credit cross-correlations;
  - policy signs: lower opportunities lower growth; broader patents lower growth; stronger antitrust lowers volatility (the 2014 WP's table as qualitative targets).
- **General methods worth building in the foundations (not in the sub-domain).** In tyche: Subbotin/AEP fitting, tail-exponent estimation, Baxter–King and HP filters, cross-correlation tables with Monte Carlo bands, unit-root tests (tyche already has filters, unit roots and Sobol'/Morris per CLAUDE.md), KDE likelihoods with MCMC (tyche's `bayes`), MSM with optimal weighting, SVAR identification plus a causal-search algorithm (PC/LiNGAM) for Guerini–Moneta comparison; in heuresis: surrogate screening (XGBoost-like boosted trees, kriging) with active learning and conformal coverage (heuresis already has surrogates with conformal intervals); a statistical model checker (sequential choice of run counts, warm-up detection, ergodicity diagnostics across and within runs) as a general tool, perhaps in kinesis or tyche.
- **Data consumed.** For macro facts: national accounts at quarterly frequency (GDP, consumption, investment, inventories, prices, wages, unemployment, R&D), e.g. US NIPA/FRED or OECD; for micro facts: firm-level panels of sales, employment, productivity and investment (Compustat, ORBIS, national business registers; Eurostat's structural business statistics and business demography for entry/exit); for crises: Laeven–Valencia banking-crisis database; for climate variants: emissions, energy mix and temperature series; for HFM-style validation: dated histories of entry, concentration and technology in one industry.
- **Order of building.** Islands model first (8 parameters, published criteria and surrogate results to compare); then K+S-original without banks, checked against SantAnnaKS/LSD seeded runs and the 2014 WP's policy signs; then banks and policy; then the labour or energy variant.

### Gaps
- No machine-readable expected outputs of K+S exist in any source read; they would have to be generated by running the LSD code.
- The exact baseline parameter file of K+S-original, the labour-matching protocol and DSK's climate equations need to be read from code or full papers before implementation.
