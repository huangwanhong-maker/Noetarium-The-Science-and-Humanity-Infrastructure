## 1. Evolutionary Economic Geography: Branching, Variety, Path Dependence and Resilience

### Takeaway
Evolutionary economic geography (EEG) has one canonical formal model, Frenken and Boschma's (2007) branching process, a Simon-type urn in which product innovations open growth opportunities for existing or new firms in existing or new cities, so that firm-size and city-size (Zipf) distributions emerge from one process; the rest of the field (related/unrelated variety, path dependence, resilience) is mainly measurement plus conceptual frameworks, with formal content borrowed from entropy decomposition (Theil), Pólya/Arthur urns, Klepper's spinoff model, NK landscapes and shift-share.

### Cited Findings

**Founding programme**
- Boschma and Frenken (2006), "Why is economic geography not an evolutionary science? Towards an evolutionary economic geography", J. Econ. Geogr. 6(3):273–302, set EEG against neoclassical and institutional economic geography: the unit is the firm and its routines, and regional development is explained by variety, selection and retention of routines in space. [M] [doi:10.1093/jeg/lbi022](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeg/lbi022)
- Boschma and Frenken (2011), "The emerging empirics of evolutionary economic geography", J. Econ. Geogr. 11(2):295–307, survey the empirical turn: spinoffs, labour mobility, networks and relatedness as the vehicles of routine inheritance. [M] [doi:10.1093/jeg/lbq053](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeg/lbq053)
- Boschma (2005), "Proximity and innovation: a critical assessment", Regional Studies 39(1):61–74: five proximities (cognitive, organisational, social, institutional, geographical), with the "proximity paradox" that too much proximity causes lock-in. [M] [doi:10.1080/0034340052000320887](https://doi.org/10.1080/0034340052000320887)

**The branching model (Frenken and Boschma 2007)**
- Published as Boschma and Frenken / Frenken and Boschma (2007), "A theoretical framework for evolutionary economic geography: industrial dynamics and urban growth as a branching process", J. Econ. Geogr. 7(5):635–649; working paper PEEG 0701 (Utrecht). [S] [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jecgeo/v7y2007i5p635-649.html); DOI [M] [doi:10.1093/jeg/lbm018](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeg/lbm018)
- Abstract-level content: economic development is an evolutionary branching process of product innovations; each product innovation provides a growth opportunity for an existing firm or a new firm, and for an existing city or a new city; firm-size and city-size distributions are two aggregates of a single process; gains from variety at firm level (economies of scope) and urban level (Jacobs externalities) are the central feedback producing path dependence; gains from size are bounded by rising wages. Zipf is among the paper's keywords. [S] [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jecgeo/v7y2007i5p635-649.html), [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/2955061)
- A 2009 Frenken chapter states that Simon's model of the rank-size rule "is still regarded as one of the canonical models of urban size distribution", which is the lineage the branching model extends. [S] [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-01554-0_10)
- Mechanism as I recall it (full text not read): with a small probability a new product appears in a new firm in a new city; otherwise a new product is assigned to an existing firm/city with probability proportional to its current number of products (diversification through routine replication, spinoffs and labour mobility), which is Simon's process at two levels and yields Zipf's law in the limit of a small entry probability. [M] (equations unverified; see Gaps)

**Related and unrelated variety (Frenken, Van Oort and Verburg 2007)**
- "Related variety, unrelated variety and regional economic growth", Regional Studies 41(5):685–697. [M] [doi:10.1080/00343400601120296](https://doi.org/10.1080/00343400601120296)
- Measure (Theil's entropy decomposition; standard formulation, [M]): with p_i the employment share of fine industry i (5-digit), grouped into coarse sectors S_g (2-digit) with P_g = Σ_{i∈S_g} p_i:
  - total entropy H = Σ_i p_i log₂(1/p_i);
  - unrelated variety UV = Σ_g P_g log₂(1/P_g) (entropy at the 2-digit level);
  - related variety RV = Σ_g P_g H_g, with H_g = Σ_{i∈S_g} (p_i/P_g) log₂(P_g/p_i);
  - identity H = UV + RV (chain rule of entropy), exact for any data. [M] [doi:10.1080/00343400601120296](https://doi.org/10.1080/00343400601120296)
- Findings for Dutch regions (40 COROP regions, about 1996–2002, as I recall): related variety raises employment growth (Jacobs externalities among related sectors); unrelated variety dampens growth of unemployment (a portfolio effect against sector-specific shocks); productivity growth is not explained by variety. [M] [doi:10.1080/00343400601120296](https://doi.org/10.1080/00343400601120296)
- Nomaler and Verspagen (2023) find unrelated variety significant alongside related variety in regressions predicting gains or losses of specialisation, the influence of related variety growing with a country's level of diversification ("only countries that are already diversified show a strong influence of related variety"). [V] [arXiv:2303.14458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14458)

**Path dependence and lock-in**
- Arthur (1989), "Competing technologies, increasing returns, and lock-in by historical events", Economic Journal 99(394):116–131: adopters arrive sequentially; with increasing returns, the market share converges almost surely to 0 or 1, which one being decided by early random arrivals (generalised Pólya urns, Arthur, Ermoliev and Kaniovski 1987); with constant returns shares are predictable; with diminishing returns they converge to an interior point. [M] [doi:10.2307/2234208](https://doi.org/10.2307/2234208)
- David (1985), "Clio and the economics of QWERTY", AER 75(2):332–337: technical interrelatedness, scale economies and quasi-irreversibility of investment as the conditions of lock-in. [M] (no DOI; JSTOR 1805621)
- Martin and Sunley (2006), "Path dependence and regional economic evolution", J. Econ. Geogr. 6(4):395–437: critique of the "canonical" lock-in model for regions (place-dependence, the problem of explaining path creation and de-locking), proposing mechanisms of layering, conversion and recombination. [M] [doi:10.1093/jeg/lbl012](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeg/lbl012)
- Martin (2010), "Rethinking regional path dependence: beyond lock-in to evolution", Economic Geography 86(1):1–27: an alternative "path-as-process" model in which paths evolve by incremental change, without an equilibrium. [M] [doi:10.1111/j.1944-8287.2009.01056.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1944-8287.2009.01056.x)
- Klepper (2007), "Disagreements, spinoffs, and the evolution of Detroit as the capital of the U.S. automobile industry", Management Science 53(4):616–631: a heredity model in which spinoffs inherit their parents' capabilities and locate near them, generating clusters without agglomeration externalities. [M] [doi:10.1287/mnsc.1060.0683](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1060.0683)
- Frenken (2006), "Technological innovation and complexity theory", Econ. Innov. New Techn. 15(2):137–155: NK fitness landscapes as models of technological search, local optima as lock-in. [M] [doi:10.1080/10438590500141453](https://doi.org/10.1080/10438590500141453)
- Neffke, Henning and Boschma (2011), "How do regions diversify over time? Industry relatedness and the development of new growth paths in regions", Economic Geography 87(3):237–265: Swedish regions 1969–2002; industries technologically related (revealed by product co-occurrence in plants) to a region's portfolio are more likely to enter it and less likely to exit. [M] [doi:10.1111/j.1944-8287.2011.01121.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1944-8287.2011.01121.x)
- Neffke, Hartog, Boschma and Henning (2018), "Agents of structural change: the role of firms and entrepreneurs in regional diversification", Economic Geography 94(1):23–48: new establishments, especially those from outside the region, introduce more unrelated diversification than incumbents. [M] [doi:10.1080/00130095.2017.1391691](https://doi.org/10.1080/00130095.2017.1391691)

**Adaptive cycle and regional resilience**
- Simmie and Martin (2010), "The economic resilience of regions: towards an evolutionary approach", Cambridge J. Regions, Econ. Soc. 3(1):27–43: Holling's adaptive cycle (exploitation r, conservation K, release Ω, reorganisation α; panarchy of nested cycles) applied to Cambridge and Swansea. - Hassink (2010), "Regional resilience: a promising concept to explain differences in regional economic adaptability?", CJRES 3(1):45–58: critique of the equilibrium bias of the resilience notion. [M] [doi:10.1093/cjres/rsp033](https://doi.org/10.1093/cjres/rsp033)

### Inferences
- The only closed-form generative content in EEG proper is Simon-type: the branching model inherits Simon's exponents, so an implementation can test it against the Simon closed forms of §5 (a = 1 − α for ranks).
- RV/UV is a two-level case of a hierarchical Shannon decomposition; the infrastructure's tyche `classes` (Jost's partition) and nomos `measurement` (concentration, specialisation and their decompositions) are the natural homes, and the identity H = UV + RV is a free check.
- "Resilience" as used in EEG has no dynamical model; its indices are shift-share arithmetic. The infrastructure's kinesis `transitions` (detectors of critical transitions with powered verdicts) is a stronger reading of "ecological resilience" than anything in the EEG literature I found.

### Gaps
- The equations of Frenken and Boschma (2007) and their exact derivation of Zipf were not read (paywalled); the mechanism above is from memory.
- I did not verify the Dutch findings of Frenken, Van Oort and Verburg (2007) at the source, nor the sample years.
- No formal (equation-level) model of the adaptive cycle for regions was found; whether any EEG paper fits an adaptive-cycle dynamical system to regional data is unknown to me.

## 2. Relatedness and Diversification: Product Space, Density, Principle of Relatedness, Skill Relatedness

### Takeaway
Relatedness is measured from co-occurrence (products co-exported, industries co-located or co-produced in plants, patent classes co-assigned) or from flows (job switchers between industries); a region's "density" around an activity predicts entry and (inversely) exit at every scale studied, which Hidalgo et al. (2018) call the principle of relatedness; out-of-sample work (Li and Neffke 2024; machine-learning entry prediction from the Pietronero group) shows relatedness predicts, with a trade-off between predictive skill and interpretability, while unrelated diversification is rare but real.

### Cited Findings

**Product space (Hidalgo, Klinger, Barabási and Hausmann 2007)**
- "The product space conditions the development of nations", Science 317(5837):482–487. [M] [doi:10.1126/science.1144581](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1144581)
- Revealed comparative advantage (Balassa): RCA_cp = (x_cp / Σ_p x_cp) / (Σ_c x_cp / Σ_c Σ_p x_cp); M_cp = 1 if RCA_cp > 1 (Mealy et al.'s statement of the convention). [V] [ar5iv 1711.08245](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.08245)
- Proximity φ_ij = min{P(RCA_i ≥ 1 | RCA_j ≥ 1), P(RCA_j ≥ 1 | RCA_i ≥ 1)}, the minimum of the two conditional probabilities making it symmetric; about 775 SITC-4 products; a core-periphery network (machinery, chemicals in the core; primary goods at the periphery); drawn as a maximum spanning tree plus links φ ≥ 0.55; countries move to products close to their current specialisations. [M] [doi:10.1126/science.1144581](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1144581)
- Density of product j around country c: ω_cj = Σ_i M_ci φ_ij / Σ_i φ_ij; distance d_cj = 1 − ω_cj. [M] [doi:10.1126/science.1144581](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1144581)
- Derived Atlas measures (Hausmann, Hidalgo et al., *The Atlas of Economic Complexity*, MIT Press 2014): complexity outlook index COI_c = Σ_p (1 − d_cp)(1 − M_cp) PCI_p; complexity outlook gain COG_cp = Σ_{p'} [φ_{pp'} / Σ_{p''} φ_{p''p'}] (1 − M_cp') PCI_p' − (1 − d_cp) PCI_p. [M] (book ISBN 9780262525428)
- O'Clery, Yıldırım and Hausmann (2021), "Productive ecosystems and the arrow of development", Nature Communications 12:1479: a directed (asymmetric) product network giving a direction of development. [M] [doi:10.1038/s41467-021-21689-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21689-0)

**Principle of relatedness**
- Hidalgo, Balland, Boschma, Delgado, Feldman, Frenken, Glaeser, He, Kogler, Morrison, Neffke, Rigby, Stern, Zheng and Zhu (2018), "The principle of relatedness", in *Unifying Themes in Complex Systems IX* (ICCS 2018), Springer Proceedings in Complexity, 451–457: the probability that a region enters an activity increases with the number of related activities present, and the probability of exit decreases, across countries, regions and cities, and across products, industries, technologies, occupations and research areas. [M] [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-96661-8_46](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-96661-8_46)
- Boschma, Balland and Kogler (2015), "Relatedness and technological change in cities: the rise and fall of technological knowledge in US metropolitan areas from 1981 to 2010", Industrial and Corporate Change 24(1):223–250: technological relatedness from patent co-classification; relatedness density RD_{i,c,t} = 100 × Σ_{j∈c, j≠i} φ_ij / Σ_{j≠i} φ_ij; entry of a technology into a metro rises with RD in linear-probability regressions with fixed effects. [M] [doi:10.1093/icc/dtu012](https://doi.org/10.1093/icc/dtu012)
- Rigby (2015), "Technological relatedness and knowledge space: entry and exit of US cities from patent classes", Regional Studies 49(11):1922–1937. [M] [doi:10.1080/00343404.2013.854878](https://doi.org/10.1080/00343404.2013.854878)
- Kogler, Rigby and Tucker (2013), "Mapping knowledge space and technological relatedness in US cities", European Planning Studies 21(9):1374–1391. [M] [doi:10.1080/09654313.2012.755832](https://doi.org/10.1080/09654313.2012.755832)
- Boschma (2017), "Relatedness as driver of regional diversification: a research agenda", Regional Studies 51(3):351–364. [M] [doi:10.1080/00343404.2016.1254767](https://doi.org/10.1080/00343404.2016.1254767)
- Bahar, Hausmann and Hidalgo (2014), "Neighbors and the evolution of the comparative advantage of nations", J. Int. Econ. 92(1):111–123: a country is more likely to gain RCA in a product its geographic neighbours already export. [M] [doi:10.1016/j.jinteco.2013.11.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2013.11.001)
- An EC report on 292 NUTS-2 regions finds inter-regional linkages raise the probability of diversifying beyond the effect of regional capabilities (published as Balland and Boschma 2021, Regional Studies 55(6):1059–1070, [M] [doi:10.1080/00343404.2020.1861240](https://doi.org/10.1080/00343404.2020.1861240)). [S] [EC brochure](https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/sources/brochure/impact_ir_linkages_en.pdf)

**Skill relatedness**
- Neffke and Henning (2013), "Skill relatedness and firm diversification", Strategic Management Journal 34(3):297–316: relatedness from labour flows among Swedish industries, SR_ij = F_ij / (F_i· F_·j / F_··) (observed over expected job switches), often mapped to (−1, 1) by (SR − 1)/(SR + 1); firms diversify into skill-related industries. [S] [Annals of Regional Science, doi:10.1007/s00168-025-01375-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00168-025-01375-5)

**Out-of-sample tests and their critique**
- Li and Neffke (2022 preprint; Research Policy 2024), "Evaluating the principle of relatedness: Estimation, drivers and implications for policy": relatedness matrices derived from co-occurrence in the portfolios of establishments, firms, cities and countries, each giving a different matrix; a structured search over tens of thousands of specifications judged by out-of-sample prediction; every matrix helps predict size and growth of local industries in US cities; a trade-off between predictive performance and interpretability; the authors argue for expanding policy frameworks that use relatedness analysis. [V abstract] [arXiv:2205.02942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02942); journal DOI [S] [doi:10.1016/j.respol.2024.104952](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respol.2024.104952)

[M] [doi:10.1016/j.chaos.2023.114071](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chaos.2023.114071)
- Albora, Pietronero, Tacchella and Zaccaria (2023), "Product progression: a machine learning approach to forecasting industrial upgrading", Scientific Reports 13:1481. [M] [doi:10.1038/s41598-023-28179-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-28179-x)

**Unrelated diversification**
- Pinheiro, Hartmann, Boschma and Hidalgo (2022), "The time and frequency of unrelated diversification", Research Policy 51(8):104323: unrelated diversification is rare and more frequent at intermediate levels of development. [M] [doi:10.1016/j.respol.2021.104323](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respol.2021.104323)
- Nomaler and Verspagen (2023): specialisations lost often had higher product complexity than those gained, so smart specialisation should aim to preserve some existing specialisations as well as gain new ones; unrelated variety also matters for density-derived indicators such as the complexity outlook index. [V] [arXiv:2303.14458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14458)

**A generative model behind relatedness and nestedness**
- Hausmann and Hidalgo (2011), "The network structure of economic output", J. Econ. Growth 16(4):309–342: a tripartite model in which countries hold capabilities and products require them; a country makes a product exactly when it holds every capability the product requires. With N_a capabilities, each held by country c with probability r_c and required by a product with probability q, the probability that c makes a random product is (1 − q(1 − r_c))^{N_a}, so expected diversity is N_p (1 − q(1 − r_c))^{N_a}; the model reproduces the negative diversity–ubiquity relation and nestedness, and convex returns to accumulating capabilities ("quiescence trap"). [M] [doi:10.1007/s10887-011-9071-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10887-011-9071-4)
- Alshamsi, Pinheiro and Hidalgo (2018), "Optimal diversification strategies in the networks of related products and of related research areas", Nature Communications 9:1328: diversification modelled as relatedness-dependent activation on a network; the optimal targeting strategy depends on the strength of relatedness and on network structure. [M] [doi:10.1038/s41467-018-03740-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03740-9)
- Jara-Figueroa, Jun, Glaeser and Hidalgo (2018), "The role of industry-specific, occupation-specific, and location-specific knowledge in the growth and survival of new firms", PNAS 115(50):12646–12653: Brazilian pioneer firms grow and survive more when their hires bring industry-specific knowledge. [M] [doi:10.1073/pnas.1800475115](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1800475115)

### Inferences
- All co-occurrence relatedness measures are outcome-based: the same data that define φ also define M, so prediction tests must hold out time (fit φ on years ≤ t, predict entry in t+Δ) or space (fit on other regions), as Li and Neffke do. An implementation should make the hold-out a property of the estimator, like tyche's blocked cross-validation.
- Proximity, density, Neffke–Henning's ratio and association-strength normalisations are all functions of a bipartite incidence matrix; they name no object in nature and belong in a foundation (tyche `networks` holds bipartite network statistics already), with the domain holding regions, industries and their classifications.
- The capability model gives closed forms for expected diversity and ubiquity, making it a test generator for ECI/FC code (simulate M from known capabilities, check recovery of the ordering).

### Gaps
- Typical effect sizes of density on entry (marginal probability per unit density) were not verified.
- The exact predictive margin of machine-learning entry prediction over density (AUC or precision figures) was not verified.
- The numbers in Pinheiro et al. (2022) on the share of unrelated entries were not verified.

## 3. Economic Complexity: Method of Reflections, ECI, Fitness–Complexity, Critiques, Prediction and City Scale

### Takeaway
ECI is the eigenvector of the second-largest eigenvalue of the row-stochastic matrix M̃ = D⁻¹ M U⁻¹ Mᵀ, and Mealy, Farmer and Teytelboym show it is exactly the relaxed normalised-cut solution of spectral clustering on the similarity graph S = M U⁻¹ Mᵀ, orthogonal to diversity by construction; Fitness–Complexity is a nonlinear fixed point (harmonic weighting by the least-fit producers) with convergence pathologies that a stable inhomogeneous map repairs. Both have been taken to regions and cities (UK local authorities, US states and metros, Chinese provinces), where complex activities concentrate super-linearly in large cities.

### Cited Findings

**Method of reflections and ECI**
- Hidalgo and Hausmann (2009), "The building blocks of economic complexity", PNAS 106(26):10570–10575: k_{c,0} = Σ_p M_cp (diversity), k_{p,0} = Σ_c M_cp (ubiquity); k_{c,N} = (1/k_{c,0}) Σ_p M_cp k_{p,N−1}, k_{p,N} = (1/k_{p,0}) Σ_c M_cp k_{c,N−1}; the iteration converges to a trivial constant, the information lying in the rankings at intermediate N; complexity predicts growth beyond income. [M] [doi:10.1073/pnas.0900943106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0900943106)
- Eigenvector formulation (as stated by Mealy et al.): D, U diagonal matrices of diversity and ubiquity; M̃ = D⁻¹ M U⁻¹ Mᵀ, entries M̃_cc' = (1/k_c) Σ_p M_cp M_c'p / k_p, row-stochastic (a Markov transition matrix); ECI is the eigenvector of M̃ for the second-largest right eigenvalue; PCI is the second eigenvector of M̂ = U⁻¹ Mᵀ D⁻¹ M, which shares M̃'s eigenvalues. [V] [ar5iv 1711.08245](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.08245)
- Proposition 1 (Mealy et al.): a country's ECI equals the average PCI of the products in which it has RCA > 1; ECI is obtained from PCI by ỹ = D⁻¹ M ŷ. [V] [ar5iv 1711.08245](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.08245)
- The Atlas standardises: ECI = (K − ⟨K⟩)/stdev(K); Mealy et al. use the unstandardised vector throughout. [M for the Atlas formula; V that Mealy use unstandardised] [ar5iv 1711.08245](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.08245)

**ECI as spectral clustering (Mealy, Farmer and Teytelboym 2019)**
- Published as "Interpreting economic complexity", Science Advances 5(1):eaau1705 (2019). [M for journal details] [doi:10.1126/sciadv.aau1705](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau1705); preprint read [V] [arXiv:1711.08245](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08245)
- Similarity matrix S = M U⁻¹ Mᵀ, so M̃ = D⁻¹ S; each row sum of S equals the country's diversity. cut(A, Ā) = Σ_{i∈A, j∈Ā} S_ij; Ncut(A, Ā) = (1/vol(A) + 1/vol(Ā)) cut(A, Ā), vol(A) = Σ_{i∈A} d_i. Relaxing the NP-hard Ncut gives (D − S)y = λDy; with the normalised Laplacian L̄_S = D^(−1/2)(D − S)D^(−1/2), the second-smallest eigenvector z^[2] (normalised Fiedler vector) gives y^[2] = D^(−1/2) z^[2]; since λ̃ = 1 − λ, ECI equals y^[2]. [V] [ar5iv 1711.08245](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.08245)

## 4. Evolution of Technologies: Recombination, the Adjacent Possible, Patents, Heaps' and Zipf's Laws, and Urban Diversity

### Takeaway
Technologies evolve by recombination: Weitzman's recombinant growth makes combinatorial potential grow faster than exponentially while growth is limited by the capacity to process combinations; Tria et al.'s urn with triggering formalises the adjacent possible and yields Heaps' exponent ν/ρ and Zipf exponent ρ/ν in closed form; US patents (1790–2010) show codes saturating after about 1870 while combinations grow linearly with patents (ΔC = 0.6 ΔP) and no rich-get-richer in code use; across US metros business types grow logarithmically with population under a universal rank-abundance law produced by a Yule–Simon process whose innovation rate falls as x₀/N.

### Cited Findings

[M] [doi:10.1162/003355398555595](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355398555595)

**The adjacent possible as an urn (Tria, Loreto, Servedio and Strogatz 2014)**
- "The dynamics of correlated novelties", Scientific Reports 4:5890 (2014); preprint arXiv:1310.1953. [V preprint; S for journal: the Nature article page exists] [arXiv:1310.1953](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1953), [doi:10.1038/srep05890](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep05890)
- Rules: an urn starts with N₀ distinct balls; at each step draw s_t uniformly and append it to the sequence; return it with ρ additional copies (reinforcement); if s_t is new, add ν + 1 brand-new distinct balls (triggering: the adjacent possible expands). Urn size |U|_t = N₀ + (ν+1)D + ρt, D the number of distinct elements. A variant reinforces only non-new draws. [V] [ar5iv 1310.1953](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1953)
- Heaps' law: ρ > ν, D ~ (ρ − ν)^(ν/ρ) t^(ν/ρ); ρ = ν, D ~ (ν/a) t/log t; ρ < ν, D ~ ((ν − ρ)/a) t, with a = ν + 1 (first version) or ν + 1 − ρ (second). Zipf: f(R) ~ R^(−ρ/ν), occurrence density P(n) ~ n^(−(1+ν/ρ)); Heaps' β = ν/ρ = 1/α only asymptotically, measured on the tail. [V] [ar5iv 1310.1953](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1953)
- Semantic triggering: balls carry labels; the next draw weights by 1 balls sharing the last draw's label, its trigger and those it triggered, and by η ≤ 1 all others; bounds min(νη/ρ, 1) ≤ β ≤ min(ν/ρ, 1) (example ν = 10, ρ = 8, η = 0.3 gives β = 0.375); reproduces the lower entropy and short-interval peak of real sequences (clustering of novelties). η = 1 recovers the basic model. [V] [ar5iv 1310.1953](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1953)
- Data and fitted Heaps exponents: Gutenberg corpus β = 0.45 (single texts 0.50–0.70); Last.fm β = 0.68 (songs) and 0.56 (artists); English Wikipedia β = 0.77 (single editors about 1); del.icio.us tags β = 0.78. [V] [ar5iv 1310.1953](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1953)

## 5. Generative Models That Reproduce Observed Statistics, and Their Closed Forms

### Takeaway
The models with closed forms suitable as checks are: Simon/Yule (rank exponent 1 − α, density exponent 1 + 1/(1 − α)); Gibrat with a lower barrier or Kesten (Pareto exponent ζ solving E[a^ζ] = 1, giving ζ = 1 for Gabaix's cities); Pólya/Arthur urns (Beta limit; lock-in under increasing returns); Tria's urn (Heaps' β = ν/ρ, Zipf ρ/ν); Youn's Yule–Simon with α(N) ≈ x₀/N; the Hausmann–Hidalgo capability model (expected diversity N_p(1 − q(1 − r))^{N_a}); preferential attachment (degree exponent 3); and Frenken–Boschma branching, which inherits Simon's.

[M] [doi:10.1093/biomet/42.3-4.425](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/42.3-4.425); the rank relation γ = 1 − α is used and verified in Youn et al. [V] [ar5iv 1405.3202](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1405.3202)

### Inferences
- Exponents from finite simulations converge slowly (Tria et al. note β = 1/α only asymptotically, on the tail), so checks should compare simulated D(t) against the exact asymptotic form with a stated tolerance, or compare finite-t expectations computed by recursion, not fitted slopes.
- The Simon and Tria processes have exact recursions for E[D(t)] (Simon: E[D(t)] = 1 + α(t − 1) trivially; Tria: a recursion in D and t) that make sharper checks than tail exponents.
- Gibrat–Kesten and Simon give the same Zipf tail by different mechanisms (stochastic growth versus entry); data on city sizes alone cannot tell them apart, a point relevant to any claim that branching "explains" Zipf.

### Gaps
- Frenken and Boschma's equations, whether they derive the Zipf exponent analytically, and whether their model was ever calibrated to data, were not verified.
- No verified source tests Frenken–Boschma's branching model against regional data (as opposed to Simon-type models in general).

## 6. Policy Uses: Smart Specialisation, Relatedness-Based Advice, and Evidence of Effect up to 2026

### Takeaway
Smart specialisation (S3) became an EU cohesion-policy precondition for research and innovation funding (ex-ante conditionality 2014–2020, enabling condition 2021–2027), and relatedness–complexity diagrams (Balland et al. 2019) are the main analytic tool offered for choosing priorities; evidence of effect is correlational and mixed: regions diversifying into related and complex technologies perform better (Rigby et al. 2022), priorities tied to existing structures raise productivity (Lo Conte, Mina and Rocchetta 2023), but many S3 priority choices were weakly matched to regional capabilities, and complexity rankings are sensitive to the measure chosen.

### Cited Findings
- Foray, David and Hall (2009), "Smart specialisation – the concept", Knowledge Economists Policy Brief No. 9 (European Commission); Foray (2015), *Smart Specialisation: Opportunities and Challenges for Regional Innovation Policy* (Routledge): entrepreneurial discovery of domains in which a region can specialise, building on existing capabilities. [M]

### Inferences
- No causal design (randomised assignment, credible difference-in-differences on S3 adoption with a control group) was found; all S3 evidence above is observational. An implementation that "advises" diversification should therefore present density and complexity as declared evidence of a stated grade, in the manner of the infrastructure's policy domain, never as a recommendation.
- Because the EU made S3 a condition for funding everywhere at once, there is no untreated EU control group, which limits identification of S3's effect; variation in the content of priorities (related or not, complex or not) is the usable margin, as Rigby et al. and Lo Conte et al. use.

### Gaps
- I found no verified evaluation published in 2025–2026 of S3's effect for the 2021–2027 period, nor the Commission's own synthesis evaluation results.
- Whether relatedness-based advice outside the EU (e.g. Harvard Growth Lab engagements) has been evaluated for effect was not established.

### Inferences
- Placement by the infrastructure's rule (CLAUDE.md §2.1, §3.7): incidence-matrix measures, entropy decompositions and urn/growth processes name no object in nature, so they belong in foundations (tyche `networks` for bipartite measures, where the complexity ordering already lives per tyche decision 0033; tyche `classes` for entropy partitions; nomos `measurement` for specialisation and shift-share; a stochastic-process module for urns, Simon and Kesten, possibly tyche `processes` or heuresis for recombination search); the sub-domain holds regions, industries, products, technologies, classifications, crosswalks, data readers and the composed models (branching, capability scaling, diversification under relatedness), with economics' `regional` LQs reused.
- Prediction should be a first-class operation with temporal hold-out and the infrastructure's forecast comparisons (tyche: naive forecasts, Diebold–Mariano, Clark–West), since the literature's own evidence standard is out-of-sample entry prediction.
- Every density, ECI or FC value used as policy evidence should carry its threshold, classification level and hold-out score, and complexity should be reported with the eigengap, given Mealy et al.'s finding that regional networks do not split cleanly.

- No verified 2026 literature was found on any of the six questions; the latest verified items are from 2023 (arXiv) and 2024–2025 (search summaries).
